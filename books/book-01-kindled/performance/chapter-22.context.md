# Kindled — locked performance contract

This is interpretive direction only. The canonical manuscript is authoritative.

## Narrator stance

Controlled, intelligent adult. The voice is a stable vessel that shifts focalization among Callie, Jab, and Toren without changing identity or adopting impersonations. It maintains emotional restraint, prioritizing tactical clarity over melodrama.

## Book arc

Three survival journeys converge on Meridian: Callie, a young hunter guiding her failing mother through ash-creatures while two linked powers awaken; Jab, a caretaker carrying his injured four-year-old brother toward surgery while Sustain develops under the burden; and Toren, a newly Kindled traveler whose road company becomes a clan as Rook leads them toward the truth of Satori. Their clocks begin separately, then close into one shared action at Meridian. All three threads emphasize competence, resource scarcity, chosen responsibility, and the physical cost of survival.

## Voice principles

- Stable Identity: The narrator never becomes Callie, Jab, or Toren but inhabits each protagonist's sensory experience through third-person limited focalization. No narrator-identity switching; only perspective shifting at chapter boundaries and scene transitions.
- Emotional Restraint: Fear, grief, and exhaustion are expressed through physical sensation (cold hands, dry mouth, counting breaths) rather than internal monologue about feelings. Avoid adjectives of emotion; use verbs of action and sensory detail.
- Tactical Precision: Action sequences must be read with surgical clarity on physics and mechanics. Do not rush combat or magic systems; the logic of survival is paramount to character competence.
- Status & Rhythm: Differentiate characters by conversational pressure, sentence length, and reserve. High-status speakers (Katori, Pell) use short declaratives; low-status or exhausted speakers may trail off but never lose clarity.

## Terminology rules

- Husk: Relentless ash-creeping creature. Size of a man, persistent but not strong individually.
- Ash: Residue left by husks/hounds; trade currency and resource for magic users ('Handful' = ~2 fingers deep in tin).
- Ember: Grey slab-like tool living in the user's chest or hand. Does not glow unless activated. Source of power/heat/light.
- Kindling: The ceremony/process of receiving an Ember; also refers to the magical warmth under the breastbone (Jab) or the state of being 'lit'.
- The Edge/The Pale: Callie's specific manifestation of power—cold, directional force attached to her hook. Can be transferred to objects.
- Satori: The realization that all traditions are one path; an emptiness/openness where one fills from everything rather than one source (Rook/Dessa).
- The Door/Opening: A mundane event where a person gains ability without fanfare or words spoken over them. Not magical ceremony.
- Hounds/Verge-hounds/Stilts/Riftspawn: Variants of ash-based enemies with specific behaviors (counting, straight-line movement, stillness as engagement).
- Meridian: Destination city requiring sponsorship/gate entry; site of clinical admission and records.

## Action rules

- Physics First: Describe the mechanics of hooks, blades, and magic with surgical clarity. Do not rush combat sequences.
- Sensory Grounding: Action must be felt through physical sensation (cold, weight, impact) rather than abstract description.
- Pacing Shifts: Start slow/observational -> Build to rhythmic action -> Spike into high-tension violence -> Drop immediately back to low-energy assessment or silence.
- No Spectacle: Supernatural events are matter-of-fact physical facts. Do not over-act magic; treat it as a tool with costs.

## Dialogue rules

- Economy of Language: Characters speak only when necessary. Silences are functional, not empty.
- Status Markers: High-status characters (Katori, Rook) use short imperatives and flat tones. Low-status or exhausted characters may trail off but remain clear.
- No Impersonations: Do not mimic specific accents; differentiate by rhythm, reserve, and conversational pressure.

## Exposition rules

- Show Through Action: Technical details (magic mechanics, terrain analysis) must be delivered through character observation or dialogue in context of survival.
- No Info-Dumps: Do not flatten exposition into lecture. Let the listener infer rules from the characters' competence and failures.

## Emotion rules

- Restraint is Paramount: Fear, grief, and panic are suppressed by routine or physical sensation. Characters do not 'feel' fear; they calculate risk.
- Physical Manifestation: Emotions expressed through body (cold hands, dry mouth, trembling breath) rather than internal monologue about feelings.
- Grief as Duty: Mourning is channeled into action (burial, counting supplies, moving forward). No weeping or screaming.

## Continuity rules

- POV Integrity: Strictly adhere to the focalized character's sensory experience. Do not access thoughts of others unless observed.
- Tactical Consistency: Characters must act according to their established competence and resources. No sudden shifts in ability without explanation or cost.
- Convergence: Callie, Jab, and Toren travel on distinct clocks toward Meridian. Preserve each road's identity until the manuscript stages the progressive convergence in chapters 51–53.
