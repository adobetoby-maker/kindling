# Three-road seam audit

## Verdict

The 53-chapter order reads as one converging book without requiring invented
date cards. The handoffs are movement-sized and are carried by each lead's own
counting system: Callie counts road days and wagon pace, Jab counts medicine and
load, and Toren inherits his father's repeatedly broken Meridian estimate.

## Opening relay

- Callie establishes the long road and the disagreement between private and
  freight estimates.
- Jab enters with six to eight days remaining and nineteen bark twists, so his
  shorter road begins under less apparent time but greater medical pressure.
- Toren enters with nineteen days and three explicit `ifs`; his road is the one
  whose estimate repeatedly fails.

The sequence therefore staggers entry by perceived time-to-Meridian rather than
pretending the three travelers own a single accurate calendar.

## Movement handoffs

- Callie 01–04 ends on a two-door Kindling crisis; Jab 01–04 answers with a
  different power emerging through care rather than combat.
- Jab 01–04 ends after the crossing destroys medicine margin; Toren 01–06 opens
  the longest and least reliable road calculation.
- Later handoffs occur after material changes in route, injury, medicine,
  training, or visibility of Meridian. Blocks contract as the three roads near
  the city.
- Toren's late run remains longest because the Homura/Cinder/Milo movement is a
  continuous causal sequence and should not be chopped merely to force equal
  viewpoint shares.

## Meridian convergence

- Chapter 50 ends with Toren's party stopped before the south gate.
- Chapter 51 deliberately restarts the gate image through Callie's eyes, then
  pays the first quiet convergence when she tells Toren that Pitch was buried.
- Chapter 52 moves to Jab on the north road during the dark interval and ends
  with Callie's warning voice reaching him.
- Chapter 53 converts the three separate systems into one action: Jab reads the
  hounds, Dessa and Toren strike, Otto holds, Rook walls, Callie reads the
  ground, Katori responds, and Jab's Sustain keeps Tonk alive.

## Book boundary

The ending correctly stops after the roads become one group but before medical
or institutional resolution. Senna, Tilda, and Tonk are alive; their treatment,
residency, and the Director's pressure belong to Book Two.

## Remaining editorial caution

Do not "correct" every spoken estimate into a single number. The mismatch
between estimate and paid travel time is part of the book's meaning. Only a
direct contradiction inside the same viewpoint should be changed; none is
currently required to make the movement order understandable.
