# Kindled — Book One three-protagonist weave

Owner direction recorded 2026-09-23.

## Locked structural direction

- Book One is one braided novel following **Callie, Jab, and Toren** on three
  independent roads toward Meridian.
- Once all three roads are active, viewpoint blocks rotate in the preferred
  order: **Callie → Jab → Toren**. Their first appearances are staggered by
  the number of travel days each road requires, so the book remains
  chronological and all three clocks reach zero together.
- The rotation happens by **movement**, not by alternating every chapter. The
  handoff is triggered by a meaningful change in **days remaining to Meridian**,
  not by an arbitrary chapter quota. Each protagonist gets enough contiguous
  space for voice, invention, and emotional momentum before the book changes
  viewpoint.
- Book One ends when the three storylines finally meet at Meridian.
- Book Two opens in Meridian and owns the medical, institutional, teaching, and
  residency consequences of all three arrivals.
- Existing prose is source material. No chapter is deleted merely because its
  current placement changes.

## The Meridian countdown is the book's structural clock

The weave should be built backward from one shared event: **Meridian Day 0**,
the day Callie, Jab, and Toren become one story. Each road begins when its own
travel requirement makes it relevant, and every movement carries an internal
`days to Meridian` value. This is the point at which the next protagonist comes
in. The hidden editorial day value is objective; the number spoken by a
character is only that traveler's best estimate and may be wrong.

This is stronger than giving everyone the same number of chapters. The travel
clock is already native to all three characters:

- **Callie counts the road.** Her personal estimate begins at fifteen days;
  Pell's freight-and-weather estimate makes it nineteen to twenty-two. Later
  she explicitly counts nine days, eight days, and finally sees Meridian four
  days away.
- **Jab counts medicine, food, rests, and the weight on his back.** His opening
  gives the road six days if the weather holds and eight if it does not; the
  completed approach has taken sixteen days. His clock is inseparable from
  Tonk's worsening leg.
- **Toren inherits a written road calculation from his father.** Hobb's Wall
  opens with nineteen days remaining, but casualties, the wall, Rook's search,
  and the Level repeatedly destroy the estimate. At Barrow Gate the road is
  recalculated as eighteen days. His is the road whose clock refuses to behave.

The existing figures are therefore excellent dramatic material but are **not
yet one compatible chronology**. The assembly pass must not silently preserve
all three old calendars. It must make a master day map first and revise only
the time references needed to make that map true.

### Opening entry relay

The drafts already contain a nearly perfect three-part introduction:

1. **Callie:** her fifteen-day private calculation is challenged by Pell's
   nineteen-day freight calculation and twenty-two-day bad-weather estimate.
   That disagreement tells us who she is and what the road costs.
2. **Jab:** the Elder measured his full road at eighteen days, or twenty in bad
   weather. When his story opens midway through it, he has six to eight days
   left and nineteen doses. The journey is already consuming the margin.
3. **Toren:** his father's paper says nineteen days to Meridian, with three
   explicit ifs. Unlike the other two roads, his estimate will be catastrophically
   wrong and will keep being rewritten.

That gives the opening rotation a shared promise without artificial sameness:
all three young people have been told Meridian is roughly **nineteen days**
away, and all three discover that a day on a map is not a day paid by a sick
body. Use each character's first serious travel calculation as the handoff into
the next protagonist.

### Countdown handoff rule

1. Work backward from the shared Meridian arrival and assign every retained
   scene a hidden value such as `D-22`, `D-21`, through `D-0`. This is an
   editorial continuity value, not necessarily the number a character believes.
2. Introduce a protagonist when that person's active journey falls inside the
   countdown. A shorter journey enters later; a delay that lengthens a journey
   moves its entry earlier or visibly breaks its estimate.
3. End a viewpoint movement when its arithmetic materially changes: a bridge
   is lost, an injury costs days, food or medicine runs short, a faster route is
   found, or Meridian first becomes visible.
4. Open the next viewpoint on the same day or the next clear countdown
   threshold. The reader should always feel that the three roads are drawing
   together, even though the characters do not know one another yet.
5. After all three protagonists are active, preserve the preferred
   **Callie → Jab → Toren** movement cycle unless action at the convergence
   requires a faster causal handoff.
6. At `D-3`, shorten the movement blocks. At `D-1`, allow scene-level handoffs.
   At `D-0`, stop rotating separate journeys and write one shared action.

The countdown should normally appear inside the prose rather than as a repeated
chapter-card gimmick: Pell revises the atlas estimate, Jab counts bark twists,
Toren reads his father's crossed-out numbers. A sparse part heading such as
`Nineteen Days` or `Four Days` may be used at major compression points, but the
character's concrete arithmetic should carry the clock.

### Intended reading effect

Early in the book, the movements are long because the roads still feel
separate. As the number falls, the blocks contract. Matching weather, distant
light, wall activity, Sag disturbances, and hound movement begin to cross the
seams. By the final day, a consequence in one viewpoint becomes the next
viewpoint's opening condition. The countdown changes the meeting from a
coincidence into an approaching collision the reader can measure.

## Convergence implementation

The later Callie and Jab material used in this assembly already contains the
required causal convergence. Book One retains only the pre-treatment portions:
Toren reaches Meridian, Callie arrives and meets him two doors down, then both
road parties answer the danger around Jab and Tonk. Diagnosis, surgery,
residency, and institutional consequences remain outside this volume.

The retained sequence is:

1. Toren's party reaches Meridian after the Breach fight.
2. Callie's ground-reading reconstructs that fight and brings her party to the
   same wall shortly afterward.
3. Callie or Katori reads the north-road hound danger before the gate staff act.
4. Toren's and Callie's parties choose to go out for the unknown boy carrying a
   child. The joined rescue is the first deed of the future group.
5. Jab's Sustain, Callie's Ward/Edge, Toren's Edge/Stride, Rook's command, and
   the supporting cast each solve a different part of the encounter.
6. Book One ends inside the wall, with Senna, Tilda, and Tonk alive but none of
   their medical outcomes settled. The emotional landing is not safety; it is
   that the three roads have become one problem.

This keeps the meeting causal and earned. It also removes the unresolved
Director-date/Sag-line conflict from Jab's current draft.

## Revision safeguards

- Record an explicit author-seat exception: the O'Connor formula's approximately
  87/13 single-lead POV allocation does **not** govern this volume. The owner's
  three-protagonist structure overrides it. Keep the sentence, readability,
  progression, scene, and action targets; measure viewpoint balance by the three
  complete roads and their narrative load, not by forcing one lead to 87%.
- Do not flatten the three leads into one voice. Keep movement-sized viewpoint
  runs and each lead's own noticing: Callie's hunter/ground logic, Jab's care and
  load accounting, Toren's training/leadership attention.
- Do not repeat the power tutorial three times. Each road should teach a
  different part of the system and let later movements assume what the reader
  already knows.
- Keep all three medical stakes distinct: Senna's long-term treatment, Tilda's
  brain growth, and Tonk's infected leg.
- Preserve the long fight scenes. Cut duplicated explanation and repeated road
  accounting before cutting action, relationship, or earned consequence.
- The existing Callie and Jab drafts are still unreviewed first drafts. Run
  movement-level continuity and reader passes before prose is promoted into the
  woven edition.
- Do not renumber or overwrite the source manuscripts. Build a new recoverable
  woven edition after the Toren spine is confirmed.
