# Kindled Book One — prologue plan

Status: `OWNER` direction recorded 2026-09-27. This is the approved structural
brief, not manuscript prose. The O'Connor seat requires the owner to select
Opus or Fable for the actual drafting run; no author model has been invoked.

## Verdict

Book One should open with a short historical prologue showing **how the old
world became this world**, then cut sixty years forward into the existing
three-road story. It should answer the reader's first practical questions—why
technology is rare, why Meridian survived, where the fractures and young
people's doors entered history—without answering the series mysteries of who
caused the Fall, what the Mars disk truly is, or whether the two events share a
deeper cause.

The prologue is one continuous viewpoint with two dated scenes:

1. **2080 — The Fall.** The unshielded world goes dark.
2. **2090 — The First Door.** A fracture opens near the surviving medical
   enclave, and one of the first witnessed thirteen-year-old awakenings keeps
   a small number of people alive.

Then:

> **Sixty years later.**  
> **Twenty-five days to Meridian.**

Chapter 1 begins exactly where it currently begins, with Callie's six empty
snares. Jab and Toren enter through the existing countdown relay. The prologue
does not require renumbering any of the 53 chapters.

## Why this is stronger than a history summary

- It gives the apocalypse a human cost before presenting the ruins as ordinary
  scenery.
- It shows why a handful of hardened medical/technical sites survived and why
  Meridian matters, preparing the reader for all three medical journeys.
- It lets the reader witness a fracture and an early door once, so Callie's,
  Jab's, and Toren's later abilities arrive as variations on a planted promise
  rather than three disconnected magic systems.
- It establishes the long series question—did the Fall merely precede the
  changed world, or crack it open?—without resolving it.
- It creates a clean scale change: world-level disaster, then sixty years later
  one girl counting eleven feet of wire because that is what disaster becomes
  in a life.

## Viewpoint and owner-supplied names

Use one close-third historical viewpoint, provisionally
`[FOUNDING WITNESS]`: an adult technician, nurse, or medical engineer working in
the electromagnetically shielded facility that will become Meridian. The same
person returns ten years older in the second scene. This continuity turns ten
years of collapse into lived loss rather than a timeline recital.

The thirteen-year-old in the second scene remains `[EARLY CHILD]` until the
owner supplies a name. The child is one of the first *witnessed locally*, never
declared the first Kindled person in the world. Neither placeholder becomes
canon or prose without owner naming.

Do not make either historical character a secret ancestor of Callie, Jab, or
Toren. The main trio should inherit the world, not a bloodline obligation from
the prologue.

## Scene 1 — 2080: The Fall

Target: roughly 900–1,200 words, one developed scene.

### Entry image

`[FOUNDING WITNESS]` is inside the shielded medical wing during an ordinary,
specific procedure. The room's heavy door, isolated circuits, old-fashioned
manual releases, and self-contained power are inconveniences before they become
the reason anything survives.

### Escalation

1. Two or more distant high-altitude flashes turn the windows white without a
   nearby blast.
2. Everything outside the shielded wing dies at once: grid, vehicles, radios,
   clocks, pumps, and unprotected medical equipment. No announcement explains
   who fired or why.
3. The protected wing stays alive on limited power. Its survival creates the
   first moral problem: close the facility to preserve power, or open the heavy
   doors and spend the future on people arriving now.
4. `[FOUNDING WITNESS]` opens the doors. The action should cost something
   immediate—reserve power, a procedure that must be abandoned, or a person who
   cannot be saved—not merely signal virtue.

### Exit image

The ordinary city is dark beyond one working medical corridor. No global
explanation is given. The emotional fact is simple: a world that assumed power
would return has just discovered it will not.

## Scene 2 — circa 2090: The First Door

Target: roughly 1,200–1,600 words, one developed action/learning scene.

### Entry state

Ten years later, the surviving wing has become the center of a rough settlement.
Electricity, instruments, medicine, and every intact component are rationed.
`[FOUNDING WITNESS]` is older, missing people, and accustomed to maintaining a
shrinking island of old-world function.

### Inciting disturbance

Protected instruments register an impossible event. A tear opens close enough
to threaten the settlement. The text may show an unexplained signal or instrument
reaction, but it must **not** name the Mars disk, show it activating, or confirm
that it caused the fracture.

### First local awakening

1. A small Riftspawn expression crosses first. Keep the threat physically
   understandable and small enough for desperate adults with ordinary tools to
   resist; the prologue is not the book's largest fight.
2. `[EARLY CHILD]`, thirteen, reaches for an instinctive answer and opens one
   door under pressure. The recommended expression is **Ward** because it saves
   people without pre-solving the later weapon progression and directly echoes
   Meridian's medical identity. The owner may choose Edge or Resilience before
   drafting.
3. The ability does not win alone. It creates seconds or position; adults still
   have to act, and the child pays a visible reserve/physical cost.
4. When the creature dies, it leaves ash. Nobody yet knows its value or name.
   The witness collects it because the instruments reacted to it, not because a
   mature economy already exists.
5. The protected equipment can document that something happened but cannot
   explain the child's door. This is the first page of a record, not the solution.

### Exit image and handoff

End on the first durable record: date, age thirteen, observed manifestation,
cost, and the unanswered question. That record is the distant ancestor of
Meridian's intake cards, Homura's paired research, Dessa's training ledger, and
the future 2680 schools.

The cut to Callie should be hard and clean:

> **Sixty years later.**  
> **Twenty-five days to Meridian.**

Then: six snares, six empty snares. Do not add explanatory bridge narration.

## Canon boundaries

The prologue may establish:

- stratospheric flashes and the EMP as the world's understood Fall;
- widespread technological death and rare survival inside hardened facilities;
- Meridian's beginning as one such medical site;
- fracture activity roughly ten years later;
- children among the first people to manifest doors;
- an early Riftspawn dying into unexplained ash;
- observation and recordkeeping beginning before anyone has a complete theory.

The prologue must not establish:

- who caused the detonations or whether they were intentional;
- that the Fall definitively damaged the Source/filter;
- that the Mars disk definitively caused the wormholes;
- the disk's true origin or purpose;
- the mature terms Ember, Kindled, Satori, Rank, Yield, or the complete
  three-door theory before people have historically developed them;
- Homura's later conclusion that every door reaches the same force;
- a secret hereditary link to the three protagonists;
- a prophecy naming Callie, Jab, Toren, Rook, or the future 2680 institutions.

## Prose and pacing target

- Total target: **2,200–3,000 words**. Long enough to attach to one witness and
  stage one clear action problem; short enough that the actual novel still
  begins promptly with Callie.
- Two scenes, one substantial break, each near the O'Connor directional
  900–1,000-word scene rhythm without forcing an exact count.
- Accessible crossover register, concrete nouns and actions, short paragraphs,
  and a high share of brief impact sentences.
- No textbook paragraph explaining the seventy-year chronology. Let machines,
  doors, triage, tools, light, silence, and the first written record carry it.
- The prologue's first mystery is global. Chapter 1 immediately makes the stakes
  intimate and countable.
