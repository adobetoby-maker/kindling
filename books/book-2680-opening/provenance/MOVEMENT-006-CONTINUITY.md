## 2. Character state at exit

**Kiva Rowan.** Third Division, Crooked Line third team. **Seated; no longer provisional;
"Catcher."** Sponsor's line and every certificate condition unchanged; "not thereby
classified" (the Chair, the 14th).
- **On the Home Line's roll as catcher** from the 13th: not a seat, not a wage beyond a
  line-hand's day rate when called; called by a Line foreman, with her sponsor's leave while
  she has one, "to set a mark, to read ground, to call a count that is hers and name the ones
  that are not."
- **Three doors.** One more, in the field, on the 5th, under a Home Line direction and Rhea
  Sorn's hand, **offered and not asked.** Still uncertified, unclassified and "not to be asked
  of her." Three clean in all (a yard; the Pit; the Old Yard). Never twice inside a fortnight.
- **Injuries.** The left arm went candle-white to a hand's breadth above the elbow. Elbow back
  Friday the 7th toward evening; wrist Saturday night; palm, thumb and last two fingers Sunday
  morning. **Aske, Monday the 10th: "as before… and not a hair less."** The first two left
  fingers are unchanged: they bend, grip and report pressure and nothing else. **Nothing lit
  until the new year** ("not a step, not a ward for a tap"). The knee is still strapped and
  complained once, running.
- **The disk.** Cold on the 5th before, during and after; cold on the oak table in front of
  twelve people on the 13th. Fourth column entry of the 10th in section 4.
- **The dream.** No recurrence. No fever is mentioned on any night of this movement.
- What she now knows: a count that she takes through two hands is wrong by half a count; a
  planted corner can be leaned to another still-door as a plank; a ward curved the other way
  turns a throw a quarter round; the nearest road for a throw may be the one with the drain
  under it; the notch on her pen and the lamps' T-0 are two things.
- **Handoffs named** (the list of the 30th, as it stands at exit): the cord, all three turns
  (Pru); the slack of a hem on her left side (she takes a right-hand corner or is called to);
  tin lids with a lip; the pin of a seal and whether wax has gone off (Anwen has the wax); a
  button and a cuff; **the count of a thing that keeps time (somebody else's, named before).**
- **The pattern.** Answers withheld: at the Halden yard ("Sorn's right that it isn't hers to
  have offered"); at the leaning post on the 1st ("Tell me… Not mend it"); to the junior on
  the 16th. One taken and paid for: she knelt and called the slack herself on the 2nd, half a
  count late, and nipped Pru's fingers; she said so to the yard.
- **Offers.** Rhea Sorn's card (section 4), signed on the back by her four on the 15th.
- Her book has never had a page torn out. It has a spring, a summer and an autumn in it.

**Mara Rowan.** Reader of the trunk for Rusk; found that the drums fall in in the order the
Ferren main reaches them; put it under seal and was right. **Given the hill, the bank and the
stopping of it on the 3rd.** Found the sluice. Built the trough. Left the hill on the night
("I can only manage it from two miles off"). Took the Ferren bank out of step with
twenty-four hands, each cell on its own count, standing between each and the next with her
left palm; gave the odd cells to Rusk at the eighteenth. **Her left, reading hand was starved
to the wrist and was eased inside the hour with 1.2 drams of Team Seven's sealed ward-trace.**
A week before she reads a drum; a month before she reads a bank. Has written eleven sheets in
seven columns, *How the Ferren bank was taken out of step, with what was got wrong first,* for
the coast boat. Her sealed page is still sealed.

**Orla Dane.** Refused Vane's saw without a reason and said so. Climbed the Graywater stair to
tell Rhea Sorn that neither of them signs for the girl. Signed for the hand on the 5th: "I've
seen it clean twice. I've seen it missed three times… And I don't know that it will work."
Moved her crate a pace north. **Jessamy Hart has answered** (section 4). She did not write in
the proof; she gave it to Kiva.

**Anwen Pryce.** Has the wax. Had the sealed reserve out of the press on the 5th. Refused it as
salt on the 4th and proposed it as medicine on the 6th. Stood the uphill end of a walking
wall, went eight paces past it alone at number fifty-eight, came off a join before it had her
and said so as she went; **Mace has left her name where it was** (*Said so as she went*). Read
the *why.* Called the step in the middle of ten wards for the four-knot string. **Has told her
mother.**

**Perrin Cade.** Proposed the four-dram loan. **380 kilos for thirty counts, cut in the stone;
a place in his Hall's second team from the new year.** Carried a litter, an old man in a
chair, and a boy out of a shaft by one arm. Two to the bar with Brask. **Two ribs sprung, not
broken;** strapped again. Said that he does mind, all of it, into a bar.

**Kellan Renn.** **Has taken the Halden yard** (Tuesdays and Thursdays, nothing owed, his own
cuff; Birch: "They've not got worse"). On Seven's book "as a blade who's told where he's
wanted." Cut a clean rung in the yard on a catcher's word that was by the book and wrong, and
watched it cook a bank. Cut the eleventh knot on Pru's word. Read his mother's hand.

**Noa Bexley.** Sat the School's mast and called the mark and then the drum. Went to the

**Jude Talley.** Seven's Kill by name. Asked aloud whether the sealed reserve or the red bags

**Pru Finch.** A catcher with a sheet of her own. Folded a ward under a loaded cord twice

**Selka Dray.** In an infirmary chair, the leg in a board; on her feet "by the third month."
**Called the count on the 5th, on three terms** (a needle on Kiva; Marten carries the chair;

National Measure under seal. "I'd have seated you on that. I'd not have seated you on the
other." Not a friend. Means to beat Kiva in a lane at Saltmere.

**Lowen Brask.** Felt the post in the dark and said what wanted saying at the gap. Called the
slack for Pru. Named the string as a hold with nobody keeping it. Two to the bar.

**Rhea Sorn.** Refused to have the three-door offered on her card on the 2nd ("It's too

**Hollis Vane.** Foreman of the response. Gave the post three days. Found the lane sheet at two
in the morning. Third direction in the ledger, and the cost after. Moved, by his own account,

**Ysra Crest.** Lent the hearing room. "Nobody in this yard will ask." A witness on the 5th.

**Others.** **Examiner Rusk:** wrong about the firebreak, said so in the street, took off his

## 4. Records and rulings

**Mara's sealed paper** (sealed by the Chair on the 28th; opened in the street on the 3rd):
*If it is the drain: drum twenty-eight, on Monday the third of next month, with two dry drums
between. If it is the copper or the ash: nothing, and I am wrong. M. Rowan, Grade Two.*

**Mara's half sheet of the 2nd** (the assumption, written "so that it can be wrong in front of
everybody"): *That the water moves because the pumps move it. Stop the pumps and the main lies
still. Then a bank may be quenched and filled fresh, and it will keep the time that it is
given.* It was wrong within the hour.

**Aske's cards.** The 30th: single doors and dark hands from that morning; *A crossing, one, in
seven days, as before. The longer thing: not on my card. If somebody with a slate means to
direct it, he sends for me and I say the price to his face, before, with the candidate
listening.* The 10th of the tenth month: nothing lit until the new year.

**The *Standard Lane Manual,* eleventh edition** (withdrawn 2603; the superseded stacks): six
ruled columns in print and a seventh, *The lane with the crooked posts. Why: because the road
isn't straight,* with the junior archivist's pencil beside it. A plate shows two rows of posts
on a curve, the last of the south row a pace out with a bar across its top. The School "set
the posts fair" in 2390 (eleven days, in the bursar's accounts) and could not shift the last.
"It kept the why and lost the what."

**The Home Line's lane sheet** (a copy in the record hand of something older; from the chest
under the bearer's box; read by the Reader on the 3rd):
*The lane with the crooked posts. At Meridian, under the south wall, on the line of the hill
drain. / What: nine posts, set as the drain runs, which is not straight. The ninth is the
sluice post. It stands out from the wall for the bar. / How: run it dark, from the sheds. Call
the turn before the eighth, aloud, and say who has the bar. Two to the bar. Nine turns shuts
the hill from the low streets. / Went wrong: ran it straight and silent, and met the ninth.
Also: two came to the bar, and nobody had said which two. / Cost: a collarbone. A cellar in the
Hythe, the night that nobody had said. / Why: because the road isn't straight. S.V.*
Nobody on the page knows whose initials those are.

## 5. The grid and the string (shown, not explained)

**What the page shows about the Step.**
1. The three Ferren pumps, on a bank in step, take their stroke together. Since the 17th of the
   ninth month the Ferren main, the storm drain for that side of the city, rises and falls on a
   count of four and four: two and a half fingers at the foot of Stopgate, a hair under the
   School's wall, and growing at the top of the hill.
2. The drums fell in in the order the main reaches them, two days apart: 19, 20, 21, then
   **25, (26, 27 dry), 28, 29.** Drums 22, 23 and 24 on the Kiln loop, where the copper leaves
   the drain, never fell in.
3. Two quenched drums did not stop it. Filled fresh, they were in step inside sixty counts.
4. In a lead-lined trough, a single plain cell on a stool, joined to a stepped bench bank by
   nothing but twelve paces of rising and falling water, leaned for an hour and went in.
5. **Nobody says that water can step a cell.** Mara: "the water in a box." Adlam's patent does
   not mention water. Why a tear's hold put the bank into step in the first place is not
   touched.

**The drill, the 2nd** (the Halden license yard; Birch's slate; a contained six-knot string):
1. The string ignored a lit gate twelve paces off, walked to the far end of the trough, and
   put two cords into the water. "It doesn't want the bright thing. It wants the thing that
   keeps time."
2. Kellan cut the top rung on Yarrow's word, with the cloth by the book hard against the
   trough. The open ash ran across the canvas and up the cord into the water. Yarrow bagged
   under thirty. **All six bench cells cooked together inside a breath;** the pump stopped.
3. **With the pump dead the water went on rising and falling. The string was pumping it.**
4. A fresh can on the stool went in at fifty-eight counts.
5. A boat-hook under the cord frosted from end to end and was taken; the fitter's left glove
   cracked.
6. Pru's ward, for one tap on the slack, folded before the haul, lifted the cord. Called half a
   count late by Kiva, it nipped Pru to the first joint. Called by Brask, it lifted clean, and
   **the water missed a beat and the cell on the stool fell out for a count.**
7. With the bung pulled and the trough empty, the cords hung ten counts and came apart into
   loose ash; the string read again and went to the gate.

**The string of the 5th.** Nobody on the page gives it a new name. The page calls it "a string
laid on its side" and "a string abreast"; Brask calls it a hold with nobody keeping it; Vane's
slate says "a string of twelve, abreast, every knot reading." It is never called a Barrel, and
no yield class is given.
1. The tear lies in the surface of the road over a shallow drain that is already keeping time,
   and runs on uphill toward ground that is not yet in step.
2. Knots rise through it side by side, eight paces apart, each on two legs, each reading,
   joined by a cord from which a frost hem hangs to the setts and stands above.
3. It does not turn to a lit gate. A head turns to a lit cell box in a house, and the cord
   bellies toward it.
4. Cut at the bottom end, the cord whips back to the next knot, and a new knot comes up at the
   top: "It's being paid out." Twelve rose; ten stood when they were cut.
5. Where the drain dives, the tear stops. It then reaches with an arm to the nearest open
   water.
6. With its arm in the main it hauls and eases the main; the water goes on moving with the
   pumps stopped.
7. Taken off the water and planted in dry ground, with the sluice shut behind it and the bank
   dead, it had nothing to drive. Cut on one word with the corner given to the ground, the hem
   lay down. The tear, no longer held, gave three husks and one ordinary string and was closed
   by the Home Line in under an hour.
8. Its ash is loud. What came up after the hem was down "reads as this year's ash reads, and no
   louder."

**How the bank was taken out of step** (the method the page shows): nothing in the water that
keeps time; the pumps stopped; every cell quenched on one word; fresh cans, each salted with a
pinch of plain ash that "has never heard a beat"; a card under each handle with its own count,
"not two alike in this room"; a reader standing between each cell and the next for a slow ten;
a cell that falls back in is quenched and filled again; each pump restarted on its own eight
cells. Twenty-six minutes. It needs the main to be still, which on the night needed the arm
off the water and the sluice down.

**Instrument evidence.**
- **The notch and the lamps came apart.** The notch on Kiva's pen fell eleven seconds after her
  second cliff, as on both earlier occasions. The lamps (every window in the School, six
  hundred on the hill, and twenty-four dead needles at the pump house, which lifted "by a hair"
  with nothing in a single can) jumped about five minutes later, at *back,* when the hem came
  down. At the Pit the two fell in the same instant. Noa: "One of them keeps time with you. The
  other keeps time with that… I don't know what either is." Whether other masts showed the
  notch on the 5th is not on the page.
- **Noa's three columns.** People: median Hold 6.1 against forty years at 5.3 to 5.5; three
  Fire readings at seventeen; Stonehand's plate 340 for thirty-one years, then 350, 355, 362,
  380 since the thaw. The other thing: tears on the district board, fourteen against a usual
  four or five; skins, none in sixty years and nine this year; a Flask that walked a bus; a hold
  stood by husks; a string abreast. Both rise, and faster since the fifth month. Saltmere
  (Yeo's wire): a harbour bank of thirty-six cells in step from twenty past two on the 17th of
  the ninth month and not out; tears on the coast board nineteen against seven; skins eleven;
  the Saltmere school's plate beaten four times. **A line Noa will not vouch for:** a ship's
  engineer "off a hull from over the water" says that his own port's bank did the same at the
  same hour. "Not entered as fact."
- Nobody on the page says why. "It's two columns." Sent to the Charter Board under the Chair's
  seal, Adlam second, with a line of Yeo's and Lumb's notion of asking who else has a keeper
  with a book.

## 9. Items for owner or editorial attention

Routes the author chose where the packet left room, and places where the draft may have gone
further than intended:

1. **The mechanism is a storm drain.** The Step walks the Ferren main because three stepped
   pumps make the water rise and fall. The page shows order, a sealed prediction, a field
   confirmation and a bench confirmation; it never says that water can step a cell. This turns
   Movement Five's "nine drums" into five and its eighteen days into ten (from the 27th).

   cost is six hundred households on boxes for five days and an examiner's public admission.

   with an arm in the water is itself a pump.

## 10. Deliberately not done

it is cold. The dream does not recur; the name Unroofed Sky is not on the page; nobody

## 11. What Book Two opens on

State on the evening of Sunday the 16th of the tenth month, 2680:

- **The grid.** Meridian has taken one municipal bank out of step by a method that is written
  down in seven columns and on its way to the coast. Seven drums wait their turn. Saltmere's
  harbour bank is still one engine. A ship's engineer says that it is not only this country.
- **The rifts.** A tear can lie down in a road over water that keeps time, pay out a string
  abreast, and pump. A long-forgotten sluice stopped one. Nobody knows how many
  drains have a gate.
- **The record.** Three sheets are before the Charter Board. A pump keeper of 2604 and a Master
  of the 2440s have both written that there was no roof. Every pump house on the coast has a
  remarks column that nobody has read. A plate has been recognized and has said nothing.
- **The people.** Kiva is seated, unclassified, on the Line's roll, stood down from every door
  until the new year, with a company's card to the end of the third month and eight fingers
  that report. Seven is fourth, in its own right, with 25.53 held and 0.8 sealed, and goes to
  the National Measure at Saltmere in the third month with Halden License, Old Four and
  Twenty-Eight. Anwen has a summer on the night van. Perrin has his Hall's second team. Kellan
  has a yard. Jude has a yield line. Pru has a sheet and a red tag. Selka Dray will be on her
  feet. Mara has one reading hand on a card until the eleventh month. Orla has a card from the
  south coast.
- Withheld for Book Two and after, intact: what the disk is for; the first book; the cause of
  the Step; why a hold stood; what the notch and the lamps are; who else has dreamed; the
  filter; the Makers; Kiva's classification; whether three doors can be made safe, taught, or
  done twice.
