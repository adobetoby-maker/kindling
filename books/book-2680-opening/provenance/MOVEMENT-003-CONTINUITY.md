## 2. Character state at exit

**Kiva Rowan.** Provisional Third Division, Crooked Line third team. **Ysra's conditions
unchanged. Orla's certificate unchanged: recovery only, dark hands.** No incident on her
file.
- **The sequence.** One correct deliberate two-door sequence, Stride into stance, on the
  26th, under a field direction and outside her certificate. About nine seconds of stance.
  Fourteen recorded misses before it (tries 1–14 as in the calendar; the tenth was in a
  sanctioned lane against her sponsor's instruction). It has not been repeated. Orla has
  seen it once and will not sign: **"Show me six."**
- What she now knows about it: a drop and a jam; that leaning one door into the other is
  still a jam; that the Ember can be shut at the top of the bound because a Stride is paid
  for at take-off; that between the doors the speed is in her body and in neither door;
  that the stance must be set before the ground, as a pan is set before the ash; that the
  speed goes into the ground through her and is not a blow to spend on an opponent or a
  lever; that being pleased opens a door as surely as being frightened, so she must count
  through it. What she does not know: what carries the work across the gap, or why the
  gap costs more than both doors.
- Skills gained: bagging on a purse sheet; calling a cord check; handing a task across on
  called words (*Yours, Mine,* a counted three); home rule nine; reading an empty push;
  the open hand (her first down in a sanctioned lane); the bar as a gate against open
  hands; shutting the Ember at the top of each bound.
- Her mistakes, in her own book: she called the whole floor dark; the foreman said he
  would leave a gate and she did not; she set the mark by the loudest pattern and never
  asked which was nearest; she walked off with her own bag and did not watch the string.
  Earlier: she broke her word to Orla in the lane.
- **Injuries.** Left knee: re-injured on the 18th ("the size of a small loaf"), strapped;
  no landings until the 2nd of next month. She bounded once on the 26th inside that ban
  and landed without harm. Left first and second fingers: unchanged through the 25th
  (slow, late; she lost a cord turn in them on the 10th and hooks the left elbow instead of
  trusting them); bone white and nearly dead on the lever on the 26th; **eased by Healer
  Aske with ward-trace ash inside the hour. Aske: not burned, "starved"; they will sulk a
  week and then be where they were on the 25th, "slow, and late, and not finished mending.
  No better."** Had she come the next day, the verdict would have been worse. **Permanent
  loss remains the Movement Five cost.** Heat-lines: all three ached their whole length
  after the sequence, "a muscle used for the first time"; not burned; the bottom line lit
  only in jams (tries 2, 4, 5, 8, 14). Nosebleed after the sequence, and again at the cart
  when she nearly laughed. Fever on the night of the 26th, a slow heat and a headache, no dream.
  **Stood down a fortnight from the 26th: no lanes, no floors, nothing lit.**
- **The disk.** Cold on the 7th and the 25th. **Warm once, on the 26th:** it began after
  she had shut the still door with the lever on its pin, not before; "warm as a hand";
  it lasted from the shelf to the top of the road stair and was cold by the keeper's
  gate. She asked it nothing; it did nothing. Kennel ash is still in three grooves. Selka
  Dray saw her holding something at her collar and was told only "a thing I wear. It's
  warm. It isn't, generally." Rhea Sorn saw the fist at the collar from across the floor.
  Orla and Mara have been told. Orla did not put out a hand to feel it.
- **The dream.** No recurrence in sleep. **One waking fragment on the 26th**, three or
  four counts, standing on the shelf with the disk warm in her fist: the height; the
  lights coming by every road; **new: each light has its own note, none in tune; she
  tries to count them and cannot keep a number past four;** the lights touch and the
  notes become one note; no sky; what is above comes down; **new: below, something small
  and exact answers, "tick."** Afterward the one note stays with her and she cannot hum
  it. She reported it to Mara the same night; Mara wrote it down, including that "tick"
  is "the word she'd use for both" the bag and the dream. The name Unroofed Sky is not on
  the page.
- Papers in her field book: Dunmore's T-0 line for the 26th; the Hanley Street copy of
  the 2446 entry with its drawn margin.

## 4. World facts established on the page

- **Practice ash.** Third Division draws **half a dram a week** from the Low Lane store, on
  Mondays, in a wax-stoppered glass the size of a thimble. The three-door drill costs "a
  hundredth and a bit." A drop cost 0.04 and a jam 0.09 on the Low Lane mast.
- **The Flask** (Druce): twenty Handfuls in the yield book, **480 drams.** The ash does not
  make one large knot; it makes **eight, one above another on one cord: "a string."** Each
  knot is about **sixty drams** and lends its own limbs; the whole stands about as high as
  a tram's roof on six thick legs, the lowest two knots down among the legs. **The top
  knot reads**, out to twenty or twenty-five paces; the others walk where it points. Cut
  the top knot and the string is **blind for "a slow hundred"** (Druce has seen eighty)
  until the next knot wakes. The yield book calls a knot **a rung.** Rungs are cut from
  the top, one at a time, each caught before the next. The last two carry the legs and are
  cut together, or "it sits down wherever it happens to be" (**the sit-down**). "A Handful's
  a fight… A Flask is a schedule."
- **Purse sheet:** waxed canvas six paces square, lead in the hem, a cord through the hem.
  The catcher sets and calls it; three more take corners; the neck is hauled shut, three
  turns and a wax seal. **Bagged inside the count is tinned, for the book;** the bag is
  carried off dark and panned at a table **within the hour.** What is tinned on a bought
  Kennel floor goes back on the Kennel's shelf.
- **Company:** four teams, sixteen hands, one chartered foreman. Each team takes one rung
  whole (hold, cut, bag, carry) in yield order while the other three mind the string; four
  wards make the wall for every rung, the cutting team's ward in the middle calling the
  step; the foreman's crew takes the bottom four. **Mark: fifty of the sixty bagged inside
  the count at Grade Second or better, nothing printed, nothing fouled, nobody hurt,** and
  the foreman's four columns for the company. Thirty-one teams; companies qualified two a
  day from Monday the 24th; a chartered company supplies the safety crew by the day
  (Graywater had Wednesday). Reserves carry yellow cards and are seated when a team is
  short.
- **A gate.** Chartered crews leave a wall lit ten or twelve paces off through every
  count, and a blind string walks to it and leans (Bettan: "It likes a gate. Always leave
  it a gate."). It was a custom and not in the Circuit's book. **As of the night of the
  27th it is a rule: "A gate shall be left lit."**
- **Professional practice at the weir:** eight purse sheets laid in a row, one a rung; the
  ward line walks the face down the row; the blade asks the catcher before every cut and
  waits for the word. Rhea Sorn, with no Stride, stands on the ground and lets her blade
  be as long as the cut needs for about seven-tenths of a second. Student blades go up the
  back of the string on their struts and light at the top of a bound.
- **Graywater relief:** the new holder brings her wall up inside the old one; the old one
  leans on it until hers holds nothing, then lets go. "If you can hear the join, it's a
  bad one." A ward-holder stands no more than four hours.
- **The south link** wants about **four hundredweight** to bring the lever level for its
  pin: "one good still-door with his boot on the shelf, or two big men." It is sprung to
  shut. The breaker-and-quench distinction from Movement One stands; opening the link
  drops the whole branch, including the Kiln's lattice.
- **The comb:** the seventh reed, one of the four unnamed, is carded on the reference
  shelf as **I, for induction.** Halloran had heard it twice in nineteen years, both times
  for ash swept from a cell that had been salted late: "A tune leaves that. Something
  that's been made to keep step."
- **Second step, attached** (new): a team may answer a Flask-yield call only as an
  attached recovery and support team under a chartered crew until it has stood one whole
  Flask under a foreman without remark.
- **Circuit tapes** are open to chartered parties thirty days after the standings post.
