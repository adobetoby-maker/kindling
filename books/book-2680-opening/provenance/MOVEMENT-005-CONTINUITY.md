## 2. Character state at exit

**Kiva Rowan.** Provisional Third Division, Crooked Line third team; **place, conditions and
sponsor unchanged**, by the Chair's own statement on the 19th.
- **Certificate** (Orla, the 24th of the seventh month, after the sixth counted crossing):
  *One crossing, Ember to still, low, to a mark. Declared to a count. On a sanctioned mast,
  read by the Chair's reader. One in seven days, the left hand seen by a healer the next
  morning. Not within three paces of open ash or any charged cell. Not certified: a stack of
  any kind. Any third door. Anything I have not seen six times.* Recovery only, dark hands,
  stands as before.
- **Chair's conditions added** (the 2nd of the seventh month): no second door on any Circuit
  floor save by a crossing declared to a count and read from a sanctioned mast by a reader of
  the office, at the time; a stack so read is an incident; not at the Bench; recovery role
  only on any floor with a contained rift. On the 19th of the ninth month the mast condition
  was made general, for any candidate with more than one door, at Selka Dray's request and
  under her name.
- **The six.** Complete: goes 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (section 3). Orla: "It says it wasn't luck.
  That's the whole of it."
- **Three doors.** Order **hand, Ember, still** ("catch, carry, plant"). Five tries on the
  travelling mast (section 3): a jam, a clean hand-to-Ember crossing, an *off,* a stack she
  called herself, and **one clean, on the 29th of the eighth month.** One more in the field,
  under direction, on the 17th of the ninth. **Not certified, not classified, "not to be asked
  of her."** She has never done it twice in a week, never without a gap of days before, and
  both clean ones emptied the hand.
- What she now knows: a ward can be passed (its blow becomes the bound; she does not push
  off); a ward has a grip and must be folded shut, not let go; the second gap comes "out of a
  place already emptied"; a stack can arrive "dressed as thrift"; a floor has a behind, and it
  may be underneath; a hold cannot be broken by taking a corner; **across a gap the work comes
  with her and the beat does not** (felt once, in the first gap at the Pit).
- Skills and corrections gained: the fold (from Pru), with the first two left fingers held
  straight out of it, "passengers"; setting the stance by its corners; saying *one* on a
  landing before her reader; asking a team's question with the bad answer stated; **"Cord.
  Yours. All of it,"** said before she fumbles.
- **The pattern she is working on.** Answer withheld: to the Chair on the 2nd ("She's right"),
  at the kitchen table when Perrin voted no, to Anwen in the drying yard, to Dray on the
  cinders. Answer given, and caught: "I'd take the yard" to Kellan on the 25th.
- **Injuries.** Left knee: strapped on the 3rd of the seventh month; not mentioned as strapped
  afterward; no trouble on any landing. Heat-lines: the bottom thread hot once (the jam of the
  1st of the eighth month). **Left first and second fingers: permanent loss of fine sensation.**
  The road: white 58 counts (go 5), 75 (go 6), 95 (go 7), 130 (go 8, to the knuckle, blue at
  the nails), four minutes (hand to Ember, the 8th), ten minutes, twenty minutes and dull to
  the morning, then the whole hand gone from the wrist for forty hours after the clean
  three-door, returning "to less" (the first finger four breaths late; the second reporting
  the push and not the pin). Friday the 16th: one gap in a lane, white nearly two hours.
  Saturday the 17th, 7 a.m., Aske's card: *No go before the 23rd.* About 2:30 p.m.: two gaps.
  The hand went candle-white to two inches above the wrist. Wrist back Sunday evening; palm,
  thumb and last two fingers Monday morning. **Aske, Saturday the 24th:** the two fingers bend
  and grip and report pressure; they do not tell edge from flat, hot from cold, or one thread
  from two; "That's gone." Ward-trace ash is no use ("nothing in there for the ash to speak
  to"). The Order's infirmarian has written that a year's rest is for a hand that is hungry,
  not one that has been emptied.
- **The choice, as the page shows it:** declined the Order's year on the 4th of the seventh
  month ("I want the six and the third door more than I want the fingers sure. I may be
  wrong. Nobody told me to."); spent the week's one crossing in a lane on the 16th; on the
  17th stated the card and the two gaps herself, heard the price from her healer across the
  floor and from the foreman's slate, said that she was frightened, and said yes.
- **The disk.** Cold throughout: after each of the four crossings, after the clean three-door,
  at the Pit. She has written in the fourth column nine times in this movement (*Cold,* with
  two longer entries: the 1st and the 20th of the ninth month). She does not mind it.
- **The dream.** No recurrence. No fever on the night of the 17th; Mara's sheet has the date
  and "Nothing. No fever."
- Her book's last line: *Rowan, K. Catcher. Hand as before.*

## 9. Items for owner or editorial attention

Routes the author chose where the packet left room, and places where the draft may have gone
further than intended:

1. **The months are numbered** (fifth to ninth). Earlier chapters only say "the ninth month"
   for Mara's re-sit. Review should check that nothing in chapters 1–32 contradicts spring as
   the fifth month.
2. **Ysra Crest's history is new:** a ward-holder on the 2660 Bench who signed Hart's entry
   and sat on the board that sealed his file, and who did not speak to Orla for ten years. It
   gives her ruling on Orla weight; it also changes how chapters 1 and 8 read.
3. **An earlier bearer crossed.** Thirza Bly's line says the plate was warm once, after her
   first crossing, and cold through nine more. It makes Kiva's experience a pattern of two
   and implies a multi-door bearer in the 2460s. It gives no cause and no use. If the owner
   wants the disk's earlier record left blank, this is the line to cut; the watch could as
   easily refuse leave.
4. **The stronger variety is "a hold":** four husks standing a square with a tear inside it,
   echoing the Home Line's own held tears. It follows the skin (a ward's echo) and the Step
   (keeping time together) and adds a tear outside any lattice. Brask names it and refuses to
   say that anybody showed them. This is the movement's largest invention and it leans on a
   new detail of Home Line practice (a line leaned from holder to holder; "the weight goes
   round").
5. **What the three doors did** is a catch: a ward under a loaded cord for one tap, the throw
   taken as a bound, the haul planted and held as a corner for ten counts, then released on
   the same word as three cuts. The new claim is that the work crosses a gap and a stepped
   count does not. It is felt, once, by Kiva, and measured by nobody. It points at Movement
   Six and should be checked against what that movement is meant to keep.
6. **The permanent loss follows three choices on the page:** the Order's year declined; the
   week's crossing spent in a lane, where Kellan fairly used the gap; and two gaps the next
   day against a healer's card, with the price read out first. Aske and the Order's
   infirmarian agree afterward that rest would not now help.
7. **The Order made a kind offer** and is shown as right about hands. This is a larger and
   gentler role than the packet's "pressures."
8. **The hearing rulings** are the author's: three seals; 56.5 advanced; a dram each to the
   shelf and the Halden office; the general red-tag rule; no ash above I-trace on any Circuit
