## 2. Character state at exit

**Kiva Rowan.** Provisional Third Division, Crooked Line third team. **Ysra's conditions and
Orla's certificate are unchanged** (recovery only, dark hands). At the Weighbridge she opened
no door.
- **The sequence, now also called a crossing.** Four sanctioned goes on the Circuit's
  travelling mast in the Old Yard (section 3). **Two count toward Orla's six.** The Pit's does
  not. She has landed on the cross at the crooked post for the first time.
- What she now knows: that she can hear the pen and must not; that a called *off* in the air
  is a thing Orla will pay for; that she can feel the quarter-dram go "all at once… like a
  breath"; that there is a second, cheaper, softer road in which the still door is opened
  before the Ember has finished shutting, and that it is **a stack**, not a crossing ("the
  gap's the price because the gap's the point… the wall between the rooms"); that the meter
  (the bottom thread) warmed in the air on that go and she did not call it.
- Skills and corrections gained: Pike's "bad-day grip" (the cord at the hooked elbow from the
  first turn; slower by a count); to look the whole circle round before kneeling; **"Who else
  reads it?"** before anything is lit, with range and duration declared; eyes on the gap
  during a count; catching a Flask rung (three, at 44, 46 and 52).
- Her mistakes, in her own book: she answered Noa with the dictionary; she called a lure and
  never asked who else could read it; four of them watched a cord and nobody watched the gap;
  she filed Lowen Brask's caution "on the left" and should have put it on the right; she was

2. One go in a night, never two ("not twice in one flood").
3. Before the line she says aloud whether the bottom thread is cold; *one* means she sits.

## 4. The four records

**The 2446 register margin** (Hanley Street; the Works' late record hand, taught to night
clerks until about 2480; read by the Reader on the 7th): *Not for the column, by the Warden's
order. A sister of the Even Hand was at the wicket before it was light. Sent down by her
Master to ask whether the city's lamps had gone in the night. She did not know why she was
sent. Told, yes, all. The Warden says a question is not a fault and is not to be entered.
Entered here. Nobody had told them. R.* The Master of the hill house in 2446 was Hesper
Annick.

**The *Founders' History.*** Ninth edition (given to first-years): *The nine-ringed plate
carried by the first teachers is preserved by the School.* Fourth edition: *The plate of nine
rings went out with the Line and is not the School's to show. It is released to one who
carries it. We have not been told why and have not thought it ours to ask.* The Reader has
asked to see the School's plate for thirty years and is told it is "being conserved."

**Directions to the Unplaced** (Home Line ledger, back section; one entry before Vane's; same
record hand; read on the 11th): *Ferren levee, the spring flood, 2471. Direction to Tansy
Wold, unplaced. Two doors, hand and still. On no school's roll and no company's. One crossing,
hand to still, called aloud before. Reason: the sluice wheel wanted a ward to clear its race
and a stance to turn it, and there was no reaching it twice. Result: turned. Eleven streets
dry. Cost: her left hand, without feeling to the wrist, that night and after. Not returned by
midsummer. She says the crossing took more than both together. I believe her. I would not
direct it twice in one flood, nor to anyone I had not watched miss. E.K., watch.* And beneath:
*Entered on the Line's roll as catcher, autumn. Hand as before.* The Line's roll for the 2470s
was recopied in the 2550s without remarks. Nothing more is known of her.

**Annick, Personal** (the eleventh box; the green notebook; read by Kiva on the 25th). The
page quotes the prologue's entry word for word and in part: *Fever again. Fourth time with
this dream…*; the roads and the different lights; *their light touches mine*; *The sky is not
there*; *Not crushing. Full*; *It is paying attention*; *Every lamp in Meridian answers at
once*; *I wake before I find out. Every time*; *I have told no one in the house*; *The sky
unroofed. That is the only way I can say it.*
- What matches Kiva's account: every road; lights all unlike; the lights touching; no sky;
  what is above coming down; the word *Full*; every lamp answering.
- What differs: Annick is **among** the lights ("mine"); Kiva has only been high up and apart.
  Annick has **no notes and no tick.** Annick gives what the lamps do (out, white, a color
  without a name); Kiva never saw. Annick adds "It is paying attention," which Kiva had not
  had words for and recognizes.
- What it does not give: **a date** (the entry cannot be tied to the lamp night; the margin

  claims the Step, and all ash exhibiting the Step, however produced.* The caution: *Not to be
  introduced to an unstepped bank under load.*
