## 1. Verdict in brief

**Keep reading. This is the strongest movement of the three, and it delivers every beat its packet asked for.**

The two-door sequence is earned the hard way. Kiva misses it fourteen times, each miss is named, priced in ash and written in a book, and the three ideas that finally make it work come from three other people's trades: Kellan's ("shut it at the top"), Perrin's ("arrive before you're there") and her own ("it's a pan"). When it lands on the shelf at `23:91–113` the reader already knows every part of it and what it will cost.

The movement's best idea is quieter. Kiva learns the right lesson from a human opponent in chapter 20 and applies it to a thing that is not a man in chapter 22. Her error burns a professional's hands and puts a branch dark. Her own summary is the truest line in the book so far: *"A man with nothing in front of him waits. A husk goes looking. I'd been fighting a man."* (`24:499`).

Three bounded repairs:

1. **The Pit is dated the 26th, and by the page's own weekdays it falls on the 29th.** One number, written five times, with a short tail of dependent dates. Around it sits a list of about twenty small located slips (ages, counts, elapsed times, two name collisions, one unestablished piece of knowledge).
2. **The cost of the two big calls never lands between people.** Anwen warned against the dark floor twice and is never heard on it again. The one dissenter, Tolliver, gets no line afterward. Rhea proposed the move that burned Stroud and does not think about it. And the link was opened before the lid was down, which is what let a second string start through, yet nobody names that trade, on the slate, at the board, or in Kiva's own column.
3. **Chapter 24 plants eight mysteries in nine short scenes, in the most clipped narration of the movement.** The reader is left with several "there is an old book somewhere" promises that blur into one another.

On the numbers: whole-text sentence and readability targets are missed, as before. What is new is that **narration alone has moved away from the formula** (mean 10.85 words against 13.09 in repaired Movement Two; sentences of forty words or more 0.78% against 3.06%). The owner ruling that carried the earlier movements rested on narration being near target. That is no longer true, and section 7 brings it back to the owner.

## 2. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**

- `17:1–43`. A scrape in the dirt, a cross, a stick with an iron ferrule, and a rule: the moment your right foot is down, I push. Forty lines later she is sitting in the dirt. I knew the task, the test and the two ways to fail before any explanation arrived.
- `17:361–375`. The ration runs out in the air. "The glass. It's empty." The cost of failing stops being a number and becomes four days with nothing to spend.
- `18:265–307`. The best Edge in the district stands in a wet meadow for forty-one seconds with the knot in reach, because a man looking at grass has said *not yet*. Kellan: "I'd have cut at five. I'd have been right about the knot, and it would have gone into the river." This is the ceiling the packet asked for, and it is shown as restraint.
- `19:129–145`. Kellan, mildly puzzled, tells her that a Stride is paid for at take-off. A friend's ordinary habit turns out to be the missing half of her problem, and Orla admits she never knew it needed saying.
- `19:385–449`. She has promised. She calls it anyway. And Brask does not brace: he lifts the litter aside "as neat and unhurried as two people moving a ladder out of the way of a door." The rule that was meant to help her tells the other side exactly when to step away.
- `20:239–299`. "On three, it's theirs." The same rule, turned round. Brask hears it, gets his own hands off, and cannot get his team's off. This is the most satisfying reversal in the movement.
- `20:423–453`. "It's a pan." Then she catches the landing, and out of habit puts the lid on. "I asked for a stance, Rowan, not a seal check." The breakthrough and the joke are the same beat.
- `21:401–407` into `22:1–5`. The best blade in her year asks his catcher, is told *not yet*, and comes down dark.
- `22:119–141`. The crowd laughs because the monster has found the drain. "Nobody had said *nearest.*"
- `22:333–351`. She decides, with relief, not to do it. She tells the person in charge. He asks who is south of it. "She could have said *the clerk.*"
- `23:161–185`. Selka Dray lands like a cat, hangs on the bar like a child on a gate, and when Kiva asks "Yours?" says "It's the *pin's,* you—yes. Mine." The scene makes an ally of her for eleven counts and leaves her objection standing.
- `24:89–103`. "Once is an accident with good manners… Show me six."

**Where it slipped.**

- `21:1–99`. The Pit is walked from north to south: Kiln, lid, capstan, lattice house, race, load board, sump, link, shelf, lever, pin, spring, door, stair. It is clear, and it is a long list of things that are about to matter. By the time Brask asks how a man gets to the shelf from the floor and Kiva thinks "*Nobody could land on that*… It was an idle thought" (`21:97`), I could see the lock being cut for the key.
- `22:147–205`. Old Four's rung. For about sixty lines there are four wards, a blade, a catcher, a foreman, a keeper and a string, and Kiva is forty paces away at a table. It is the one stretch of the Pit where I had to place people by effort.
- Chapter 24 from `24:155` to `24:285`. Grading, the board and the ash office. Every scene is correct and every scene closes a column. It is the loop the packet asked for, and it reads as a loop.
- The dates. I did not notice the 26th on the way through. I noticed it the moment I laid the days side by side, and in a book that counts everything a reader who counts will too.

**Which person matters.** Kiva, more than in either earlier movement, because for the first time she is wrong on purpose (`19:395–399`) and wrong in good faith (`21:139–145`), and pays for both. After her, Lowen Brask. He is the most alive secondary figure in the book: a rival who beats her with courtesy, loses by one, sits down next to her on the brick and tells her the one true thing nobody else was placed to see (`20:321–335`). Then Selka Dray, and Perrin, whose "I'd like it said that I mind" (`24:321`) is the most adult line anyone speaks.

**What I expect next.** Six witnessed tries on a sanctioned mast, with Orla on the crate, and at least one of them going wrong. The attached shift under Rhea Sorn, where she will finally have to speak to Kiva. Dray's renewed objection heard against the tape. Halden reading the same tape. Anwen leaving. And one of the several old books opening.

**Would I continue.** Yes, without hesitation.

## 6. Repair brief — three priorities

One bounded same-author pass. Do not shorten either fight, the weir, or the shelf. Keep the chapter breaks where they are; "Not yet" and "Rowan. Seven. Catcher." are the two best endings in the book.

### Priority 1 — Make the calendar and the counts agree with themselves

**Location.** C1–C17 and K1 in section 4. The high item is C1: `17:179`, `20:405`, `23:517`, `24:267`, `24:401`, with `24:425` depending on it.

**Observed.** The Flask is dated the 26th. Every weekday on the page puts it on the 29th. Around that sit about twenty small slips: the crooked post's age, Rhea's three viewings, Jude's five meets, two floors that are three, a skin cut once that was cut twice, three different lengths of time for one dram, a countdown that loses four gratings, two near-rhyming pairs of names, a pronoun the book has already given Halloran, and a fact Noa has no shown way of knowing.

**Effect on the reader.** This book teaches its reader to count. Kiva counts through fear, the team ledger is exact to a tenth, and the climax turns on "a grating every four counts." A reader who has learned that habit will lay the days side by side, and the central event of the movement will be on a Sunday. Each small slip costs a little of the trust the exact ones earn.

**Proposed scope.** Change the date to the 29th at the five places and re-anchor the dependent ones (`24:425`; the checkpoint's calendar from "Mon 24th" onward, the tape date and the stand-down date). No scene moves and no weekday changes. Then one clause or one number at each of C2–C15 and C17. Rename Tolliver and Merrin, which are author-invented minor names used six and five times. Give Halloran her pronoun. For K1, add a clause or bring it to the owner. As part of the same line pass, let two of the four adults who say "I want that understood" say something of their own (4f). Reconcile the checkpoint afterward.

**Strength to preserve.** Almost everything else reconciles (4b): fourteen tries, two lane scores, the Pit's ground and clock, the hour to the pan, the shares. That exactness is the book's signature. The repair is small because the draft is careful.

## 8. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Same model, informed read.** I knew what the packet asked for before I read a line, so I cannot report what a reader who did not know would have wanted. My sense that the shelf is built for the key (`21:35–99`) is the kind of thing an informed reader over-notices. The younger lens may simply enjoy seeing it coming.
- **Priority 2 is a judgment about feeling.** Another reader could find the restraint admirable: nobody melodramatic, everyone owning their line. I think the events are right and the accounting is thin. If the owner prefers the cooler register, the Anwen and Vane insertions are still the two I would keep, because one pays a planted line and the other fills a gap in the world's own rule.
- **Priority 3 trades suspense for focus.** Cutting seeds risks losing one the owner needs for Movement Four. The brief names which I would keep. It does not know the owner's plan beyond the map.
- **C1 is a certainty.** It is weekday arithmetic on the page. The other items in 4a are small, and three (C9, C10, C11) rest on my reading of distance and time in fast scenes.
- **The metrics are exact for their definitions and no more.** The syllable estimator is heuristic. The progression lexicons are mine and include ordinary senses ("gate," "company," "string," "reserve"). The narration split treats any sentence containing a quotation mark as speech. The ASR-derived paragraph and scene targets are not the same thing as typography. The development proxy plainly undercounts this prose.
- **Scores are not comparable across reviews as measurements.** They use the same rubric and anchors as the Movement Two review, by the same model. One movement does not show which seat is better across a series.
- **What I did not do.** I did not open the cold read. I did not re-read chapters 1–16 as prose. I did not run or time anything aloud; read-aloud judgments are from the page.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Movement 003 numerical alignment - Kindling 2680 Book One, chapters 17-24.

Stdlib only. Read-only against the manuscript. Loads the Movement Two script's
block() and every shared definition unchanged (it in turn imports the Movement
One script), so all three movements are measured the same way.
Usage: python3 metrics_m003.py BOOK_ROOT metrics_m001.py metrics_m002.py
"""
import re, sys, statistics, hashlib, collections

ROOT, M1PATH, M2PATH = sys.argv[1].rstrip('/'), sys.argv[2], sys.argv[3]
RUN_MARK = "print('SHA-256 (first 16) / whitespace words')"
src = open(M2PATH, encoding='utf-8').read()
src = src[:src.rindex(RUN_MARK)]          # keep M2's definitions, drop its own run lines
sys.argv = [M2PATH, ROOT, M1PATH]
ns = {'__name__': 'm2defs'}
exec(compile(src, M2PATH, 'exec'), ns)
m1, block, words, clean, sections, sentences, per10k = (ns[k] for k in
    ('m1', 'block', 'words', 'clean', 'sections', 'sentences', 'per10k'))
M1, M2 = ns['M1'], ns['M2']
M3 = ['chapter-%02d' % i for i in range(17, 25)]

# Movement Three viewpoint map, identified by reading every section; guarded by first words
POV2 = dict(ns['POV_CHECK'])
POV3 = {
    'Mara Rowan had brought eleven years of books': 'Mara',
    'Anwen Pryce had been told three things': 'Anwen',
    'Lowen Brask sat down on the away line': 'Brask',
    'Perrin Cade had asked, once, to be told': 'Perrin',
    'The lamps went out, and the thing in front of Rhea Sorn': 'Rhea',
    'Noa Bexley had the tape pinned': 'Noa',
}
def use(povmap):
    ns['POV_CHECK'].clear(); ns['POV_CHECK'].update(povmap)

# Movement Three additions to the progression lexicon (defined before counting)
PROG3_CI = [r'rung', r'rungs', r'knot', r'knots', r'string', r'purse sheet', r'purse sheets',
            r'bagged', r'jam', r'jams', r'sit-down', r'first step', r'second step', r'attached',
            r'rule nine', r'skin', r'skinned', r'reed', r'induction', r'foreman', r'company',
            r'companies', r'gate', r'reserve', r'reserves']
PROG3_CS = [r'Off']
TICS3 = [
    ('want/like it|that said|noted|known|understood|written',
     r"(?:want|like) (?:it|that) (?:said|noted|known|understood|written)"),
    ("it's said", r"it(?:'|’)s said"),
    ('"I want that understood"', r'i want that understood'),
    ('"hm" + stop or comma', r'hm[.,]'),
    ('"four hundred"', r'four hundred'), ('"two hundred years"', r'two hundred years'),
    ('"a month ago"', r'a month ago'), ('"six weeks"', r'six weeks'),
    ('bollard', r'bollards?'), ('heron', r'herons?'), ('furniture', r'furniture'),
    ('"like a"', r'like a'),
    ('"as a/an/one X [Y] <verb>s" simile',

    ('"not yet"', r'not yet'), ('"catcher?"', r'catcher\?'),

]

def body(f): return '\n\n'.join(sections(m1.load(f)))

def split_rule(files):
    """Narration / speech / ledger rule from MOVEMENT-001-TARGETED-RECHECK.md, kept per file."""
    res = {}
    for f in files:
        out = {'narration': [], 'speech': [], 'ledger': []}
        for sec in sections(m1.load(f)):
            for para in [p for p in re.split(r'\n\s*\n', sec) if p.strip()]:
                lines = [l.strip() for l in para.strip().split('\n') if l.strip()]
                sents = sentences(para)
                whole_it = all(re.fullmatch(r'\*[^*].*\*', l) for l in lines)
                label_it = all(re.match(r'\*[^*]+:\*\s', l) or re.fullmatch(r'\*[^*].*\*', l) for l in lines)
                if whole_it or label_it:
                    out['ledger'] += sents
                else:
                    inq = False
                    for x in sents:
                        n = len(re.findall(r'["“”]', x))
                        out['speech' if (inq or n) else 'narration'].append(x)
                        if n % 2: inq = not inq
        res[f] = out
    return res

def rhythm(files, label):
    print('\n-------- NARRATION SLICE BY CHAPTER %s --------' % label)
    res = split_rule(files); tot = []
    fmt = '  %-11s narration sents %4d mean %5.2f median %4.1f <=5 %4.1f%% >=30 %4.2f%% >=40 %4.2f%%'
    for f in files:
        ls = [len(words(clean(x))) for x in res[f]['narration']]; tot += ls
        t = clean(body(f)); q = sum(len(words(x)) for x in re.findall(r'[“"][^”"]*[”"]', t))
        print(fmt % (f, len(ls), statistics.mean(ls), statistics.median(ls), 100*sum(1 for x in ls if x<=5)/len(ls),
                     100*sum(1 for x in ls if x>=30)/len(ls), 100*sum(1 for x in ls if x>=40)/len(ls))
              + ' | quoted words %4.1f%%' % (100*q/len(words(t))))
    print(fmt % ('TOTAL', len(tot), statistics.mean(tot), statistics.median(tot), 100*sum(1 for x in tot if x<=5)/len(tot),
                 100*sum(1 for x in tot if x>=30)/len(tot), 100*sum(1 for x in tot if x>=40)/len(tot))
          + ' | pstdev %.2f' % statistics.pstdev(tot))
    longs = [(f, x) for f in files for k in ('narration', 'speech', 'ledger') for x in res[f][k] if len(words(clean(x))) >= 40]
    print('  sentences >=40 words: %d' % len(longs))
    if len(longs) <= 30:
        for f, x in longs: print('    %s (%d w) %s...' % (f, len(words(clean(x))), clean(x)[:70]))

def extras(files, label, table=False):
    A = clean('\n\n'.join(body(f) for f in files)); W = len(words(A)); low = A.lower()
    print('\n-------- EXTRAS %s (words %d) --------' % (label, W))
    def m3hits(t):
        tl = t.lower()
        e = collections.Counter()
        for x in PROG3_CI: e[x] = len(re.findall(r'(?<![A-Za-z\-])(?:' + x + r')(?![A-Za-z])', tl))
        for x in PROG3_CS: e[x + ' (cs)'] = len(re.findall(r'(?<![A-Za-z\-])' + x + r'(?![A-Za-z])', t))
        return e
    e = m3hits(A)
    print('  M3 additions: %d (%.1f/10k); top: %s' % (sum(e.values()), per10k(sum(e.values()), W), ', '.join('%s=%d' % kv for kv in e.most_common(14))))
    for f in files:
        t = clean(body(f)); n = sum(m3hits(t).values())
        print('    %-11s M3 additions %4d (%.1f/10k)' % (f, n, per10k(n, len(words(t)))))
    print('  tics / phrases (count, per 10k):')
    for lab, pat in TICS3:
        n = len(re.findall(r'(?<![a-z])(?:' + pat + r')(?![a-z])', low)); print('    %-72s %4d %6.1f' % (lab, n, per10k(n, W)))
    if table:
        print('  sections (file, index, words, first words):')
        for f in files:
            for i, s in enumerate(sections(m1.load(f))):
                print('    %s %2d %5d  %s' % (f, i, len(words(clean(s))), clean(s)[:48].replace('\n', ' ')))

print('SHA-256 (first 16) / whitespace words')
for f in M1 + M2 + M3:
    raw = m1.load(f); print('  %-11s %s %6d' % (f, hashlib.sha256(raw.encode()).hexdigest()[:16], len(raw.split())))
use(POV3); block(M3, 'MOVEMENT THREE, chapters 17-24'); rhythm(M3, 'M3'); extras(M3, 'M3', table=True)
found = [k for k in POV3 if any(clean(s).startswith(k) for f in M3 for s in sections(m1.load(f)))]
assert len(found) == len(POV3), set(POV3) - set(found)
use(POV2); block(M2, 'MOVEMENT TWO as now on disk (repaired), chapters 9-16'); rhythm(M2, 'M2'); extras(M2, 'M2')
block(M1, 'MOVEMENT ONE as now on disk (repaired), chapters 1-8'); rhythm(M1, 'M1')
use(dict(POV2, **POV3)); block(M1 + M2 + M3, 'CUMULATIVE chapters 1-24 (prologue excluded)'); extras(M1 + M2 + M3, 'ch 1-24')
