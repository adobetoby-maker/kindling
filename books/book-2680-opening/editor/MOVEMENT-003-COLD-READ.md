## 4. Repair brief — three priorities

The developed action in chapters 19, 20, 22 and 23 should stay at its present length. None of these asks for a fight to be shortened.

### Priority 1 — The dark-floor error is over-insured and under-reasoned

- **Location:** `ch21:113–181`, with feeders at `ch18:213`, `ch20:501–547`, `ch21:97`, `ch21:201–211`, `ch21:287`.
- **Observed:** Kiva sets her mark deliberately clear of live copper — "I don't want it near the wall. That's the board's feeder… as far from the wall as from the race" (`ch21:121`) — and has already logged a blind string stopping on a feeder plate (`ch18:319`, `ch18:403`). Fifty lines later she argues "Where else is there? There isn't a cell on the floor" (`ch21:173`), and nobody present, including two professionals, names the race. Meanwhile she is told to leave a gate by Bettan, Anwen (twice), Druce, Vane and the example of every other company, and the narrator adds hindsight twice ("that had seemed like most of an answer", `ch20:547`; "It was an idle thought", `ch21:97`). The link on its shelf is described three times.
- **Effect on the reader:** the adult reader knows at `ch21:35` that the string will take the race and at `ch21:97` that Kiva will jump to the shelf, so two chapters of approach play as confirmation, and her blind spot looks arranged rather than reasonable. The younger reader keeps the suspense but may conclude that all the grown-ups said so.
- **Proposed scope:** lines, not scenes. (a) Give the false belief its in-world reason once: let someone raise the race on the terrace and have it honestly dismissed, or remove Kiva's explicit feeder-avoidance line so she is not contradicting herself. (b) Cut the two hindsight nudges. (c) Describe the link twice, not three times. (d) Drop one or two of the gate warnings; the remaining ones will weigh more.
- **Strength to preserve:** the vote and Vane's slate ("I'm not a vote. I'm a slate… I've never had to say why", `ch21:161–181`); Druce's misdirection toward the Kiln (`ch20:539`); "Nobody had said nearest" (`ch22:141`); everything from `ch22:95` to `ch23:185` exactly as paced.

### Priority 2 — The aftermath repeats one move: announce and withhold

- **Location:** `ch24` (nine scenes, about 6,300 words) and `ch23:519`.
- **Observed:** within roughly six hundred lines, six people signal knowledge and defer it — Rhea's two-word card (`ch23:519`); Mara's "Not tonight. ### Priority 3 — Ledger audit

- **Location:** the table in section 3, items 1–8 first.
- **Observed:** a calendar that does not close (17th / 18th against the 26th), a tin count, a pace count, a post older than its original, an aisle of two widths, half-point calls that name different sides for the same event, a role word given to the wrong team-mate, eight cuts where there were seven.

## 5. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- The two lenses disagree most on clarity (5 against 7) and on the Pit's foreshadowing: what over-insures the adult's suspense may be right-sized for a thirteen-year-old. Priority 1 trades a little safety for the younger reader against a real gain for the older one.
- Priority 2 risks thinning a deliberate long-game mystery. I can only say it reads as crowded here; whether each thread is needed is a series-level call I have no evidence for.
- Noted but not prioritized: the shared dry register of the adult mentors, the shared "I want that said" idiom, comparison density in the action chapters, and pronoun avoidance around two characters. Each is a located weakness in the scores above; none outranks the three priorities.
- This is one read of one movement by a model that may share habits with the drafting seat. It does not show how these chapters land with an actual thirteen-year-old or an actual genre reader, and it proves nothing about other movements.
