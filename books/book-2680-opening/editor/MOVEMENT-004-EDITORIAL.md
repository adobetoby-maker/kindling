## 1. Verdict in brief

**Continue. This is the strongest movement of the four on the page, and it needs a small repair, not a structural one.**

- The six tries are the best progression writing in the book so far. A called *off*, a clean crossing, a soft cheap success that is wrong, and a dear one chosen on purpose teach the rule, its price and its abort without a lecture (`27:235–329`, `28:3–65`, `28:115–255`, `28:319–377`).
- The Weighbridge call is clear, costs something real, and keeps Kiva inside her paper. The students fail by doing exactly what a qualifier praised three minutes earlier (`29:299`), and recover by declaring roles and reasons aloud (`30:273–351`).
- The records change things between people each time they are opened. The request book costs Orla's standing with Kiva (`25:281`), the patent gives Halden a claim (`26:155`), the 2471 entry writes Orla's rules (`27:215–231`), and the bearer's book gives Kiva a small true reason and makes her choose to write in it (`32:269–321`).
- **One real canon fault sits in the movement's key evidence scene.** While reading Annick, Kiva is said never to have had "paying attention" and never to have seen what the lamps did. Chapter 7 has her say both to Mara (`31:279–289` against `07:295–299`). The author's checkpoint repeats the error. This must be fixed before Movement Five argues about what matches.
- **The dialogue has drifted into one period idiom, measurably.** "I'll not / I'd not / I've not" runs at 14.2 per 10,000 words here; it was 0.0 in Movement One. Students, professionals and archivists now share one voice. "That" is at 182.9 per 10,000 against a source figure of 92.1 that the formula says to tighten below.
- Narration recovered what Movement Three lost: 12.85 words a sentence, 28.1% short, 2.54% long. Chapters 25–27 sit at 13.8 to 14.9. Sections average 1,099 words, on the proxy for the first time. The whole-text miss remains a dialogue effect, and dialogue is now 39.9% of the words.

## 8. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- Same model as the author. I am likely to be generous to this prose and to share its blind spots. The dialect finding is the sort of thing I would expect a human ear to catch sooner and mind more, or possibly less; the counts are sound, the weight is a judgment.
- I have not re-read chapters 1–24. Checks against them were by search. I found what I looked for; there may be contradictions I did not think to look for, especially in chapters 9–16.
- The two lenses are constructions. The thirteen-year-old in particular is my guess at one, and its clarity score is the least certain number in section 3.
- Priority 2 trades some of this movement's charm for consistency and distinct voices. If the owner hears the period idiom as the book finding its voice, the tradeoff runs the other way.
- Priority 3 trades a little of the book's reticence for ease. I would name six callbacks, not all eleven.
- Progression thirds are by chapter count on an unfinished book. The development proxy is blind to this prose's vocabulary. The crude speaker attribution for the dialect count placed fewer than half the instances, so "who says it most" is from reading, not from the script.
- One movement does not show whether the narration recovery will hold under the pressure of the Regional's action chapters.

- "That" inside and outside quotation marks: `\bthat\b` over the text matched by `[“"][^”"]*[”"]` and over the remainder.
- Corrective pairs: `(?:\bisn['’]t|\bwasn['’]t|\bis not|\bwas not|\baren['’]t|\bit['’]s not|\bthat['’]s not)\b[^.?!"“”]{1,60}[.;]\s+(?:It['’]s|It is|It was|That['’]s|That was|That is|They['’]re|They were)\b`

**Known false positives.** Those listed in the three earlier appendices, and: "go / goes" is mostly the ordinary verb; "thread" counts ordinary thread and the bottom thread alike; "comb" counts the grader's and the field comb; "bearer" counts the litter's bearers in earlier movements; "Step" is counted only capitalized and still catches a sentence-initial "Step."; the "ready" motif row excludes "already" by its boundary but includes "ready" in other senses (13 hits, 6 of them "answer ready"); possessive have also matches the auxiliary before "more" and "nothing"; the verb-plus-"that" pattern counts some demonstratives ("said that" meaning "said it").

**Not captured by the script.** Every reader score, every finding in section 4, the manual beat lists in 5e, the callback and calendar lists in Priority 3, and all read-aloud judgments are readings of the page. Each is cited by line.
