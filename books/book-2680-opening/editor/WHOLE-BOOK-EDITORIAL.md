## 2. The answer in brief

The book works as one novel. Its six seams hold, its ending pays the promises `BOOK_MAP.md`
names, every reserved disclosure is still unspent, and its two best payoffs (the crooked post
and Kiva's changed way of asking) are earned across all 48 chapters rather than inside the
last movement.

**No structural or prose repair is required.** One small canon reconciliation is required
before Book One is called complete: three verified contradictions, all clause-level, one of
which sits under a payoff in chapter 47 and needs an owner ruling. Everything else in this
review is a plan change, a taste note, or an optional future-edition note.

The largest reader risk is not a defect in any movement. It is the opening: a 16,600-word
prologue across ten strangers and six centuries before the protagonist arrives. That is an
owner decision about presentation, not a repair.

## 3. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Early and more than once in the prologue: Teo Barrasa ruling a
*why* column into a dialysis ledger ("If I don't write the why, they'll make one up"), and
Pell Garrow, thirteen, making a ward by instinct in front of the first tear. Then firmly in
chapter 1: a needle that sticks at two, a rival who says "I hope they find a category for you.
Then we'll know what we're comparing," and a mother who reads the conditions and says "No." I
was committed by the Cellhouse (chapters 5–6), where a twenty-second success teaches Kiva the
wrong lesson and the board prints RUN FAILED over a run in which nobody on her team did
anything dishonourable.

**Where it slipped.** Three places. First, the middle of the prologue: snapshots 5 to 7 are
each good and each a new stranger, and by the seventh I was waiting for a person to stay
with. Second, chapters 9 to 14: the count of sixty, printed ash, sweepings, the comb, shares
and tins all arrive inside six chapters. Each is dramatized, none is a lecture, and the load
is still heavy. Third, the long quiet stretch from about chapter 31 to chapter 36: records,
hearings and one sanctioned go a week, with chapters 35 and 36 covering some ten weeks partly
in summary. It is where the book earns chapter 39, and it is also where a restless reader
would put it down.

**Who matters.** Kiva, Mara, Anwen and Perrin, in that order and without effort. Then Orla,
Kellan, Selka Dray and Pru. Then Rhea Sorn, Lowen Brask, Noa and Vane. The surprise is how
much I cared about people who never soften: Dray, who calls the count from a chair and is
still "not a friend," and Brask, who refuses a point with "No. Thank you. Not that one."

**What I expected next, at each turn, and whether I got it.** After the Cellhouse I expected
a redemption match; I got a lane lost 9–7 that was the first thing she did right. After the
first true sequence at the Pit I expected the power to become a tool; I got a fortnight's
ban, eleven teams that lost their day, and a healer who would not hurry. After the Regional
I expected the Step to be solved by Kiva; it was solved by her mother, an examiner who
admitted he was wrong in the street, a sluice nobody remembered, and Kiva taking one arm off
the water for about four counts. Each time the book gave me something smaller and better
than what I was braced for.

**Would I continue?** Yes. I want Saltmere, I want Jessamy Hart to read the book, I want to
know what Noa's "*Not one thing*" turns into, and I do not need the disk explained to want
them.

## 5. The book as one novel

### 5.1 Opening promise and the prologue

The promise the book makes is in two sentences that predate its protagonist by five hundred
years: "The lane with the crooked posts. Why: because the road isn't straight. S.V."
(prologue 4), and "There isn't a column" (prologue 11). The whole novel is the distance
between a reason that survived without its thing and a girl for whom no column exists. It
keeps that promise to the letter.

**The prologue is useful, and nearly all of it is spent inside Book One.** Snapshot 4 pays in
chapters 43, 44 and 48. Snapshot 8 (Femi Oduya's test cell) pays as the first leaf of the
Pellow book in chapter 31. Snapshot 9 gives the Ferren and a Saltmere company. Snapshot 10
gives the vote that retired the reasons column, the Even Hand, and Annick's notebook, paid in
chapters 25, 31 and 43. Snapshot 11 is, in effect, chapter zero. Snapshots 1 and 2 give the
book its ethic. Snapshots 5, 6 and 7 are the least load-bearing for this volume.

**Its cost is where it sits.** Ten viewpoint strangers and 16,621 words stand between the
cover and the heroine. An adult genre reader used to mosaic openings will take that as a
strength. A thirteen-year-old may not reach chapter 1. This is the only place where the two
lenses come apart by more than a point, and it is a decision about order and presentation,
not about sentences. See the optional notes in section 9.

### 5.2 Kiva

**Agency.** She acts from the first chapter and is punished for acting unsaid: over the rail
undeclared (ch 6), the sequence tried in a lane against a promise (ch 19), the lure thrown on
her own word (ch 29), the slack called with a hand that cannot feel it (ch 43). Her best
decisions are refusals: she does not run for the link until directed (ch 22), she declines
the Even Hand on an unshined reason (ch 34), she will not stand at the post to mend Vane's
drill (ch 42), and she turns down the sealed reserve as bait (ch 44).

**Attachment and vulnerability.** The body keeps the account. The knee strained in chapter 4
is still strapped in chapter 46. Two fingers go dull in chapter 6, white for forty-one counts
in chapter 28, and lose fine feeling for good in chapter 40. Nothing resets, and rank never
repays it.

**Progression.** One pattern carries all six movements: a public success teaches her the
wrong generalization, and the next movement charges her for it. Tuning by hand (ch 5) leads
to the three-door panic. A lane win by stillness (ch 20) leads to the dark floor (ch 21).
"Cloth's down" (ch 29) leads to Nairn's shin. "We take a corner!" (ch 37) leads to Dray under
the hem (ch 38). East served once (ch 39), and east is the drain (ch 46). Each correction is
the same act: say it aloud first, and name the price first.

**Final changed state.** It is behavioural, and that is why it convinces. She asks ("Anwen.
My cuff."). She names what is not hers (the handoff list of chapter 42). She withholds the
ready answer, which Anwen named as her fault in chapter 24. She gives a junior the question
instead of the answer: "I'd sooner you told me. Run it." Her classification never moves: the
Chair's last word on it is "She is not thereby classified."

### 5.3 Team and mentors

The book's most distinctive habit is that friends state their mixed motives aloud and are
believed. Perrin is grateful and jealous and says both (ch 7); his arc ends with 380 kilos
cut in a stone and "I minded properly, and at length." Anwen chooses the casualty in chapter
6, takes the line she wants in chapter 24 without apology, has to be asked in chapter 35, and
says "Mine" in chapter 39. Kellan's line runs from "what we're comparing" to "Renn. Seven.
Kill," and then honestly complicates itself with the Halden yard. Pru goes from narrating
disasters to teaching the protagonist a fold (ch 36) and writing her own red tag (ch 47).

The mentors are separated by what each refuses. Orla will not sign early and will not
pre-read the box. Mara will not give advice she has not earned and will not recalibrate a
mast to hide a trend. Rhea will not ask, and finally rules a *Before* column on her own
slate. Vane restores a cost column that someone tidied away. Aske will not round the price
down. Across 300,000 words not one of them lies to her, which is rare and is the book's
moral weather.

**One cost of that weather:** nobody is a villain, including Halden, the School and the
examiner who is wrong. An adult genre reader may miss a human antagonist. I record that as
taste. The book would be worse with one.

### 5.4 Training, competition and rift response

The three modes alternate and each escalates on its own track. Training runs from an
eleven-second drill (ch 3) to a first three-door sequence (ch 36). Competition runs from an
open lane lost 3–½ (ch 4) to fourth of sixteen at the Regional (ch 40). Response runs from a
Kennel floor (ch 9) through License Day, the Pit, the night van and the hold, to a hill of
sixty-two houses (ch 45–46). The modes also feed each other: Brask's "Arrive before you're
there" comes out of a lane and lands at the Pit; the Home Line's four columns come off a
response floor and end on the Chair's board.

Two observations on balance.

- **Team Seven loses most of what it enters,** and the book is right to let it. Progress is
  shown through placings (ninth, sixth, fourth) and through what the losses are for.
- **The two door-climaxes share one mechanism.** The Pit (ch 38–39) and Stopgate (ch 45–46)
  both put a cord under load, three doors, a price said first, and "I'm frightened." / "Then
  yes." The second is not a repeat: it is offered and not asked, the mark is tested and
  turned, the count and the corner and the bar are given to others, and Mara's hand answers
  hers two miles off. A cold reader may still hear an echo in the consent sequence. That is
  a risk the design accepts, and I would not reopen it.

### 5.7 The ending and the Book Two pulls

**Locally satisfying.** Every item in `BOOK_MAP.md`'s ending promise is on the page: a
public place without a finished explanation (the board, ch 48); doors combined under
understood rules (ch 36, 39, 46); a cost that rank does not reset (ch 40, 47); the disk
recognized as the bearer line's (ch 32, 48); a bounded offer ("either of us may say no. No
seat is promised"); evidence of acceleration (Noa's three columns, ch 47); and a second
historical account of the dream (ch 47).

The last scene is the right size. A junior who asked why the post is crooked in chapter 42
runs the lane to the bar in chapter 48 because she was given the reason with the rule. The
closing image pays the opening promise without a speech.

**Clean pulls.** Saltmere and its harbour bank; Hart's card; three sheets before the Charter
Board; a ship's engineer "not entered as fact"; Dray on her feet by the third month; a hand
that reports on eight fingers; a company's card until the last of the third month; a cut can
in the Works' store that nobody took up. None of them requires Book One to have explained
anything it withheld.

### 5.8 Read-aloud clarity and repeated habits

The prose is built for the voice: short declaratives, concrete nouns, counts spoken aloud.
Across six movements these habits recur often enough for a listener to notice.

- **"I want that said." / "It's said."** It begins as Anwen's (ch 6) and becomes a liturgy in
  many mouths. As a team rite it is one of the book's pleasures. By the last movement it also
  comes from officers and strangers, and it loses some of its ownership.
- **Perrin's "I mind" and "I'd like it known."** Thinned in the Movement Six repair, rightly.
  It is now mostly his.
- **Negation-led beats.** "She did not…," "X said nothing," "It was not A. It was B." These
  are frequent in every movement and are the single most audible tic.
- **Domestic similes** on a fixed frame ("as a woman feels for a tram," "like a clerk who has
  the right answer and takes his time bringing it"). Individually good; collectively a
  signature.
- **Chapter-ending field-book pages.** Structural and earned, and predictable by the
  midpoint.
- **Action refrains** reused between the two climaxes ("Arrive before you're there," the pan
  set "by its corners," "the wall between the rooms").
- **Ledger readings.** Lines of drams with decimals read aloud (ch 24, 41, 48) are honest and
  hard on the ear.
- **Cast size late.** The Stopgate night names dozens of people. On the page the ordinals
  and roles keep it clear. In audio, a listener will hold perhaps a dozen.

None of this needs repair. If the book is narrated, these are the places where a narrator's
choices will matter most.

### 5.9 Reserved disclosures

All unspent at the end, confirmed against the manuscript and against section 10 of the
Movement Six checkpoint.

- **The disk's origin.** The first book is lost. Mars appears once, as a rumour in a record
  of 2096 (prologue 3). The Reader asserts "the first teachers" and nothing earlier.
- **The filter.** Named only as one of three street stories (ch 2) and as a marginal note
  (prologue 4). Never tested, never confirmed. Noa's columns show acceleration and no cause.
- **Glory.** A rank word only. No Glory figure is on stage.
- **S.V.** Initials only. "Nobody on the page knows whose initials those are."
- **The shared-dream record.** Two independent accounts older than Kiva (Annick, undated;
  the keeper, 2604), not collected, not named on the page as the Unroofed Sky. Mara's page is
  still under the Reader's wax.

## 6. Plan changes and taste, kept apart from canon

**Plan changes (no contradiction on the page; owner awareness only).**

- **Secondary viewpoints.** `BOOK_MAP.md` planned "four or five." I count nine people who
  hold a section: Mara, Orla, Kellan, Anwen, Rhea, Perrin, Noa, Vane and Brask. The share
  stays near the planned 13 percent. Every chapter heading still reads "Kiva:".
- **The dream's second witness is a pump keeper,** not a high-rank practitioner as the
  universe bible describes. The author flagged this. It makes the phenomenon wider as well as
  older, and I think it is the better choice, but the bible and the page now differ and the
  owner should rule which stands.
- **The Step travels by a storm drain,** and the last night's expression is unnamed and
  unclassed. Both were the author's routes where the packet left room.
- **Whole-text cadence targets are unmet** because dialogue is deliberately clipped. The
  owner accepted this in Movement One. Do not chase it.

**Taste (mine; no action implied).** No human antagonist. A protagonist whose victories are
mostly refusals. A middle stretch that chooses hearings over floors. A final chapter with no
fight in it. I would defend all four.

## 7. Verified canon contradictions

Each was found in the read and re-verified by search in the current manuscript. None appears

- Chapter 44, Jude: "We've two drams on our own book that came off a husk on the Sag in the
  fifth month, before any of this got into the ash. Ward-trace. No hair. It's sat under wax
  for five months" (44:131). Chapter 47, Anwen: "It came off a husk on the Sag in the fifth
  month. Ward-trace. It's never had the hair" (47:55). The comb agrees with her: "The seventh

So the source is wrong (the Pit's rung, not a Sag husk), the count from the Sag is wrong (one,
and it was spent), and the grade contradicts the card. The month is also one out: by the

**Why it matters.** Aske's note asks for "plain ward-trace inside the hour," and chapter 48
says there is none to be bought because "it's all had the hair since the thaw." The reserve
saves Mara's reading hand because it is plain. That is the payoff of a thread laid in
chapters 14, 24, 35 and 44, and it is one of the best in the book. As written, the only plain
dram the team ever sealed was used on Kiva in chapter 24. In a novel whose ethic is "I want
it written whose it was," this is the one place its own ledger does not bear out its claim.
Confidence: high.

**2. Rhea Sorn's culvert has two dates.** Chapter 8 (line 299): "She had been nineteen, a

## 9. Optional future-edition notes

These are not repairs and none should hold up the book.

1. **The prologue's placement.** The single largest lever on a young reader's opening pull.
   Options that need no new prose: leave it; move snapshot 11 (the Kindling) to stand
   directly before chapter 1 with the ten historical snapshots as a front section a reader
   may enter later; or carry snapshots 5 to 7 as interludes. Each has a cost to the mosaic.
   An actual thirteen-year-old's reaction would settle this better than any review.
2. **The anniversary.** Either give the six-hundredth year one dated line near the end or
   let it stay weather.
3. **The quiet middle.** If an edition wants more pressure in chapters 31 to 36, the place
   is the ten weeks summarized in chapters 35 and 36. I would not add a floor.
4. **Viewpoint labelling.** Nine secondary viewpoints under headings that all say "Kiva:". A
   listener gets no cue at a cutaway beyond the first sentence.
5. **Refrains for audio.** "I want that said" could return to fewer mouths; negation-led
   beats could be thinned by a narrator's pacing without touching the text.
6. **The quench-box tag.** "For emergencies" would land, unforced, when the reserve comes out
   of that box in chapter 47.
7. **Halden's claim.** One line of status in the last movement would keep the "however
   produced" case from reading as dropped while the red row grows.
8. **For the Book Two plan, not for this book.** At the Pit the notch and the lamps fell
   together; at Stopgate they came apart by five minutes. Noa's "two things" has to hold
   against both nights. And the universe bible's line about who records the dream fragments
   should be brought into agreement with the keeper of 2604.
