## 4. Numerical formula alignment

Kept apart from the reader scores. None of this is a gate.

### 4a. Method

- **Tool.** One standard-library Python script, printed whole in section 4e and run on the
  eight chapters as embedded in the compiled prompt. To reproduce: copy the block to a file
  outside the repository and run `python3 metrics_m006.py editor/MOVEMENT-006-EDITORIAL.prompt.md`.
- **Coverage.** Chapters 41–48 only: 50,806 regex words (50,763 whitespace tokens without
  headings and section rules; the authorship record's `wc -w` is 50,866 with them). The rest of
  the book was not supplied, so every book-level figure below is derived from numbers already
  recorded in `AUTHORSHIP.md` and is marked as such.
- **Sentence tokenizer.** A regex split after `.`, `!` or `?` (and after a dash that closes a
  quotation), with `Mr.`, `Mrs.` and capital initials protected. A paragraph break always ends
  a sentence. A line of speech with its tag is one sentence.
- **Speech, record, narration.** A sentence is speech if its first word lies inside double
  quotes, record if it lies inside italics outside quotes, narration otherwise. This is my
  rule. The earlier movements' script was not supplied and I was not authorized to read it, so
  my narration figures are close cousins of the recorded ones, not the same definition. The
  speech share I get (54.6%) matches the 54.7% recorded for Movement Five, which suggests the
  rules are near.
- **Syllables.** A vowel-group heuristic with corrections for silent *e*, *-ed*, *-es*,
  contractions and a few compounds. I checked a random 200-token sample by hand and found one
  miscount (a numeral). It gives 1.179 syllables a word; the earlier script reported about
  1.25 on similar prose, so I also print Flesch figures at that rate as a bound. Proper names
  and coined terms are few and short and do not move the result.
- **Lexicons** for progression vocabulary, development markers, reporting verbs, combat terms
  and fillers are in the script. They are project-specific and none borrows a source novel's
  name. A suffix count of *-ly* is not a count of adverbs.

### 4d. Meaningful departures

1. **Missing long sentences, not missing short ones.** The formula's rhythm is a wide spread.
   Here the spread is 7.25 against about 26, and only 21 sentences in 5,840 reach forty words.
   Chapter 43 has none. There are 41 runs of six or more consecutive short sentences; the
   longest is fourteen. In chapters 45 and 46 that is fight cadence and right. Elsewhere it is
   uniformity.
2. **Narration is getting shorter movement by movement.** The record gives a narration mean
   of 13.8 after the Movement One repair, 13.09 for Two, 12.03 for Three and 11.69 for Five.
   This draft is 10.98, with no chapter above 12.11 and no chapter above 1.34% long sentences.
   The Movement Three brief set the aim for narration outside action at "roughly a
   thirteen-word mean and roughly three percent forty-word sentences." The definitions are not
   identical across scripts, so the trend is indicative. It is still the one sentence figure
   the owner's own ruling leaves open to repair.
3. **Reading level cannot be reached by joining sentences.** At 1.18 syllables a word, grade
   6.8 would need a mean sentence of about 21.7 words. With speech at 54.6% of sentences and
   the standing ruling that dialogue stays clipped, the Flesch targets and that ruling cannot
   both be met. This is the conflict recorded since Movement One. I report it and do not
   propose chasing it.
4. **The source's two section targets disagree.** 8.7 breaks per 10,000 words implies about
   1,150 words a section, not 950. This movement matches the rate exactly and so misses the
   mean. I keep the source's values as written and report the conflict. No repair.
5. **Viewpoint shape.** Kiva's 87.2% is on target and no second lead has appeared. Six
   secondary viewpoints in one movement and nine in the book is wider than "4–5." Mara's
   pump-house section is the largest and earns it. No repair.
6. **Dialogue-driven, tag-heavy.** "Said" appears 635 times. The formula's secondary signal
   describes a book led by action and interiority; this movement is led by talk. That is the
   book's settled voice. The cost shows in read-aloud, where long exchanges are a run of "said
   X."
7. **"That."** At 162.8 per 10,000 words it is 1.8 times the level the formula calls hot and
   about twice the 63–80 recorded for Movement One. It sits mostly in speech: "I know that it
   is," "I thought that it was rather restful," "Miss Dane says that we're to run it." It is a
   large part of the period register, and it is used by a thirteen-year-old junior, a
   fourteen-year-old fitter's boy and a pump keeper alike.
8. **Openers.** 9.0% of narration sentences begin "It was" and 3.2% "There was"; "it was a"
   and "there was a" run at 14.6 and 13.6 per 10,000 words against a background of 2 to 4. It
   is the signature cadence, and at one narration sentence in eight a listener will hear it.
9. **Late teaching.** None. Progression vocabulary is low and rises where it should: broad

## 5. Repair brief: three priorities

One bounded same-author repair. No scene is cut, moved out of its chapter or shortened to save
words. The developed action stays whole: the Halden drill, the evacuation, Anwen's walking
wall, the arm, the catch, the bar and the pump house. Most fixes below are a clause or a
figure.

### Priority 1. Handholds at the peak (chapters 44 to 46, with two roots in 42 and 43)

- **Where.** 45:115–145 and 45:261–275 (how the string grows). 45:11–23, 45:397 and 46:423
  (Mara's clock). 41:301 with 43:45 (four hours against half an hour). 46:201 ("four days").
  44:353 ("Halfway down"). 46:141–195 (east, north, and where the hem will hang). 46:231–299
  (three sets of numbers in the catch). 42:249–271 (how a litter meets the post). 46:37.
- **Observed.** At the moment of greatest pressure the page asks the reader to hold a street
  of numbered knots that grows by two different rules, a compass last given two chapters
  earlier, a pump-house timetable that does not quite fit its own minutes, and a count inside
  a count inside a count. Each slip is small (C1, C5–C8, C10, C11, C16). They cluster where
  attention is dearest.
- **Effect on the reader.** The thirteen-year-old keeps the fear and loses the picture. The
  adult stops to do arithmetic. Neither doubts the outcome, so the cost is trust in the
  mechanism, and this climax asks to be judged on its mechanism.
- **Proposed scope.** About fifteen clause-level edits. Give the string one growth rule, said
  once by Brask or Kiva. Let one sentence account for Mara's nineteen hands. Give Rusk's half
  hour its reason. Correct "four days" and "Halfway down." Before the step test, say again in
  a phrase which way the gate lies from the post. In the catch, mark once whose numbers are
  whose. In the drill, let the two litters meet the post in a way the yard's map allows.
  Records follow the page.
- **Preserve.** "There's no go *there*" and the step test exactly as it runs. The turn called
  aloud and said back. "It's known." "I hadn't said what." "I know what." Dray's voice from
  the chair. The arm coming along the wall. Every word of the consent scene from 45:415 to
  46:109.

### Priority 2. The reckoning (chapters 47 and 48): right the record and let the cost be felt

- **Where.** 48:367 and 48:387 (the columns). 47:373–421 (Noa's Saturday). 47:191–209 (who
  hears about the dream). 47:195 (Annick's date). 48:249 against 47:243 (which knot).
  48:303–461 (the crate, the first shed and the post). 41:401, 46:613, 47:421, 47:81 (elapsed
  time). 47:287–293 (the arm's four days). 47:231–269, 48:9–161 and 48:237–255 (the run of
  readings).
- **Observed.** Three things in the same stretch. First, the record itself is wrong in the
  book's last pages: Kiva writes in "the fifth, … under the collarbone," where the sheet as
  read puts the collarbone under *Cost* and *Went wrong* one column earlier (C2); one scene
  sits a week out of order with no signal (C3); and C12, C13 and C15. Second, a reserved
  matter is aired in front of a Halden officer and three others and nobody notices (K1), and
  a date is given to an entry the ledger calls undated. Third, about nineteen documents are
  read out or written down in 12,460 words, while the one cost that was named before the
  climax returns in about a hundred.
- **Effect on the reader.** A book whose argument is "write it down, with the reason" cannot
  misplace a column on its final page; the attentive reader it has trained will see it. The
  out-of-order Saturday makes the last fortnight feel unmoored. The recital flattens the
  ending's pulse for the younger lens and for the ear. The quick refund makes the price read
  as a ritual and not a risk.
- **Proposed scope.** Correct the column and the collarbone, and settle once what the seven

- **Preserve.** Pru's finger on the page. "We've three hands between us that report."

### Priority 3. Whose phrase is it (dialogue, all eight chapters)

- **Where.** The "I'd like it known / noted / written / understood" family: 19 uses. Eleven
  are Perrin's (41:331, 41:465, 42:325, 44:151, 44:313, 45:233, 46:205, 46:387, 47:63, 48:251,
  48:277). The rest are Kiva's (41:371, 46:105), Aske's (42:17), a fitter's (43:241), Vane's
  (44:65), Noa's (47:375) and two "It's noted." "I find that": nine uses across Mara, Rusk,
  Dray, Rhea and Adlam (42:407, 43:533, 44:271, 45:23, 46:67, 46:427, 47:133, 47:209, 47:225).
  "I'd sooner / I'd as soon": fourteen, across at least seven speakers. Optional "that" in
  young or plain mouths, for example 48:393 and 48:205.
- **Observed.** The adults share one dry, understated idiom, and its signature phrases have
  spread from the characters who own them. Perrin's catchphrase is the movement's best running
  joke and pays off at the bar. By then six other people have used it. "That" stands at 227
  per 10,000 words of speech, and a thirteen-year-old junior speaks with the same grammar as
  the Placement Chair. The authorship record shows that the Movement Three and Movement Four
  repairs addressed exactly this, and it has grown back in a first draft.
- **Effect on the reader.** By ear the speakers blur. In a room of ten adults, which this
  movement has several times, the listener depends on the tag, and the tag is "said" 635 times.
  Perrin's payoff lands a little softer than it has earned. The younger reader hears a period
  voice in mouths her own age.
- **Proposed scope.** A dialogue pass of roughly thirty to forty single-line edits and no
  more. Leave the catchphrase to Perrin, thin his own uses before chapter 46 by two or three,
  and keep Kiva's "I'd like that written" at 46:105, which echoes the Pit. Reword the other
  speakers' uses. Leave "I find that" to Mara. Drop the optional "that" for Pru, Ned, Jude,
  Perrin and the juniors where the line allows. While in the quiet passages, join a few
  narration sentences toward the standing narration aim (section 4d, items 1 and 2) in
  chapters 41, 44, 47 and 48 only. Do not touch speech length, and do not touch the cadence of
  chapters 45 and 46.
- **Preserve.** Pike ("That's what the year's made of"). Dray entire. Pru entire ("I'm
  narrating"; "That came out bigger than I meant"). Aske's refusal to round the price down.
  Mara's levelness. Rhea's "I'd like you to notice that I can learn," which is the right use
  of that grammar. Halloran's "It's what's left when I've done being frightened."

## 6. Tradeoffs, uncertainty and owner decisions

- **Same-model blindness.** I share the author's habits. Things I find natural may be the
  tics a human reader would notice first. A human read of chapters 45 and 46 aloud would test
  Priority 1 better than I can.
- **Earlier chapters.** I could not check the layout of the Old Yard, the plate exhibition or
  Kiva's School calendar against chapters 3 to 19. C13 and the Perrin item in 3c need those
  chapters.
- **Measurement.** My script is not the earlier movements' script. Whole-text sentence figures
  are robust to that; narration splits and Flesch figures are comparable only roughly. I have
  given the bound.
- **Priority 2 against the book's method.** Reading records aloud is this book's signature and
  an earlier repair deliberately lengthened a reckoning chapter. Lightening two recitations
  trades a little of that texture for pace. If the owner prefers the full recital, the
  corrections in Priority 2 still stand without it.
- **Priority 3 against the period voice.** The retained "that" is part of what makes the prose
  sound like itself. I recommend thinning it only where it flattens age, not as a count.
- **Not in the brief, for the owner.** Whether the string of the 5th gets a name or a yield
  class. Whether the 2604 witness should outrank a pump keeper. Whether the release at *back*
  deserves a street cutaway, at the cost of about a point of secondary viewpoint. Whether the
  dialogue ruling still outranks the formula's sentence and reading-level targets, which it
  cannot meet as things stand.
- **No release gate.** Nothing here is a pass or a fail. I would continue reading. The repair
  is small, and the movement's strengths are structural and already on the page.
