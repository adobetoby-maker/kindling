## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught**

- ch 25 sc 4, "Please." and "I've no ham." This is the first moment I liked Kiva rather than merely following her. The margin that follows ("Nobody had told them") is the first real hook.
- ch 25 sc 5, Orla's name in the black book. A genuine turn: the mentor who preaches a "why" column has cut a report to fit.
- ch 26 sc 4, folding the sheet: "I'm not done." The one relationship in the movement that is allowed to stay unresolved, and the better for it.
- ch 27 sc 5, the first sanctioned go: she can hear the pen, and calls "Off" in the air. A miss that is plainly worth more than a landing.
- ch 28 sc 3, the stack. For me the best scene in the movement. The softest, cheapest, best-looking landing is the dangerous one, the sponsor would have signed it, and the weakest person in the lane says "No" against a number he has wanted for a month.
- ch 28 sc 5, the dear one: two fingers the color of candle wax, and a count to forty-one.
- ch 29 sc 5 through ch 30 sc 5, Weighbridge Street. "Nobody light anything." Three tram mechanics getting under a leg. Pru walking backward downhill, narrating. "Fifty-five." / "Because nothing happened."
- ch 31 sc 2, Mrs. Cobb's first leaf: "Cells come back clean or they don't come in."
- ch 32 sc 4, five hundred years of "Nothing" in the fourth column, and "Will they put why?"

**Where it slipped**

- ch 25 sc 1–2. A Monday-night yard scene that folds back into that afternoon, back out to the yard, then jumps to Wednesday. I had to re-read to place the bench conversation in time.
- ch 26 sc 2, the patent room. The explanation of the Step is brisk and clear, but Mr. Adlam materialises at Noa's elbow a second time, and I felt the author's hand.
- ch 26 sc 4. One scene carries the new sheet, rule fourteen, Pru's cord lesson, Noa's figures, the confession, Orla's name and the fold. It holds, just.
- ch 31 sc 1 and sc 3–4. After the street, a long stretch of who-may-have-which-tin and yield splits. Mrs. Cobb rescues it; Kellan's half sheet does not.
- ch 32 sc 1 and sc 3. A notice board hands me three claims, two hearings, a reserve seating and a null result; then Orla on a bench lists what is coming. By the last page I had been told the upcoming calendar four times.

**Which person matters.** Kiva, plainly, but the people I would follow out of the room are Noa (ch 28 sc 3), Pru (ch 28 sc 4, ch 30 sc 3), and Anwen (ch 26 sc 4, ch 29 sc 3). Orla matters most to Kiva; Rhea Sorn became interesting only once she was wrong (ch 30 sc 3).

**What I expect next.** The room on Tuesday the 3rd, where a boy with a strip of paper must tell a Placement Chair that a Torch cannot tell a crossing from a stack with a stick. A go that goes wrong with "ash for four, and a hundredth" and no margin. The hand staying away longer than it should. Halden's "however produced" reaching past the bag toward the person who called it. A watch vote on the plate. And, sooner or later, a crossing in the field that her paper forbids.

**Would I continue.** Yes. As the adult reader, without hesitation. As the thirteen-year-old, yes, but I would have skimmed parts of chapters 31 and 32 to get there.

## 6. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Entry point.** My clarity and connection scores are depressed by arriving at chapter 25. A reader with the first twenty-four chapters would likely score both a point higher. The time-folding in ch 25 sc 1–2 and the items in section 4 would still stand.
- **A restrained heroine.** Kiva's signature ability is used only in a practice yard, and her best field work is a mistake, a slower second attempt, and a sentence about a bag. I think this is the movement's strength and I have not listed it as a repair. It is a real cost for the younger lens.
- **Voice.** The uniform dry honesty of the adults is the price of the book's manner. I flag it and leave it; priority 3 addresses only the labelling that makes it conspicuous.
- **Length.** I make no recommendation on word count and gave no credit or penalty for it. The Weighbridge sequence earns its length.
- **Scores.** These are editorial judgments by one simulated reader in two lenses, not measurements. They are not averaged, they are not a release gate, and they imply nothing about sales.
- **Seat comparison.** No other run was supplied, so nothing here compares seats or models. One movement would not settle that in any case.
