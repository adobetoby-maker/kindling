## Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Everyone is fair.** Adlam, Birch, Brask, Dray, Kellan, the Chair and the watch all behave honourably. That is the book's ethic and I liked it; it also means the only menace is the phenomenon. An adult reader may want one person who cuts something to fit.
- **The elders share a voice.** The Ferren, Mara, Ysra, Kellan's mother, Aske, Mace, Pike and Stroud all speak in the same dry capping aphorism. The students are distinct. Most scenes also close on a quotable line, a rhythm the ear predicts by ch 36.
- **The success when it counts.** The three-door had landed once in four tries. It lands at the Pit. I found it earned, because both failure modes were shown and the price is permanent, but a sceptical reader could call it convenient.
- **Series memory.** I read these chapters without the first thirty-two. Some of my "slow" may be payoff I could not feel, and some of my "clear" may be luck.

## Repair brief — three priorities

**1. Make the Pit's clock keep time (ch 39, scenes 1–2; one line in Perrin's section; the knot counts in ch 38's last page and ch 39's "seven knots").**

- *Effect:* the price-first ritual, the movement's best idea, appears to happen in stopped time, and the catch then runs in fast time. The deadline stops squeezing.
