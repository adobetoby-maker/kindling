# Kindling 2680 — prologue plan

Status: authorized for drafting with Movement One. This plan describes architecture,
purpose, and boundaries for the required historical prologue.

Revised 2026-10-27 (planning only; prose rewrite is a separate ticket): the
record-keeping thread is removed and replaced by one physical object, the leaning
post (§4); every snapshot row now names a concrete Turn (§5); a motif budget and
banned phrasings are added (§8). Rationale and prose-pass targets:
`editor/MOTIF-BUDGET-REVISION.md`.

## 1. Purpose

`BOOK_MAP.md` §3 already requires one historical prologue before Chapter 1,
sitting outside the six eight-chapter movements, carrying a cold reader from
the 2080 Fall to the 2680 anniversary without resolving the reserved cosmology.
This plan supplies that architecture.

The prologue's job is substantial but disciplined: let a reader who has never opened
a Kindled book feel the 2080 Fall, the recovery, the first century's energy
revolution, and six hundred years of consequence as human events, then show Kiva's
anomalous Kindling and first Unroofed Sky fever dream four years before the
collegiate story begins. It is not a world-building appendix and not a
summary of the present Kindled trilogy's plot. A reader of the present trilogy
should recognize the institutions (Meridian, the Home Line, the crooked lane)
arriving as history; a reader who has never heard of them should still follow
the human thread without needing to.

## 2. Placement and scale

- Precedes Chapter 1. Does not count as one of the six movements and is not
  itself subject to the O'Connor six-or-more-chapter floor.
- Scene-led throughout. No snapshot is a lecture, a textbook paragraph, or an
  encyclopedia entry rendered as prose. Every snapshot is one person, in one
  place, on one day, doing something concrete.
- Target scale: eleven dated, scene-led snapshots, usually 900–1,600 words each.
  The expected prologue is approximately 11,000–17,000 words. It may be longer than
  a normal chapter because the owner wants the energy and social transformation
  understood rather than summarized, but every scene must turn on one person's
  immediate problem and choice.
- Each snapshot is explicitly dated (a year or a "N years after the Fall"
  marker) so the reader always knows how much time has passed since the last
  one, without needing a narrator to say so directly.

## 3. Canon boundaries

**May do:**
- Show the 2080 EMP's visible effects (light in the sky, total hardware
  failure, immediate social collapse) through one household's night.
- Show the failed restoration decade (2080–2090) through one person's labor,
  per `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md` (book-01) owner ruling: a small number of
  pre-hardened medical/research facilities survive intact, most
  infrastructure does not.
- Show the Mars-disk activation, the first unstable wormholes, the first
  Riftspawn, and the first Kindlings as lived, frightening, un-named events —
  no character in these early snapshots has rank vocabulary, a classification
  system, or the word "Kindling" itself until it is plausible for that word to
  exist.
- Show the slow, centuries-long institutionalization of door schools, medical
  practice, and the Home Line's field tradition — including, explicitly, the
  moment or stretch where a later generation simplifies or loses a founder's
  reason while keeping the founder's method. Show it once, in Snapshot 10,
  as a committee choice about a practice. This directly serves the
  series promise already stated in `SERIES_MAP.md`: *"institutions preserved
  the founders' methods but simplified away the reason those methods
  worked."*
- Show ash expanding from scarce healing fuel into industrial energy through
  attempts, accidents, shortcuts, and engineering choices. Include radiation-safe
  Kindled work, recovered nuclear knowledge, ash-nuclear synthesis, compact reactors,
  household power, flight, and the social rise of industrial houses and devoted
  schools.
- Show that Sustain-trained workers can shed active radiation burden under strict
  limits; they are not immune, fixed tissue damage remains, and Ward still does not
  move radiation.
- Let the Unroofed Sky appear first as a fragment in an older Glory practitioner's
  private notebook (found and shelved once, in Snapshot 10), then fully in Kiva's
  fever dream. The overlap must be visible to
  the reader but not fully explained to Kiva.
- Let characters across every era hold and voice theories about the Fall's
  cause and the filter — a nation-state exchange, a deliberate reset, later
  (post-Homura only, see §5) the failing-filter hypothesis — without the
  narration confirming any of them.
- End in 2676 with Kiva's three-door Kindling and the crooked post. Chapter One
  then opens four years later in the 2680 collegiate season.

**Must not do:**
- Put Rook on the page as a witness to the 2080 EMP or any pre-Homura event.
  Current canon places him generations later (born well after the Fall,
  Kindled at thirteen, recruited to Homura at fourteen). No snapshot may
  use him, his mother, or any named Book One/Two character as a 2080-era
  figure.
- Prove, confirm, or narratively settle the filter cosmology, the Makers, the
  Mother/Father myth, the disk's true origin, or the first cause of the Fall.
  The prologue may show belief, measurement, and memory. It may not show
  authorial confirmation of any of them.
- Reuse named systems, terminology, or scene sequence from the Iron Prince
  research pool. Every invented figure, place, and phrase in the prologue is
  original to this series.
- Let any pre-Homura-era snapshot originate or anticipate the failing-filter
  theory. Per `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md` (book-01), that theory is first held by
  "Homura-era theorists" — it cannot appear earlier in this timeline without
  rewriting already-locked canon.
- Resolve whether the detonations and the filter damage are connected. Keep
  both the nation-state/deliberate-reset ambiguity and the filter-trigger
  question exactly as open as `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md` (book-01) leaves them.
- Turn the energy history into a triumphalist lecture. Every advancement has fuel,
  labor, failure, ownership, safety, and distribution consequences.
- Exhaust the Unroofed Sky mystery. The prologue establishes the repeating images;
  it does not prove whether they are memory, prophecy, resonance, or manipulation.

## 4. The shared physical thread: the leaning post

Owner ruling, 2026-10-27, verbatim: *"i dont like all the write it down. record it
talk inside a book. it annoys me."* This section replaces the earlier thread, a
class of hand-kept record carried "in nearly every era," and removes it. No
snapshot is built on someone writing down what happened, copying a page, or
explaining why records matter.

**The thread is one place, not a document:** the leaning ninth post of the Home Line
training lane under Meridian's south wall, the "crooked post" of canon
(`UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, Historical artifacts; `STATE_LEDGER.md`, Movement Six).

**Why this object:** it is the only recurring thing the plot already pays off.
Movement 6 reveals it as the handle of a sluice gate on a lane laid over the hill
drain (Chapters 43 and 48). It is already canon-planned to survive (`PLANNED
survival`), it is physical, the prologue already ends on it, and it needs nobody to
keep, copy, or explain anything for it to last.

**How it travels:** it stays where it is and changes hands, meaning who runs the lane
and what the lane is for. It does not appear in every snapshot. It cannot exist
before the Home Line does, and the prologue does not pretend otherwise.

| Snapshot | What the post is doing |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Absent. The thread has not begun. |
| 4 (~2152) | A working lane. Students run it in the dark, call the turn aloud before the eighth post, and name who has the bar. Nobody explains the mechanism. Its reason is **spoken once**, by a trainer, as a flat answer to a student's question: *"Because the road isn't straight."* |
| 5-9 | Absent. No glimpses, no callbacks. |
| 10 (~2603) | The School straightened the other posts in 2390 (off-page). This one survives as a ceremonial oddity that students laugh at. A revision committee uses its line as the example of dead weight while it retires the manual's reasons column. This is where the reason is lost. |
| 11 (2676) | Kiva, thirteen, asks Mara why it is crooked. Mara does not know. The prologue ends on the post. |

**Hard limits:**
- The prologue never shows or hints the sluice function, the drain, or "nine turns."
  A leaning post, possibly with an iron fitting, is all the reader gets.
- The reason line (*because the road isn't straight*, initials S.V., as in Book Two
  Chapter 48) appears spoken once (Snapshot 4) and as printed text once (Snapshot
  10). That is its whole prologue budget.
- Nobody narrates the post as a record, a lesson, or a symbol.

**Pairing with Book Three (postscript = intention, prologue = inheritance):** the
pairing now holds at two points only, the post (which `POSTSCRIPT_PLAN.md` §4 already
allows as a visual anchor) and the one reason line. A reader of both books
recognizes where the line began; a reader of only the prologue sees a lane, a post,
and a sentence that outlives its meaning. The prologue no longer echoes the
postscript's copying act, and `IDEA.md`'s "may echo" language allows that. The
postscript itself is not edited here. See `editor/MOTIF-BUDGET-REVISION.md`.

## 5. Recommended snapshot architecture

Eleven snapshots, each with an explicit causal/temporal handoff to the next.
Order, dating, and function are the load-bearing elements; exact word count
and point-of-view character are drafting decisions. Each row names a **Turn**: the
concrete event or choice the snapshot hangs on. Draft names in parentheses are from
the existing prologue draft and are suggestions, not locks. Where a Turn is marked
"suggested," the author may substitute any concrete household or workplace choice
that does the same job.

## 6. Transition logic into Chapter 1

- The final snapshot (2676) ends on the crooked post, not a thesis statement.
  Chapter One begins with a clear **Four years later — 2680** marker and Kiva's
  collegiate placement day, not a second origin scene.
- No snapshot may end with a line that functions as a thesis statement for
  the book ("and so the age of three doors began" or similar). Let the human
  beat carry the transition.
- The prologue's last unresolved question in the reader's mind should be
  ordinary and personal (what happens to this household today), not cosmic
  (what caused the Fall). The cosmic questions stay open, but they should not
  be the last thing the prologue foregrounds before Chapter 1.

## 7. Drafting choices resolved

1. Use the leaning post (§4) as the one shared physical thread. Record-keeping is
   not a thread, a habit to be shown in every era, or a scene's action (owner ruling,
   2026-10-27). The prologue's independent meaning does not depend on the postscript.
2. The author may use original, one-scene named figures in snapshots 1–5. They do
   not become continuing cast or hidden ancestors.
3. Tamar Vey is the locked historical name and function in Snapshot 6. The author
   owns his exact story, demonstration, humor, and dialogue while preserving the
   fuel-versus-instruction insight and his mortal, catalytic limits.
4. Use the planned eleven snapshots unless two adjacent scenes genuinely duplicate the
   same human choice. Do not collapse the energy revolution into one montage.
5. Snapshot 11 ends on the crooked post before Chapter One begins.
6. Retitle at the prose pass. The prologue title *Somebody Should Write It Down* is the
   banned phrasing; working replacement *The Leaning Post*. Snapshot titles that name
   the motif change too: 2 *The Machine Book* becomes *The Annex*, 4 *The Copy* becomes
   *The South Lane*, 10 *The Superseded Stacks* becomes *Item Eleven*.

## 8. MOTIF BUDGET (prologue)

Added 2026-10-27 after the owner ruling in §4. A "beat" is one scene-level moment, not
one sentence. Over budget means cut at the prose pass. The book-wide table is in
`BOOK_MAP.md` §9.

| Recurring motif | Prologue budget |
|---|---|
| The leaning post | Snapshots 4, 10, 11 only; one beat each; no glimpses elsewhere |
| The reason line (*because the road isn't straight*, S.V.) | Spoken once (4); printed once (10) |
| Record-handling as an action (writing, logging, copying, filing, ledgers, notebooks, catalog cards) | Zero, except three named plot-load-bearing beats: pencil line beside the printed reason (10), the retained notebook and its card (10), the assessor's *no column for this* (11) |
| Characters explaining or arguing why records or reasons matter | Zero |
| Figures (fuel burned, rotation times, ash spent) | Said aloud or chalked where the crew sees them; on the page only when a decision turns on them |
| Wind-up watch (Snapshot 1 texture) | Optional; one mention |
| Titles | No prologue or snapshot title names record-keeping |

**Banned phrasings** (prologue and book): "write it down" and "writing it down" in any
tense; "somebody should" / "someone should" (as a reason to record); "for the record";
"put it in the book"; "put it in the column"; "the why" as a noun; "keep a record" /
"kept a record"; "so it isn't lost"; "in ten years somebody will open this"; any line
in which a character says why records, reasons, or ledgers matter. Quoted `Why:` lines
appear only in the named beats above.
