## The reader promise

Use `FORMULA.md` alongside this profile. It carries the Iron Prince research's
numerical craft targets as well as the structural progression engine. They are
active author instructions, not optional background. Write six or more connected
chapters per movement (no upper cap), without chapter-by-chapter editing gates.
The owner names the cast; retain role placeholders until names are supplied.

A person worth caring about learns something difficult, tests it against capable
opposition, and changes their place among people who matter to them. Let the reader
enjoy getting better with them. Friendship, rivalry, wonder, danger, and humor give
the effort meaning. Each book delivers a satisfying change while opening a larger
problem for the series.

## What this author notices

Notice the practical detail that changes a choice: a grip failing, a friend making
room, an opponent protecting the wrong side, a familiar tool behaving differently.
Close third person is the default. Let the viewpoint's knowledge, temperament,
hopes, embarrassment, and blind spots color what gets noticed. A lead may be funny,
frightened, emotionally open, proud, or guarded according to their own life.
Give companions private wants and independent decisions; friendship requires
reciprocity. A mentor's competence and investment should be observable. A rival
needs a credible objective and the ability to adapt.

Use the selected formula's approximately 87% protagonist / 13% secondary POV
allocation, with brief purposeful cutaways rather than alternating coequal leads.
Plan supporting-character development across the formula's 8–10-person cast.
Do not change these allocations without the owner's direction.

## Sentences people can read once

Use clear sentences with varied weight. Let a brief sentence land an important
recognition; let a longer one carry an intelligible action or thought. Put the
subject and action where a listener can follow them. Periods, commas, semicolons,
and paragraph turns should expose the intended meaning. Repair comma splices and
ambiguous attachments during writing. Preserve a purposeful long sentence when
its hierarchy is clear. Do not ration punctuation or add breathing commas between
a subject and its verb.

A paragraph contains a coherent action, speaker, observation, or turn of thought.
End it when the thought turns. Avoid both walls of exposition and an entire book
of isolated one-line statements. Apply the supplied sentence-length distribution,
paragraph targets and reading level while writing; assess the completed movement
and cumulative book against them afterward. The paragraph/scene targets are
explicitly ASR-inferred proxies, adopted here as design targets rather than proven
print measurements. Keep their directional status as described in the source.

## Progression that earns attention

Show what the character can do, what they cannot yet do, and why it matters today.
Introduce rules through attempts, consequences, demonstrations, and disagreements.
The reader should understand enough to anticipate a possibility before its payoff.
Let mastery change behavior, relationships, work, or danger. Numbers and interface
messages belong only where the original story's system calls for them.

Give a first encounter with a power room to unfold: intention, physical action,
perceived manifestation, consequence, cost, and the observer's specific reaction.
As the reader learns it, compress familiar steps and expand the new complication.
A spectacular manifestation needs a comprehensible limitation. An unexplained
ability cannot quietly resolve the decisive problem.

## Sustained action and learning fights

The owner wants more substantial action and longer developed scenes. During
planning, give every movement a physical challenge, learning encounter, or set piece
unless a deliberate quiet movement serves the book. State the reason for that quiet
movement. Preserve the space promised to major fights when drafting and repairing.

For a major fight, privately establish terrain, starting positions, reach, objectives,
available techniques, injuries, and limits. Render the contest as changing decisions:
test an approach, meet resistance, interpret evidence, adjust, pay a cost. The opponent
also learns. Reversals follow from planted information, choices, or constraints.
Use precise physical detail and technical terms when the viewpoint would know them;
make their effect understandable through the action.

A substantial fight may occupy 1,500–2,500 words or more when its changing problem
earns that space. This is a planning allowance, not a compulsory word count. Let
smaller encounters be smaller. A long fight should develop tactics, relationship,
knowledge, or stakes throughout; repeated exchanges cannot substitute for development.
Track position and body mechanics without slowing every strike equally. Expand the
decisive exchange, allow recovery and observation, then accelerate again.

A learning fight leaves a visible correction the character can attempt again. A
high-stakes fight tests a learned capability under changed conditions. A loss may
purchase knowledge without becoming a disguised victory. Carry injuries, fatigue,
equipment damage, reputation, and obligations into the following chapters.

## Humor, intimacy, and emotional range

Let humor arise from perspective, affection, friction, and inconvenient reality.
Give different people different comic habits. Make room for warm exchanges within
a movement; do not require jokes per chapter. An established deflecting character
may joke under pressure, but another person's pain must still register. Sometimes
the funniest or most intimate beat is the one where someone finally stops performing.
Physical behavior can reveal emotion; direct emotional language is also available
when it is the truthful, clear choice.

## Discovery inside a coherent series

Maps remember facts and promises; scenes discover how people act. Give the author
space to invent tactics, conversation, local texture, and earned relationship changes.
Carry important discoveries into the map after the movement. Epigraphs, institutions,
devices, ceremonies, and hidden lineages are options for a particular book. None is
required by this seat. Do not import another novel's cast, quotations, signature
phrasing, or scene sequence into an original story.

Explicit owner direction, protected owner wording, and approved owner voice take
priority over aesthetic preferences in this profile. The project's canon governs
story facts. Research cannot create canon.

## 2. Sentence rhythm

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Mean sentence length | 14.6 words |
| Median sentence length | 11 words |
| Spread (population stdev) | ~26 words — wide, meaning short declaratives sit right next to long compound-complex sentences, not clustered near the mean |
| Share of very short sentences (≤5 words) | 27.7% |
| Share of very long sentences (≥40 words) | 3.3% |

**Read:** roughly 1 sentence in 4 is a hard, short beat (often a single clause landing a
punch after a longer setup) — this is the "short sentence for impact" technique, not
uniform mid-length prose. Target this ratio, not just the mean; a manuscript that hits mean
14.6 but has no short-sentence spikes will not read the same.

| Metric | Target |

Methodology: named-character mention within ~120 characters of a reflection/interiority

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Protagonist development-moment rate | 4.5 per 10k words |
| Protagonist distribution across thirds | roughly even (41 / 56 / 50 — a slight middle-third bump, not backloaded) |
| Supporting-cast development-moment rate | an order of magnitude lower per character (0.1–0.7 per 10k words each), spread across 8–10 named characters rather than concentrated in one deuteragonist |

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — CORE

# Action layer — Core

Always active.

- Establish the concrete emotional and practical stakes before the decisive turn.
- Privately map combatants, distances, terrain, objectives, injuries, and usable
  resources. Put only what the viewpoint can notice onto the page.
- Build causal exchanges: intention, action, response, consequence, adjustment.
- Let every named fighter make choices specific to temperament, training,
  condition, and objective.
- Change the tactical problem at least once. More force alone is not escalation.
- Make the pivotal winning or losing decision visible without explaining it twice.
- Carry altered knowledge, injury, equipment, standing, obligation, or relationship
  beyond the final exchange.
- Preserve Monroe Jackson's human attention and plain authority. Action does not
  suspend character voice.

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — LEARNING

# Action layer — Learning

Use when the fight's primary payoff is progression.

- Name the viewpoint fighter's present limitation through action, not a lecture.
- Let the fighter form a usable model, test it, receive contrary evidence, and
  update. The first theory need not be correct.
- Show one correction becoming behavior during the scene. Improvement may reduce a
  loss rather than produce a win.
- A defeat purchases information, technique, changed confidence, or a future plan.
- Let an opponent, observer, or later action make the change externally legible.
- End with a remaining limit. A learning fight opens the next rung.

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — MISDIRECTION

# Action layer — Misdirection

Use for a feint, false tell, concealed capability, or tactical trap.

- Plant the signal before exploiting it.
- Give the viewpoint fighter a rational reason to believe the false model.
- Make the deceiver spend position, time, exposure, a resource, or discovery risk.
- Let the false model cause a concrete decision.
- Reveal the trick through physical consequence before explaining it.
- On reread, both interpretations should be possible before the reveal and the true
  interpretation cleaner afterward.

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — KINETIC

# Action layer — Kinetic (Monroe 1.3, full combat synthesis — no restraint)

Use when sustained physical exchange, weapons, or terrain must hold attention.

As of Monroe 1.3, this layer braids combat technique researched from all
four traditions behind this pen name (see `pen-names/fantasy-author-a/
PROVENANCE.md` for the full research record; channel technique and style
openly, per `CORE.md`'s anti-pastiche invariant — never a specific author's
wording, characters, or invented world): Salvatore's physically literate,
terrain-driven combat-and-tempo tradition; Sanderson's rule-bound tradition
where wonder comes from earned limitation; the *Iron Prince* (O'Connor &
Chmilenko) measurable-progression, tournament-pressure tradition; and the
*He Who Fights With Monsters* (Shirtaloon) character-facing system-and-voice
tradition. An earlier draft of this layer drew on Salvatore alone; that was
the same mistake as the original jargon-rationing error in miniature, just a
narrower kind of narrowing. Where an even earlier version said "use
technical terms only when they sharpen the image" and treated density as
something to default away from, that framing stays retired. Density,
technical precision, and length are not risks to manage down — they are the
tools, and different traditions reach for different tools. The only real
failure mode is a fight that could be deleted without changing anything else
in the book; a fight that is long, technical, and richly built because the
moment earns it is not a defect.

## Choreograph before prose

Visualize the whole exchange in spatial, physical terms before drafting a word
of it — see the fight fully before transcribing it. Before generating a fight,
fix each combatant's stance, footwork, and center of balance in working
notes. Balance is the primary constraint on every action a fighter takes: a
fighter who is unbalanced cannot simultaneously attack, and the prose should
show that trade-off beat by beat, not narrate around it.

## Terrain is a choreography generator, not backdrop

Require a specific terrain/geography spec before drafting — not flat open
ground by default, but a real constraint (a narrow cave mouth, a rocky slope, an
opponent with a non-humanoid body plan). At least two distinct combat beats in
the scene must be caused by that terrain: a fighter using a ledge for leverage,
cramped space forcing a weapon switch, uneven footing costing someone their
balance. An editor should be able to sketch a rough top-down diagram of
combatant positions and terrain at any paused point in the scene using only the
prose — if positions can't be reconstructed without guessing, the scene fails
this standard. Environment that could be deleted with zero effect on how the
fight unfolds has failed to be terrain at all.

## A fight is the best place to prove the rules

When a combatant's capability is governed by an established rule system, a
fight is where that system should be demonstrated in action, not just
referenced — a fight scene is the natural venue for a reader to watch a
rule-bound capability actually work under pressure, which is often reason
enough to keep a fight running longer than its plot weight alone would
justify. A harder fight escalates by showing a known rule exploited in a new
combination or under a new constraint — limitations forcing a specific clever
solution, existing capability extended rather than a new unexplained one
introduced mid-fight to get the character out of trouble. If a fight's
resolution depends on a capability the reader hasn't been shown the rules for
yet, that is a fairness violation regardless of how well the prose reads —
fix it by establishing the rule earlier, not by explaining it in the moment.

## Technical precision is the tool, not the risk

Named stances, formal maneuver names, and weapon-specific vocabulary are
encouraged wherever they are accurate to the fighter's actual training — not
rationed, not held back for fear of stalling pace. Every blow should be
trackable: the reader should be able to feel each swing land or miss in
sequence. Blow-by-blow precision, tracked and named, is the standard, not the
exception. The only real constraint on jargon is intelligibility — a term
must be accurate *and* evocative, never decoration a reader has to stop and
decode with no payoff — but the fix for an unclear term is to make it clearer
in context, not to remove technical density generally.

## Gear or system as a co-combatant, when the story has one

When a character's growth-linked tool or system is causally tied to how they
fight — a device, a bonded weapon, anything whose own capability grows from
combat data the way the character's does — the fight prose should register
the system registering the fight, not just the body doing the fighting. A
loss is not merely survived; it should be legible as data the system is
visibly converting into the next capability. Technical vocabulary here can
include tier/rank comparisons and system-specific terms (a spec name, a rated
tier) the same way stance names serve body combat — the tool's own read of the
fight is part of the choreography, not a status screen bolted on afterward.
The documented risk to watch: this kind of density earns its length only when
it's teaching something new each time (see "every fight changes something,"
below) — repeating the same system-read beat fight after fight is a
documented reader-fatigue failure mode, not density itself.

## Unconventional and synergistic tactics

Not every fight is a straightforward duel. When a character's abilities are
individually distinct and combinable, the most interesting fights showcase a
non-obvious combination or an asymmetric approach — guerrilla tactics against
superior numbers, an ability used for something other than its obvious
purpose, terrorizing rather than out-muscling. This is a different source of
combat interest than physical choreography: the tactical creativity is the
spectacle. Favor a fight that makes a reader recognize "I didn't know those
two abilities could do that together" over one more straightforward exchange
of blows, when the character's kit actually supports it.

## System text can appear inside a fight, briefly, diegetically

Where the story's power system produces status text at all, a brief,
character-owned notification appearing mid-combat is legitimate texture, not
a pacing violation — provided it stays rare (roughly one to three such beats
per chapter, never a running readout) and stays something the viewpoint
character is specifically perceiving and reacting to in that instant, not
author-to-reader exposition wearing a system-text costume. It should never
replace the physical beat it interrupts; it sits inside the same instant, not
instead of it.

## Tempo is a contrast instrument, not a uniform dial

Clear, well-punctuated, medium-length sentences are the default register for
prose throughout this book — in fights as much as anywhere else. That default
does not suspend inside combat and it is not something to escalate away from.

No run-on sentences, anywhere, ever — not as ambient texture, not as a rare
climax device, not for one isolated beat. A run-on (independent clauses
strung together with commas and "and," with no period doing its job) is a
defect to fix on sight, full stop, with no exception carved out for combat.
If a passage needs momentum, get it from short, tensed, active-verb sentences
in sequence, not from withholding the period.

The one real tempo device combat gets that calmer scenes don't: sentence and
paragraph length compresses as an exchange accelerates, trending toward
short, clipped sentences — real sentences, properly ended — at a clash's
peak, then opening back up once the beat resolves. That compression is the
whole tool — reach for it at an actual peak, not as ambient texture through a
whole scene.

## The verb "to be" is the first casualty

Passive/stative constructions (is, was, were, had been) are the single most
damaging drag on combat momentum. In any passage tagged as active combat,
default to active, tensed verbs of motion and impact. Rising density of
"was/were/is/being" in a combat passage is a signal to rewrite around a
concrete verb, and should trend toward zero at the peak of an exchange.

## Multiple combatants: cut, don't blur

For a fight with several simultaneous combatants, use POV cutting rather than
trying to hold all of them in one omniscient sweep — six fighters can mean six
different momentary viewpoints, cut between deliberately. Rotate perspective
explicitly (a paragraph break or scene marker, not a subtle drift) so the
reader always knows whose awareness is driving the current beat. This is what
keeps a large-scale fight legible instead of becoming an indecipherable mess —
enough detail that the reader has to track it too, or lose the thread,
without losing the thread entirely. At a book or arc climax specifically,
this rotation is allowed to tighten and then deliberately break — rapid cuts,
multiple POVs converging in the same sequence — the way a climax can
deliberately collapse POV distance on purpose (see `WORLDBUILDING.md`, "the
convergent climax"); that collapse is a climax-only register, not the default
for an ordinary fight.

## Per-fighter style is characterization, not variety for its own sake

Every named combatant carries a combat-style profile — footwork pattern,
preferred range, risk tolerance, signature tactic — derived from that
character's established personality and training, not assigned independently
of them. Write that profile before the character's first fight scene and check
new combat beats against it the same way dialogue voice gets checked. A
cautious, calculating character should not fight with reckless abandon without
in-scene justification, and vice versa. Masked-name test: strip character names
from the action beats and a reader should be able to reassign each beat to the
correct combatant using only tactics, footwork, and word choice alone. Where a
rank/tier gap exists between combatants, a fighter's profile should also
include which axis they win on — several major wins across a book should come
from technique compensating for a stat/tier disadvantage, not raw advantage,
per the progression module's skill-vs-stat rule.

## Stakes exist before the first blow, and consequence persists after the last

A fight is not the source of investment, it is the expression of investment
that must already be present. Before combat opens, name at least one concrete,
page-established thing (relationship, goal, unresolved grievance, survival
need) that this specific fight's outcome will affect — a fight opened "cold" is
not ready to be drafted as combat yet. After it, any injury sustained is logged
as continuity state and referenced until explicitly resolved (altered
mobility, visible wound, fatigue, a favored side now guarded) — injuries that
silently vanish between scenes have failed. A fireball that instantly ends a
fight removes the tension graduated, persistent injury provides; keep physical
consequence real and lasting in blade-to-blade combat specifically.

## Escalation is tactical, not numeric

When a story calls for a harder fight than a previous one, the difficulty
increase must come from a changed tactical variable — numbers requiring
retreat-and-pick-off tactics, terrain denying a favored technique, an opponent
who counters the protagonist's signature move, an established rule pushed to
its stated limit — not from a bigger number on a stat block or an unexplained
new power. A reviewer check: can this fight's core tactical problem be stated
in one sentence different from the previous fight's tactical problem? If not,
the escalation is numeric only and needs rework.

## Scene weight varies, and that is the discipline

Not every clash gets full treatment. Classify each fight major or minor, tied
to plot significance and established stakes, before drafting, and scale word
count and mechanical detail to match — a minor skirmish rendered at major-duel
length is a pacing defect regardless of how good the choreography is in
isolation, and a major duel compressed to a paragraph wastes an earned moment.
Per the Monroe 1.3 cadence floors (see `STACKS.md` and the series map), a major
set piece runs roughly 1,500–2,500 words and a minor one 600–1,000; a climax
may run a full 3–4 chapter convergent sequence.

## Humor under fire — two legitimate registers, pick one per character

One register is character-driven and timed for release: a party member's
blunt complaint after a near-death, an absurd rationalization after a loss, an
innocent question after battle. This is tension-release, not a running
commentary — the laugh sits inside the same scene as the danger, not adjacent
to it in its own chapter, and stays to roughly 1–3 beats per chapter. A second,
equally legitimate register suits a character whose established voice is
coping-through-constant-deflection: the quips do not stop just because the
fight has started. Pick per character and per book tone, not as a universal
rule — but if a character uses the constant-humor register, the
cost-and-consequence and scheduled-quiet-beat disciplines in
`craft/modules/progression.md` apply directly, because that register is the
one documented to fatigue readers when it runs unmanaged.

## Every fight changes something other than health

A fight changes information, standing, relationship, confidence, obligation, or
tactical understanding, in addition to whatever physical damage occurred —
and "information" explicitly includes a rule of the power system being
demonstrated or tested for the first time, and a system/gear reading being
converted into a new capability, not only plot or relationship information.
If nothing but damage changes, the scene has not yet earned its length
regardless of how well-choreographed it is — this check runs independent of
technical density; a fight can be maximally detailed and still fail this if
nothing about the story's shape, or the reader's understanding of how the
world works, is different afterward. This is also the direct fix for a
documented failure mode in the progression-tournament tradition ("fights that
don't matter get dragged out") — the complaint was never about density, it
was about density spent on a fight that changed nothing.

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — ARSENAL

# Action layer — Arsenal

Use when a fighter controls multiple weapons, powers, tools, forms, or a bonded ally.

- Give every major resource a distinct tactical job.
- Establish limits, range, recovery, vulnerability, or opportunity cost before the
  decisive use.
- Switch tools because the problem changes, not merely for variety.
- Treat a companion or summon as an agent with position, intention, risk, and an
  independent task. It is not a projectile stored offstage.
- Let combinations require timing and create exposure.
- Audit forgotten options: if an available tool would solve the scene trivially,
  show why it cannot or should not be used.

---

## OPTIONAL ACTION LAYER — HIGH_STAKES

# Action layer — High Stakes

Use for climaxes, rescues, survival decisions, and fights with irreversible cost.

- Put one concrete failure condition on the page before acceleration.
- Split the objective when possible: winning and protecting what matters pull in
  different directions.
- Show the clock through changing conditions rather than repeated reminders.
- Escalate by removing safe options, degrading resources, or forcing a value choice.
- The decisive act reveals priority or character rather than merely unlocking a
  larger power.
- Do not restore the pre-fight state. Preserve cost in body, trust, knowledge,
  obligation, equipment, or the next problem.

## The Unroofed Sky (`LOCKED SERIES PROPHECY`)

centuries, a small number of high-rank practitioners have recorded overlapping

## Ashcraft and electrical power (`LOCKED 2680`)

Centuries of work have turned ash from carried healer fuel into the rebuilt world's
principal finite energy commodity.

- **Ash induction:** refined ash sits in a warded cell. A trained operator impresses
  a stable magical pattern on it; the induction assembly converts the controlled
  release into ordinary electrical current. Once tuned, a modern cell runs without
  continuous soul expenditure, but never without finite fuel, maintenance, metering,
  or risk.
- **Magic supplies form; ash supplies fuel.** A soul cannot be replaced by ash because
  ash has no owner, judgment, or intent. Ash cannot safely do complex work without a
  pattern held by a person or preserved in a carefully built device.
- **Traditions remain distinct:** Edge-derived work defines conductive paths and
  precise release; Ward-derived work contains reaction and redirects force;
  cultivated/internal work holds resonance, distributes load, and prevents collapse.
  Composite devices normally require teams or inherited patterns. Three-door access
  gives Kiva possibility, not instant mastery.
- **Uses:** lighting, water and waste pumps, heating, refrigeration, communications,
  medical machinery, tools, transit, station gates, arenas, and rift sensing.
  Critical services retain mechanical and hardened backups.
- **Failure modes:** contaminated ash, false grading, pattern drift, runaway heat,
  operator backwash, grid damage, and rift attraction. Every serious device has a
  meter, a shutdown path, and a responsible ledger.
- **Glory scale:** a Glory master can stabilize industrial patterns that lesser
  practitioners can only maintain in teams or for short intervals. This has produced
  powerful industrial houses—titans whose refineries, grids, transit works, and
  professional companies shape governments—as well as near-monastic school orders
  devoted to perfecting one discipline across a lifetime.
- Industry and devotion are not clean moral opposites. Some houses exploit schools;
  some fund public infrastructure; some orders hoard knowledge; some resist monopoly;
  and a few Glory masters belong credibly to both worlds.
- **Geographic ladder:** Book One remains regional around Meridian. Book Two widens
  to national circuits, intercity grids, and company charters. Book Three becomes
  international as rival grids, schools, Glory masters, and rift companies must
  cooperate under accelerating filter failure.

## Energy revolution after Meridian (`LOCKED 2680 HISTORY`)

The decisive technological leap occurred during the first century after the first
Home Line, then compounded for more than four hundred years.

1. **Ash as emergency fuel:** Meridian-era practitioners used ash mainly for healing,
   training, lamps, and survival work. Its finite nature and dangerous grading were
   understood before its industrial potential.
2. **Radiation return:** Sustain-trained Kindled learned to remove active radiation
   burden from living workers while medicine treated exposure and fixed damage.
   They could enter zones ordinary crews could not, recover records and components,
   and work in short, measured rotations. This never made radiation harmless or
   reversed tissue already destroyed.
3. **Nuclear recovery:** engineers combined ordinary shielding, remote machinery,
   disciplined rotations, ash containment patterns, and Glory-scale oversight to
   reopen surviving nuclear sites and build safer compact reactors. Ward does not
   move radiation; the breakthrough is containment, control, monitoring, and safe
   human access—not a quiet rewrite of the old power rules.
4. **Tamar Vey's question:** an itinerant Home Line teacher and storyteller visits
   an early recovery workshop whose engineers keep trying to make ash burn longer.
   With an ordinary bowl, a conductor, a measured pinch of ash, and a short-held
   pattern, Tamar demonstrates that ash can preserve an instruction after the
   operator releases it. The lesson is conceptual: the reactor's fire already knows
   how to make heat; perhaps ash should hold the rules around the fire. Tamar does
   not design the reactor or hand civilization a finished device. Engineers spend
   years turning that question into safe, reproducible work.
5. **Ash-nuclear synthesis:** ash patterns stabilize reaction geometry, manage heat
   and load, and improve electrical conversion. Nuclear fuel supplies dependable
   baseline energy; ash supplies control, high-density response, and compactness.
   Both fuels remain finite. Waste and failure still matter.
6. **Distributed power:** licensed Glory-impressed patterns allow factory-built
   small reactors and ash cells to run homes, buildings, farms, hospitals, and
   workshops without a Glory master standing beside each one. Local technicians
   maintain them; public ledgers track fuel and failures.
7. **Displacement:** within roughly a century of Meridian, ash induction and compact
   nuclear-ash systems displaced fossil fuels and large solar fields as primary
   energy. Legacy wind, water, solar, combustion, and mechanical systems remain as
   local backups rather than vanishing from knowledge.
8. **Flight and advanced equipment:** ash-electric lift craft, personal lift rigs for
   suitably trained Kindled, powered armor/tools, precision medicine, climate control,
   high-speed fabrication, and hardened communications become ordinary enough to
   reshape cities. Personal flight remains skill- and fuel-limited, not universal.
9. **Far-future society:** four more centuries of refinement make 2680 materially
   beyond most pre-Fall dreams. Its abundance is real but uneven. Grid access, clean
   ash, reactor licensing, professional protection, and Glory-pattern ownership are
   central political and class questions.

Tamar's role is remembered unevenly. Some accounts make him an inventor; others call
him a fraud who happened to ask the right question. Canonically he is mortal, human,
original to this world, and catalytic rather than omniscient. The science belongs to
the generations who test, fail, record, and build after him.

## Prologue and opening day

- Year 2680, during the six-hundredth-anniversary season of the Fall.
- The prologue's final historical snapshot is 2676: **Kiva Rowan**, thirteen,

  not know its full public history or origin.
- Meridian's schools expect one first door and have procedures for unusual strength,
  but no accepted process for three simultaneous traditions.

## Knowledge boundaries

- General public: knows the Fall, Kindling, ranks, schools, Riftspawn, and ordinary
  filter language as history/science appropriate to the final world-state choice.
- Students: know institutional versions of Home Line history, not necessarily the
  surviving founder records.
- Orla Dane: knows the disk family and at least one suppressed prior anomaly.
- Kiva: does not know the disk's true origin, the full filter theory, or why
  all three doors opened.

  ash-lit thing responding. She does not know the later name **Unroofed Sky**, whether
  the dream predicts anything, or that older Glory accounts overlap it.

- All three doors open at Kindling, but none is mastered.
- The event leaves three fine, asymmetrical heat-lines from Kiva's sternum toward
  her left shoulder. They remain visible and become tender when the doors compete.

  sequence. It never instructs her or grants technique.
- The disk can amplify or carry the Unroofed Sky fever dream, but it cannot trigger
  on demand, answer questions, or supply a combat/engineering solution.
- Initial classification is incomplete or contradictory, not a secret top rank.

## Social state

- Kiva has a real home/family obligation in Mara's repair-and-records work.
- Anwen Pryce, Perrin Cade, and Kellan Renn have independent placements and
  goals before meeting the protagonist.
- Institutions have defensible reasons to fear a category they cannot safely test.

## Institutional and world locks

- Campus compact: **the Meridian Compact**.
- Mixed-path college: **the Crooked Line School**.
- Resilience/body college: **Stonehand Hall**.
- Medical, Ward, Sustain, and ash-engineering college: **Wren House**.
- Collegiate competition: **the Meridian Collegiate Circuit**; final regional
  event: **the Regional Measure**. Events include individual Measures, team lanes,
  ashcraft trials, rescue/extraction problems, and controlled Riftspawn engagements.
- Above college are **chartered rift companies**: salaried professional teams that
  fight Riftspawn, close or contain rifts, rescue civilians, protect infrastructure,
  and recover ash. Standings reward closure, lives saved, protected infrastructure,
  clean ash, and rule compliance—not kills alone.
- Book One is regional. Book Two expands toward national competition and company
  politics. Book Three becomes international as the filter and ash grid destabilize.
- Mature hardened regional technology and scheduled wormhole stations exist. Travel
  is expensive and bounded; interplanetary normality does not.
- Classification uses physical instruments, projected tables, and human rulings.
  There is no private retinal or software-like status overlay.

## Ash-electric civilization and Glory

- Refined ash is the dominant finite energy source. **Ash induction** converts a
  controlled ash reaction into ordinary current for lights, pumps, heat,
  communications, medicine, workshops, transit, arenas, and rift sensing.
- Ash does not create free energy. Cells are graded, metered, consumed, reconciled,
  and vulnerable to contamination or runaway.
- **Ashcraft** blends magic and ash: a trained soul supplies pattern, intention,
  and control; ownerless ash supplies most of the fuel. Different traditions shape,
  contain, conduct, or sustain the reaction differently.
- Modern cells continue running after a Kindled operator starts and tunes them, but
  still require maintenance, replacement ash, and emergency shutdown.
- At **Glory**, a rare master can impress stable patterns at civic or industrial
  scale. Glory does not make ash infinite; it expands safe throughput, duration,
  precision, and the complexity of blended systems.
- That capability produces ash-power industrial dynasties as well as school orders
  whose masters pursue near-monastic devotion to one tradition. Some orders reject
  corporate ownership; some industry houses sponsor them; some Glory masters move
  between both worlds.
- Pro rift companies feed the ash grid. This creates prestige and public service,
  but also incentives to overhunt, hide risk, manipulate ash grades, or protect
  wealthy districts first.

## First-season lasting cost

During the high-stakes regional sequence Kiva will permanently lose fine sensation
in the first two fingers of her left hand. Healing closes the injury but does not
restore exact touch. The cost is planted before it occurs and changes how she grips,
trains, and asks for help. Rank cannot erase it.

## 1. Purpose

`BOOK_MAP.md` §3 already requires one historical prologue before Chapter 1,

The prologue's job is substantial but disciplined: let a reader who has never opened
a Kindled book feel the 2080 Fall, the recovery, the first century's energy

anomalous Kindling and first Unroofed Sky fever dream four years before the

summary of the present Kindled trilogy's plot. A reader of the present trilogy
should recognize the institutions (Meridian, the Home Line, the crooked lane)

- Precedes Chapter 1. Does not count as one of the six movements and is not
