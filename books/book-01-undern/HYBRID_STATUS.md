titles, and its own specific canon/lore kept intact) with decisions and
relationships imported from *Hobb's Wall* — not spliced prose, but the same
emotional beats rewritten at Tallow's speed. Named for the market town where
Cinder Company winters (Ch. 18) — a real place in Tallow's own text, not yet
used as an edition name.

than the original if Tallow Gate continues to be revised independently —
resync from `books/book-01-tallow-gate/` before doing further reconstruction

## The editing rule for every future transplant

For each imported beat: What changes because this scene exists? Who makes the
consequential choice? Can it be carried in half the words Hobb's Wall used?
Does it advance the road, the relationships, or both? Has the same emotional
point already been made? If Hobb's Wall spent 800 words on a turn, find
Tallow's own way to make the same turn land in far fewer, in Tallow's voice —
do not import the word count along with the beat.

## Known continuity note

Movement 4's reconstruction keeps Tallow's own account of Cinder Company (no
named individual responsible is ever identified — "I've never once found out
who paid them"). **This means Movements 5–6, when reconstructed, must not
import Hobb's Wall's Tull/Hoyle material** — that naming is specific to Hobb's
Wall's own version of events and would contradict what Movement 4 already
established here. Any future Movement 5 reconstruction pass needs its own
antagonist-confrontation solution consistent with Tallow's "never finds out"
throughline, not a copy of Hobb's Wall's.

## Chapter-level quality test (apply to every future reconstruction pass)

Every chapter should deliver at least three of: a new pressure; a consequential
choice; a changed relationship; new information that alters prior
understanding; a memorable visual or physical event; an ending that creates
forward pull. A chapter delivering only explanation and travel should be
merged or rebuilt rather than passed as-is.

## Character-agency requirement (apply across every future pass)

Toren should make at least one decision per movement Rook would not have made
for him. Dessa should perceive or attempt something before being instructed.
Wyck should challenge the group's hierarchy and eventually choose loyalty.
Milo should actively create family cohesion, making his eventual loss
structurally devastating. Rook's choices should increasingly contradict his
claim that these people are merely his responsibility — the progression is
the book's real engine: *cargo → students → family → equals → chosen over
revenge.*
