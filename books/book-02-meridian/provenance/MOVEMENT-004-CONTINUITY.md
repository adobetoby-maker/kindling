## The wash (D+46), as staged

**Terrain:**
- The last slope of the old line drops east to west onto a 250-pace embankment across the wash's bottom. The embankment is a man's height and a half high, with a bed four paces wide of packed stone. Iron posts stand along its north edge every 20 paces.
- North side: the wash's bed, full of grey ash drifts (off the hill, contaminated). They are deepest against the embankment's north foot, half as high as the bank, around the culvert.
- South side: a dead reed marsh standing in black water, running to the river. It is "the dark side".
- The culvert is a stone arch 180 paces from the east end and 70 from the west. The south face fell out long ago and the arch settled onto the fall, so that half is "sitting on stone". The north side has been undercut by the wash, so the crown hangs on that side and is cracked across nearly to the south edge. An iron post stands in a lead collar in the north parapet's ring stone and leans out over the wash.
- The west end runs into a cut through a low ridge. The counter reads under the red at the cut's mouth and about 30 twenty paces in.
- The pump house is 1 mile beyond.

**Counter readings:**
- D+45 dusk scouting: 14 at the east end; 40 in gusts; a guessed "a hundred, maybe" at the drift foot by the arch.
- D+46 at dawn with no wind: 12 at the start, then 20, 26 and 30.
- In the gust: 60 inside the lee and 90 outside it. After Rook's lip kills, 90 inside and 120 outside.
- At the lip: 110, 120, then 140 after Jab's kill. Buzz, red, after the fourth husk.

**Wind:** west-north-west off the hill, blowing the drifts across the bed. It was down at dawn, returned at about 100 paces, and dropped again at midday.

**Positions at the start (and what was carried):**
- Callie on the north (windward) edge with the hook and the second counter, carrying a settled Handful.
- Pell at Duchess's head on the south side, knife on his belt.
- The cart in the middle of the bed, carrying 3 home jars, the empty lead carrier and 5 vent jars.
- Jab at the tailboard on the north side, with 8 fence jars in the frame.
- Dee beside Jab with the glass and the road jar (3) in her arm, and a settled Handful.
- Rook at the tail on the south side, to fence the marsh; half a turn.
- Toren on the south side by the north axle, carrying a full settled Handful.

**Exits:** back east (250 paces, reading 14) until the arch; forward west (70 paces to the cut) after it. Never the reeds (chest-deep) and never the drifts.

**Sequence (glass turns are minutes; Rook's lit time is the clock before the red, Dee's glass after it):**
1. At about 100 paces the wind returns. "Lane"; the lee runs in the gusts.
2. Three husks rise out of the drifts and come at the lane. Rook crosses to the north edge with his blade lit and kills two on the lip; the wind throws the grey over everyone. Dee: "Not in the wind."
3. About two glass turns after Rook lights, the north wheel reaches the crack. Toren sets from a bed slab on the south half ("Set"). The north half of the crown drops and the cart hangs on his strut ("Holding"). The wheel in the hole takes some of it; the strut carries **the lean**, "less than a wheel on the gaps, not much less", and it does not roll past. He counts breaths under it. Duchess screams in the shafts.
4. Two hounds come out of the reeds at the dark under the cart. Rook recrosses and fences one; the other goes round.
5. Pell, dragged onto the north half with Duchess, cuts both traces at the right place. Duchess bolts east. The rotten north edge tips Pell off the bank into the drift, breaking his right ankle on the arch's wing wall. His knife goes into the wash.
6. Jab goes down the bank into the drift to Pell; Dee orders "Nobody else comes down". Callie twice fails to roll the lee down the loose bank ("There's no still").
7. Dee lies on her belly on the lip and reaches the road jar down to Jab's left hand, his back to the bank. He runs draw ten, vent three into the drift downwind.
8. The third husk comes along the bank foot at Jab's hands. He goes to let the knife come, finds his left hand on the jar, takes it off, and only then lets the knife come on his left fist. He kills the husk with the lit hook, and the grey sifts over Pell ("Every one you kill down there goes on him").
9. The second hound goes under the cart and hits Rook high in the back, reopening the old wound. Rook kills it. He has been lit 12 glass turns, two over his half working turn. Dee: "You're off… That's the Director's word, and it's mine." He sits. Dee: "Toren. Say it." Toren: "Holding. Half the stone gone, and a bit. It's the lean, not the wheel… The slab keeps moving. I keep catching it." (About ten glass turns under the lean.)
10. Jab calls *jar* before the road jar (3) is dry. "Then the frame." Dee: "That's the fence's… it comes off the forty-eight." Jab: "Open it… It's mine to say." Dee lifts jar A out of his rack from the lip.
11. At the fourth draw Jab's palm goes warm (lodging). He calls *vent* himself and vents 8, then 10 breaths. Dee cannot rotate him: Rook is off, and Dee must stay clean as watch. Jab: "You come down, there's nobody clean."
12. The fourth husk hits Jab at the hip. He falls forward over Pell like a sack, hands kept where they were. Jar B breaks on the rail and its clean ash runs down his back into the drift. Callie hands the Ward over to Edge on a count of two and cuts the husk from the lip; its grey falls on them. Buzz.
13. Callie taps the stuck needle once: true red, a body trapped. Dee names **Jab** (Pell), **Toren** (the cart) and **Callie** (the lane, "Not down the bank… along the top… lean it"). She takes Rook out and they go west to the cut. The glass runs from the red; Dee calls **every** turn from the cut with the three names, and Callie hears only some ("The wind would have perhaps one in two").
14. Callie, on the lip a pace east of the broken crown (her side catching as she leans), holds a *still* lean-to lee off the curve on the lip's stone, over Jab and Pell and not over herself. Standing still costs less than walking but more than a door ("thinner and longer, over two bodies… harder than the hook ever had"). She has about half her settled Handful ("not much more than three turns of the glass" at that price), so she runs the plan from the fire: **lane** when she sees a gust lift the grey off the drifts upwind, **down** in the lulls (5–20 breaths). Jab draws the same in lulls; the lee does nothing about the ground.
15. Toren: "The stone's under a quarter… Every time, I catch it, and catching's dear." The slab shifts. Callie: "Ground… the ring stone… cut… on the springing on the good side." He lets go for a hand's drop (left arm jarred, "Same place… It's in") and re-sets on the ring stone: "It's not moving." The catch empties his stone.
16. Callie hears the call for turn **2**. Toren: "The stone's gone… I'm on my own now. Two turns of the glass of that, and a bit, and then I'm at my floor."
