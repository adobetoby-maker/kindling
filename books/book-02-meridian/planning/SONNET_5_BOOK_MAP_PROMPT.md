## Owner decisions — treat these as controlling

- Series title: **Kindled**.
- Book Two working title: **Meridian**.
- Book Two keeps **all three protagonists: Callie, Jab, and Toren**. None may be
  demoted to a cameo or supporting role.
- Jab's available first-book continuation is considered complete by the owner.
  Callie's and the Hobb's Wall/Toren material are not considered complete and may
  need expansion or redrafting. Do not claim otherwise.
- Book Two opens in Meridian after the three roads became one group at the end of
  Book One. The medical outcomes, residency decisions, and institutional costs
  belong here.
- Residency pressure and repeated battles at the **Sag line** are major story
  pressures. Interpret the owner's spoken “slump” as the already-canonical Sag
  line unless the manuscript proves otherwise.
- A purposeful trip outside Meridian to accomplish a concrete, necessary objective
  must become a core adventure, not a brief errand or optional side quest.
- Retain each protagonist's existing coaches and learned traditions:
  - Toren: Rook/Elias Marsh.
  - Callie: Katori's field/combat judgment and Pell's ground-reading/cartographic
    discipline.
  - Jab: the Elder's practical teaching and communal ethic remain foundational;
    his local Meridian instruction may deepen through the joined cast.
- Add one new senior teacher, currently named `[MERIDIAN MASTER]`, who has opened
  more doors than Rook and can teach access, control, combination, recovery, and
  safe reserve use. Do not canonize a permanent name. The owner names characters.
  Recommend the teacher's door count, limits, history, teaching flaw, personal
  cost, and reason for teaching Meridian's youths. Do not make this person
  omnipotent, unbound, or a source of effortless advancement.
- Deeply teach and dramatize reserve-ash: what it is, how it is collected, graded,
  cleaned, stored, carried, taken into a person's reserve, spent through different
  doors, overdrawn, contaminated, lost, and priced. Exposition must arise through
  work, training, disagreement, failure, treatment, and combat.
- Meridian's youths will need to begin training as defenders because the Sag is
  worsening. Make this necessary and consequential, but do not turn every child
  into a disposable soldier or erase the moral conflict in asking them to defend
  the station.
- Progression, training, power discovery, fights, relationships, and found-family
  pleasure are the book's engine. Institutional politics are pressure and
  consequence, not the dominant genre engine.
- Preserve the established three-door domains, rank ladder, Guard discovery,
  twin-kindled rule, and the fact that opening another door must be earned.
- Preserve medical stakes for Senna, Tilda, and Tonk. Their care is not scenery.
- Keep the recurring pleasure of a capable group solving concrete problems with
  distinct abilities. Every major fight must change understanding, capability,
  relationship, injury, resources, or obligation.
