### 2. Red-call breach and rule: PASS

**The breach is owned (ch. 21 ll. 389–393).**
- The Director reads Hallet's line. It matches Dee's pre-slide rule in ch. 20 l. 51 ("him too").
- She asks, "When she called red, who obeyed it?"
- Dee answers, "Nobody did… That's mine. It isn't theirs."
- Callie owns calling it and then going in, and the Director confirms that calling it was her job.

**The rule (ll. 399–403).** It separates a clear pen from a trapped body.
- Anyone not needed to get the body out or to cover the source leaves at once.
- The fewest stay, and the watch names them aloud.
- They stay only until the person is out and the source is covered.
- Nobody re-enters until the counter is under the red.
- Every stayer, time, reading and reason goes in the book.

**Why it is workable and ethical.** Every step is observable: named aloud, timed, and closed by a counter threshold. It gives nobody discretion to stay for courage.

**It is applied consistently:**
- Dee applies it against herself (l. 407). By the rule, Rook and, once his cloths were thrown, Sowerby should have left.
- The recall itself falls inside the rule, because it began after the counter read 90 (ch. 20 l. 283).
- Ch. 20 l. 659 ("Tonight I'm writing the other thing") plants the rule's origin. Dee's "Dessa copied it in under the lines yesterday" matches the D+37 ledger and the D+38 review.

**The breach is not praised.** The Director says, "Nobody is being praised on my paper for staying in that pen… It bends for the fewest people, for the shortest time" (l. 411).

**Not a defect:**
- Under the rule's letter, the coat removal (ch. 20 l. 259, done while still red) would have fallen to Jab or Callie rather than Sowerby. That is the point of a stricter rule, not a contradiction.
- Dee does not name herself among those needed. See owner item 5.
