## 2. Whole-book reader response (unscored)

Meridian works as one book, not six movements. It keeps the promise its first line makes: *Callie had said it, so Callie counted it.* Counting is the book's spine. Callie counts to hold on to her mother and learns to stop counting her. Toren counts people and learns that the way home is the number his father never priced. Jab counts his brother's breaths and learns that the thing he is for has a floor. The three roads run beside each other for three movements and braid in the last three. The braid is made from things the reader has watched them practise: the handoff words, the struts set from the ground, the hook's still curve, the hook-tip light. It is not made from anyone saying they are a team.

The strongest material is where cost is visible. The pen with the dead box is one. So are the wash, the room under the hill, the pump's flap turned into a way to fight, and Tilda opening Callie's fingers off her wrist one at a time. The patient scenes never cheat. Nobody is cured, and every ward visit moves something. The Caul Hill fight is the biggest and cleverest action in the book. The return hold is a smaller echo that pays off *Everybody back* as a working call rather than as a slogan.

A reader will feel the length. Across 317,000 words, three things add weight, especially for a listener: the procedural ledger, the recurring surface cadences ("the way…", "did not say anything", "for a long time") and the very short sections. The ash arithmetic in Movements Three and Six is correct and satisfying on the page, but dense by ear. None of this breaks the book. It is the texture of the accepted voice.

## 5. Audit questions — findings

1. **Causal progression: yes.**
   - Intake and diagnoses create the clocks (M1).
   - The machine's fragility plus the Sag failure make the mission necessary and make Jab visible (M2).
   - The refusal, then the pen's proof of cost, produce the ledger and the narrow yes (M3).
   - The road consumes the margin and cuts the way home (M4).
   - Caul Hill (M5) leads to the return, then to proof, then to the accord (M6).
2. **No movement duplicates another's job, and nothing promised is absent.**
   - M6's hold deliberately inverts M2's failure: "count the holder" becomes "everybody back".
   - M3 is the longest preparation movement, and the pen emergency keeps it moving.
   - Every map beat is present or carried by a recorded ruling.
3. **The Caul Hill climax remains the largest action: yes.**
   - Across chs 34–39 it is the only named creature and the only line crossed on purpose, and it is where all three capabilities land in sequence.
   - The return hold is shorter (chs 44–45), has no named creature and no Yield, and serves as public proof.
4. **Distinct leads: yes.**
   - Callie reads, holds and learns to hold the edge rather than the person.
   - Jab feels and draws, and learns to stop.
   - Toren counts, sets and asks, and learns that leading means pricing the way home.
   - Voices stay distinct: Callie's weights, Jab's river and valley, Toren's sums with ifs.
5. **All three are necessary: yes.**
   - At every decisive exchange (the rack, the pen, the wash, the door, the point, the road line) removing any one lead fails the plan on the page.
   - Callie's absence was priced at "two people" (ch 23).
6. **Relationships change by evidence, not declaration.**
   - The evidence: the steps (the eyes on the belly, then the hook), the handoff words, "Don't check", "I was waiting for you not to need me to" (ch 27), and the same-watch request (ch 48).
   - "It's what we are" follows those acts; it does not replace them.
7. **The rules are taught before decisive use and kept consistent**, with the one-configuration nuance in 4b.
   - Ward, Sustain, Guard and Edge.
   - The ash cycle: intake, settle, spend, vent.
   - Floors; active burden versus fixed damage.
8. **No capability quietly inflates.**
   - Jab's sensing is bounded: one mile, one datum; the D+55 field is corroborated, fallible and costly.
   - Callie's shared Ward is priced every time, with nosebleeds and her floor.
   - Toren's Guard is paid by the breath, and Edge only comes after release.
9. **Costs carry forward.**
   - Palm mark, ribs cracked, Pell's ankle, Rook's infection, the technician's hand, Callie's floor.
   - Reserves are measured at every gate and halt.
   - No body is reset between movements.
10. **The three patients** progress in observable scenes and end honestly, without cure or pause (see §3).
11. **The supporting cast keeps agency without taking the book.**
    - Dessa commands; Katori kills; Wyck teaches heels; Otto becomes "the one who doesn't move".
    - Pell supplies the wheel and the pump; the leads execute, per ruling.
    - The day holder earns "Next time, shout". Rook and Dee's argument is not won, only changed.
12. **The Director stays human.**
    - She says "I want… I'll take"; her pencil stops at "deepest lost the most"; "Then you should *get* one"; the technician cried.
    - The accord follows the demonstrated result and owned paper, and is explicitly partial.
13. **Milo, the Home Line, the lanes and ledger, and the school seed** are all paid in proportion to their setup.
14. **Continuity:** three copy-level contradictions (4a). No clock, ledger, injury or knowledge contradiction was found.
15. **The ending** closes the mission (the machine is honest) and the emotional promises (not counting, supper first, *Everybody back*). It leaves only the intentional horizons: the hill, the Sag surges, the brass, the disk, Katori's words, and the forty households.
16. **No unresolved setup reads as an accidental omission at the level of story.** Small dangling items (Dessa's unseen line, Wyck's eight paces, Toren's arm for Sowerby) read as ongoing life and are listed as optional.
