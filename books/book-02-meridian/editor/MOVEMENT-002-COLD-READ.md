## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** The day holder's saucer-sized hollow in the gravel ("You did not wear a hollow like that into gravel in a week", ch9:39) hooked me before any explanation arrived. From then on I watched her in every scene, which is exactly what the movement's ending wants. The test-weights conversation in the shed turns a failing machine into something I could feel: a family of boxes all bending to agree with the one that lies. Dee Wren's arrival ("Elias," ch9:439) is a strong turn. The strut that "stood up" because it was late (ch12, fall sequence, and "It held because it was late," ch12:359) is the best power-discovery beat in the movement. The Sag lift (ch14:445 onward into ch15) is where I stopped reading critically.

**Where it slipped.** In the middle of Chapter 10 the book turns into a series of committees. There is Callie's review, then the Director's long room, where terms are negotiated clause by clause. Chapter 11 opens by describing the same review room again for Jab. Chapter 13 is a long run of drills (jam, swap, handoff counts, settling, the Katori–Dee argument, walking in the Ward, widening it over Wyck, then over Toren, then the weighbridge, Sowerby and the scan). Each beat is good, but by the time the scan arrived I was tired of drills. Across the movement I also started to hear the prose's tics before I heard the scene. The tics are listed under Priority 2.

**Which person matters.** Jab matters most. His arc (the floor, "Did not say yes," going below the line, waking under Tonk's bar) is the spine of the movement and it lands. Close behind: the day holder, a near-silent character the book makes me care about by never letting anyone watch her. Then Senna, and Callie's mother, whose "Strike that" (ch10:223) is the most satisfying line of adult agency in the movement. Tonk supplies nearly all the joy.

**What I expect next.** An expedition to Caul Hill for reference boxes. The pressure: two yellow lights and "four weeks was a week ago." The means: ash lying in drifts there, jars, and someone to hold the others up. It will be Toren, Jab and Callie, carrying Senna's "write the ifs down first," and it will be opposed by Dee's rule about floors. I also expect a quieter story about whose sister Katori is, and about Dee recognising her face (ch13, the shed doorway).

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The ending (ch16:477, "Waiting, Callie thought, for somebody to count them") ties the machine, the Sag and the hill together, and ties them to the motif of counting.

## 3. Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge, disclosures

With no canon supplied, these are **apparent** issues found inside the movement.

### Knowledge boundary — the most important one
- **ch14:625** says Toren "had seen a grey shape at the south lip leave its hollow … and go down, alone, into the white … Nobody else had been looking at her." But **ch15:285** has Toren report "Wyck asked where the holder was. Nobody had her on the count." **ch16:323** (Dessa's book) says "NOBODY SAW." And in **ch16**, Dee credits Wyck's question alone for sending her down. On top of that, Toren was in chest-deep fog at the bottom of the east track when the fold happened, so it is doubtful he could physically see the south lip. Katori set up his role in ch14 ("you'll be the one who knew where everybody was"). As written, that setup is either quietly contradicted or quietly unpaid. Either Toren didn't see her, or he saw her and told no one, and the movement never mentions the second possibility.

### Day count and timing (apparent drift; check against the continuity ledger)
- **Yard start and the scan day:** the Director says on day 14 that the yard starts "the day after tomorrow". Ch11 puts the first yard morning on day 16. Ch12 then runs a "third morning" (ch12:3, which would be day 18) plus a following sack day, and ch13 then needs a jam day, a shed day, *and* a following morning before the scan that Callie calls "The twenty-first day, today" (ch13:659). The sequence fits only if some of those days are compressed or merged.
- **ch13:495:** "Thirty-eight, alone … Yesterday." The 38-second measurement happens in ch12 at least two days earlier, unless she was re-measured off the page.
- **ch14:543:** the fence rail "five days ago", against ch16's "coming out for six days, since the rain."
- **ch15:165:** "every night for eleven nights" since the shed session (day 15), against a lift on day 25, which is ten nights.
- **ch10:453:** "the sixteenth day, the day after tomorrow's tomorrow". Spoken on day 14, the sixteenth is the day after tomorrow. If this is meant as childlike phrasing, it reads as an error.
- **ch10:483:** "I've known them twelve days." This is plausible only if they met on day 2. It can't be verified here.

### Hardware handling
- **ch14:297:** Toren "had not taken [the stone] out since the glass". Yet ch12 (on the steps) and ch13 (at the jam) show him forming the spike, and ch12 describes it going "back into the stone." If the spike can be called while the stone stays in his pocket, one line saying so would settle it. Otherwise the statement contradicts those scenes. Low to medium confidence, because the rule may be set in earlier books.

### Power limits
- **Callie's Ward in the hook** lasts to 58 seconds at the pipe (ch16; Dessa's book). Her body Ward measured 38 seconds standing (ch12). The movement never quite states that a Ward in steel costs less than one worn on the body, though the jam scene implies it. Readers who track numbers, and this book trains them to, will notice. One clause of explanation would do.
- **Rook holds the whole bottom alone for 31 minutes** after the Director had said he can do "an hour" sharing the line. This is plausible and the cost is paid ("R.: flat"). No problem found.
- **Dee's settled-jar Sustain** (ch15 "an empty room"; ch16 measuring her floor before and after) is internally consistent and well demonstrated.

### Knowledge and backstory tension
- **ch15:499** has Rook say that Dee at fourteen "didn't know how, and she did it anyway," and "came back off the river with the word." **ch16:125** has Dee say she has settled ash "ten thousand times since I was eleven" under Orrin Vell. Both can be true: she could settle ash but didn't know Sustain. The text doesn't say so, and a reader may read the two lines as a contradiction about what Dee knew at fourteen.

## 4. Repair brief (three priorities)

### Priority 1 — Settle who saw the holder go
- **Location:** ch14:625; ch15:285; ch16, Dee's account and Dessa's ledger (ch16:323, ch16:339).
- **Observed issue:** Toren's retrospective "He had seen her go" contradicts "NOBODY SAW" and Wyck's role as the one who noticed. It also leaves the setup of Toren as runner unresolved.
- **Effect on the reader:** it blurs the movement's thesis ("the ones who hold the line are the ones nobody watches," "COUNT THE HOLDER") at the moment it should land hardest. An attentive reader will ask why Toren said nothing.
- **Proposed scope:** a small, targeted change, one of two ways:
  - (a) Revise ch14:625 so Toren *understands* after the fact. He heard "*weight*" and now realises what it meant. He did not see her.
  - (b) Keep the sighting and give Toren a short beat in ch16 owning it ("I saw her go. I was the runner. I didn't say."). A matching entry in Dessa's ledger would turn the inconsistency into the movement's most honest failure.
- **Strength to preserve:** the full ch14–15 action geometry, Katori's two-second kill, Jab's "Mine," and Toren's light held through a fall. Don't shorten the fight.

### Priority 2 — Lighten the epithets and tics for clarity and read-aloud
- **Location:**
  - "the woman with the good boots": 31 uses across ch10–16, including in Callie's POV after the Director has called her "Miss Wren" in ch10.
  - "the weighbridge girl": never named, despite joining the yard and being injured in ch14.
  - "face did nothing at all, which was how she knew": ch10:373, ch13:665, ch16:445.
  - "like water off a tipped plate": ch13:411, ch13:509, and ch15.
  - Dee's "about four seconds without touching" look: ch9, ch11, ch12, ch15.
- **Observed issue:** too many adults are tracked by description, and the tags repeat until they sound mechanical.
- **Effect on the reader:** a younger reader loses track of referents in multi-woman scenes (ch10 office, ch15 rescue). Read aloud, the repeated tags pull attention away from the scene.
- **Proposed scope:** line-level only.
  - Let Callie move to "Miss Wren" or "Dee" once she has heard the name, keeping "the good boots" as an occasional callback.
  - Give the weighbridge girl a name. She is frank enough to volunteer it on the plate in ch10.
  - Cut about half of each repeated tic, and keep the first use of each.
  - Add a name at the start of the paragraph wherever a scene opens on "she" (ch15, "She came out of the fog…").
- **Strength to preserve:** the motifs that do real work: "fell like a sack," "something left," "write why," "light where you don't want them," Dee's four-second look in her first appearance and at the holder. Punctuation fixes are welcome, but keep the sentence rhythm of the action passages.

### Priority 3 — Run a day ledger and ease the middle stretch of drills
- **Location:** day references at ch10:453, ch12:3, ch13:495, ch13:659, ch14:543, ch15:165. Density in ch13 overall, and the repeated review-room setup at the start of ch11.
- **Observed issue:** the day count doesn't quite close between the yard start and the day-21 scan. Ch13 stacks roughly ten training and domestic beats. Ch12–13 breakthroughs repeatedly follow the same pattern of aphorism then success.
- **Effect on the reader:** readers who track numbers, and the book invites them to count, hit friction. Mid-movement momentum sags just before the lift, which is the part that most needs pressure building.
- **Proposed scope:**
  - Fix the numbers against the continuity file.
  - In ch13, consider moving one beat (for example the Ward-over-Toren test, or part of the settling scene) so it lands closer to the lift, or merging the handoff counts with the Katori line.
  - Compress the review-room recap in ch11 to a sentence, since Callie has just described the room.
  - Let at least one drill breakthrough come without a handed-over aphorism, or let one fail openly, as Jab's floor does.
- **Strength to preserve:** the jam sequence with its two keys and three answers (ch13:125 onward), the counted handoff gap, Callie's mother at the weighbridge ("There's water in that"), the "Thistle" / "test weights" gain-and-loss day, and the day-21 scan scene.
