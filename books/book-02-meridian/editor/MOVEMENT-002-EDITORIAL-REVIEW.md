## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where my interest caught:**
- **Ch. 9:** Toren walks to the shed about "the fourth box". The test-weights explanation turns a machine fault into a moral image the whole movement then uses. The single word "Elias" (ch. 9, l. 439) is the movement's first real jolt.
- **Ch. 12:** the late left strut comes up out of the ground (l. 151 onward). The steps scene then ends on "Look at the hook" (l. 481). That is the best teaching scene in the movement: two boys fix each other's faults through what their dead parents said.
- **Ch. 13:** the jammed culvert. Three answers to one problem, then swapped problems, make the same-obstacle premise vivid.
- **Ch. 14–15:** from the warm iron and two shadows to "Mine" (ch. 15, l. 251). The action is legible because Dessa's map was built first, and it adapts: husks go to the light, hounds go to the dark.

**Where it slipped:**
- **Ch. 10:** the household review and the Director's office are a long run of terms-and-leverage dialogue. Both are well written, but they come one after the other in similar rooms.
- **Ch. 11 opening:** this is a second review in the same room, with the same three people, described again. It repeats ch. 10 before the shed pays it off.
- **Ch. 13, back half:** stages 3–4, Katori against Dee, the sack walk, widening the Ward, Tilda at the weighbridge, Sowerby and the D+21 cycle all run on without much release.
- **Ch. 14:** the adults argue for an hour and three quarters. It is useful, and it reads as useful rather than urgent.

**Who matters most:** Jab, then Tilda, then Tonk. The movement's emotional peak is Jab's nine minutes on the slab and his waking to Tonk sitting up without him.

**What I expect next:**
- Someone says Caul Hill out loud as a plan.
- Jab's "bucket" either returns or doesn't, and "Don't check" pays off.
- The Director asks again.
- The D+32 cycle runs on two yellow lights.
- Katori's sister thread surfaces through Dee.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 3. Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge boundaries and reserved disclosures

Status key:
- **Internal** means the manuscript contradicts itself.
- **Plan** means the only evidence is the book map, the packet or a continuity note.
- **Apparent** means canon would be needed to confirm it, and canon was not supplied.

### 3a. Logic seams in the Sag lift (the most consequential)

1. **Rook's handover at minute 20 never happens. (Internal; high confidence.)**
   - Pell's plan says "*M. takes the line at the twentieth minute*" (ch. 14, l. 227).
   - "For eighteen minutes it went well" (l. 365), and Rook is "waiting for the twentieth minute".
   - The ledger puts the fold at "Minute twenty-one" (ch. 16, l. 319).
   - Yet at the fold the day holder is still holding, and Rook only takes the bottom after she collapses (ch. 15, l. 113).
   - Nothing on the page says the handover was held back. A reader who has just studied Dessa's map will notice.

2. **Toren saw the holder leave her hollow, but nothing follows from it. (Internal; medium-high confidence.)**
   - Ch. 14, l. 625: "He had seen a grey shape at the south lip leave its hollow… Nobody else had been looking at her."
   - Katori had just told him the runner is "the one who knew where everybody was".
   - Ch. 16 then says "NOBODY SAW" and "nobody had the holder on the count", and credits only Wyck and Jab. Toren's seeing is never acknowledged, confessed or written down.
   - Either the line contradicts the ledger or it is an unpaid guilt beat. As written it reads as the first.

3. **Toren knows something he couldn't. (Internal; medium confidence.)**
   - Ch. 15, l. 285: Toren tells Katori, "Dessa sent her. Wyck asked where the holder was."
   - Toren was in the west door and then crossing the bottom in fog. The page gives him no way to know what was said at the south lip.

4. **Two hundred paces "is two minutes at a run". (Physical plausibility; low-medium confidence.)**
   - Ch. 14, l. 197, and repeated in ch. 16 ("two minutes late").
   - Unless the figure includes the kit and the stretcher crew, 200 paces is well under a minute at a run.
   - It matters because the Director's rule is argued on exactly this number.

### 3b. Arithmetic and day counts

These characters count everything, so small slips cost more trust than usual.

- **Ch. 13, l. 495:** "Thirty-eight, alone… Yesterday." The 38 seconds was D+17; the widening is D+20.
- **Ch. 13, l. 535:** "Her mother had been the tally for three days." She started on or after D+14, and this is D+20, so it should be about six or seven days.
- **Ch. 16, l. 433:** Jab, on D+28, says "yesterday she did a quarter of a Handful into Rook's hands". The demonstration was D+26.
- **Ch. 14, l. 171:** Katori says "ten days of the yard". The yard opened D+15 and this is D+22, so about seven or eight.
- **Ch. 12, l. 495:** "in seventeen days". Toren has known Jab since D-0 night, so it is D+18.
- **Low confidence:** ch. 10, l. 3, "every morning for ten days" (Callie arrived D-1, so about thirteen) may simply mean since she started lifting.

### 3c. Bodies and injuries

- **When Toren's arm broke. (Apparent; medium confidence.)**
  - The Director says, "You killed a Breach… with your arm broken, …and two days later you did the same thing with your legs" (ch. 10, l. 471).
  - Ch. 9, l. 9 dates the break to about eleven days before D+11, which is D-0.
  - Ch. 12, l. 153 places the breaking noise "on the slabs at the bottom of the Sag on the night of the north road".
  - The Movement One continuity note says "landed on it D-0".
  - Unless Book One broke the arm at the Breach and re-broke it on D-0, the Director has the order wrong. The legs clause is unclear either way.
- **Wyck's injured side.** Still unresolved. The drafting author already flagged it: the manuscript follows Movement One (right side, two numb fingers), and Book One reportedly says left wrist and three fingers. This needs an owner ruling. Nothing in ch. 9–16 makes it worse.
- **Tonk "flat… for ten weeks" (ch. 15, l. 375). (Plan only; low confidence.)**
  - BOOK_MAP §4 speaks of a "six-week pattern".
  - The manuscript's own clock ("Nobody had made it at Jab in two months", ch. 11) roughly supports ten weeks.
  - This is a plan discrepancy, not a manuscript error. The map may be the one that's wrong.
- **Otherwise, bodies carry correctly and none reset between scenes:**
  - Toren's splint and his palm reopening
  - Callie's ribs and nosebleeds
  - Wyck re-strapped for six weeks
  - Tilda's right leg failing twice, "thistle" found and "test weights" lost
  - Senna's hands and feet
  - Rook's tremor

### 3d. Power limits

### Departures and conflicts for the owner

These are not automatic repairs.

1. **Sentence and scene rhythm.**
   - The sentence proxy runs short (about 10.5 against 14.6).
   - Typographic scene breaks come about three times as often as the target's ASR pauses. Because typography and pause segmentation differ, the break comparison is weak.
   - A real per-sentence distribution is needed before anyone rewrites for rhythm. To the ear, the prose already alternates short hits with long "and… and" chains, which is the formula's intent.
2. **Progression-vocabulary density.**
   - This book expresses its system through concrete working metaphors (the well, the bucket, the floor, turns of the glass, a Handful) more than through labelled terms.
   - By the core lexicon, the teaching movement runs far below the implied first-third density (about 83 per 10k if the source's third-by-third ratios are applied).
   - With the metaphor terms added, it comes near the book-wide mean of 58.
   - Whether the owner wants more explicit system labels here is a genuine conflict between the voice and the formula's front-loading target. I recommend deciding before anyone adds labels, because stat-block language would fight this narration.
3. **Dialogue tags run well above the source's rate.** The formula treats this as secondary and the packet's read-aloud rule favors it. I recommend no action.
4. **Viewpoint share** is settled by the packet's override. The one remaining point is the Toren-lean wording, which Movement Three's planned Jab lean will rebalance.

## 5. Repair brief (three priorities at most)

### Priority 1: close the three logic seams in the Sag lift, and pay off Toren's sighting

- **Location:** ch. 14, ll. 227 / 365 / 425–455 and l. 625; ch. 15, l. 285; ch. 16, the ledger around l. 319.
- **Observed issue:**
  - The plan's minute-20 handover to Rook silently doesn't happen.
  - Toren "had seen" the holder leave her hollow, yet the book records that nobody saw her and never comes back to it.
  - Toren reports what Wyck said at the south lip, which he had no way to hear.
- **Effect on the reader:** the movement taught readers to trust Dessa's map, so they check the fight against it. Each unexplained seam weakens the best action sequence of the book so far. The sighting is also the strongest unused beat: the runner who "knew where everybody was" didn't count the holder, which is exactly what "COUNT THE HOLDER" is about.
- **Proposed scope:** a few sentences.
  - One line around minute 18–19, when Pell's single "listen" blast goes, saying the handover is held; for example, Rook keeping his hands down on Pell's signal.
  - Change Toren's knowledge at ch. 15, l. 285 to something he could know ("Dessa's sending her"), or give him Wyck's shout carried across the fog while he is crossing.
  - For the sighting, either cut "He had seen her go" or keep it and let it cost him. One line in ch. 16 would do: Toren's hand in the ledger or on the steps, "T. saw her leave the hollow. Didn't count her." That fits Senna's dictated "why" at the bottom of that page.
- **Strength to preserve:** the full developed fight — its length, the map-first layout, the husks and light against the hounds and dark, and Callie's 58 seconds at the pipe. The owner asked for this action, so **do not shorten it**.

### Priority 2: fix the counting slips, and settle the uncertain names and bodies

- **Location:** ch. 13, l. 495 ("Yesterday"); ch. 13, l. 535 ("three days"); ch. 16, l. 433 ("yesterday"); ch. 14, l. 171 ("ten days"); ch. 12, l. 495 ("seventeen days"); ch. 10, l. 471 (the Director's account of the arm and legs); ch. 16, l. 125 ("Orrin Vell", "since I was eleven"); Wyck's side, which is carried over from before this movement.
- **Observed issue:** day counts that don't match the movement's own day map; a Director line that seems to misdate Toren's break; a newly given full name; and an age that may conflict with the book's Kindling age.
- **Effect on the reader:** this is a book about people who count. Callie, Jab, Pell and Dessa's ledger make precision the characters' virtue, so an attentive young reader who catches "yesterday" for two days ago loses a little trust. The unclear Director line is a stumble when read aloud.
- **Proposed scope:** line edits only, plus checks against `NAME_REGISTRY.md` and `CANON.md` (Vell; the Kindling age; which injury came from the Breach and which from D-0) and the owner's ruling on Wyck's side.
- **Strength to preserve:** counting as a character trait. Keep the numbers; only make them agree.

### Priority 3: read-aloud texture — the epithet and the repeated simile frames

- **Location:**
  - "The woman with the good boots" appears in 41 lines, 13 of them in ch. 13 alone. That continues well after Dee is named to all three viewpoints: "Dee Wren" at the reunion in ch. 9, "Miss Wren" from the Director in ch. 10, and Jab's "Don't tell Dee" in ch. 12.
  - Ch. 15 uses the epithet in Jab's narration even though Jab thinks "Dee" elsewhere in the same chapter.
  - Repeated frames: "like water off a tipped plate" (4), "about four seconds" (7), "his/her face did nothing at all, which was how X knew", and "the way you…" (106 lines).
- **Observed issue:** a six-word epithet on nearly every attribution slows dialogue and reads as authorial distance rather than as a viewpoint's choice. A few signature similes return often enough to be heard.
- **Effect on the reader:** a thirteen-year-old may briefly wonder whether "the woman with the good boots" is someone new. An adult hears it as a tic. When read aloud, it lengthens the most technical teaching scenes.
- **Proposed scope:** after the Director's office scene, name her the way each viewpoint would: "Dee" for Jab and Toren, "Miss Wren" or "Dee Wren" for Callie. If Callie's epithet is deliberate, keep it for Callie and give it one line of motive. Cut each repeated simile frame by about half, keeping the first and best use of each.
- **Strength to preserve:** the plain, cadenced narration, the short follow-up sentences, and the clean "said" attribution the packet asks for. This is a thinning pass, not a restyle.

### Noted but not prioritised

- The movement is about 14% over its packet band.
- If cuts are ever wanted, the lowest-cost places are the repeated room-and-people description at the start of ch. 11's review, and some of ch. 14's argument. Keep Pell's list of seven ifs and "Signed. Not agreed. All four."
- Do not buy length back from ch. 12's teaching, the jam, or the lift.

## 6. Uncertainty and limits

- **Same model as the author.** This reviewer is the drafting model in a fresh context, so its blind spots and preferences may match the author's. A human or cross-model reader is still needed.
- **Canon not verified.** Section 3 flags apparent conflicts only.
- **Many metrics are unmeasured.** The sentence distribution, Flesch scores, paragraph median and non-"said" reporting verbs were not counted. The sentence mean is a proxy with a known upward bias in sentence count, which pulls the mean down.
- **The development-beat count is by hand** and depends on judgment.
- **Scores reflect one read of one movement.** They show nothing about any author or seat across a series, and they are not a sales or audience forecast.
