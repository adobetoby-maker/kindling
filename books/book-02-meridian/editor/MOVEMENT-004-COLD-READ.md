# Movement IV Cold Read — Meridian, Chapters 25–32

## What kind of read this is

This is a **simulated cold read by the same model in a fresh context**. It is not a human reader, and it is not an independent model.

- **Same model family as the drafter.** The reader here is the same model that drafted the movement. Its taste, blind spots and habits overlap with the author's. It is likely to under-notice tics it would produce itself.
- **Mostly, but not fully, blind.** No drafting conversation was carried into this session. However, the session harness injected a short automatic memory digest at startup. It contained one-line log entries from the drafting session, such as chapter titles and notes like "ash arithmetic confirmed" and "Duchess treatment scene added". So this reader knew some chapter titles and that continuity passes had happened before reading. It had seen no drafting prose, plan, bible or continuity checkpoint.
- **What was read.** Only `editor/MOVEMENT-004-COLD-READ.prompt.md`: the review brief plus the eight supplied chapters, read in full and in order (ch 25 → 32, about 53,750 words). Phrase frequencies were then counted with `grep` on `manuscript/chapter-25.md` … `chapter-32.md`.
- **What was not read.** No series bible, plan, earlier chapters or `MOVEMENT-004-CONTINUITY.md`. No canon evidence was supplied, so everything under "Canon / state checks" is an **apparent** inconsistency inside this movement, not a verified canon violation.
- **No files changed.** The manuscript and every other file are untouched. This report is the only file written.

Coverage: all 8 declared chapters, complete. The movement ends on a deliberate open question (ch 32), not a truncation.

## Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**
- Ch 25, the tailboard (Pell drops it so Jab never has to take the frame off). It is small, physical and kind, and it made me trust these people before any ash mechanics arrived.
- Callie's Ward tearing "two steps", then Rook's "It wants ground." A rule stated as a joke about an old woman on a wet floor.
- Ch 26, Toren's dark lane: a tactic borrowed from Katori's lamps, then tested by real hounds, with Rook lying awake and deliberately *not* getting up.
- Ch 27 is the strongest chapter in the movement. The copper-wire man and his daughter with the iron bar, Dee's "I'm the one who says what it costs", Toren's "It's yours", and Jab's *no* to the hands.
- Ch 28's wheel lesson ("A lot of still, one after another") is a genuine idea-turn that the reader can see before Callie finishes it.
- From the moment Rook steps into the drift in ch 29, I was reading for plot and stopped noticing prose.

**Where it slipped.**
- **Ch 28 middle** (about the second through sixth halts): the lee is tuned across many halts, each ending in a price. The discoveries are good, but the rhythm of "try / tear / how much? / settled, X" repeats about six times.
- **Ch 29 opening**: the rules recital, then the long plan read-back at the fire (about lines 2315–2361 of the compiled prompt). This is the second complete ledger recital in two chapters.
- **Ch 32 first third**: the cut rotation, three patients in sequence, is quiet and earned after ch 30–31. It still delays the ten-and-three discovery by about 1,500 words.

**Which person matters.**
- **Jab** carries the movement's moral spine: he chooses in the ditch, calls "open it" in the drift, and makes the ten-and-three discovery.
- **Callie** carries its emotional arc: "It did not feel like trust. It felt like forgetting."
- **Toren** is the load-bearing structure, literally.
- The quiet surprise is **Dee**. Her ch 28 fireside speech about Rook ("It's the best thing about him and it's going to kill him") reframes every earlier order she gives him.

**What I expect next.**
- A decision at first light about going *under* the wrong sky, which the Director forbade.
- Rook's wound turning hot overnight ("Tomorrow we'll know").
- The fifty-fourth-day cycle pressure on Senna now being unreachable.
- Probably someone going to the edge of the fence to test whether ash thrown or spilled is taken.

**Would I continue?** Yes, immediately. Ch 32's last line gives the book a new antagonist-shaped question without naming it.

One small attribution blur: in ch 32 Callie assigns jar 1 to "Pell and Toren and me" and jar 2 to "Rook…". But jar 1 "ran low" partway through Rook's draw. This is harmless.

## Repair brief (three priorities at most)

### 1. Make the ledger say the right thing once, and stop unit collisions

- **Location:** ch 28, the first plant-lift try ("Settled, five eighths") and the later settled figures that day. Also ch 26 / ch 29 / ch 30 "turn" usages.
- **Observed issue:** Callie's stone figure is off by a quarter, and the day's spend and re-settle may not balance. "Turn" means both a glass-minute and a reserve measure.
- **Effect on reader:** The adult who is tracking the numbers (and the book trains them to) catches the 5/8 and briefly distrusts the ledger, which is the book's main suspense instrument. The 13-year-old loses the thread when Rook's "half a turn" becomes "twelve turns… two over".
- **Proposed scope:**
  - Correct the ch 28 figures, which is a handful of numerals.
  - Optionally have Miss Wren say one closing total at the sixth halt.
  - Add a single clarifying clause where Rook's limit is first given in minutes (ch 29, "Half a turn. Ten minutes"). For example, tie it explicitly to *his reserve*, or rename the reserve unit where it appears in narration.
  - Do not reduce the out-loud counting ritual.
- **Preserve:** Callie's flat ledger voice ("Road, seven and three quarters") and Miss Wren's "That's what cheaper is." The ritual is characterisation, not bookkeeping.

### 2. Fix the two picture-breaks in the climax; do not shorten it

- **Location:** ch 30, Jab's knife sentence ("He let the knife come on his other fist. He could not…"). Ch 31, walking the cart onto the post ("a pace in front of the front of the cart" vs "A hand's width west. Another."). Optionally ch 31, Dee's "every turn" vs "Four turns".
- **Observed issue:** A sequence that contradicts itself (ch 30), and a distance/direction mismatch in the single most technical move of the rescue (ch 31).
- **Effect on reader:** The reader stops to re-picture the scene at the one moment the movement asks them to see precisely. A read-aloud listener hears a knife appear, then not appear.
- **Proposed scope:** Sentence-level only. Reorder the knife beats: he reaches, realises the left is on the jar, lets go, then the knife comes. Make the post distance and direction agree, either by moving the post to "a hand or two off the front corner" or by letting the strut push through a stated arc. Keep every beat of the fight and the rescue at its current length, per the owner's request for developed action.
- **Preserve:** The escalating counter numbers, the "Ground / Set / Rolling / Step / Lane / Busy / Clean" call-and-response, Callie taking Toren *out of her eyes* on purpose, and the husks killed against the outside of the lee.

### 3. Thin the recurring tags so the key lines keep their weight

- **Location:** Movement-wide, heaviest in ch 28 (lee-tuning halts 2–6), ch 29 (fire plan read-back) and ch 32 (opening rotation; "the way…" 34 times in ch 32).
- **Observed issue:** Several phrases recur often:
  - "Nobody said anything" (16 times)
  - "the way…" similes (194 times)
  - "Six. And a horse." (8 times)
  - "the low careful laugh"
  - "went down to look" (8 times)

  Separately, the price-after-each-try rhythm in ch 28 repeats about six times.
- **Effect on reader:** The adult begins to hear the machinery. More importantly, the ch 31 payoff ("Six. And a horse. Over there.") and Rook's final laugh land slightly softer because both have been heard many times.
- **Proposed scope:** A light line pass:
  - Cut or vary about a third of "Nobody said anything" and the weaker "the way" similes.
  - Hold "Six. And a horse." back from one or two ordinary nights so the ch 31 variant and the ch 32 pump-house count ring.
  - In ch 28, merge one or two tuning halts into summary. Keep the discoveries (footing, speed, one side, per-body cost, watch who's inside) and Miss Wren's book entry verbatim.
- **Preserve:** The count as Toren's character, Rook's laugh as a callback (just rarer), and the whole ch 28 fireside (Callie's "It felt like forgetting" and Dee's "I've got him"). These are the movement's emotional high points.

## Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- Scores are editorial judgements from one simulated read, not measurements. The same-model reader probably **over-credits** the progression logic, because it thinks the way the drafter thinks. It probably **under-flags** stylistic tics it shares ("the way…", stacked *and*s). A human or different-model read should weight priority 3 more heavily than this one does.
- Some stumbles (Dee/Miss Wren, Elias, "the twentieth", Vell, Keel, Wyck, Katori) may be fully established in chapters 1–24 or in book one. They are listed because a movement-only reader meets them without a bridge, not because they are errors.
- No comparison run or seat labels were involved, so there are no cross-run claims.
