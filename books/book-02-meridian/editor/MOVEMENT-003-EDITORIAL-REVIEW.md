- **What the reviewer had and did not have:**
  - It had no memory of the drafting session.
  - It did read the author's own continuity checkpoint (`provenance/MOVEMENT-003-CONTINUITY.md`), the packet, the book map, and the canon excerpts supplied in the compiled prompt. So this is **not a blind cold read**. The formula section below is explicitly an informed pass.

## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**

- The first page caught at once. Jab sits in the dark with a tremor he has watched in Rook and never understood. He goes down to "a place where a step should have been". Otto has kept the stove up for hours and can't hand him the cup, so he sets it between Jab's boots.
- Several early beats land on character rather than on the system:
  - Mrs Lask's list: *Not fit. Not asked. Not to be sent for.*
  - Rook admitting he walked past Vell's table with his nose in the air.
  - Dee's "Then sit at mine."
- The Director putting a finger down for each question is the clearest statement of stakes in the book so far. The reader knows exactly what the next four chapters have to produce.
- Other beats that caught:
  - Jab's hook-tip light coming back unasked, and Toren's "Don't check."
  - The planted screws: "There were eleven. I've had seven off it." A genre reader will predict the failure from that line. That is good suspense, not a spoiler.
  - The pen emergency is the movement's best sequence. "Tell the boy the ink'll come off now" is its best payoff line.
- Chapters 22–24 are the emotional peak:
  - Jab keeping his hands behind his back.
  - Tilda opening Callie's fingers off her wrist one at a time.
  - Tonk untying the knot himself and saying "I'm in the fifth bed. On the *right*."

**Where it slipped.**

- Ch. 18 opens by re-describing the paper the reader saw written in ch. 17.
- Mid ch. 19, the long-table exposition (Miss Wren's six-step speech) runs a little long before Callie's lock turns it into drama.
- Ch. 21 builds the ledger over about 3,500 words. Then it has every item recited again, line by line, in the Director's room (ll. 353–375). The second pass is the only real sag in the movement.
- Two small spatial stumbles pulled me out: the chalk ring in the pen (see §3a), and Dessa being "somehow" already at the lip in ch. 24.

**Which person matters.** Jab, most, because his arc turns inward: he learns to call his own floor, and to lose the ability to be needed. Tonk is the most vivid figure in the movement. Callie and Tilda carry the strongest single chapter (23).

**What I expect next.**

- The road north: something at a culvert, and Duchess stopping with her ears back.
- The door at the bottom of the yellow stair, which has to be *pulled*.
- The first real test of "out without them": Rook unable to do his share, or Jab near his floor inside the fence, and Dee having to turn them round.
- The rip the Director forbade them to enter coming to them anyway.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 3. Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge boundaries and reserved disclosures

### 3a. Physical staging seams (the most consequential)

1. **The chalk ring in the pen (ch. 20).**
   - **The contradiction:**
     - The technician draws the ring "round the stool, six feet out, all the way round" (l. ~23).
     - Dee kneels "just outside the chalk" and holds her forearm "a foot from the box" (l. 81). A forearm can't reach five feet.
     - Jab "knelt at the edge of it, and reached in and got the grey-haired man by the left arm" (l. 209). The man is on his knees at the stool, about six feet away.
     - Callie levers the box from outside the chalk with a four-foot pipe laid across the stool rim. That puts her hands inside the ring.
   - **Why it matters:** the movement makes position a moral fact. Toren's dose is priced by breaths "inside the chalk", and only Toren and the technician are inside. The geometry therefore carries the drama.
   - **Likely fix:** a ring about three feet out makes all three placements work, and keeps Toren the only helper who crosses it.
2. **The home line gets ahead of the party (ch. 24, l. 487).**
   - At the gate Dessa writes the departure page, Katori says goodbye to Rook, and Ines and Otto are present.
   - Two hundred paces later, all of them are already at their Sag posts. The narration concedes Dessa got there "somehow".
   - With her leg, and the party moving at a cart's pace, this isn't possible without a stated pause. One line fixes it: the watch goes out through the gate first to take its places, and the party waits for Dessa's "Go now."
3. **Minor.** Dessa's page says "First light", but the party first spends the eighth hour in the ward. This works if winter first light is late; the clock is otherwise loose enough to pass.

### 3b. Arithmetic, day counts and accounting

This book makes counting a virtue, so these matter more than their size.

- **The store is never debited for the pen.**
  - The pen used "six clean Flasks" (ch. 20, l. 43) and spent 19 Handfuls. The movement never says where those Flasks came from.
  - The store is unchanged afterwards: 31 Flasks and 2½ Handfuls, with 4 thin and 27 clean (ch. 21, ll. 101 and 355; ch. 23).
  - The watch's jars that Otto brings to the plate (end of ch. 19; ch. 23) also never reach the book.
  - The Director reads the store book every evening, so she would ask. **Fix:** state that the pen's Flasks came from the long building's medical stock, or debit the store and adjust the later figures.
- **The abort line cuts into the budget.**
  - Eight Flasks (64 Handfuls) go inside, with an abort at "two Flasks left inside". That leaves 48 usable Handfuls.
  - The ledger's projection is 56 Handfuls "if nothing went wrong" (ch. 21, ll. ~121–155).
  - This may be intended as margin, since the last line pays for the way out. Callie would say so. One line in ch. 21 settles it.
- **Senna's testimony is inflated as it is repeated.**
  - Senna dictates (ch. 18) that her fingertips went "like I'd had them in snow" on the *sixth* day, and that the men who went deepest lost most.
  - Others then restate it as "Mrs Voss lost her hands in eleven days" (Director, ch. 18, l. 67; ch. 22, l. 45), "a fence that took a woman's hands" (ch. 18, l. 77), and "Senna's crew lost their hands in eleven days" (Dee, ch. 21, l. 103). Dee's exposure rates are then built on that line.
  - `BOOK_MAP.md` attributes Senna's *present* neuropathy to the active course, and Movement Two shows her hands going now.
  - Senna's own "it took my hands and my feet and twenty years" (ch. 24) is fine as her framing. The Director's and Dee's versions are factual claims, and they overstate the record. **Apparent inconsistency** with the dictated page.
- **Day and count slips:**
  - Ch. 18, l. 207: "His hands had been shaking for four days". On D+31 that should be about six (since D+25; ch. 17 says "three days" on D+28).
  - Ch. 18, l. 239: "He had not built them from the knee for eleven days". The correction came on D+18, so on D+32 it is fourteen.
  - Ch. 18, ll. 45 and 87: "You've brought six people" and "six people behind him". Five people came with Toren (Rook, Dee, Pell, Jab and Callie).
  - Ch. 19, l. 411: the stone "settled at the long table in the morning… not been able to spend all day". The long table was D+33; the rack drill is D+34 (ch. 20 and the continuity note). Either "yesterday" or restage the table session.
  - Ch. 21, l. 427: Pell writes "concur" "for the first time anybody had seen him". The day before, he wrote "*The way home. T. Concur. P.*" in the margin of the same paper (ch. 21). It can be defended ("on something he was going to have to walk"), but the phrasing invites the objection.
  - Unverified: "two operations and three pictures" (ch. 24, Little). This movement shows one operation and one picture. Check against Movement One.
- **An ambiguous referent in the ledger.** Ch. 24, l. 451: "weighed by T. at the weighbridge". Everywhere else in Dessa's ledger "T." means Toren ("T. saw her leave the hollow", "T. said don't check"). Here it means Tilda. Write "weighed by Tilda" or "by C.'s mother".

## 4. Numerical formula alignment (informed, not blind)

### Method

- **Tool:** Python 3.14. The script is in Appendix A. It was run over `chapter-17.md` to `chapter-24.md`, and over ch. 1–8, 9–16 and 1–24 for comparison.
- **Word count:** whitespace split, 64,254, which matches the author's `wc`. Prose words (headings and `---` excluded) are 64,314.
- **Paragraphs:** non-empty lines that are not `---` or headings.
- **Sentence tokenizer:**
  - Splits after `.`, `!` or `?`, optionally followed by closing quotes or brackets, then whitespace, then a capital letter, digit or opening quote.
  - It protects `Mr.`/`Mrs.`/`Dr.` and single-capital initials ("D. W.", "E. M.").
  - `"Why?" he said.` stays one sentence, because the next word is lower-case.
  - A 30-sentence random sample was inspected by hand: all 30 splits were correct.
- **Syllable estimator:** a vowel-group heuristic with silent-e and -ed/-es rules.
  - A refined version (Appendix A, `syl2`) was checked against 68 hand-counted words. It misses 7, with a net −5 of 143 syllables, an undercount of about 3.5%.
  - The first and refined estimators give the same Flesch result to 0.1.
  - If you correct for the undercount, Flesch Reading Ease falls by about 3.5 and the grade rises by about 0.5. The departure from target is unchanged.
  - Proper names such as "Sustain", "Meridian" and "Handful" are counted by the same rule.
- **Comparability with Movement Two's review:** that review used a regex-only sentence proxy, and its sentence figures are not comparable with these. The lexicon regexes below reuse Movement Two's *verbatim*, and the whole book has been re-counted with this script so the movements can be compared.

### Departures and conflicts for the owner

These are not automatic repairs.

1. **Sentence rhythm is a dialogue effect, not a narration defect.**
   - Narration alone lands almost exactly on the formula: mean 14.8, median 10, 27.9% of sentences at five words or fewer.
   - The whole-text miss comes from speech ("Yes." "I know." "Eleven.") being about a third of the words.
   - The source's standard deviation of about 26 around a mean of 14.6 cannot be reached by punctuated prose without very long outliers (100+ words). It may be an ASR artifact, from unpunctuated transcript runs.
   - Recommend no rhythm rewrite. The owner should decide whether the target applies to all text or to narration only, and whether to keep the SD target. It is reported here unchanged, as the prompt instructs.
2. **Reading level is far easier than the target** (Flesch 97.7 against 72.3; grade 2.2 against 6.8). It has been this way in every movement.
   - Two things drive it: the short dialogue sentences, and monosyllabic diction (1.17 syllables per word).
   - Raising it would mean longer or more Latinate words, which would fight the read-aloud voice the packet asks for.
   - This is a real voice-versus-formula conflict and needs an owner ruling. Do not patch it by inflating vocabulary.
3. **Progression front-loading runs the wrong way by plan.**
   - `BOOK_MAP.md` §7–8 places ash ladder stages 5–9 in Movement Three, so the teaching density rises in the middle third by design.
   - The formula wants the first third at about 1.7× the later thirds.
   - Adding system vocabulary back into Movement One, or suppressing it here, would both damage the book. The owner should choose which governs. Final confirmation needs the whole book.
4. **Scene breaks are still about twice the proxy rate.** Many `---` markers are small beat turns inside a single location. Comparing typography with ASR pauses is weak evidence, so this is low priority.
5. **Reporting verbs** run at about 4× the source rate, on "said" alone. This is secondary and use-to-taste, and it helps the read-aloud goal. Recommend no action.

## 5. Repair brief (three priorities at most)

### Priority 1: close the physical staging seams in the two set pieces

- **Location:** ch. 20, l. ~23 (the ring "six feet out"), l. 81 (Dee's arm "a foot from the box"), l. 209 (Jab "reached in"), and the lever placement at about ll. 190–201; ch. 24, ll. ~440–487 (the gate, then the watch already at its posts).
- **Observed issue:** the pen's ring radius makes three stated positions impossible. In ch. 24 the whole home line reaches its Sag posts ahead of a party that left the gate before them, and the narration admits it with "somehow".
- **Effect on the reader:** the pen sequence teaches the reader to track who is inside the chalk, because that decides who pays. A reader who pictures six feet notices that Dee's arm, Jab's reach and Callie's lever don't work, and trust in the movement's best action scene drops. The departure's final tableau depends on the watch being in place, and "somehow" breaks the spell at the emotional peak.
- **Proposed scope:** a few words and one sentence.
  - Change the ring to about three feet, and check that Callie's "thirty feet" bench and the 18-inch capsule distance still read correctly.
  - In ch. 24, add one line: the watch goes out ahead to take its places, or the party waits at the gate until Dessa's call carries back.

### Priority 3: read-aloud texture — the new epithet and the number tics

- **Location:**

## 6. Uncertainty and limits

- **Same model as the author.** This is a fresh context, not an independent reader. A human or cross-model cold read is still needed. `MOVEMENT-003-COLD-READ.prompt.md` exists for that purpose.
- **Canon is only partly verifiable.** Book One manuscripts, `CANON.md` and `STATE_LEDGER.md` were not supplied. Claims that touch them are flagged as apparent: Tonk's "two operations", Rook's time at Homura, and Senna's twenty-year history.
- **The metrics are reproducible but approximate:**
  - The sentence tokenizer was hand-verified on a sample, not on the whole text.
  - The syllable estimator undercounts by about 3.5% on a hand list.
  - Typographic paragraphs and scene breaks are not the ASR pause measures the targets came from.
  - The development-beat counts are manual and depend on judgment.
  - Front-loading is provisional at about 56% coverage.
- **Scale of the evidence.** The scores reflect one reading of one movement. They say nothing about any author, model or seat across a series, and they are not an audience or sales forecast.

def syl2(w):
    w=w.lower().strip("'’"); w=re.sub(r"['’](s|d|ll|re|ve|t|m)$","",w)

    if w.endswith('e') and not w.endswith(('le','ee','ye')): n-=1
    elif w.endswith('le') and len(w)>2 and w[-3] in 'aeiouy': n-=1
    if w.endswith('ed') and not w.endswith(('ted','ded')) and not re.search(r'[^aeiouy][lr]ed$',w) and len(VOW.findall(w[:-2]))>=1: n-=1
    if w.endswith('es') and not re.search(r'(s|x|z|ch|sh|g|c)es$',w) and len(VOW.findall(w[:-2]))>=1: n-=1
