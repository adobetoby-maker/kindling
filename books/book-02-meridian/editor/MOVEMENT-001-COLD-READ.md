## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**

  the right word first, and a stranger's two-second decision turns on it. I was in
  from that point.
- **Tonk: "That's one." (L149–159).** This was the first laugh, and it came from
  character, not from a joke.
- **Hallet's "Persons in party." / "Two." (L537–569).** "It's only a column" is the
  best sentence in the chapter.
- **Sowerby's self-correction** after "Is he alive?" (L847–851), and Keel's "Nine"
  with the one amputation told straight (L977–983).
- **Tilda refusing the number: "I'm not giving you the last sum, love." (L1863).**
  This is the emotional peak of the movement. It then pays off in Callie letting her
  mother's third breath go uncounted (L3113–3123).
- **The stone fitting Katori's hand (L2905–2917).** It's physical, and it lands
  without explanation. "I'm Rook" as the reply is exactly right.
- **The knocking dropping to ten (L3641–3651).** A skill already established
  (Callie counts) becomes the alarm. It's also the best suspense in the movement.

**Where interest slipped.**
- **Ch1 roster of the wounded (L93–115).** Seven people arrive, each with a
  backstory from a fight I didn't see. I held on because of the barrow, not the
  roster.
- **Ch3 recap of the south-gate window and "referred" (L1453–1471).** Flashback
  about an institution I can't place yet.
- **Ch3 overall.** Two formal set-piece declarations sit in one chapter (Senna
  L1155–1205, Rook L1481–1503). Both are good, but the second arrives before the
  first has settled.
- **Ch5 long-coat exposition on the Sag (L2261–2277).** Necessary, but it's a man
  explaining the economy on a step.
- **Ch6 bread and Milo (L2583–2655).** I don't know Milo, so the grief lands at
  about half weight for a cold reader. The staging is still moving.

**Which person matters.** Jab. The desire (keep Tonk alive, keep the promise) is
concrete. He is honest at real cost ("That one I did. I'm sorry."), and his arc
moves from carrying to letting go of the hand. Callie is a close second. Tonk is
the movement's engine of warmth.

**Which event I expect next.** At the Director's meeting on the thirteenth day, the
three summonses will turn out to be about the failing calibration train, not only
the north road. Someone will propose going past "four hundred miles" to find a
replacement reference box. The likely target is a pre-Fall site like the ones Senna
stripped for copper (L3779–3785). The Sag's instability and the "thinner" ash
(L3345–3363) are probably tied to the same failure (both point at 2130). Close
behind: Jab's signing decision, and Tonk's two-week picture competing with Senna's
cycles for a rationed machine (L3763–3767).

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The final chapter converts a
movement of aftermath into a clock.

   - Ch5, L2195: "The cord came back from the hatch that evening with everything

## 4. Repair brief (three priorities)

The movement contains no extended on-page fight, so no priority below shortens
action. None asks for cuts made to hit a word count.

### Priority 1 — Physical-token and position continuity pass

- **Location:**
  - L2741 ("Rook had said *Tuesdays*")
  - L3863 ("Two doors down, on the step of eleven")
  - The cord chain at L2195–2197, L3415, L3487 and L3587–3597
  - The Ch5 step scene's time of day (L2233, L2305, L2375)
- **Observed issue:** The book's pleasure is exactness: counted chairs, counted
  knocks, a box three rows down and four along. The few places where objects,
  positions or attribution drift stand out more because of that.
- **Effect on the reader:** It interrupts trust at emotionally loaded moments. The
  cord confusion lands in the Ch8 yellow-line handoff, one of the best beats in the
  movement. A careful teen reader will stop to reconcile "it's not metal" with "the
  metal on its cord."
- **Proposed scope:** Line-level, about 6–10 sentences:
  - Re-attribute "Tuesdays" to Senna, or give Rook his own echo line.
  - Change "two doors" to "one door" in Ch8.
  - Name the tether as a different cord (bark twine, a bandage strip, a bootlace)
    the first time it appears in Ch5.
  - Make the Ch8 "still tied" line match where Jab sleeps.
  - Either move the Ch5 Rook scene to dusk and reconcile it with Ch6, or drop the
    lamp-lighting beat.
- **Strength to preserve:** Tonk holding on first, "hard, for about three seconds,"
  before letting go at the line (L3599–3601). Also Toren's "Third row down" payoff
  (L3663).

### Priority 2 — Distinguish the wise adult voices

- **Location:** Recurrent phrasing across many speakers:
  - "I'd like that understood / noted / noticed / minuted / written down": L413,
    L1013, L1175, L1345, L2613, L2791, L3679, L3817.
  - "…a column for that": L619, L1781, L2229, L2579.
  - "extremely rude": L983, L1165, L1913.
  - "the way you say the road goes left / the door is on the left / read out a
    weight": L301, L693, L1843.
- **Observed issue:** These characters share one dry, precise, gently formal
  register: Hallet, Sowerby, Keel, Senna, Tilda, Rook, the long-coat man, Katori and
  Wyck. Individually each line is good. Together they begin to sound like one
  narrator's wisdom rather than nine people.
- **Effect on the reader:** It flattens the ear for a thirteen-year-old reading or
  listening. For the adult reader it signals authorial voice and weakens the moments
  where an echo *is* meant to mean something. Tilda picking up Senna's "extremely
  rude… I've been practising" should read as the two women bonding, not as the
  book's house style.
- **Proposed scope:** A targeted dialogue pass, not a rewrite:
  - Assign each tic an owner. "Column" belongs to Pell and the window. "Understood /
    minuted" belongs to Senna.
  - Trim or re-voice roughly half the other occurrences.
  - Give one or two institutional adults a less polished rhythm (interruptions,
    fragments, a wrong word).
  - Keep all scenes intact.
- **Strength to preserve:**
  - Hallet's "I'm sorry, sir. It's only a column." (L569)
  - Sowerby's "I should have started there, and I didn't." (L851)
  - Rook's "It's a choice with a price on it… The other kind's a menu." (L2355)

### Priority 3 — Orientation for the cold entry, Ch1–3

- **Location:**
  - The Ch1 roster (L93–115).
  - The first uses of terms: Ember (L275), conduit (L297), door (L677), Flask /
    Handful (L129, L2003), reserve-ash (L2263), "at fourteen minutes" (L1761).
  - The Ch3 south-gate recap (L1453–1471).
  - Unanchored references: the Breach (L1093), the Level and Milo (L1171, L2633),
    Homura and Vera Kess (L2843).
- **Observed issue:** The movement opens after a large unseen event and leans on
  prior-book knowledge in its first three chapters. Most of it can be inferred from
  context. A few terms carry mechanical weight (what a conduit is, what a Flask is
  worth) that the plot later depends on.
- **Effect on the reader:** It adds early friction for a new reader and some for a
  younger one. Grief beats tied to off-page people (Milo's bread, L2633–2655) land at
  partial weight. A series reader is unlikely to notice any of this.
- **Proposed scope:** A clause-level anchor on first use of about five load-bearing
  terms, in the POV character's own idiom. For example, Callie in one half-sentence
  on what the grey coat means by "conduit". Also one concrete image the first time
  Milo is named in the book. Don't add explanatory paragraphs, and don't recap Book
  1. The Sowerby "bad thing / good thing / bill" triad recurs three times, and its
  variations already refresh it: Senna's is summarised, Tilda says "Stop." It doesn't
  need structural change.
- **Strength to preserve:**
  - The opening line and the pace count (L67).
  - Ch1's refusal to explain the fight, which keeps the gate scene in present tense.

## 5. Uncertainty

- **Simulation.** An AI model reading in a fresh context produced this review. It is
  not evidence of how any human thirteen-year-old or adult genre reader would
  respond. The two lenses are imagined perspectives, not samples.
- **Series knowledge.** I have no Book 1 context, by instruction. Several "slipped"
  moments in §1 and all of Priority 3 may disappear for a returning reader. The
  scores for Opening pull and Clarity / flow are the ones most sensitive to this.
- **Canon.** Without supplied canon, §3 can only report internal apparent
  contradictions. Some items (the cords, the paces, Tilda's day count) may be
  consistent with facts established elsewhere.
- **Scores.** These are editorial judgments on the stated anchors, not calibrated
  measurements. Differences of one point between lenses are within noise. No
  composite or release gate is implied.
- **Scope.** This is one movement. It says nothing about how the series as a whole
  paces its action, and no conclusion should be drawn about any drafting seat or
  model from it.
