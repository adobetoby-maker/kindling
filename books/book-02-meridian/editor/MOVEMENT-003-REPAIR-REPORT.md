### The red-call breach (ch. 21, the Director's review, D+38)

- The Director reads Hallet's line (*Red called… everybody out, the technician as well*) and asks: **"When she called red, who obeyed it?"**
- Dee answers **"Nobody did"** and owns it: she called Toren in, kept Rook and Sowerby in, and "That's mine. It isn't theirs."
- Callie: "I called it. And then I went in." The Director: calling it was her job, and she will call it on the hill.

**The rule as read.** Dee wrote it the night of the pen ("the other thing"), and it was copied under the lines on D+37:

> *Red, and nobody trapped: everybody out, at once, no argument.*
>
> *Red, and a body trapped: everybody who isn't needed to get that body out or cover the source goes out at once. The fewest who can do it stay, and the watch says their names out loud as they go in. They stay until the person's out and the source is covered, and not a breath longer. Then they come out too, and nobody goes back in until the counter's under the red.*
>
> *Every time, that night, in the book: who stayed, how long each one was in, what the counter said at each of them, and why.*

- **Dee applies the rule to herself:** by it, Rook should have gone through the gate at *red*, and so should Sowerby once his cloths were thrown. Only Toren, Callie and Jab were needed.
- **The Director signs it and forbids reading it as heroism.** She says: "Nobody is being praised on my paper for staying in that pen… It could as easily have been three more of you in the east corridor and him dead under the box anyway. A line's a line. The only thing that bends it is a body you can reach. It bends for the fewest people, for the shortest time, and it goes in the book with their names. If it isn't that, it's out."
- The recall itself stays within the rule, because it began after the counter read 90, under the red.
