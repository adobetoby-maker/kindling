## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** It caught at once: "Jab woke in the dark… and knew where everybody was" and then "Rook was hot." (41:1–5). Pell's plan to take the cart apart and bring it over "in pieces" gives the opening a concrete problem the reader can hold (41:95–110). Several moments pulled me forward:
- Callie letting go of the horse's rope, "Lead's cheaper than you" (41:~150).
- The second wheel jumping, Dee letting the rope run, and the wheel lying in the drift at a hundred and ten.
- Toren on the post asking "Who goes in?" instead of going. This is the best single character beat in the movement, and Pell names it in the next chapter: "That's how you know it's yours" (42).

The movement has two peaks.
1. **Ch 41, the wash crossing.** The problem is physical and the rules are clear. There is one real failure (the wheel), and the fix costs something (three quarters of a jar).
2. **Ch 45, the Sag.** It runs from Callie's lee held "on the steel" to Toren and Katori splitting *hold* and *kill* at the top of the dark lane. Then the big push, Jab saying *off* before Dee does, and Callie at her floor with blood in her voice. Dessa running out of lines on the departure page ("There's not enough lines", 45:~675) is the emotional landing, and it earns it.

After the Sag the movement turns to homecoming, and it mostly works. Ch 47 is the warmest chapter in the book so far. Tonk's "Did he eat inside?" questions lead into the chair with Jab's hands behind his back. The Director then writes Tonk's term, "First: supper at the fifth bed. Daily. (T.'s term.)", above everything else on the page. The "released to bearer" socket (46:~245–285) and Toren's hand on his shirt make a strong, quiet hook. Ch 48 turns it into a stated reveal.

**Where it slipped.**
- (a) **The road home, ch 42 second half and most of ch 43.** It repeats wheel checks ("Same"), meal counts and culvert counts. Every refrain is in character, and some of it is the point: Callie learning not to count people. But by the fourth culvert and the third meal sum, the pressure is being *reported* more than *changed*. The rain, Toren reading the road, the washout "six breaths" and the ninth-hour scene are the real events in ch 43, and they get a little buried.
- (b) **The ledger read-alouds.** Callie's ledger is spoken in full at 47:235–249, the weighman's board follows in ch 48, and Callie also reports the jars to her mother in ch 46. A reader who has not been doing the arithmetic hears three accountings of the same ash in three chapters.
- (c) **Small timeline and count seams.** Section 4 lists them. None stopped me, but each made me look back.

**Which person matters.** In this movement it is Jab. He carries the fish home, feels the Sag from two miles, stops himself, and turns down the roll on his own terms. Toren's arc (asking, delegating, handing the lane kill to Katori, the disk) is the structural spine. Callie gets the most private growth: the "six breaths" at the washout, and then "I didn't have to be the wall."

**What I expect next.** Rook goes to the south huts to find the four men who paid in brass. Katori says "the rest". Somebody asks Toren about the disk, probably Callie, who "kept" what she saw. Someone plans a spring return to Caul Hill, with the rope left "for somebody coming the other way" as a callback.

**Would I continue?** Yes. The brass chit and the disk are strong open threads, and the household endings are satisfying enough that the next movement starts from trust, not debt.

## 2. Two lenses

These are reading lenses, not demographic research. No sales inference.

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** This reader will love the horse, the wheel in the drift, the hound held off by "a finger of the hook", and the Sag fight. The dark-lane choreography (strut narrows the lane, Katori kills through the gap) is easy to picture. Tonk and Jab will land hardest. The weighman's "I don't like crews named after boys" will get a laugh. What loses this reader: the fractional jar accounting (quarters, halves, "seven and three quarters, clean"), the meal-cup arithmetic, and the board-chalking scene. They will skim those. Callie's blood at the Sag will worry them, and nothing afterwards tells them she is all right (section 4).

**Adult genre reader.** This reader will value the logistics as texture: the cart in pieces, tallow as grease, rain caught in canvas. They will notice the cost model working, because every spend is named. They will enjoy the institutional ending (the censure written "Not praised. Not a model"), the review clocks, and the standing sheet with "an adult on every watch". This reader is more likely to notice the stated lessons ("I didn't have to be the wall", "I hold. You kill", "That's how you know it's yours"). Most are earned, but the movement tends to say its lesson out loud right after dramatizing it. They are also more likely to catch the seams in section 4.

## 4. Exact stumble points

1. **Meal count skips a night (42:179, 42:289, 43:77, 43:247, 43:585).** The count is "Fourteen cups… and a bit" on the 50th, then three cups go into the pot that night. On the 51st, after eating, Callie says "Eleven cups". That should be eight if the 50th's meal is counted. Every later number (8, then 2 on the 54th) follows from the 11, so the slip is at 42:289/43:77. A reader who is tracking Callie's arithmetic, which the text invites, will catch it.
2. **The siding camp is east and west in the same scene (43:173, 43:245, 43:275).** The camp is "at the east end of the siding". Thirty lines later Callie is "standing here at the west end of the siding". The approach paragraph (43:173) says they came "to the west end of it from the other side", which makes the geography harder to rebuild.
3. **"Four beds on the right… The fifth had Tonk." (47:15)** This contradicts itself in one breath.
4. **Which morning Tonk did the chair (47:87, 47:197, 47:407).** "In the morning Tonk did the chair" follows the night of return, so it is the 56th. The Director's meeting is on the 57th, yet Jab says Tonk told him "This morning. After the chair." The same kind of overlap: Tilda "weighed them this morning" (47:239, 57th), but ch 48 has the ash locked in the east shed since the 55th and weighed at the plate on the 58th, with the home Flasks weighed there "three times".
5. **"I read about fifteen" (46:163).** Callie says this, then reads 110 and 106, and the technician says "That's what you wrote." "Fifteen" reads like a count reading that contradicts the numbers. If it means "about fifteen boxes", say "boxes".
6. **Callie's blood (45:563, 45:587, 45:711) is never picked up.** With cracked ribs, blood in the voice and down the chin reads as a possible lung injury. In ch 46 Keel re-straps the ribs "with his mouth shut", and her mother touches the strapping, but nobody names the blood. The reader carries an alarm the book seems to have dropped.
7. **Dessa's six lines (45:681).** "*E. Marsh. D. Wren. P. J. C. T. Voss.*" In this movement Pell and Rook are never given surnames. "Mr Marsh" (47, the standing sheet) implies Rook, and the order of the list suggests the same, but a cold reader cannot tell who "E. Marsh" and "P." are. Aloud, it is a string of letters.
8. **"It came up to thirty feet" (45:251).** It is the only distance in feet I noticed, in a book measured in paces and hands.
9. **Toren "had not gone back to eleven at all" (46:325).** Callie's hedge ("did not think") covers it, but Toren has been in the shed at the socket (56th) and at the Director's table (57th). A lighter touch ("had hardly left it") would avoid the snag.

**Physical state.**

- Exception: Callie's blood (item 6).

**Power limits: questions for the owner, not errors.**
- **Jab's sensing range and warning.** Ch 44 has him feel the Sag's heave from two miles, pick out named people inside it, and get "a breath before. Two" of warning. The text points back to a lean felt "at a mile on the forty-third", so this is presented as growth. But the two-breath warning becomes the load-bearing mechanic of the whole Sag crossing. If the plan does not intend this range or this early warning yet, it is the largest capability step in the movement.
- **The lee "on the steel".** Callie kneeling beside the hook, not behind it, and holding a lean-to "line" longer than a door, is a new technique discovered under pressure. It is well prepared by her failed six breaths at the washout. The owner should confirm it doesn't pre-empt a planned later lesson.
- **Toren's spend at the lane.** He runs the stone through a hound and a heel-light, then down to his own two turns and holds an empty strut. This is consistent with the costs given.

**Knowledge boundaries and reserved disclosures.**
- Ch 48 states outright that the disk has nine grooves on each face, matching a nine-ribbed socket annotated "released to bearer", and that Toren now connects the two ("He had it."). If the plan reserves Toren's *conscious* connection for later, this is where it is spent. The reader connection in ch 46 is subtle, and the ch 48 confirmation removes the doubt. I can't tell from the manuscript alone whether that is intended.
- Rook reveals only "It's the same brass". The name and number are withheld, which is good discipline.
- Katori's "I've not said the rest" is referenced, not spent.

## 6. Repair brief (at most three priorities)

### Priority 1: Fix the count, place and day seams in the road home and the homecoming

- **Location:** 42:289 / 43:77 (meal); 43:173, 43:245, 43:275 (siding east/west); 47:15 (bed count); 47:87 / 47:407 / 47:239 against ch 48's weighing (day of the chair, day of Tilda's weighing); 46:163 ("about fifteen").
- **Observed issue:** Six or seven small contradictions in a movement whose main voice is exact counting.
- **Effect on the reader:** Callie and Toren's credibility rests on the numbers being right. Each slip makes the attentive reader stop trusting the ledger, and that trust is what makes "Not touched" and "Nine and three quarters" land.
- **Proposed scope:** Line-level only, about 10–15 words per site. For example: correct "Eleven" to "Eight" (and carry the numbers forward), or drop the 50th's pot; make the siding camp one end; "Five beds on the right"; "Yesterday. After the chair"; decide on one weighing day for the home Flasks; "about fifteen boxes".
- **Strength to preserve:** The counting texture itself, including Callie's refusal to count *people* on the ninth hour (43, the ninth-hour scene). Don't thin the refrains to fix the arithmetic.

### Priority 2: Pick up Callie's blood, and make two read-aloud lines parseable

- **Location:** 45:563, 45:587, 45:711; ch 46 gate and ward sections (Keel re-strapping, mother's hand on her side); 45:681 (initials list); 45:251 ("thirty feet").
- **Observed issue:** A visible injury signal is raised at the climax and never resolved. The six-line list can't be followed by ear or by a cold reader.
- **Effect on the reader:** The thirteen-year-old lens will worry about Callie through ch 46 and 48 without release. Aloud, the initials list turns a key emotional beat into noise.
- **Proposed scope:** One sentence in ch 46 that names the blood's source (bitten lip or tongue, or Keel checking her breathing and finding the lung clear), *or* a deliberate open thread if the owner wants it. Rewrite the list with enough first names or roles to be followed (it can stay terse), and change "feet" to paces.
- **Strength to preserve:** "I'm at my floor. I'm stopping." and the hook lying flat: Callie stops on the ribs' word, which is the whole arc. Keep the blood if it serves that; just don't let it disappear.

### Priority 3: Compress repeated accountings so each ledger scene has its own job

- **Location:** ch 43 wheel and meal refrains after the second halt (43:~1–40, 43:~75–85); jar tally to her mother (46:~125–130); Callie's ledger to the Director (47:235–253); the board chalking (48:~40–95).
- **Observed issue:** The same ash is accounted for in full three times in three chapters, and the road chapter repeats its checks without escalation between the real events.
- **Effect on the reader:** The thirteen-year-old skims, and may skim straight past something new (the drift combed east at 43:~50). The adult enjoys the first accounting and tolerates the third.
- **Proposed scope:** Give each scene one purpose. The mother gets "Home, twenty-four. Not touched." and little else. The Director gets the full ledger (it's the audit). The board scene keeps Tilda's knot and the naming, and summarizes the chalk. In ch 43, trim one or two "Same" check-ins between events. This is a trim of perhaps 150–300 words across the movement. **It does not touch the wash crossing, the Sag crossing, the lane fight or the big push.** Those are the developed action the owner asked for, and their length is earned.
- **Strength to preserve:** Tilda's knot ("So nobody could say a sack had been opened when it hadn't. Or hadn't when it had.") and the weighman's "I'll believe it. Once." These are the best lines in the board scene.

## 8. Uncertainty and tradeoffs

- **Contamination.** The start-of-session memory notes told me the title payoff ("Home Line") and that there had been fixes to a culvert reference, Wyck's clearance and an hour clash. I likely under-scrutinized those spots and over-rated surprise in ch 48. A human cold reader, or a model without those notes, should re-check the keep-reading and connection scores.
- **Same-model bias.** A model reviewing its own family's prose tends to like its cadences. The "the way…" density and the stated-lesson habit are exactly what that bias hides. A human reader may weigh them more heavily than I did.
- **Arithmetic.** I checked the ash and meal sums by hand from the text. I'm confident in the meal slip and the ledger reconciliations, but I could have mis-carried a fraction elsewhere.
- **One movement, one reader.** Nothing here shows whether one drafting or review seat is better than another across the series.
- **Owner-only calls.** Whether Jab's two-mile, two-breath sensing and Toren's conscious disk/socket link are intended at this point are plan questions. I have flagged them in section 5, not scored them as errors.
