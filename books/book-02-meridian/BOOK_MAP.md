## 0. A conflict this map has to name before anything else

Two prior authoring sessions already produced full Book Two architectures:
`books/book-01-hobbs-wall/BOOK_2_MAP.md` ("The Ledger") and
`books/book-01-tallow-gate/BOOK_MAP_2.md` (untitled). Both are well built and
both share the same load-bearing assumption: **the protagonist set is Toren,
Dessa, and Wyck**, with Rook and Senna as the adult/elder pressure, and Callie
and Jab held at the edge of the frame as neighbors whose material is "kept,
but compressed to a single movement's complication."

That assumption conflicts directly with the owner's ruling for *this* book:
Callie, Jab, and Toren are **all three** protagonists, none demotable. The two
existing drafts are not wrong — they are answers to a different brief, written
before this one existed. Their actual content is largely reusable:

- **Fully reusable as locked mechanism**, independent of which characters carry
  it: Guard as Stride's second shape (`CANON.md`, cross-confirmed by both
  drafts already), the Sag line and ash-lifting economy, the medicine/residency
  gate split, Director's-office-style institutional pressure, the "no
  power inside the wall" no-power-inside-the-line convention*, and the
  general engine correction ("growth, powers, and fights are the spine;
  institutional pressure is load underneath it, not the plot").
- **Not directly portable**: both drafts' *character* engine — Toren, Dessa,
  and Wyck each privately believing their door is capped, and each breaking
  that belief across three call-outs — is built for a five-person, single-lead-
  adjacent cast. It cannot be lifted wholesale into a book that owes Callie and
  Jab full arcs, or Dessa and Wyck end up re-absorbing the book's whole growth
  budget again, exactly the imbalance the owner is correcting.

  <sub>*The Hobb's-Wall draft's "no worked power inside the wall" rule does not
  appear in `CANON.md` as locked — it is that draft's own invention to force
  growth outward onto call-outs. This map does not adopt it as written; see
  §11, continuity risks.</sub>

**This map's resolution:** keep the mechanism (Guard, ash-lifting, the Sag,
the Director's-office pressure, the "discovery under real pressure, not a
workshop" rule), discard the character engine, and rebuild the growth engine
across three protagonists plus a defender cohort. The owner then joined the
two proposed outward objectives into one load-bearing mission: the team must
recover the failing treatment-machine part at Caul Hill **and** reach and
secure the new ash-rich rip there. The large ash reserve is not bonus loot; it
is the fuel that lets Rook, Jab, and a surviving Homura cross-medic recall the
radiation burden from exposed bodies long enough for the party to enter, work,
fight, and come back alive. `OWNER`.

## 1. Verdict: the strongest Book Two engine

**The team survived the road. Meridian is where they find out that surviving
it wasn't the same as being safe — and that the only place any of them
actually gets better is back outside the wall, doing the work Meridian cannot
spare enough qualified adults to do.** Concretely: Meridian's Sag line is
worsening faster than its registered defenders can cover; a new and more
dangerous rip has opened near Caul Hill; and the one machine that keeps Senna
and Tilda alive needs a part from the irradiated plant beside it. Reaching the
part and securing the rip are one mission. The team must carry large stores of
graded ash because Sustain can recall active radiation from a living body only
while someone keeps paying for the work. That necessity turns three separated
arrivals into one company: Callie, Jab, and Toren go with Rook and the Homura
cross-medic, while their coaches and cohort make the expedition possible.
Training, combat, ash economics, and found-family bonding all happen because
the mission must be prepared for, survived, and paid for. Institutional
politics supplies pressure and consequence. It does not replace the adventure.

## 2. Book promise, emotional promise, progression promise, irreversible ending change

- **Book promise:** the three roads that became one group at Meridian's gate
  become one working company by this book's end — not by declaration, by
  having solved a problem together that none of their three separate roads
  could have solved alone.
- **Emotional promise:** each protagonist discovers that the cost of the road
  didn't end at the gate. Callie's counting, Jab's caregiving arithmetic, and
  Toren's leadership weight all find a new, harder object — a mother who
  won't let her count, a brother who will need him for thirteen years, a
  grandmother whose hands are already going — and each has to grow a
  different kind of strength to carry it that isn't the strength that got
  them here.
- **Progression promise:** all three protagonists cross a real threshold
  under real stakes, never merely in a drill: Toren gets Guard to hold on
  purpose; Callie's twin-kindled Ward (Edge and Ward together,
  `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`) is tested at group scale; and Jab learns that his
  "second thing" is Sustain — sensing, supporting a failing body, and, with
  ash, recalling active radiation from tissue. Jab's rare fit for the Caul
  Hill run makes him Meridian's primary recruit, which gives his growth a
  human cost: the station first sees a scarce medical asset where Tonk sees
  his brother. None of the three becomes what the adults already are. They
  become people whose separate capabilities can keep one another alive.
- **Irreversible ending change:** the ceiling every one of them believed in —
  Callie's "I only read ground," Jab's "it doesn't come when I call it,"
  Toren's "it's out and I don't know when it's coming back" — turns out to
  have been an untested assumption, not a fact. That, not the accord with the
  Director's office, is the book's real irreversible change. Layered under it:
  a named household's residency injustice gets fixed, at real cost, using the
  team's new capability as the actual instrument of the fix; the machine that
  keeps Senna and Tilda alive is repaired, not cured; and Cinder Company's
  remnant is confirmed re-forming under a new name, off-page, opening Book
  Three's canvas.

## 3. The central outward mission

### One mission: the part and the rip (`OWNER`)

The former Options A and B are now one operation. Senna's treatment shed runs
on a single, sixty-year-old machine (`CANON.md`, Ch. 27–28), and a part in its
calibration train is failing. The only known compatible salvage is at Caul Hill
— the irradiated pre-Fall plant where Senna received her original exposure.
A new and more dangerous rip has opened at or beneath the same complex. It has
made the site harder to enter, but it has also left the one resource the team
cannot make the attempt without: deep stores of ownerless reserve-ash.

The objectives cannot be separated:

1. Carry enough clean, graded ash from Meridian to fund the approach and the
   first radiation-recall cycles. The team cannot count on ash it has not yet
   won.
2. Cross the contaminated approach while Rook, Jab, and `[HOMURA MEDIC]`
   rotate Sustain duty, recalling active radiological burden before it fixes
   into lasting tissue damage.
3. Reach the calibration assembly and recover the compatible part.
4. Contain or collapse the new rip, kill its new Riftspawn expression through
   a full-team action, and recover the resulting ash so the healers can bring
   everyone home.

The third former option — making the disk mystery the journey's reason — stays
retired. The disk receives one costly clue late in the book, not the book's
engine.

### Why it must leave Meridian

The shed's machine cannot be repaired from spare parts on hand; Meridian's own
hardened depot inventory has nothing compatible (a callback to Annice Fell's
ledger, Ch. 28: things go into that depot, they do not come out). The active
rip also cannot be ignored: if it grows, it changes the Sag from a strained
defense line into an open road for stronger Riftspawn. Caul Hill is roughly a
week's travel past the Sag's worst stretch — close enough to reach inside the
machine's failure clock, far enough to be a true expedition.

### Why adults cannot simply solve it

Two separate, compounding reasons, both institutional rather than a plot
contrivance:

1. **Every ordinary defender who could lead it is already spoken for.** Meridian's
   registered defenders — the people who can actually hold a road shut —
   are stretched thin covering the worsening Sag line itself (this is the
   book's own opening pressure). Pulling one of them off the wall for a
   week to escort a salvage run means a week of the Sag going unheld, which
   the Director's office will not authorize under any argument.
2. **The site selects for a very rare team, not for youth.** Caul Hill's
   radiation cannot be endured by general toughness or fast healing. Sustain
   can recall the *active burden* from tissue, but it cannot undo old damage
   after that damage has fixed. Rook and Jab can do the work; the Homura
   cross-medic understands how to direct it, rotate healers, and increase the
   amount of ash a Kindled soul can safely hold and spend. Callie and Toren
   supply the Ward, Guard, and extraction capability that keep those healers
   alive. The party is selected by complementary doors and training, not by
   the premise that children tolerate radiation better than adults.

### What failure costs

If the part is not recovered, Senna's and (per the same shed, same cycle)
every other patient's course begins to fail within roughly eleven days — the
same interval already established as the treatment's own clock (Ch. 27–28).
Failure is not abstract: it is a countable number of cycles before Senna's
marrow support lapses, stated on the page the way Pell states a distance.
Secondary cost: if the mission is caught leaving without authorization, the
whole clan's residency review is jeopardized, which threatens every household
riding on it (Callie's, Jab's, the Hobb's Wall clan's).

### How it joins the Sag / residency / ash / defender threads

The mission is staffed, provisioned, and made survivable using everything the
book's other threads have taught. The team carries large stores of lifted,
graded ash as fuel, not as trade cargo and not as a lucky find. Toren's Guard
and Callie's widened Ward protect the Sustain rotation. Jab senses changes in
the bodies under his care and learns to recall radiation deliberately. Rook
teaches him the lived technique; `[HOMURA MEDIC]` teaches the repeatable
capacity discipline: take ash in, settle it without confusing it for personal
reserve, spend it through Sustain, vent the harmful load, and refill before
the soul bottoms out. The defender cohort covers the gap at Meridian while
the expedition is away. The ash secured at the rip then becomes the material
proof that the team can protect Meridian and alter the exploitative residency
economy, rather than simply argue against it.

## 6. Youth-defender cohort design

- **Purpose:** the Sag is worsening faster than Meridian's adult defenders
  can cover, and the station's own liability doctrine (registered Kindled
  residents are sorted into labor by door) already pulls Kindled teenagers
  toward dangerous work informally. The cohort formalizes that pull into
  something with adult oversight, rather than pretending it isn't happening.
- **Membership logic:** not "every Kindled kid at Meridian" — a small,
  specific group built from need and consent: Callie, Jab (if his household's
  situation allows; his instinct to keep working through the Elder's ethic
  makes this an easy yes for him and a harder one for the book to justify
  given Tonk), Toren, plus willing members of the Hobb's Wall clan (Dessa,
  Wyck) and at least one Meridian-native trainee to keep the cohort from
  reading as "the Book One cast plus scenery." Recommend one new named
  Meridian local, invented at drafting, who has no Book One history and
  therefore reacts to everything (the ash economy, the found-family's
  closeness, Rook's method) the way a reader coming in cold might.
- **Adult safeguards:** `[HOMURA MEDIC]` oversees formal reserve and Sustain drills; Rook
  oversees live Sag-line rotations; no cohort member holds a line alone
  without an adult in immediate support distance; injuries and overdraws are
  logged and reviewed, mirroring the station's own paperwork culture (a
  believable, in-world safeguard rather than an authorial promise the plot
  ignores).
- **Training progression:** shadowing → supervised jar-and-sack work behind
  an adult's held line → controlled short holds under direct supervision →
  full rotations with an adult in support range → the Caul Hill mission as
  the first expedition beyond Meridian's support perimeter. It is not an
  unsupervised youth test: Rook and `[HOMURA MEDIC]` travel, while Katori and
  Pell divide field/navigation coaching and the defense of the people left
  behind.
- **Moral pressure:** the book should not resolve whether this is right. The
  Director's office is correct that the Sag needs more hands than it has;
  Pell and Senna's mother-thread (Callie's own mother's "you don't get to
  spend yourself on me") are correct that this trades children's bodies for
  a shortfall adults created. Both positions stay true at once. The named
  residency-injustice case (§3, §11) should be a cohort member's family —
  someone who took the ash-lifting-equivalent contract without a found
  family able to absorb the cost, exactly as both prior drafts already
  recommend — so the moral weight lands on a specific person, not a policy
  debate.
- **Avoiding the faceless-academy trap:** no uniforms, no ranked class
  structure, no shared dormitory-and-bell-schedule texture. Training happens
  in the same huts, the same stove-side conversations, and the same north
  yard the rest of the book already lives in. Every cohort member keeps a
  private want unrelated to combat (Callie's counting, Jab's caregiving,
  Toren's leadership anxiety, and whatever the new local recruit wants that
  has nothing to do with the Sag at all).

## 7. Ash-learning ladder

Grounded directly in the drafted Rook ash-collection scene
(`book-01-tallow-gate/book-02-drafts/movement-01-ash-collection.md`) and the
Ch. 27–28 / Ch. 18 "the jar" material — this ladder should be taught through
exactly that kind of scene, not through explanation.

1. **Recognition and collection.** Reading a drift's Yield by eye and feel
   before touching it (Flask vs. Handful, "a fair one" vs. "a dirty one," per
   the ash-collection draft); the flat-palm technique, working a span at a
   time, holding it low and open rather than closed. Failure mode: hauling —
   trying to pull ash faster than it comes, which yields less, not more.
   Teaching scene: Rook's drafted scene itself, adapted to include a cohort
   member learning alongside Wyck and Dessa rather than only overheard.
2. **Grading.** Handful / Flask / Barrel / Well (`CANON.md`, Yield) as a
   trade vocabulary, learned first as an insult-proof way to size up danger
   ("Handful or Flask?"), then as the literal unit the weighbridge pays in.
   Teaching scene: a weighbridge dispute (Ch. 18's "that's thinner than it
   ought to come" beat is the model) where a cohort member's read is
   challenged and has to be defended with evidence, not confidence.
3. **Cleaning and contamination.** Silt, ditch-water, and "a dirty Handful"
   as concrete hazards — contaminated ash burns unreliably and is priced
   down, or in the worst case damages whoever spends it (a training-safe
   version of an overdraw, introduced here in miniature). Teaching scene: a
   cohort member's first real weighbridge failure, graded down publicly, and
   having to learn why rather than being told the rule.
4. **Storage and carriage.** Jars, sacks, flasks, folded canvas between them
   (all attested in Ch. 18, 27, and the ash-collection draft); why quantity
   and grade both degrade under bad handling, and why a station's economy
   treats a jar of clean Flask as worth defending on the road.
5. **Taking it in — ash held by the soul.** Ash is ownerless, unlike a
   person's expended reserve. Skin-contact absorption is deliberate and
   unhurried: the same flat-palm stillness used to collect it, turned inward.
   Ash does not become a second organ or named storage tank; the soul holds
   it as usable reserve. Rushing intake causes spill, nausea, false fullness,
   or a lock that prevents clean spending.
6. **Increasing usable working depth.** `[HOMURA MEDIC]` teaches that a
   Kindled soul does not become metaphysically larger in a week; the person
   learns to use more of the depth already present. Intake, settle, spend,
   vent, rest, repeat. Capacity is measured by clean cycles and recovery, not
   by how much ash someone can force in once. This is the training that makes
   the Caul Hill reserve practical instead of merely heavy cargo.
7. **Radiation recall through Sustain.** Rook teaches Jab to feel the active
   harmful burden in a body without confusing it with old injury. Sustain
   draws or recalls that burden while ash pays for the work. The technique
   prevents further damage during exposure; it does not erase damage already
   fixed in tissue and therefore does not retroactively cure Senna. The
   healer must vent the recalled burden and rotate out before it begins to
   lodge in the healer instead.
8. **Spending through a door under pressure.** Absorbed ash can power Edge,
   Stride, Ward, Sustain, or Resilience without first emptying personal
   reserve. Teaching scene: a controlled Sag-line failure where ash-fuel,
   not personal endurance, is the difference between holding and breaking.
9. **Advanced team use and overdraw.** The expedition uses a measured
   rotation rather than a vague shared pool: one person works, one holds the
   reserve, one watches for spill and overdraw. Spending past safe working
   depth causes visible damage even when ash remains available. This is why
   the party needs stores, trained bodies, and discipline rather than one
   miraculous healer.

## 9. Three substantial action sequences

1. **The Sag-line failure (Movement 2).** Tactical problem: a held line under
   direct, close-range pressure, where the danger is a lapse of seconds, not
   raw threat scale — the same problem the ash-collection draft and Ch. 27–28
   already establish. What's different here from Book One's fights: this is
   *training* breaking in real time, not a road ambush; the tension is
   "who's watching whom," and the cost should land on the cohort, making the
   danger of the youth-defender program real rather than theoretical.
2. **The rip-road approach (Movement 4).** Tactical problem: an advancing
   party has to protect its own healers while every pause consumes ash. The
   threat is not only attack; it is being delayed until the Sustain budget
   fails. Callie's moving Ward, Toren's Guard, and Jab's recall first become a
   repeatable team cycle here.
3. **The Caul Hill Sustain-echo (Movement 5).** Tactical problem: attrition
   helps the enemy. The new Riftspawn recalls loose ash and uses it to repair
   itself and nearby spawn. Callie must isolate the current, Toren must make a
   stable physical opening, and Jab must sense and interrupt the renewal
   pulse. The adults keep the radiation lane survivable, but the three

## 13. Recommended final book length

**Recommend approximately 155,000–170,000 words.** Reasoning: Book One ran
roughly 200,000–212,000 words across ten movements and three geographically
separate roads that needed independent establishment. Book Two is
structurally denser but geographically simpler — one location (Meridian) plus
one outward arc — across six movements rather than ten. Using this project's
own established chapter-length convention (roughly 3,500–4,500 words per
chapter, drawn from the existing movement tables in `COUNTDOWN_WEAVE.md` and
both prior Book 2 drafts) and six movements of 6–8 chapters each (roughly
40–44 chapters total), the book lands naturally in this range without padding
the institutional material or shortchanging the three protagonists' training
and combat space the owner has ruled must stay the engine.
