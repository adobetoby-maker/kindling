# Movement Five, ensemble revision (ch 33–40): O'Connor review

**Reviewer status: this is a fresh-context simulated cold read. It is not independent and it is not a human read.** I'm Claude Opus 5.5, the same model that produced this revision in an earlier session. That drafting conversation isn't in my context. This session's startup memory did inject one-line summaries of it: the ch 33 opening was compressed, some group pronouns in ch 34, 36 and 39 were replaced with names, and a continuity ledger and author report were filed. I didn't open any of those files. Where this review agrees with the revision's choices, treat that with some suspicion, because we probably share blind spots.

**Coverage:** I read all eight supplied chapters (33–40) in order, and the movement is complete. No canon bible, plan, earlier movements or pre-revision edition was supplied, so every canon finding below is based only on evidence inside the movement.

## Reader response (unscored)

My interest caught on the first page, when Toren's silent headcount stops at Rook. It caught again at several later points:
- the gate that won't open when pulled but opens when pushed
- the gatehouse wall with eleven strokes and a twelfth abandoned halfway down
- the grey climbing the corridor wall into the tray

Ch 35 is where I stopped being able to put the book down. Jab puts his palm on the rope, "It thought he was a hole in it," he hears Tonk's "*Don't be asleep*," and Dee takes his wrist. Ch 36 has the best piece of physical logic in the movement: Toren alone at a door that opens toward him, which means that from the other side it pushes open.

My interest slipped in three places: the pump-house council in ch 33, the fireside arithmetic in ch 37 (though the pump analogy pulls it back up), and the pricing at the cart in ch 40.

Jab is the character who matters most, because he's the only one whose gift is also the danger. Callie is close behind, standing with her back to the room. I expect the wash to be crossed in pieces with Rook's last fever powder in hand, racing Senna's 54th, and I expect the Director to react to a sky that lost its count when the lifter stopped. I would keep reading.

## Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge, disclosures

**What holds (checked inside the movement):**
- **Fence ash ledger:** 40 going in → 32 when jar one runs dry → 29 back at the pump house, "One over today's line" of 28 → 25¼ after settling → 24 when jar three opens ("Eight above the line") → 16 at "That's the line" → 13 after jar four's three Handfuls under the line. Every figure spoken aloud reconciles.
- **The lifter's ash:** 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 dirty + Toren's half jar (4) = 32 lifted → 6 spent drawing everyone at the river → "twenty-two, clean. Four, dirty" → 1½ spent on Dee overnight → "twenty and a half clean."
- **Containers:**
  - Five vent jars: two left by the chalk-cross stone on the 47th, three left under the hill on the 48th.
  - The frame goes from "three full… three empties and a broken socket" to four empties, then five empties to lift into.
- **Rook's fever powders:** the first on the 47th, the second on the 48th, the third on the 49th, then "One left."
- **Stair geometry, both directions:** going down it's thirty steps east, a landing, then twenty steps west, arriving at the bottom facing west. Going up it's "twenty steps' worth… to the landing, and thirty more."
- **Bodies:**
  - Toren is one-handed throughout.
  - Callie's left ribs, which she walked into on the 44th, crack on her own fixed conduit ("the same two").
  - Jab's right palm heel turns white, then blisters, and he learns to draw "round it."
  - Pell never stands.
  - Dee's left wrist stays crooked.
- **Power limits:**
  - Struts cost by the breath, two at once cost double, and Toren stays "soft", never locked.
  - "I'm at my floor. I'm stopping." is honored by Toren at the landing, Callie at the door, and Dee, who stops drawing.
  - The fixity of Callie's Ward is what cracks her ribs, so a limit becomes a cost.
- **Rules and the Director's brief:**
  - Nobody sits (knees are policed twice), and the boxes are moved only by tongs and strut.
  - Red means everybody out, obeyed on the 47th. A healer reaches her floor once. The line is crossed once, named out loud, and set down for report.
  - The sky is counted, the drum is left alone, only the first door is used on day two, two boxes are taken, the open third box is left, and the three home jars are untouched.
- **Knowledge and disclosure:**
  - Characters hedge their guesses ("Maybe"; "I don't know which").
  - The drum, the other rooms and the nature of the sky all stay closed.
  - The one new disclosure (the sky lost its count when the lifter stopped) is observed, not explained.

**Apparent inconsistencies** (these rest on movement evidence only; none are verified against canon):
1. **Ch 35 counter reading.** "'Forty,' she said. 'At a hand. It's hot.'" The room around it had just read "A hundred… near the corner" (ch 34) and "A hundred and ten… At the door" (ch 35). Everywhere else in the movement, a source reads above its surroundings: a live box reads 110 in a room of 65–70, and the tray reads 70 over a floor of 60. I'm highly confident this breaks the movement's own counter logic, but I haven't checked it against counter canon.
2. **Departure day.** Ch 33 says the carrier had "ridden for seven days" (on the 47th), and ch 40 says home had been "breathing without her for eight days" (on the 49th). These put the departure one day apart unless the two counts use different conventions. Low confidence; check the canon departure date.
3. **Bridge geometry (ch 33).** "the sledge was as wide as all three planks together" doesn't fit "Jab walked on the outside plank with the frame": he has to be ahead of the sledge, not beside it. Low stakes.
4. **Words with two meanings by ear.** These are not contradictions, just collisions when read aloud:
   - "turn": one turn of the glass in ch 38, but twenty of them in ch 39's "one working turn".
   - "the room": the counter's background reading, and also the literal room; both appear in ch 40's first pages.
   - "lifters": Rook's human crew in ch 37, one chapter after the creature is named "the lifter".
   - "the stair's ash": used in ch 33, one chapter before "the stair" becomes Caul Hill's stair.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1: in ch 33, let the pump-house council speak decisions, not inventory**
- **Location:** ch 33, from "Callie said the ash first." through "Toren wrote it on the back of the paper under the rest."; also the sentence "They went in at noon."
- **Observed:**
  - Before Toren makes his call, Callie recites the whole frame: five whole jars, A and C empty, the road jar, five vent jars, one socket, and three settled Handfuls.
  - She also recites the superseded plan ("forty-eight for five people… The rooms were thirty").
  - This follows the morning's coat, floor and hook readings, so most of the chapter is accounting before they reach the gate.
  - "They went in at noon." then jumps ahead of the meal that comes before it.
- **Effect:** This is where Reader A is most likely to stop. Heard aloud, more than a dozen numbers in a row bury the four that actually decide anything: forty inside, sixteen the line, twelve today, and twenty-eight as today's line.
- **Scope:** This scene only, at sentence level, roughly 80–150 words net:
  - Keep those four numbers and the rules in dialogue.
  - Move the empty jars and the vent-jar count into one line of narration; ch 37's "the last three vent jars" and ch 40's grading depend on that count.
  - Replace the 48/30 figures with what they mean ("priced for five with three healers; it's four with two").
  - Move "They went in at noon." below the eating paragraph.
  - Leave the morning readings alone, because they calibrate the counter for everything that follows.
- **Preserve:** Toren's "Twelve… Half."; "Twenty-eight's today's line… Sixteen's the line. Both."; "If a healer's lost, everybody out" (it pays off in ch 39); and Rook's "Then I don't come in."

**Priority 2: let Callie's realization arrive once, after the door (ch 37 → ch 40)**
- **Location:**
  - ch 37, from "She understood it now, with her palm on the paper." through "She would be holding the door so that they could carry it."
  - ch 40, from "She had not carried them." through "where she could not see them do it."
- **Observed:** The insight is complete the night before the fight, then stated again after it in almost the same words: "so that the three people behind her could do what only they could do, where she could not see them do it" against "so that they could do the thing only they could do, where she could not see them do it."
- **Effect:** Reader B hears the payoff before it's earned and then hears it again. The movement's best image of the idea, the single look back and Jab's "Busy" in ch 38, arrives already explained.
- **Scope:** Roughly 60–120 words across both chapters:
  - End ch 37 on her fear and the open question: she has always held what she could see, and tomorrow she'll be looking at a corridor. Keep Dee's line, "who's worth the lee", and her mother's fingers opening one at a time.
  - In ch 40, cut the echoed passage and keep what's new: "holding the edge of them", "choosing what to hold", and the ache in the palm.
  - If the echo is deliberate, reword the ch 40 version so it reads as growth rather than repetition.
- **Preserve:** Rook's "You'll want to look. Don't." and "Once. Everybody does."; the look and "Busy"; and the look back from the cut in ch 40.

**Priority 3: numbers and terms that survive being read aloud**
- **Location and fix:**
  - (a) ch 35, "'Forty,' she said…": raise the number above the room's reading, or move the reading to the pump house.
  - (b) ch 39, "one working turn": say "twenty turns of the glass" instead.
  - (c) ch 40, first pages: keep "the room" as the counter term, and name the literal room ("that room", "the room under the hill").
  - (d) ch 34, "She's been looking at that line for five weeks": three people were just named, so name her, unless the ambiguity is deliberate.
  - (e) ch 36, the passage "She had not said *come back without it*…": add one clause saying what that line meant to Toren, because it depends on a scene off the page.
- **Observed:** One number breaks the counter's own rule, and four phrases change meaning when heard.
- **Effect:** The movement teaches readers to trust the counter and the ledger, and one off-scale reading spends that trust. A listener also can't see capital letters or italics.
- **Scope:** Five sentences, under 60 words net.
- **Preserve:** Callie's flat, number-reading voice.

**Don't cut:** the fight, from ch 37's "Ten… Go." through ch 39's "Three. Nothing." Its length is earned, and the owner asked for developed action. None of the priorities above touch it.

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- A realization lands hardest once, after the act that earns it. Priority 2 only removes text: ch 37 keeps the fear, ch 40 keeps the meaning, and the ch 38 look-back does the work in between.
- Priority 3 matters more than its size suggests, because the ledger reconciles to the quarter-Handful everywhere else. When a book is that exact, one off-scale number reads as an error rather than as texture.

## Uncertainty and tradeoffs
- **Shared blind spots:** One I can see but may be under-weighting is the pattern "It was not X. It was Y.", which recurs more than a dozen times. Examples: "It was not dust lying in a trough. It was a rope" (ch 34), "It was not hot sand. It was hot water" (ch 35), and "But it was not a husk. It was all of it." (ch 39). Early on it makes the lifter usefully strange; by ch 39–40 it's an audible rhythm. A human ear should decide whether to thin it.
- **The two readers are simulations:** One real young reader trying ch 33 and ch 37 would settle Priority 1 faster than further review.
- **Priority 1 trades completeness for pace:** It gives up a complete spoken ledger to gain pace. If the recitals are a deliberate signature of the series, keep them and make only the "noon" reorder.
- **No comparison was possible:** The pre-revision edition wasn't supplied, so nothing here says whether this revision improves on it. One movement also can't show which model or seat is better across the series.
