## 7. Unresolved questions

1. **The Director's wording: the brief conflicts with ch 21.**
   - **The conflict.** The brief asked for ch 32's "do what you have to and you come home", and staged chs 33 and 36 now use it. But ch 32:543 is Dee *quoting* the Director. The Director's own words, spoken on D+38 in ch 21:449, are "you do what you have to **do** and you come home". Canonical chs 33:289 and 36:343 follow ch 21. Toren learned the ch 21 speech by heart.
   - **Options.**
     - (a) Keep the repair. The staged text then matches ch 32, but not ch 21 or canonical 33/36.
     - (b) Restore "have to do" at staged 33:257 and 36:315. The staged text then matches ch 21 and canon, and ch 32:543 is the one line to correct at a later canon pass.
   - **Recommendation: (b).** Those are the words the Director said. It is a one-word change in each of two lines. I did not make it, because the brief named ch 32 explicitly.
   - **Related, and unchanged:** Toren's ch 33 recitation abridges ch 21. It omits "and bring me the count" and "You're going to lift at the edge of it if the ledger says you have to lift." Canonical ch 33 abridges the same way.
2. **Toren's stone capacity.**
   - **The question.** Is "Stone's full" a capacity (one Handful), or Dee's word for "everything you settled is still there"?
   - **Current state.** The checkpoint binds a stone of 1½. The page now says that is half as much again as he has ever carried.
   - **If it is a capacity.** The checkpoint's 1½ needs an owner ruling. Ch 41:53 ("Stone's full" after he settles one Handful) would then imply the stone shrank back.
3. **The pump's age.**
   - **What I did.** Removed it, under the brief's rule that the checkpoint must establish it.
   - **Case for reinstating.** Canonical ch 37:9 has "seventy years", and canon consistently uses seventy years for pre-Fall works. So it would date the pump house to before the Fall, while the sky over the hill reads as twenty years.
   - **Owner choice.** Reinstate it and add it to the checkpoint, or keep the pump undated.
4. **Noticed outside the three priorities and not changed.** These are inherited from canon unless marked.
   - **Ensemble-introduced:** ch 33:459, "Somebody laughed on the far bank, and it was Rook." Rook is on the pump-house bank, but 33:11 and 33:169 use "the far bank" for the fence side. The cold read flagged the switch. Canonical 33:505 has only "Somebody laughed. It was Rook." Dropping "on the far bank" would fix it.
   - Ch 36:137, "two turns and not the bit". This is consistent with Dee's dawn line: "The bit'll come back by noon, if you don't spend it before" (33:115). He most likely spent it. This is editorial review item 5.
   - Ch 33:129, "It's the stair's ash". Everywhere else in this movement "the stair" is the yellow stair, which they have not yet entered, so a listener may hear that one.
   - Ch 40:113, Dee's "at a hundred and thirty" for the lifting window. Ch 39 reads 140 when the lifter fell and 110 at the fifth turn, and falling.
   - Ch 33:427–451, bridge geometry. Jab walks "on the outside plank" while the sledge is "as wide as all three planks". It works if he is ahead of the sledge by the rail, which "took the front rope" implies.
   - "Turn" still has three senses: a turn of the glass, a bend of the road (*Turn*), and own measured in turns. The one clash the cold read found (ch 39) is fixed.
   - The "It was not X. It was Y." pattern and the repeated "the bottom of a pond" (cold read). Formula drift (author report §7). The first draft's own editorial questions (author report §8).
