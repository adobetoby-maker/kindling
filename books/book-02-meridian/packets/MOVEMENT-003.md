## What this movement is for

Turn the idea of a Caul Hill mission into a measured operation that can plausibly
leave Meridian. The Director initially refuses it because Meridian cannot spare its
only holder and because the proposed party cannot yet state an ash budget, exposure
window, healer rotation, abort line, or home-defense plan. This refusal is practical,
not villainous. Rook and Dee prepare the evidence anyway: one teaches the bodily
feel of Sustain, the other teaches reproducible capacity and stopping.

The movement's pleasure engine is controlled improvement. Use attempts, bodies,
objects, disagreements, and consequences to dramatize ash ladder stages 5–9:

1. **Taking ash in.** Skin-contact absorption is deliberate and unhurried. The soul
   holds it as usable reserve; there is no second organ or game inventory. Rushing
   creates spill, nausea, false fullness, or a lock that prevents clean spending.
2. **Working depth.** Intake, settle, spend, vent, rest, repeat. A soul does not grow
   metaphysically larger in a week. Usable depth means clean repeatable cycles and
   recovery, not forcing in the largest possible quantity once.
3. **Radiation recall.** Sustain senses and draws only the active harmful burden in
   a living body while ash pays for separation. It cannot reverse fixed damage,
   regrow marrow, cure Tilda, straighten Tonk's leg, or undo Senna's old injury.
   Physical decontamination and ordinary medicine still matter.
4. **Venting and rotation.** A practitioner must vent the recalled burden before it
   lodges in them. One works, one holds reserve/support, and one watches the clock,
   symptoms, spill, and abort line. Continuing past safe depth makes the healer the
   next patient even if ash remains in the room.
5. **Spending through a door.** Settled ash can fund a known door without first
   emptying personal reserve. Callie, Jab and Toren should each discover one useful
   economy or constraint through practice, not receive a generic power increase.

Jab is the movement's progression center. He has to accept instruction from the
same institution that is trying to put a labor claim around his gift. Let him set
terms: Tonk and his household are his boundary; learning how not to vanish into a
patient is not consent to belong to the register. The Director may record the
value of his ability, but she cannot own it.

Rook must admit that Homura had a repeatable capacity method he never fully learned.
Dee must admit that protocol without Rook's relational feel cannot teach Jab what
to find in a body. Their disagreement should produce a better method, not a winner.

## The other two progression lines

Keep all three protagonists learning together often enough that their Ember remains
visibly personal:

- **Toren:** Jab coaches edge-light economy—lighting only the useful section of the
  crooked spike and holding it through impact. Toren continues building Guard from
  Stride: from the ground up, soft behind, structural rather than Ward. This movement
  may achieve a repeatable braced hold under controlled load, but the full moving
  field payoff belongs later. Do not straighten his shape or erase the late left
  strut that taught him the correction.
- **Callie:** refine the long hooked Edge she already owns and practise a deliberate
  Ward-to-Edge handoff. She cannot run full Ward and full Edge everywhere at once.
  The work is knowing which load to hold, when to let it transfer, and where to cut.
  Do not give her a new straight blade or group-scale mastery early.
- Use at least one practical combined drill in which Jab's economy, Callie's handoff,
  and Toren's brace solve different parts of one changing load. Let a mistake alter
  the next attempt rather than handing everyone immediate success.

## Controlled exposure becoming a real emergency

The movement's substantial action/emergency sequence is not a fight and must not be
a contrived child test. Meridian already possesses failing calibration hardware and
an unnamed grey-haired technician established in Movement Two. A plausible route is
a low-source calibration piece in an old shielded housing, brought under Dee,
Sowerby and the technician's control so Jab can learn what an active burden feels
like at the edge of safety. The author owns the exact hardware and staging, but it
must fit the post-Fall world and ordinary contamination practice.

The control fails for a physical, planted reason—old seal, cracked housing, dropped
carrier, contaminated worker, or comparable failure. It exposes an adult/technician,
not a disposable unnamed child. Establish positions, shielding, handling rules,
available ash, who watches time, who can touch whom, and the abort line before the
failure. Then let the emergency change the plan.

Jab and Rook perform the first paired radiation recall. Dee controls the rotation
and vent; Sowerby/ordinary medicine handles contamination and injury. Callie and
Toren solve necessary physical containment/access problems within their present
limits, rather than standing aside. The result proves recall works and also proves
an improvised trip will run out of clean ash. Nobody is miraculously cured. The
exposed person survives with a specific, carried consequence.

This sequence should be developed enough for comprehension, fear, adaptation,
cost, and recovery. It is not the Caul Hill climax and should not imitate a monster
fight.

## Medical clocks and departure heartstrings

Patients continue to live through the preparation; they are not props waiting at
the start and finish.

- **Tonk:** give him one small, medically credible independent movement after the
  trapeze-bar gain—transfer partway to a chair, pull himself upright, or another
  effort that leaves the frame and damaged leg true. He remains four: funny, angry,
  frightened, and literal. At departure he assumes the old carrying frame means he
  can go. The answer is no. He releases Jab's end of the cord by his own choice and
  entrusts the wooden fish or another already-established object as a promise to
  return. He must not be carried to Caul Hill.
- **Tilda:** show at least one clear morning and then a bad one close enough to
  departure that Callie means to stay. Tilda, lucid and acting with authority,
  refuses to be made the reason Callie abandons the mission that keeps the shed
  functioning. Return her `stop holding` lesson without turning it into a speech or
  surrendering her right not to hear the prognosis number.
- **Senna:** D+32's cycle happens. Show a concrete loss of hand/foot function and a
  concrete gain in institutional authority. She builds or dictates the first route
  and exposure ledger for Caul Hill, supplies a detail or existing object only she
  could provide, and names that this is a road she can plan but never walk.
- **Rook:** ability returns by functions—rise, short hold, one rotation, paired
  recall—not by reset. His reduced margin must appear in the mission budget.
- **Dessa and Wyck:** staying at Meridian is a necessary home command, not being
  discarded. Dessa's record/observer work and Wyck's body-discipline lane are the
  beginning of a durable yard. Keep their injuries real.

Intercut or otherwise connect the two hardest releases: Tonk letting Jab leave and
Tilda sending Callie. Toren witnesses enough to understand that leading means
returning people to the beds and rooms that made them go. Do not kill a patient to
manufacture permission.

## Where we leave pressure

Leave Meridian with a measured expedition crossing the gate or poised to cross it,
carrying finite ash and specific promises. Radiation recall has worked once under
controlled conditions and nearly cost too much. Jab has terms rather than ownership,
Callie has chosen trust over holding everything herself, and Toren has begun to
understand leadership as a return obligation. The people staying behind are active
participants with home-line work. The road, the rip, and Caul Hill remain ahead.

The final feeling should be departure bought by preparation and love—not a dry
mission briefing and not children running away because adults would not listen.
