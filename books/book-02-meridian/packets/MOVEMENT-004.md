## Where we enter — the next moments

Begin on the old north road immediately after the final line of Chapter 24, on the
morning of D+41. The travelling party is Pell with Duchess and the cart, Dee, Rook,
Jab, Callie and Toren. Do not skip straight to Caul Hill. Six days of exposed road
are part of the price, the teaching ground, and the relationship work.

Read Chapters 17–24 in full and use `provenance/MOVEMENT-003-CONTINUITY.md` as the
binding state ledger. Consult earlier chapters only to verify a fact. The movement
must carry forward, visibly:

- the 12-Flask loan: 8 on Jab's rebuilt frame and 4 on the cart; one cart Flask is
  for the road and three are the untouchable route-home reserve;
- the inside turn line of two Flasks, the clean/dirty separation, twenty-minute
  healer rotations, Rook's half-turns, and the rule that any early threat shortens
  the work rather than moving the abort line;
- Rook's healing back wound and reduced margin, Callie's healing ribs, Toren's
  newly unsplinted but weak left forearm and leather cuff, and Jab's faint palm
  redness after the pen;
- Little buttoned over Jab's left chest; Tonk keeps both ends of the cord;
- Callie's second counter and its sticky red needle; Dee's brass glass and book;
  Pell's atlas and route sheet; Toren's copied plate, signed paper, and stone; the
  empty lead carrier on the cart;
- the machine clock: D+43's cycle can be improvised left-handed, but the next cycle
  around D+54 needs the Caul Hill boxes; expected return is no earlier than D+53;
- the Director's narrow permission: retrieve the live boxes with the wheel, count
  the sky, lift only at the rip's edge if required, do not enter the rip or go

- all medical and residency clocks held at Meridian. These remain emotionally
  present without cutting away into remote omniscient scenes.

The opening should let departure have an aftertaste. They have crossed the Sag,
but are not yet practiced as a travelling unit. Establish walking order, cart pace,
rest cadence, sleeping protection, who reads the route, who reads exposure, and how
the ash is counted without turning the scene into a briefing recap.

## What this movement is for

Make the road itself teach the first reliable three-person field cycle. Every delay
has two prices: time against Meridian's machine and ash spent keeping exposed bodies
safe. Progression must arise from actual road problems, practice under fatigue, and
one substantial approach fight—not abstract lessons.

The movement should produce these earned advances:

1. **Callie — moving Ward.** Her hook already carries a personal Ward and she can
   hand it to Edge with a two- or three-count gap. On the road she learns to shape a
   narrow moving protection around other bodies for short intervals, choosing who
   and what is inside. It is not a group dome, not effortless, and not permanent.
   Direction, footing, speed and settled ash matter. Her final success should be
   materially necessary in the approach fight.
2. **Toren — reproducible Guard.** Build from the ground up: heel light, crooked
   spike/strut, soft behind, structural rather than a second Ward. He must learn to
   place and renew it under changing ground and impact. Preserve the late left strut
   that taught the correction. His weak left forearm limits leverage. By the end he
   can reproduce a useful moving sequence, not sustain an unlimited wall.
3. **Jab — short recall cycles.** He learns to identify active radiation burden,
   perform a small clean recall, vent, accept the watch's check, and return to usable
   reserve without disappearing into the patient. He does not reverse fixed damage
   or cure injuries. Let him struggle with the difference between helping now and
   saving capacity for everyone later.
4. **The adults — coaching without replacement.** Dee owns protocol and the clock;
   Rook owns relational feel and field judgment; Pell owns route, cart and physical
   road sense. They coach, argue, and take costs. They do not solve the young leads'
   decisive tactical problem for them.

Use repeated attempts sparingly and cumulatively: each try must change the next.
Let the party discover a compact call-and-response vocabulary for the three-part
cycle in action, but do not turn the prose into game UI or named combo attacks.

## The six-day road

Treat distance, weather, food, sleep, water, horse care, cart geometry, and damaged
infrastructure as real. Senna's route is good but twenty years old. The old north
road lasts about two days; the iron-post line runs west toward Caul Hill. The party
cannot know every washout, settlement ruin, contamination pocket, or post-Fall
change in advance.

The exact scene plan belongs to the author, but the road should include:

- a first-day adjustment where the weight and walking order prove different from
  the yard plan;
- a night or enclosed-rest problem that forces them to define a defensible camp
  without spending the ash meant for the plant;
- at least one route choice where Pell's map, Callie's ground reading, Toren's count,
  and Jab's bodily sense offer different kinds of evidence;
- one smaller exposure or injury before the major fight, giving Jab a chance to run
  a clean short cycle and proving that even a success has an ash cost;
- a road-visible reminder of who is waiting in Meridian, carried through objects,
  habits, promises or choices rather than repeated internal speeches.

Do not invent a convenient settlement with endless supplies. Human traces may be
encountered, but do not add a new continuing named character; the owner names
continuing cast. One-off unnamed travellers, remains, signs, shelters, or old records
may appear if they serve the road and remain one-off.

## Approach fight — the first full three-lead cycle

Before the party reaches the Caul Hill plant, combine three simultaneous hazards:

- rising active radiation or contaminated ash that makes time inside the area costly;
- unstable ground/road structure that threatens the cart, jars, or a trapped body;
- lesser rip spawn attacking in a way that delays movement and scatters loose ash.

Stage positions, exits, ash carried, counter readings, injuries and object movement
before acceleration. The reader must be able to understand where the cart, Duchess,
the adults and all three protagonists are. The action should run long enough for
failure, adaptation, cost, a tactical turn, and a comprehensible finish.

The solution requires all three young leads in sequence:

- Callie's moving Ward controls the radiation/ash lane and keeps selected bodies or
  the reserve moving without pretending she can cover everyone at full strength;
- Toren's Guard/Edge creates a stable physical route and holds an impact or collapse
  long enough for extraction;
- Jab performs bounded recalls on exposed people and recognizes when to stop, vent,
  rotate, or spend the contingency.

Rook and Dee keep the healing/rotation lane viable and Pell protects the cart/route,
but none may steal the three-protagonist solution. Keep the lesser spawn distinct
from the Movement Five primary creature. Ordinary damage can kill them; they do not
yet reconstitute on-page as the final enemy will.

At least one real injury must be carried forward. It cannot be erased by Sustain.
The fight must consume enough ash that the original inside plan is no longer intact.
Do not spend the three home Flasks casually. If the contingency is opened, state the
arithmetic and consequence clearly.

## The turn — what Jab hears in the rip

Near the end, after the fight has produced loose ash and the party can sense or see
the Caul Hill distortion, Jab perceives a rhythm in the new rip that resembles
Sustain: something at or beneath its center is recalling loose ash toward itself.
This is perception and dangerous inference, not a full creature explanation.

The evidence should be physical and shared enough that the others can act on it:
ash moving against wind or grade, the counter pattern, a repeated pull after damaged
spawn fall, or comparable observable effects. Jab may recognize the bodily logic,
but do not give him omniscient knowledge of the creature, Cinder, the disks, or the
rip's ultimate origin.

This discovery confirms the mission's central trap: the live boxes and the needed
ash sit inside the same tactical problem. There is no clean salvage-only route and
no safe way to finish the book's mission merely by walking around the rip.

## Human pressure and relationships

The road should deepen the group, not pause the story for bonding scenes.

- **Callie:** leaving Tilda was an act of trust, but distance makes holding harder,
  not easier. Let her leadership show through selection: what she protects, what she
  allows others to carry, and when she opens her hand.
- **Jab:** Tonk's fish is a promise, not a talisman that makes him fearless. His
  terms with Meridian matter whenever an adult tells him whom to heal or when to
  stop. Give him at least one no that is respected and one yes that costs him.
- **Toren:** `Everybody back` must become behavior. He tracks people and the way
  home, but he cannot reduce them to numbers. Let him make one leadership decision
  that preserves another person's agency rather than merely ordering them.
- **Rook and Dee:** their two teaching traditions should continue to irritate and
  improve each other. Rook's body must show that he is not reset. Dee's authority on
  the road is real, including authority over him.
- **Pell:** make his presence necessary through route judgment, Duchess, the cart,
  or a field decision. He is not scenery or a walking map.

No romance is required or assumed. Warmth, irritation, humor and fear should arise
from tasks and choices rather than banter pasted over danger.

## Where we leave pressure

End with the party at or near the Caul Hill fence/plant after the approach fight,
not with the central creature defeated. They have achieved their first genuine
three-protagonist field cycle, but the price makes the original plan obsolete:

- ash is below plan and the arithmetic is explicit;
- at least one injury must travel into Movement Five;
- the route home is threatened, damaged, contaminated, cut off, or newly expensive;
- the boxes and the rip are visibly the same tactical problem;
- Jab has recognized the Sustain-like recall rhythm;
- the party must decide how to approach Caul Hill with fewer clean options than the
  Director authorized.

The final feeling should be earned competence meeting a larger, stranger problem:
they are better than when they left Meridian, and therefore capable of understanding
how bad the next thing is.
