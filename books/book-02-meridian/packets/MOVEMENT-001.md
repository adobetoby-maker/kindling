## Where we enter

Book One has just ended on the north-road rescue. Jab has spent himself through
Sustain to bring Tonk back; Callie and Toren have helped get them to Meridian.
All three road parties are now inside or being admitted, but they are not yet
one household or one team. Their sick and injured people are alive, untreated
outcomes remain open, and residency is conditional.

Read `SOURCE_POOL.md` and every source it lists. Preserve the strongest existing
scenes, observations, humor, medical facts, and emotional payoffs, but reseam
them into one concurrent Meridian opening rather than stacking three separate
mini-endings. The rewritten chapters must stand as the canonical Book Two
opening; do not assume readers will consult the discarded source order.

## What this movement is for

Turn three arrivals into one shared human problem. Cross-cut the admissions,
declarations, treatment shed, diagnoses, surgery, residency pressure, and grief
for Milo so the reader experiences one place receiving all three roads at once.
Give Callie, Jab, and Toren full interior presence, distinct noticing, and a
meaningful causal handoff between viewpoints. The owner explicitly overrides
the O'Connor seat's normal 87/13 single-lead POV ratio for this series: these are
three co-protagonists. Rotate in developed scenes or chapters; do not head-hop.

The movement must establish, through dramatized scenes rather than summary:

- Tilda's inoperable left-side brain growth, palliative medicine, fits,
  right-side deficits, word failures, refusal of a prognosis number, and
  retained authority over her choices.
- Tonk's infected fracture, dead-bone removal, four pins and external bar,
  first infection clock, damaged growth plate, likely limp and later surgery;
  keep him four years old, funny, angry, frightened, and specific.
- Senna's active-radiation course, eleven-day lifelong marrow-support cycle,
  growing loss of sensation, and the likelihood she will lose walking without
  making her passive or cured.
- Rook's puncture wound and reduced margin, Dessa's leg, Wyck's collarbone,
  wrist and numb fingers, Toren's forearm/palm/leg, and Callie's ribs/head.
- The medicine/residency split, capability cards, conditional households,
  Rook's real name, his four-season ash-lifting debt, and the station's
  defensible but exploitative arithmetic.
- Callie and Toren's quiet first recognition, then a real connection among all
  three protagonists rooted in what each notices about the others' burdens.
- The first ash-lifting exposure using the supplied Rook scene as source. Let
  ash handling be concrete work, not lecture.
- The treatment shed's single machine as a fragile shared dependency. Its
  weakness should emerge naturally near the movement turn; do not announce the
  entire Caul Hill mission yet.

This is deliberately the book's quietest movement. Its action is urgent medical
and institutional choice. Do not bolt on a fight merely to satisfy an action
quota; the joined rescue immediately before the opening is still in every body.

- The three protagonists are Callie, Jab, and Toren. None is demoted to a

- No copied phrasing, names, scene order, or terminology from the Iron Prince

- Use the actual Book One closing and medical source facts. Do not reset the

- Keep prose clear for listening: repair ambiguous comma chains while writing;
  let thought turns become paragraph turns; preserve purposeful long sentences

## What the author owns

Own the eight chapter boundaries, scene selection, causal transitions,
viewpoint order within the owner-preferred Callie → Jab → Toren rotation,
dialogue, humor, local Meridian texture, and how the retained source scenes are
transformed. You may combine, split, shorten, expand, or rewrite source scenes
where needed for one coherent novel. Preserve their facts and strongest earned
moments, not their old chapter numbering.

Use spacious scenes. Let difficult conversations and procedures finish. Do not
summarize a diagnosis already dramatized well in the source merely to cover all
three strands. If output limits intervene, save only complete chapters and leave
a precise continuation checkpoint; continue the same movement and author run.

## Where we leave pressure

Leave all three households conditionally inside Meridian with the medical
baselines established and the first practical bonds among Callie, Jab, and
Toren formed. The treatment machine's failing calibration train becomes the
shared external clock. Rook has begun paying the ash-lifting debt. The reader
should understand why the north yard will pull all three protagonists together,
but deliberate cohort training has not begun.

The ending pressure is not a mission briefing. It is the realization that the
single machine holding several lives together is beginning to fail, and that
Meridian has no replacement on hand.
