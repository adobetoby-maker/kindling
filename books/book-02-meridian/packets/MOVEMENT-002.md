# Movement brief — The North Yard

- PWA book id: `kindled-book-02-meridian`
- Series id: `kindled`
- Author: Opus 5.5
- Seat: Monroe Jackson 1.3.0 / O'Connor
- Chapters: 9–16
- Intended movement scale: approximately 48,000–55,000 words. Preserve full
  scenes, instruction, recovery, humor, and the Sag-line action. Do not pad to
  reach a quota and do not compress later chapters to fit one response.
- POV: Callie, Jab, and Toren remain three co-protagonists. This movement may

## Where we enter

Movement One ends on D+10. Tonk's pin-site fever is being treated and the bone
looks clear only on a poor image. Senna has completed a slow, uncertain marrow
cycle and needs another at D+21. Tilda's medicine helps the headache but not the
growth or its right-side and language effects. Callie, Jab, Rook, and Toren face
the Director's office on D+13; Jab's separate review is D+14; Tonk's two-week
picture is D+16. The treatment machine is drifting, hand-corrected, and without
a spare. All three households remain conditional.

Read the eight canonical chapters together before writing forward. Carry every
body, clock, object, debt, promise, and knowledge boundary in
`provenance/MOVEMENT-001-CONTINUITY.md`. Chapter 8's final image—the single
yellow light waking slowly—is the movement seam. Do not recap Movement One in
summary; let consequences enter through what people have to do next.

## What this movement is for

Turn three conditionally admitted households into the beginnings of a defender
cohort and turn Meridian's north yard into a real teaching place. The station's
need and bureaucracy should force action, but the pleasure engine is learning:
three young people attempt the same practical problems through different doors,
compare results, fail visibly, correct one another, and begin to understand why
their differences form a team.

Use the D+13/D+14 reviews to establish terms, leverage, and consent. Meridian
recognizes Jab as a scarce medical recruit without owning him. Callie must not
be reduced to ground-reading labor; Toren must not inherit command merely
because he has led before. Rook, Katori, Pell, and Dee Wren disagree honestly
about safe training and institutional use. No adult is a cardboard villain.

Dee Wren is existing canon: the fourteen-year-old Homura survivor Rook walked
south for two months after the station fell eleven years ago, making Dee about
twenty-five now. Dee is the cross-medical practitioner the owner requested,
with access and control across more doors than the young protagonists. Do not
state an exact door count or complete personal history. The reunion with Rook
must carry the human weight of two people who survived the same destruction
and have not become the same kind of healer. Dee knows Homura's repeatable
capacity protocol; Rook knows embodied rescue under real pressure. Neither
invalidates the other.

The movement must dramatize rather than merely explain:

- The north yard's first three practical lanes: mixed-door field work,
  Resilience/body discipline, and Ward/Sustain medicine. Dessa's ledger and
  Senna's oral memory become working records, planting the future schools
  without naming a formal academy.
- Ash ladder stages 1–4: distinguish source-power from clean ash; measure
  personal reserve; absorb only a small known amount; let it settle without
  immediately spending it. Use objects, bodies, mistakes, and demonstrations.
- A same-obstacle/three-answers sequence. Jab forms the stable fish-knife metal
  and deliberately lights its working edge. Toren must retain his own ugly
  spike and attempt its light rather than copying Jab's shape. Callie's hook is
  already a curved blade expression; she works on her mobile personal Ward and
  its handoff to Edge rather than receiving an unearned straight sword.
- Toren's first deliberate attempt to hold Stride rigid as Guard. It fails, but
  leaves a concrete correction he can try again. Guard is structural Ember
  hardware angled to take a hit, not Ward and not a new door.
- Callie's first supervised attempt to widen her body-hugging Ward toward
  another person. Let the limitation be comprehensible and costly.
- Jab learning that his danger-sense, body support, and the warmth Tonk felt are
  one Sustain domain. Rook identifies the lived instinct; Dee names and tests
  the repeatable parts. This is recognition and early control, not mastery.
- Tonk's D+16 image and infection progression, Tilda's uneven response and at
  least one choice that preserves her authority, Senna's increasing loss of
  sensation and D+21 machine cycle, and honest recovery/setback for Rook, Wyck,
  Dessa, Toren, and Callie. No body resets between scenes.
- Warmth and humor inside the work: Tonk remains four; Senna remains sharp;
  Callie counts; Jab does arithmetic; Toren notices load and position. Let the
  young people enjoy some part of becoming capable.

## The Sag-line failure

The movement's substantial action sequence is a supervised hold that becomes a
real failure, not a contrived unsupervised youth test. Establish terrain,
starting positions, the held line, ash stores, adult support distance, the
cohort's assigned tasks, and what counts as success before the failure begins.
Then let the problem change.

The line breaks or folds under direct pressure. An adult or trainee suffers a
specific, trackable injury. The opposition adapts; the young people must also
adapt. Callie, Jab, and Toren each contribute through their distinct current
limits. Jab senses the injury before he sees it and stabilizes the casualty,
then empties himself because he has not yet learned ash-funded working depth.
His act saves time, not every consequence. The sequence should be developed,
spatially legible, and long enough for tactics, cost, fear, and correction to
change—not a short proof-of-power vignette.

Afterward, Rook identifies what Jab did as Sustain. Dee demonstrates that clean,
settled ash could have funded part of the work and kept the healer functional.
Meridian's interest in recruiting Jab hardens. The lesson is not that the child
should have spent more of himself; it is that skill, reserves, rotation, and an
abort line keep the healer from becoming the next patient.

## What must remain true / withheld

- Public byline is Monroe Jackson. O'Connor remains internal.
- The owner overrides the seat's normal 87/13 single-lead allocation: Callie,
  Jab, and Toren are co-protagonists.
- Sustain can recall an active radiological burden later, but this movement
  does not yet complete radiation-recall training. It cannot reverse fixed
  damage, destroyed marrow, Tilda's growth, Tonk's structural injury, or
  Senna's fixed nerve damage.
- Working depth increases safe use of ash already held by the soul. It is not
  rank, a second storage organ, or limitless capacity. Forced intake can spill,
  create false fullness, or cause overdraw.
- Do not launch the Caul Hill expedition in this movement. Let the failing
  machine, medical clocks, worsening Sag, Dee's knowledge, and the cost of the
  line failure make the combined mission necessary.
- Do not pay off Toren's Guard, Callie's group-scale Ward, Jab's radiation
  recall, the Sustain-echo creature, the disk clue, or Cinder's remnant early.
- Preserve Milo's death and the group's grief; do not use grief as a single
  speech and declare it resolved.
- Introduce no new continuing named character. An unnamed Meridian-native
  trainee may participate, but the owner names continuing cast.
- No romance is assumed. Do not force a triangle or commitment.
- No copied wording, names, scene order, or proprietary terms from the Iron
  Prince research. Apply only the selected abstract numerical craft formula.
- Prose must read cleanly aloud: clear referents, controlled long sentences,
  punctuation that exposes hierarchy, and thought-level paragraph turns.

## What the author owns

Own all eight chapter boundaries, the exact POV sequence, the shape of the
reviews, how Dee reaches or is found within Meridian, the reunion, the training
problems, dialogue, humor, tactics, and the specific Sag-line casualty. Develop
scenes rather than servicing a checklist. Plans describe the change; the author
discovers how people create it.

Write chapters 9–16 as one continuous creative run. Do not stop for per-chapter
editing or scoring. If output limits intervene, save complete chapters and a
precise continuation checkpoint, then resume the same Opus session and movement.

## Where we leave pressure

Leave the north yard physically established with three lanes and a first real
ledger. All three protagonists have attempted distinct progression and learned
something usable, but none has mastered the needed expedition capability. Jab
is publicly valuable and institutionally targeted after the Sag-line failure.
The medical and machine clocks are worse, not forgotten. Rook and Dee have shown
why the team needs both instinct and protocol. By the closing scene, the reader
should understand why a Caul Hill part run and ash-rich rip operation are becoming
one unavoidable mission, without ending on a dry mission briefing.
