## Where we enter

It is the end of the second month of 2681. Kiva Rowan, seventeen, seated and unclassified,
is on a coast boat with Team Seven, Healer Aske, and a fever just broken; Perrin has counted
the days off the shed door ("Sixteen"). **The gate fortnight at Saltmere begins Tuesday the
1st of the third month; the National Measure's first board is Monday the 14th.** Saltmere
itself has not been seen on the page. Team Seven is Kiva (catcher), Perrin Cade (captain,
carry, still door), Anwen Pryce (hold), Jude Talley (kill, arm in a sling and shin still
mending), with Pru Finch reserve and second catcher; Kellan Renn stands for Halden License
and keeps Seven's Meridian book. The team has a Flask license in its own right, a method
(the handed crossing) rehearsed on a yard and never live, a book holding **6.23 drams**
(about 5.63 uncommitted), and a sealed medical tin of **2.0 plain ward-trace** bound by
the Order of the Even Hand: *For a hand. Not for a bank, a floor, a crossing or a sale.*

Kiva has four of Orla's six hand-to-Ember crossings (Sundays the 1st, 8th, 15th, 22nd of
the second month, each on the Circuit's mast, read by Noa), and the hand sulks longer each
time (last: cold to mid-forearm, warm in two nights). The Chair's ledger enters the fifth and
sixth for the Saltmere gate mast, read by Noa with Orla present, "one in seven days and not
faster; not certified until the sixth." Aske's card holds: single doors, declared; one
crossing in seven days at the most; the hand seen next morning; no three-door. Kiva has not
told anyone that she dreams; the dream returned on the ferry and she kept her mouth shut. The
disk is cold. Her left fingers say that a thing keeps time and never what time.

Orla Dane and Mara Rowan have each decided, in scenes Kiva did not witness, to go south:
Orla by the Circuit's transit to find Jessamy Hart and "not use her to say it"; Mara by the
Works' transit in the first week, with Examiner Rusk, having written Officer Yeo "I will not
promise to read your bank." Kiva has been told neither. Rhea Sorn's Graywater van is coming
south; her card for Kiva has **two nights left, her card ends on the 30th,** and Pike's hands
are due out of their dressings about the 4th. Dinah Coombe will be on the Longshore.

## Pressure and what this movement is for

Saltmere is proud of its harbour bank. Thirty-six cells have been in step since twenty past
two on the 17th of the ninth month and have not dropped a stroke in six months. The Works'
people from Meridian arrive as guests telling their hosts that their best machine is a
hazard; Mara's twelve sheets already failed there, because a harbour is not a storm drain.
That is a fair position held against a fair position. Many sensible people like the stepped
bank.

The movement widens the ladder from Meridian's regional circuit to a national one and lets
Kiva meet, for the first time, **schools that solved the same problem a different way.** The
Ropewalk School teaches two doors **laid**, open together and agreeing, which Meridian calls
a stack and forbids; it is not foolish, it was built against people and the spawn of forty
years ago, it wins lanes, and its circuit has a column for it. Selka Dray's sealed mast rule
(any candidate with more than one door read from a sanctioned mast by a reader of the
Meridian office) now makes the host's best candidates an incident, and Meridian is disliked
for it. Let the lay beat Seven fairly in the seeding rounds, and let Seven learn from losing.

Underneath, a second problem is quietly turning: **Jessamy Hart** (Orla's candidate of 2659,
hurt at the Bench in 2660, now the fitter with the watch at the harbour pump house) turns out
to be the man who can tell the Works when the harbour is still. The harbour keeper's book
(the pump house's keeper is **Peverell**, who does not want to be the keeper who stopped the
pumps) has an entry for the night of the 11th of the third month, 2604, the same night as the
Ferren keeper's entry that Kiva and Mara have read. **Something stands in the basin at each
spring tide, and stands a little taller each time.** Hart and Mara seal a prediction about
it, to fall due on the morning of Wednesday the 23rd.

Orla and Hart meet once, badly. Kiva meets Hart, and learns from him what a ruined hand can
still be asked.

Midway the movement leaves the lanes. **Graywater's van is called south on a coast night
under a Charter Board warrant** — a professional call on tidal ground, where the forms work
the flood and the ebb and no sheet lies still. Kiva rides as Pike's second and speaks only
the far edge, two words, once; Rhea goes short on something (see the card: *if T7 is called
on a night of mine, she is T7's and I go short*). Show what a coast call teaches that the
Pit and the yard could not, and let it bear on the harbour.

**Ambrose Halden, the Proprietor** (Glory, alive in 2604, holder of the Halden foundation's
pattern) asks to see the plate and is answered by a watch's vote. He then asks Kiva for the
three-door, on a Halden mast, for a price. **She says no.** He is not a villain. He has
believed for seventy-seven years that stepped lamps are the ones that go white and not out,
and he has a defensible reading of what the dream means. Let him be the most interesting
adult Kiva has yet argued with, and let the argument cost him nothing he has not already
paid. A Glory master's presence is a place he cannot leave, a time he cannot exceed, or a
thing he must hold (bible §9).

**Exit.** Seven is seeded in the middle of twenty-four. The prediction is under seal. Orla and
Hart have spoken once, badly. The Measure's first board is on the doorstep, and what stands in
the basin has been seen by more than one pair of eyes.

## Promises to keep moving (consequential turns, not a script)

- **The fifth and sixth hand-to-Ember crossings** go at the gate mast with Noa and Orla
  present. The card is one in seven days at the most, the last was Sunday the 22nd, and the
  Slack on the 23rd needs an unspent crossing, so place them by the calendar and not by
  convenience; the sixth is Orla's certification; Aske says the price before each; the hand's
  reach up the arm is the visible cost (record how far it goes and how it comes back).
- **Dray's mast rule at the gate**, run by Noa with the Chair's reader's stand. Kiva is
  read; Seven is read; the Ropewalk's laid pair is read. Dray is on her feet or nearly; she
  has not changed her mind about Kiva and does not need to.
- **Open thread from Movement Two:** at the Flask license floor (ch. 13) Perrin's stance and
  Anwen's hand door on Kiva's still ground agreed on one thing for a moment, and Noa's
  notebook asks *Q.: is it a stack? Q.: whose?* Nobody on the page says whether a sanctioned
  mast would have read it. Use it or retire it by the end of the movement; do not leave a
  season-ending ambiguity that a reader worries about without a line.
- **Plain ash.** Nothing killed comes up plain. The gate combs every dram. Mr. Adlam of the
  Halden technical office, honest and often astonished, brings old bench books (the Step's
  count was slower once; it is quickening) and offers a fair price for Seven's plain ash and
  takes no for an answer. The Order's binding is Seven's own signed word: honor it, or break
  it on the page at a cost the team says aloud. Somebody has been stretching civic stock with
  soot; that surfaces small and human, not a conspiracy.
- **Jude's wish to see the sea,** and what he asks about ash and what it is for, may meet the
  Saltmere Company without a draft being made yet.
- **The notch** that follows Kiva's cliff by eleven seconds at Meridian follows it sooner on
  the coast. Nobody knows what that distance is a distance to. The public calls it a coast
  problem.
- **Kiva's book** goes to Orla on the first Sunday at Saltmere (the 6th). It records the
  fever, not the dream. Orla has not been told that Kiva dreams. Whether and how Kiva does
  tell anybody is a decision made on the page, and it is not made this movement.
- **Pike's hands** are due out of dressings about the 4th; slow. **Pru** is a catcher with her
  own sheet. **Perrin** is a captain; his envy of nobody is not yet a problem. **Anwen** leads
  a wall. Give the five of them choices of their own, not only reactions to Kiva.

## What must remain true / withheld

- One crossing in seven days at most; no stack; no three-door, ever asked casually. The
  Proprietor's ask is refused on the page and the three clean sequences stay three. Nothing
  heals the two fingers.
- The handed crossing is **not** used live against anything this movement. It may be
  rehearsed at Saltmere with a borrowed link, but its first live use belongs to the
  Longshore in Movement Four.
- **Students close no tear** and cut only under a foreman's slate. A coast night belongs to
  professionals; Kiva is a second who does not open her mouth until the bag is sealed.
- The Hunt, the Carry, the Bench, the lane brackets and the Graving are not played this
  movement. Do not play the Slack, do not kill the Saltmere Company catcher who dies in
  Movement Four, and do not bring the point of light or the hull.
- The words **Unroofed Sky** and **Breach** do not appear. The dream stays private to Kiva
  and Mara (plus the fever to Aske, Pru and Seven). The disk stays cold and unexplained, even
  beside the Proprietor. If the dream recurs, it costs her in the body first.
- Do not completely remove the filter; no pure Glory presence in the world; no cause of the
  Fall; no single doctrine. Nobody is a villain: not Halden, not Dray, not Carrow, not
  Peverell, not the Charter Board.
- Reserved identities: the Saltmere Company catcher who dies later is a name to be written
  when written, and this movement may introduce him or her as an ordinary colleague; do not
  telegraph the death. Juniper Carrow and Maud Lyle are new people with their own wants.
- Preserve injuries, cards, knowledge boundaries, dates and ash balances exactly.

## Craft lessons from Movement Two (measured, simulated, same-family)

- The prose came out bimodal: short beats plus a few 60–90 word "and…and" chains, with the
  middle band thin (median 6 against the formula's 11; 44.6% at five words or fewer against
  27.7%; narration long-tail 6.2% against 3.3% after repair). Write the middle: clear
  8–25 word narration sentences carrying one thought, a few longer compound-complex sentences
  where hierarchy stays easy to follow aloud, short beats for contrast. Do not chain
  semicolons into 60-word breaths.
- Ration mannerisms. "I'd like it known" is Perrin's alone; Coombe's knots belong to her
  entrances; "slowly," "scratched his jaw," "pale eyes," "something moved at the corner of
  her mouth" and "thirty years" each appear rarely. Give each person their own tells.
- Each figure, rule and tally arrives once. Jude reads the book when it changes the next
  choice, not as a recitation. Mara explains a rule once.
- Keep door vocabulary exact: a **plank** is two like doors on one ground; a **stack** or a
  **lay** is unlike doors open and agreeing; a **ward** is the hand door's tray; people are
  not "wards." Causal "since" is ambiguous aloud; use *because*.
- Count the days correctly: the shed-door number is 44 minus the date of the second month
  (16 on the 28th); date every figure against the calendar before it is spoken.
- Run the contests on one idea a child could state and an adult could appreciate; both
  readers loved the pack, the lodger in the seam, and the mill. Teach rules through attempts
  and consequences. Do not repeat the van-slate template a third time; open a van scene in
  the middle of the work.
