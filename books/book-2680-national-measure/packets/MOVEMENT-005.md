## Where we enter

It is the night of Wednesday the 16th of the third month, 2681, at Saltmere, after the Hunt on
the Longshore. **Three boards are done and Team Seven stands ninth with 53, one mark off the
roll of eight** (Halden License 65, Old Four 64, Holloway first 59, the Ropewalk first four 58,
Team Twenty-Eight 57, Westerley first 56, Westerley second 55, Holloway second 54). The fourth
board, **the Lane**, runs in brackets from Thursday the 17th, a declared-role lane (three for the
litter over a team's own line, one for a down, two halves a down), over three days to Saturday
the 19th. Seven needs two places in the Lane. The national license roll takes the first eight
fours for the year.

A Saltmere Company catcher, **Davy Penhale**, died on Mallow Creek under the far end of a stow
making the recovery the book prescribes; a Tarn reserve, **Bridie Hebden**, lived under his ward.
Seven's live handed crossing ("the pass in three") held for the girl and was too late for the man.
The Ropewalk's **Maud Lyle** has lost her ward for good; her captain **Juniper Carrow** has shut
her own stance in front of her school and says she is learning to declare. **Dinah Coombe's**
page (the far edge belongs to the second, as a place; "the book is written for a sheet") went to
the printer on the night of the 16th with a new head line naming Penhale, and every officer has it
from five on Thursday. The public line is still "the worst Hunt in memory", a bad rift season, a
gifted generation. Halden's claims clerk entered every red bag as *claimed, under the patent of
2391*, nine-tenths of the Hunt, and Kellan Renn told Kiva. The Proprietor wrote in his own hand
to Placement Chair Ysra Crest on the 12th for the sequence "on any terms she sets"; Kiva knows
because Adlam told her and Orla read it in her book.

**Kiva Rowan** is seventeen, seated, unclassified, with a warm left hand (three mornings after
the sixth crossing, certified) and a new cold burn across the knuckles and first two fingers
of her right; she writes left-handed for the week. Her calendar is a hard constraint, not a
decision for you to make for her: **the sixth crossing was Thursday the 10th, so the next is
possible from Thursday the 17th; one crossing in seven days at most, and the Slack at dawn on
Wednesday the 23rd needs an unspent crossing (Movement Six).** A crossing on Thursday the 17th
through Tuesday the 22nd would leave her none on the 23rd that stays inside her certificate.
So there is no crossing in the Lane week. She fights on one door, declared, and by the time the
lane arrives she knows what the week's crossing is for. Aske says the price twice. Three doors
stay three clean. Her fever with the dream came on the ferry and not at Saltmere (the 11th's fever
had no dream); the ferry's white lights are hers alone. She has told nobody the dream; Mara has it
in full and holds Kiva's sealed page.

The team book holds **6.26 drams** and a sealed medical tin of **2.0 plain**, bound *For a hand.
Not for a bank, a floor, a crossing or a sale.* Perrin's seventh rib is cracked and Aske's card
says nothing heavier than a cup for a fortnight; Pru Finch's ten fingertips are frosted; Jude's
left arm is frosted; Bridie's legs are burned by cold. Rhea Sorn's card ends on the 30th with one
night left. Pike's hands close halfway. **The prediction** Hart and Mara sealed on the 11th and
the Board holds is due to be opened at noon on **Wednesday the 23rd** (the dawn spring slack at
about 6.09; the Hoop at four feet eleven). Mara has decided not to read the bank herself: **thirty-six
hands, taught by the 22nd**, with Hart to teach them what the quiet feels like and Mara on the
landing with a watch to say *now*.

## Pressure and what this movement is for

The competition is read to its end, and the thing under it is put on a table in public. The
movement should feel like a national event arriving at its biggest days, and the people the
reader knows should be asked what they will say out loud.

- **The Lane, over three days, in brackets.** Developed lane fights against rivals who have
  adapted: **Carrow on one door** (she has shut her stance; declares edge only) and **Selka Dray
  on her feet** (the mast rule's author, who has said she means to beat Kiva in a lane "there").
  Set terrain, starting positions, reach, objectives, injuries and limits privately first; the
  opponent learns too; costs carry forward. Seven is hurt (Perrin's rib, Pru's hands, Jude's arm,
  Kiva's right hand), so the lane has to be won with what Seven can do. **Kiva loses the exchange
  with Dray by choice, on a declared count, with a needle on her**, and says why beforehand to
  Dray's face; **Seven wins the lane on the other three**. Do not make the loss a defeat that
  Kiva suffered or a win Dray steals: it is a decision about what the week's crossing is for.
- **The Charter Board's hearing on the three sheets** (the three sheets under the Chair's seal
  that the Board has not answered). Make it a drama with people who want things: the Clerk of the
  Measure, the Chair (Ysra Crest), the Halden claims, Dray's rule, Kellan's numbers, Noa's columns,
  Mara and Hart and Peverell, the Order. **Kellan Renn reads out figures that cost his own house.**
  **Adlam brings the old Halden bench books**: the Step's count was slower once (48 a minute in
  2402 and 2431; 50 in 2604; 55 in the ninth month of 2680; 57 now). Noa runs the reading of
  remarks columns with Lumb's notion; returns from other cities come in.
- **Kiva puts the dream on a table, by her own choice.** She decides what to say and whom to say
  it to; the decision is hers and made on the page, with its cost. Before the Charter Board, **Mara's
  sealed page is opened**: timed and sealed before Kiva ever read Annick, it proves that her account was
  her own. Beside it lie Annick's entry, the Ferren keeper's line of 2604, the Saltmere keeper's line
  for the same night (found by Peverell, the ring, the lamps, "something was attending"), and the first
  returns of the national reading. **Two living Glory masters say under their own names that they have
  dreamed it: Clemency Thrale, Master of the Order of the Even Hand (she has not been past her gate in
  eleven years and would like to see the sea again), and Ambrose Halden, the Proprietor (a hundred and
  three, left hand on a glass case he has not lifted for more than a minute since 2611).** Their
  limits must be visible on the page (a place, a time, a thing held); they do no student's or fitter's
  work. Close contact with Glory is a trigger (Kiva's fever and the dream's bodily cost; her doors lie
  down near Thrale and march near the Proprietor). **A Board clerk borrows Annick's phrase, and the
  papers print the Unroofed Sky.** This is the first appearance of the phrase on the page; nobody can say
  what it means, and the two masters read it in opposite ways: Halden, step the country so that its lamps
  go white and not out; the Order, one door, one quiet thing, let nothing in that asks. The Works and the
  Line: whatever is coming, do not be one engine tonight.
- **Draft boards** sit through the Lane days. A company may card a student; a student may say no; a
  card is not a seat. Offers are on the table for **Anwen** (a seat of her own, which she will tell
  Kiva first and plainly), **Kellan** (his house wants what he will not give), **Jude** (the Saltmere
  Company and the sea, and a question about what ash is for), and **Perrin** (envied, and he knows now
  it is no better). Kiva's own offers wait for the end of the third month.
- **Plain ash for the Slack.** Taking a bank out of step needs a jar of plain ash a cell, thirty-six of
  them, from ash that has never heard a beat. Nothing killed now comes up plain. Thrale holds forty jars
  and has already given Seven 1.2 drams; Adlam's offer for Seven's 2.0 was refused; the soot-cut
  winter stock is spoilt. Establish by the end of the movement where thirty-six jars are and what they
  cost, honoring the Order's binding or breaking it on the page with a price said aloud.
- **The Measure is read in full.** No board is struck. The Ropewalk School's first four wins the
  National Measure (Carrow, the nation's favourite, a lay with a door shut); Halden License is second.
  **Team Seven finishes seventh of twenty-four and makes the national license roll, which takes eight,
  by one mark**, warranted for the Slack. Seven wins one board outright, the Carry (done). The place is
  earned by a four and a method, not by three doors. Everyone in the city now knows that Kiva dreams.

**Exit.** The Measure is over. Seven is seventh, on the roll by one mark, warranted for the Slack. The
dream is a public fact with no agreed meaning. The sealed prediction falls due on Wednesday the 23rd,
four days after the final. Offers are on the table for four of the team. Rhea's last night and the
last of the third month are ahead.

## Promises to keep moving (consequential turns, not a script)

- **Relationships.** Kiva and Dray: unpersuaded, a lane, a loss by choice, still not friends. Kiva and
  Carrow: each uses a piece of the other's method later. Kiva and Orla: the sponsor's line, a true
  sentence with Hart, no absolution; Orla asks the Chair to strike her own line when it is no longer
  needed. Kiva and Mara: Mara cannot own the choice; Kiva watches her take the national risk. Kiva and
  Rhea Sorn: *either of us may say no*; Rhea has been giving her second more than advice. Kiva and Pru:
  catcher and cord, now equal. Kiva and Hart: what a ruined hand can be asked.
- **Pike** can still use his hands halfway. **Coombe** has her page out. **Yeo** wants Kiva's columns and
  a second. **Ivey** has them copied. **Peverell** is not the keeper who stopped the pumps. **The
  Proprietor** is sure of his reading and not certain he is right. **Thrale** wants one thing quiet.
- **The tide clock.** The basin's slack is held near a quarter past six at the springs while the sea
  walks round the clock, and walks with the table at the neaps; nobody can say why. Use it, do not
  explain it.
- **The notch** (seven, then six seconds after a single cliff at the gate mast; eleven at Meridian after a
  second cliff), the Step's quickening count (48 → 57), and the hair at 12.44 on every pen with nobody on
  the mast: keep the evidence accumulating fairly. Noa draws no line between his columns; he may add a
  fourth and will not draw one.
- **Perrin lifted against Aske's card at the spur and nothing happened on the page.** Carry it: Aske
  notices, or the rib remembers.
- **Jude's frosted arm** is "not worse" by his own account; Aske has not been shown it. Let it matter or
  retire it.
- Give Perrin, Anwen, Jude, Pru and Kellan choices of their own, not only reactions to Kiva.

## Craft lessons from Movements Two to Four (measured, simulated, same-family)

- The sentence distribution has been bimodal, then over-corrected, then bimodal again: median 6–7 against
  the formula's 11; 40–46% at five words or fewer against 27.7%. Movement Four's long tail was restored in
  the climbs and the pass (2.6% of narration at forty words or more), but the planning and aftermath
  chapters trotted. Write the middle on purpose: clear 8–25 word narration sentences carrying one thought,
  a few well-built 35–55 word sentences with one main clause in descriptive and reflective passages, short
  beats for contrast. Join runs of short reactive sentences ("Kiva did not answer." / "It's said.") when
  the thought is continuous. No *and … and* chains over about 45 words.
- Scenes: movement Four finally landed (section mean ~1,000 words; ~8.4 internal breaks per 10k). Keep
  continuous fights, hearings and dinners unbroken; break where place, time or viewpoint changes. Do not
  end a movement in a survey of twelve short sections.
- A shared idiom kept spreading ("I'd want it said", "I'd like it known", "said nothing", "before the
  water", "frost on a window", "expect nothing else"). Give each person their own voice: Jude diffident
  (use "I'd want it said" only for his heaviest lines), Anwen "I want it said", Perrin alone "I'd like it
  known", Pru scarlet, Aske's price speeches, Orla rough and brief, Hart plain and gauge-like, Mara dry,
  Kellan sums, Coombe drills, Dray flat. Ration "slowly", "said nothing", negation runs, and causal
  *since* (use *because*). Give each simile one home.
- Do not telegraph a turn twice and then restate it eight times afterward; say each fact once where it
  matters and move on. Each figure, rule and tally arrives once.
- Check the page against itself: a heat time that does not support a printed ranking, a clock that does not
  reconcile, a water that runs both ways. The counting readers test every sum first.
- Door vocabulary: a **plank** is two like doors on one ground; a **stack** or a **lay** is unlike doors open
  and agreeing; a **ward** is the hand door's tray, and people are not "wards"; "hand door" is the hand
  tradition, not Kiva's cold hand.
- Make a hearing a contest between people who want things, with a clock, a surprise, and a cost, not a
  procession of exhibits. The contests that worked had one idea a child could state and an adult could
  appreciate, geometry stated, both sides adapting, and a visible cost.
