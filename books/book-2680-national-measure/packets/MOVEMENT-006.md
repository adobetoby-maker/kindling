## Where we enter

It is the night of Sunday the 20th of the third month, 2681, at Saltmere. The National Measure has been read:
the Ropewalk School's first four won, Halden License second, and **Team Seven finished seventh of twenty-four
on 69, on the national license roll by one mark, warranted (as a roll four) for Wednesday**. The Charter
Board's finding named the dream (**the unroofed sky**, "for want of a better", no agreed meaning) and **warranted
a Barrel response at the Saltmere harbour pump house at the dawn slack on Wednesday the 23rd**, on the keeper's
leave: the Saltmere Company's officers; the Works' reader on the landing; the keeper's fitters on the cells; **the
Order's thirty-six jars of plain ash, given at a price said aloud**; any four on the roll may stand by name on the
Board's warrant and ash; no four is obliged. The sealed prediction (Hart and Mara and Peverell, 11th) opens at
noon on Wednesday: the dawn spring slack at 6.09 by the pump house clock, not before 6.07 nor after 6.12, lasting
18–22 minutes; the ring at the outfall (the Hoop) will rise from the mud and stand at four feet eleven; the water
inside will keep the bank's count, four and four, at fifty-seven to the minute or faster; nothing else in the
basin will keep a count. The Board has told nobody what the thing in the basin is, because nobody knows.

**The thing in the basin** stands up at each spring slack and grows each time (a hand high in the ninth month,
4' 1", 4' 6½"). It is keeping the harbour's time. It is the first Barrel-class expression on the page. The
harbour's thirty-six-cell bank (the Regiment, fifty-seven to the minute, in step since twenty past two on the
17th of the ninth month, 2680) has not dropped a stroke in six months and is the only thing standing between the
low town and the sea; the harbour water moves twice a day and is still only at slack, for about twenty minutes. The
Ferren method needs still water and a count that is not the caller's; Mara's twelve sheets failed here in the
winter because a harbour is not a storm drain. **Mara has decided not to read the bank herself: thirty-six hands,
each on its own count, taught by the 22nd, one jar of plain a cell.** Hart teaches what the quiet feels like; Mara
will stand on the landing with a watch and say *now*. Peverell's pumps will stop for eleven minutes at the slack by
her own hand; a foot of water in Ropery Row; "move the coal up a stair". The Harbour Committee has signed the Order's
price: **the thirty-six cells never to be stepped again while the pump house stands, each its own count, not two
alike.**

**Kiva Rowan** is seventeen, seated, unclassified, with a warm left hand (six mornings), a healing right, and
**the week's crossing kept for this Wednesday (the sixth was Thursday the 10th; one crossing in seven days at most;
nobody has asked for it yet)**. Aske's card: the seventh past the elbow, five nights' sulk. Three doors stay three
clean. Her dream is public: she put it on the table herself; she told the Board's commission about the white lights
(ferry only, lower, nearer, "I'll not have it said I saw more"); **the colour with no word is hers alone and not on
the table**; Mara has learned there is a white and a thing she was never told and will not ask. Two fevers near
Glory masters, the third on Sunday with no dream ("Nothing came"). Her doors lie down near Thrale and, near the
Proprietor, want to march and to beat. The disk is cold. She has made a yes/no log at a quarter to seven since Hart's
method (yes, every day). Her ten sheets of Pike's craft and the pass sheet are copied; Ivey, Yeo's catcher, has a
second; **Coombe's page** (the far edge belongs to the second, "the book is written for a sheet") is in every officer's
pocket.

**Team Seven** holds **5.15 drams** (sealed medical tin, 2.0 plain, unopened, bound *For a hand. Not for a bank, a
floor, a crossing or a sale*), and four people with offers: **Anwen** has said yes to Graywater (Doran Mace's ward line,
her own place, from the 1st of the fourth month; told Kiva first); **Jude** has the Saltmere Company's card
(Yeo; "come and find out"; will answer after Wednesday) and a frosted left arm; **Perrin** has taken down Halden's
offer and has the Municipal Works' (Rusk) up, with a cracked seventh rib and Aske's card against lifting; **Kellan**
(Halden License, second) refused Halden's junior-assessor card in writing and holds a white card with a Halden mark
whose contents are not on the page; **Pru** is "on a sheet". Dray ("On my feet.") and Carrow ("I want to be on the
wall") will be there. Rhea Sorn's card ends on the 30th; **she has not written Kiva's, and will not before the
30th**; Pike's hands close halfway; Orla's sponsor's line has been struck on evidence; Orla and Hart have each said one
true sentence and nothing is mended. **The Proprietor** has testified (he has dreamed it eleven times; read Annick in
2610; the 2611 settlement followed; "I'm sure of my reading. I'm not certain I'm right"); his left hand lies on a glass
case over the foundation cell and has not lifted for more than a minute since 2611; the foundation's claim on "all ash
exhibiting the Step, however produced" has not been relinquished. **Thrale**, who has been past her gate twice in her
life, is in Saltmere an hour at a time ("a day's work"); the Order's cellar holds four jars.

## What this movement is for

This is the end of Book Two. It has to deliver a complete, satisfying national-scale ending with its three meanings
paid (the competition, the nation counting what it has, and the beat itself), and it must not be a bridge to Book
Three. The movement has three parts of unequal weight that you may divide as the story wants: **rehearsal** (Monday and
Tuesday, in developed scenes), **the Slack** (Wednesday dawn, the longest and most sustained set piece in the book), and
**after** (the rest of the month and the last of the third month, to Thursday the 1st of the fourth).

### Rehearsal, with one bad assumption found in time and one that is not

Developed scenes of people learning to do a thing together that has never been done at this scale. Build the
operation from parts the reader already owns: the handed crossing (catch, carry, plant, three people with unlike doors,
one caller who has been inside both gaps; it costs almost no ash and a great deal of declared trust; it cannot be done by
strangers); Hart's quiet; Mara's thirty-six counts; Coombe's drills; Ivey's second; Yeo's closing crews; Pike's far
edge; Carrow's lay and Dray's feet; the Graving. **The Graving** (a dry dock roofed with tiers; a floating gate keeps
the harbour out; with the gate shut and the sluices closed it is a basin of still water whatever the tide does outside)
becomes the purse that catches the ash when its gate is shut. Find, in rehearsal, one bad assumption in time to fix it
and let one survive to the dawn. Nobody is rehearsed past what the cohort can bear.

### The Slack: the operation as the map gives it (intent, not choreography)

At the dawn low-water slack of Wednesday the 23rd, a mixed cohort from the Measure's roll and three professional crews
take Saltmere's harbour bank of thirty-six cells **out of step**. The water is still for about twenty minutes. The thing
standing in the basin has to come off the water first.

- **Students** stand the wall, carry, cut on a foreman's slate, and bag. **Kiva calls three handed crossings in a row and
  tests every mark first.** Students close no tear.
- **When a link fails, she supplies it herself: one certified crossing, on a sanctioned mast, read by the office's
  reader.** It is the first decisive thing she has ever done that was entirely inside her certificate. **When a second
  link fails, she makes a second crossing in the same hour, against her healer's card, with the price said first.**
  (A third failing link is not offered: three doors stay three clean.) Place the crossings by the calendar and by
  Aske's card, which Aske says twice.
- **Professionals** close the tear: fitters and keepers under Mara Rowan, Jessamy Hart and the harbour keeper
  Peverell fill thirty-six cells on thirty-six counts. Mara does not read the bank herself and does not touch a cell
  until she decides to; whatever she decides, she decides on the page.
- **The Proprietor holds the harbour's pumps on his own pattern** so that the low town does not flood. The operation
  cannot finish while he holds it, because his pattern is the Step. **He lets go: of the bank, and, in writing, of his
  foundation's claim on "all ash exhibiting the Step, however produced."** His limit is visible: his left hand on the
  glass case, not lifted for more than a minute in seventy years. What it costs him to lift it must be shown and must
  be real. **Master Thrale holds a ward across the dock mouth and cannot move while she does.** Both limits are on the
  page; no Glory master does a student's or a fitter's work.
- **For some seconds two Glory fields from unlike traditions touch. Every lamp on the coast shows a point of light.**
  Three people who have dreamed (Kiva, and the two masters) are seen to count together. Do not explain it.
- **The bank comes out.** The harbour's pumps stand (the Proprietor's hold ends); the low town takes water. **A
  professional died on this coast earlier in the book (Penhale); nobody dies at the Slack.** Carrow and Dray stand on the
  wall and each uses a piece of the other's method. Anwen leads a mixed ward line. Rhea takes a count from her own second.
  Jude, Perrin, Pru, Kellan and Noa have work in it that is theirs. The prediction opens at noon; read what is written
  against what happened, honestly, including where it was wrong.

### After

- **The Charter Board orders every municipal bank in the country put on its own count and kept there**: a slower,
  dearer, deliberately uneven grid. It is a real national answer and it costs light, money, and an industrial house
  its oldest property.
- **What it does not settle is whether the Proprietor was right.** He has believed for seventy-seven years that
  stepped lamps are the ones that go white instead of out. Do not settle it; do not make him a fool or a villain.
- **Kiva's cost:** the hand is gone to the wrist for three weeks and returns as before. What does not return is the
  rate: **Aske's card now reads one crossing in fourteen days.** Three doors remain three clean. The sequence was asked
  for twice in this book (the Proprietor in the third movement; a Glory master at the Slack) and refused twice.
- **The last of the third month.** Rhea Sorn and Kiva look again, as the card promised. Either may say no, and each says
  no to something. Rhea names no seat. She writes Kiva's name on the Charter Board's travelling commission, under another
  officer's hand, with *either of us* on it again. Kiva takes it on two conditions of her own (choose them; make them hers
  and not a pattern). **Team Seven's four go four ways by their own choices:** the offers on the table (Graywater for
  Anwen; the Company for Jude and what he asks about ash; the Works or elsewhere for Perrin; Kellan's white card; Pru's
  sheet) resolve on the page, each in a scene.
- **Relationships** to land: Kiva and Pike (each has been the other's second); Kiva and Rhea (a count taken, a name given
  away); Kiva and Orla (the sponsor's line struck; a certificate in Kiva's name); Kiva and Mara (watching her take the
  national risk without owning it; the seal broken by both, done); Kiva and Anwen (equals about to work in different
  places); Kiva and Perrin (envied, captain, a board won); Kiva and Kellan (he put his numbers where they hurt his house;
  a card); Kiva and Pru; Kiva and Dray (calls the count on her feet; not a friend); Kiva and Carrow (each uses a piece of
  the other's method); Kiva and Hart (what a ruined hand can be asked); Orla and Hart (no absolution).
- **The last image.** Wires vouch what the ship's engineer said (his unvouched line from Book One): ports over the water
  logged the same point of light in the same minute. A hull stands in from the south with a light on its bow that is like
  no lamp in Saltmere. Somebody else has dreamed, and has come to see who answered. **The disk is cold. Kiva writes that
  down.** The one on the hull is not met, named or explained.

## What must remain true / withheld

- One crossing in seven days at most (Aske's card), until the Slack's second crossing against it with the price said
  first; the card afterwards reads one in fourteen days. **No three-door. No fourth use.** The three clean stay three. If a
  Glory master asks for a fourth, she says no and says why. Nothing heals the two fingers, the lost Ropewalk ward, or any
  death. Costs are not reset.
- The **disk is cold** throughout, even beside two Glory fields touching: no origin, no nine rings, no first bearer's
  book. **The colour with no word stays hers alone.** "Breach" remains unused.
- Reserved (do not spend): the complete loss of the filter; pure Glory presence worldwide; the cause of the Fall; what the
  pressure is, why it keeps a count, who or what is attending; the disk's purpose; whether Hesper Annick died in 2461;
  any returning figure from the first trilogy; a doctrine that makes the schools obsolete; anything settled between
  nations; any international convergence beyond "it has begun" (the hull, the wire). Kiva's classification: no column is
  made for her.
- **Nobody is a villain:** not Halden, not the Works, not the Order, not the Board, not Dray, not Carrow, not the
  Proprietor, not the Committee. A Glory master on the page has a limit the reader can see. Every climax capability is
  shown and priced earlier (it is: the handed crossing, the week's crossing, the far edge, the quiet, the counts, the
  jars, the Graving, the lay, the hands).
- Students close no tear. Cuts are on a foreman's slate. Ledgers reconcile to the hundredth: the team's ash, the thirty-six
  jars, the Slack's yield and its shares, the cost of the crossings; check every sum, clock, weekday and count before it is
  spoken. The red bags stay evidence.
- Preserve injuries, cards, knowledge boundaries, dates and ash balances exactly. Dream knowledge moves only by a decision
  made on the page.

## Craft lessons from Movements Two to Five (measured, simulated, same-family)

- The sentence distribution has moved between bimodal and over-corrected (median 6–7 against the formula's 11; 40–46% at
  five words or fewer against 27.7%). The long climbs and passes read best aloud (35–55 words, one main clause); the
  planning, hearing and aftermath chapters trotted. Write the middle on purpose: clear 8–25 word narration sentences
  carrying one thought, a few well-built 35–55 word sentences in descriptive and reflective passages, short beats for
  contrast. Join runs of short reactive sentences when the thought is continuous. No *and … and* chains over about 45 words.
- Scenes: keep continuous action, hearings and dinners unbroken; break where place, time or viewpoint changes; developed
  scenes near 900–1,000 words before meaningful turns. Do not end the book in a survey of twelve short sections: let the
  last chapters be scenes.
- A shared idiom keeps spreading ("I'd want it said", "I'd sooner", "on the table", "said nothing", "before the water",
  "frost on a window", "expect nothing else"). Give each person a distinct voice: Jude diffident ("I'd want it said" only for
  his heaviest lines), Anwen "I want it said", Perrin alone "I'd like it known", Pru scarlet, Aske's price speeches, Orla
  rough and brief, Hart plain and gauge-like, Mara dry, Kellan sums, Coombe drills, Dray flat, Rhea one piece of advice a
  season. Ration "slowly", "said nothing", negation runs, and causal *since* (use *because*). Give each simile one home. Do
  not state the theme over action that has already shown it.
- Do not telegraph a turn twice and then restate it afterward; say each fact once where it matters. Hearings and evidence
  readings must not re-read what the reader has just seen. Each figure, rule and tally arrives once.
- Check the page against itself: a score that does not follow its events, a clock that does not reconcile, a knowledge
  source that cannot exist, a water that runs both ways. The counting readers test every sum first.
- Movement Five's slips, as lessons: a score that omitted a down (check every lane against the rule); a paper cited for what only
  four people heard in a shut shop (every knowledge source must exist on the page); a public list of occasions that left out the
  occasion the sealed page proves (a list a character speaks must agree with the record she points to); evidence read aloud three
  times across three scenes (say it once, where it matters); a declaration rule ("and no change") that made the later temptation
  unreal until a line made the cost explicit (state the rule and its price before the choice); a dropped reopening quotation mark
  where a tag was inserted mid-speech (scan quote parity). Make the operation's rules and prices plain before the choices that
  spend them.
- Door vocabulary: a **plank** is two like doors on one ground; a **stack** or a **lay** is unlike doors open and agreeing; a
  **ward** is the hand door's tray and people are not "wards"; "hand door" is the hand tradition, not Kiva's cold hand.
- The contests that worked had one idea a child could state and an adult could appreciate, geometry stated, both sides
  adapting, and a visible cost. Make the operation trackable in one reading: fix the bearings, say who is where, keep the
  clock.
