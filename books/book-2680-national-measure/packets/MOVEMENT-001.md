## Where we enter

The evening of Sunday the 16th of the tenth month, 2680, is the last page of Book One. The
first free day is **Monday the 17th.** This movement may open on it or later; it ends by
**Saturday the 30th of the first month, 2681.** The months run thirty days: the eleventh
begins on a Monday, the twelfth on a Wednesday, the first month of 2681 on a **Friday.**

Kiva Rowan is seventeen, seated in Third Division as a catcher, unclassified, and on the Home
Line's roll. Her first two left fingers bend, grip, and report pressure, and nothing else.
The arm that went white to above the elbow has come back to where it was. **Healer Aske has
forbidden anything lit until the new year: not a step, not a ward for a tap.** She catches
with dark hands and her voice. She names her handoffs before she fumbles them. The disk is
cold. The dream has not come back.

Team Seven is fourth in the region and second step in its own right. Its book holds **25.53
drams, with 0.8 of plain ward-trace sealed as medical** and no way to make it up. No warrant
for Saltmere has been posted. Kellan has the Halden yard twice a week and is on Seven's book.
Perrin's ribs are strapped and his Hall's second team wants him from the new year. Anwen has
a summer place on the night van's ward line. Jude has 7.8 drams by name. Pru has her own
sheet.

Rhea Sorn's card is signed on its back by the four and goes back to her on **Monday the 1st
of the eleventh month:** catcher's second to Pike; two nights in the month from the first of
the new year, when the healer's card is up; dark hands; no door not on Kiva's certificate; Pru
for the cord; Team Seven first; until the last of the third month; either may say no; no seat
promised. A second sets nothing and calls nothing. She watches, and she says afterward what
the master did that he did not say aloud. She keeps her mouth shut until the bag is sealed.

The Ferren bank is out of step. **Seven drums (19, 20, 21, 25, 26, 27, 28) are still in,** to
be done one a day with the wardens' winter stock. Mara's reading hand was starved on the 6th
and eased; she may read a drum, and not a bank until the eleventh month. Her eleven sheets
have gone to the coast. Saltmere's harbour bank is still one engine.

**Orla Dane has Jessamy Hart's card and has shown it to nobody. Kiva does not know that he
has answered, that he fits pumps on the south coast, or that he has asked to read what she
writes.** Kiva's dream is known in full to Mara alone; the Chair knows only that one came.
The four, Pru, Noa, Rhea, Pike, Vane, and Aske do not know that she dreams.

## What this movement is for

It opens the book on work. A returning reader should be reoriented by what people do with
their hands in the first pages, and a recap paragraph should not be needed.

Its pressure: **for eleven weeks Kiva has no door, and then for a month she has doors and may
not use them where it counts.** Everybody round her is getting on. What is she for, dark? The
answer the movement earns is small and real. She becomes somebody who can see what a skilled
person does and say it so that a third person could do it. That is the root of everything
she builds later in this book. She should not know that yet.

Three things must be in practical view by the last page:

1. **The professional ceiling.** What a chartered catcher of twenty-six years does that no
   student does, and the night on which it is not enough.
2. **The damaged hand under new constraints.** Dark-hand work, a right-hand corner, a count
   that is somebody else's, and one honest test of what two dead fingers can still be asked.
3. **The National Measure as a bill and a date.** An entry to sign, a warrant to pay, a board
   that is a live hunt, and a four that is not yet good enough for it.

## Consequences to land

These are the few fixed turns. Order, staging, and everything between them are the author's.

- **The card goes back signed,** on the 1st of the eleventh month.
- **Orla tells Kiva about Hart.** It is Orla's own choice, on the page, before the new year,
  and it is not dragged out of her. Kiva decides what a stranger may read. She sends method,
  in her own copy. **The dream does not go south,** and the scene should let the reader see
  her decide that.
- **Kiva's figures are read again** at the new year, for the first time since Classification
  Day, on the Circuit's instruments. Bounds: Draw falls a long way from 3.1 and is still poor
  beside a good single-door student; Ward Hold rises most, from the folding work; Stance
  rises a little from 6.2; Edge has hardly moved from 3.0. The rank frame holds **Flame** with
  one door declared and the others shut, and goes to dashes with two. There is no first door.
  She is not classified. Exact numbers are the author's and are then recorded.
- **Each single door is relit under Aske's eye** before any crossing is thought of. No
  three-door sequence occurs in this movement. Nobody with authority asks for one.
- **Two nights on Graywater's van,** both in the first month. The first is a learning night.
  On the second, the book answer fails against an expression that mixes two behaviors the
  crews know separately, and a professional pays for it. The plan expects that to be Pike,
  and expects his hands. The author may find a truer cost, so long as the ceiling is shown
  and carried forward.
- **Kiva begins writing down Pike's craft** in seven columns, because he asked a second to
  tell him what he did not say. He should dislike some of what he reads.
- **Kellan chooses his four.** The National lets a fighter stand for one four only, declared
  at entry. The plan expects Halden License, with his name left on Seven's book for calls,
  and expects the choice to be his and to cost him something at the Rowan table.
- **Seven's entry is signed under a captain.** The plan expects Perrin, who keeps the word and
  the brake, and expects him to mind.
- **The warrant is posted** at more than Kellan's ten: planned at three drams a head, five
  heads, fifteen. The book that had "something over" does not.
- **Saltmere answers Mara.** They tried the eleven sheets and failed. The harbour's water
  does not lie still. It should arrive as a fact from working people, with a detail Mara can
  use.
- **There is no plain ward-trace to be had.** The reserve stays at 0.8 unless it is opened
  for a shown medical need and entered so.

## Action and learning this movement owes

Developed scenes, each a changing problem. No montage of eleven weeks.

- **Drum work,** early, with dark hands. A drum is a small bank: one cell alone on an open
  link. Let something about one of the seven refuse the method that worked on the hill, so
  that the reader learns the method again by watching it bend. Mara is in charge and has one
  hand on a card.
- **A dark-hands floor or yard with the four,** in which Kiva's usefulness is in question and
  Pru's is not.
- **The first van night:** terrain, positions, the *before* column read out, Pike's hem, and
  what a second can and cannot see from her knees.
- **The second van night:** the major set piece. It may take 2,000 words or more. Set the
  ground, the reach, the roles, and the limits privately before writing it. The thing they
  meet obeys spawn anatomy and combines it. The crew tests, meets resistance, adjusts, and
  pays. Kiva is forbidden to set or call. What she does inside that rule, or at its edge, is
  the scene's question, and its consequence carries into Movement Two.
- **One honest test of the two fingers.** They report pressure. Hart knew a stepped bank "with
  the hand, the bad one." The planned finding is narrow: through something solid, they may
  tell her that a thing is keeping time, and not what time. It is not healing, not a combat
  sense, and not reliable. If the page does not earn it, leave the question open and say so
  in the checkpoint.
- **A human contest** somewhere in the eight: Seven against people who adapt, with Kiva on one
  declared door after the new year, and a correction the four can try again.

Carry every injury, strap, card, and dram forward. Perrin's ribs take weeks. Mara's hand has a
date on it.

## Formula, applied to this movement

The owner's numerical formula governs. Write toward it across the eight chapters, then measure.

- **Viewpoint:** about 87% Kiva. About 6,200–6,800 words of cutaway across three or four of
  the five lenses (Anwen, Kellan, Mara, Orla, Rhea), each for something Kiva cannot see.
  Orla with the card and Mara with the coast's answer are the natural two.
- **Progression vocabulary:** this is the front third. The formula's 58 hits per 10,000 words
  and 1.7 ratio work out to about **80 per 10,000 here** and about 47 after Movement Two.
  Teach hard, through attempts: the license steps, the national roll, the boards and how they
  are marked, the warrant, Draw and Hold and what the re-reading means, a second's duties,
  the slate's six columns, plain against stepped ash, yield and grade and resonance.
- **Development:** a Kiva realization or decision about every 2,200 words, so twenty-one to
  twenty-four in the movement. Small beats for at least eight others; Pike, Pru, Orla, Mara,
  Kellan, and Perrin are owed most here.
- **Sentences:** mean 14.6 words; median 11; about 27.7% at five words or fewer; about 3.3%
  at forty or more. Short beats beside long ones, not a steady middle.
- **Paragraphs and scenes:** median paragraph about 18 words; scenes near 950 words; about 8.7
  breaks per 10,000 words. These are directional proxies.
- **Readability:** Flesch Reading Ease near 72.3; grade near 6.8.
- Section 8's filler, adverb, and tag figures are secondary. Do not chase them.

Book One's Movement One missed the sentence targets on the first pass because the dialogue
ran clipped. Let narration carry the longer sentences here from the start.

## Protected boundaries

- **Nothing lit by Kiva before Friday the 1st of the first month, 2681,** and then only as
  Aske's card allows. Dark hands on Rhea's floor all movement.
- **No door on Rhea's floor that is not on Kiva's certificate.** No crossing in the field.
- **Three doors: three clean, not asked, not done.**
- **The fingers do not improve.** The arm is as it was.
- **The disk is cold** and does nothing. A fourth-column line is owed only after a crossing.
- **The dream does not recur in this movement.** The words "Unroofed Sky" do not appear. No
  one new learns that Kiva dreams, except by a decision of hers made on the page; the plan
  expects none.
- **Hart** is a card, a hand, and a trade. He is not met. He does not forgive.
- **Saltmere** is not reached. No Saltmere competitor, Glory master, or Board officer comes on
  stage except by letter, wire, or notice. Dinah Coombe may be announced and not met.
- **No one explains** the Step, the hold, the notch, the lamps, the filter, the plate, or why
  nothing is plain. People may count, compare, and be wrong.
- **Students close no tear** and cut only under a foreman's slate.
- **The ledger is exact.** Every dram spent, lent, earned, or refused is entered and adds.
  The red bags are evidence. No free practice ash.
- **Nobody is a villain,** including Halden, the Works, the Board, and Rhea Sorn.
- **Selka Dray** is in a chair, mending, unpersuaded.
- No commit, push, publication, narration, review, scoring, or repair inside the run.

## What the author owns

Where in the winter the book opens. How eleven dark weeks are weighted against one lit month.
Which drum misbehaves, and how. The van's ground, the crew's talk, the thing on the second
night and what the crew ends up calling it. Whether Pike is the one hurt. What Kiva does at
the edge of a second's rule. The re-reading's exact figures. How Orla says it. What Kiva
copies out for Hart and what she finds she cannot. The Rowan table when Kellan chooses.
Perrin as captain. Humor, weather, food, the laundry next door, and every line of talk. Where
the eight chapters break. The last image.

Use movement-sized causality. The dark weeks should teach the thing the van nights test. The
second night should change what the four must train for. The warrant should make that
training a matter of ash. The last chapter should leave Seven facing Movement Two with a
date, a bill, a captain, and a catcher who has started to write somebody else's method down.
