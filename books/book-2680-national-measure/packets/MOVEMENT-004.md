## Where we enter

It is Monday the 14th of the third month, 2681, seven in the morning, the first day of the
National Measure. Kiva Rowan's hand is warm after a four-night sulk (six of six
hand-to-Ember crossings, certified by Orla); Aske's card says the next crossing is Thursday the
17th at the soonest. She stands down from the Bench: Team Seven is seeded **twelfth of
twenty-four** after the seeding lanes (won one, lost two, twelve points for, sixteen against;
the lay of the Ropewalk beat Seven fairly; Seven's correction, *make it walk*, is in Kiva's
name in the book). The Measure has four boards: **the Bench** (a bank of cells with a fault
planted: find, quench, re-tune, restart), **the Carry** (rescue and extraction against a
clock), **the Hunt** (a licensed live hunt on tidal ground under company officers, marked on
clean yield after shares, less every remark, with every team's ash combed for the Step), and
**the Lane** (not this movement). The Bench and the Carry are Monday and Tuesday; the Hunt is
Wednesday the 16th. The national license roll takes the first **eight** fours.

The team: Perrin Cade (captain, carry, still door; ribs that still tell him things), Kiva
(catcher), Anwen Pryce (hold; leads a wall), Jude Talley (kill; arm out of the sling for
contests only), with Pru Finch catching when Kiva is stood down. The book holds about **4.16
drams** (see the ledger), a sealed medical tin of 2.0 plain still bound *For a hand. Not for a
bank, a floor, a crossing or a sale*, and a Flask license in its own right. The handed crossing
has been rehearsed on a yard, never live; at Saltmere it was rehearsed once in the Company's
van yard with a borrowed still door, **Trewin** of the Tarn, who was late a count at the second
gap. Perrin wants a second plant.

The harbour is the other clock. Mara Rowan, Jessamy Hart and Keeper Peverell sealed a
prediction (opened at noon on Wednesday the 23rd): at the dawn spring low-water slack on the 23rd
the ring at the outfall (the Hoop, which children call it) will stand at four feet eleven. The
Works have not said whether they mean to stop the stepped bank (the Regiment, fifty-seven to the
minute); Mara has not touched a cell. The Proprietor asked Kiva for the three-door at a price and
was refused; he is not done. Two fevers in a month, both near a Glory master; no dream this
time. Kiva has told nobody of the white lights.

## Pressure and what this movement is for

**Three boards in three days, and the third is alive.** The Measure should feel like a
national event, with crowds and press and the Board's flat calls, and also like a place where
people the reader knows are put under load: Perrin on his own board, the Bench without its
catcher, the Hunt on tidal ground where nothing is clean.

- **The Bench, without Kiva.** She watches. What a catcher does when she cannot do the thing
  is this book's argument about travel: what she can say that another person can use. Pru
  holds her place on the floor; Kiva's columns and her voice matter from the tiers. Let the
  Bench be a real board with real banks and a planted fault, not a montage, and let it
  connect to the harbour's problem without solving it.
- **The Carry, which is Perrin's.** A full scene with a clock. He has the captaincy and
  something with only his name on it. **Seven wins this board outright**, on his back, and he
  finds that being envied is no better than envying. Do not make it a walkover: the Ropewalk,
  Halden, and the Tarn are good at carrying. Ribs, weight, an opposing method, a clock.
- **The Hunt, on the Longshore: the book's midpoint set piece.** Tidal flats and creeks,
  Hunt Marshal Dinah Coombe (who "will be on the Longshore") in her grey Board coat, coast
  forms that work the flood and the ebb, every team's ash combed for the Step, yield scored
  after shares. Nothing is clean. Develop terrain, starting positions, reach, objectives,
  injuries and limits privately before you write it; let the forms learn too.
  - **The lay is entered.** Coombe's drill said it could be: a laid door is an open door. A
    Ropewalk player loses a door, perhaps for good. **Maud Lyle** (Ropewalk Hold, laid ward
    and stance, whom Anwen warned about the lodger and Wenna) is the planned candidate; the
    author may choose differently if the story is better. The cost is a door that does not come
    back; no rank, ash or device restores it.
  - **A professional catcher of the Saltmere Company is killed making the recovery that the
    book prescribes.** The book answer fails against a coast form, as the Pit's did in Movement
    One. **Davy Penhale** (red-bearded, talkative, "one fathom, two fathom", who held the purses
    on the coast night and was an ordinary colleague) is the planned candidate because he was
    planted as one; the author may choose another Company catcher. Do not make the death a
    lesson delivered by the narrator. Make it a person's death that other people carry out of
    the book's mud: Yeo, Pike, Rhea (whose card ends on the 30th), Jude (who has been
    wanting the sea), Kiva (who held this man's purse pole in the dark).
  - **The handed crossing is used live for the first time**, by Seven and one borrowed still
    door from another circuit (Trewin of the Tarn is planted for it, and was late a count in
    the yard), against something live; **it holds.** It may hold at a cost and in a way no
    rehearsal predicted. It does not close a tear, kill anything, or mend a bank, and it cannot
    be done by strangers. Students close no tear: the Hunt is under company officers and
    cuts on their slate.
  - The Hunt scores clean yield after shares, less every remark. Seven's place on it, with the
    Bench and the Carry, is the exit below.

**Exit.** After three boards **Seven stands ninth**, a place off the roll of eight. The
Ropewalk's captain, **Juniper Carrow**, has shut one of her own doors and is learning to
declare. The Measure goes on under a death. The Lane (Thursday the 17th to Sunday the 20th) is
next, and Kiva's seventh crossing is possible from Thursday the 17th.

## Promises to keep moving (consequential turns, not a script)

- **Plain ash and the Step.** Every team's ash is combed. Nothing killed comes up plain;
  richer than ever and nearly all of it reads I above trace. The sealed 2.0 stays sealed and
  bound; if anyone tries to buy, borrow or break it, the binding is Seven's own signed word.
- **The harbour clock.** The prediction stands under seal. Hart's hand on the grating, Mara's
  hand in her pocket, Peverell's pumps. Someone must decide, before the 23rd, whether to touch
  a cell. The tide's behaviour at Saltmere (the basin slack moves only a minute day to day,
  against the sea gate's table lagging twenty-three minutes) was left unexplained on the page;
  use or retire it deliberately, and do not explain what the pressure is.
- **The notch** after a single cliff (seven seconds, then six at the gate mast) and the Step's
  count quickening (48 in 2402; 50 in 2604; 57 now): keep the evidence accumulating fairly.
  The public line this movement is "the worst Hunt in memory", a bad rift season, a gifted
  generation. Nobody says the Unroofed Sky.
- **Rhea Sorn** has one night left on her card, which ends on the 30th; Pike's hands close
  halfway; Rhea and the Company officer Yeo both lose someone if the death is a Company
  catcher.
- **Jude** wants to see the sea and to know what ash is for. The Company is watching; no draft
  is made yet.
- **Dray** is on two feet; she has not changed her mind about Kiva. **Kellan** stands for
  Halden License on the other side of the Bench and the Hunt; his house is at the Counting House.
- **Kiva** can say what the thing did and what it meant for the catcher, in plain words, to
  somebody who can use it. She is not the only hand that can do the thing; that is the book.
  Let a Saltmere catcher read her seven columns.

## What must remain true / withheld

- One crossing in seven days at most; the next is Thursday the 17th at the soonest. No stack;
  no three-door (the three clean stay three; no fourth). Nothing heals the two fingers. If
  Kiva makes any crossing this movement, the card and the price are said first and it is
  small.
- **Students close no tear.** The Hunt cuts on the company officers' slate. The death is not
  the student's fault and not the author's lesson.
- The Lane brackets, the Charter Board's hearing, the sealed page being opened, the two Glory
  masters' testimony, the Graving as a national board and the Slack are not played this
  movement. Do not bring the point of light or the hull.
- The words **Unroofed Sky** and **Breach** do not appear. The dream stays private to Kiva and
  Mara (plus the fever to Aske, Pru, Orla, Perrin and Seven). She has told nobody of the white
  lights. If the dream recurs it costs her in the body first, and she does not tell. The disk
  stays cold.
- Do not completely remove the filter; no pure Glory presence worldwide; no cause of the Fall;

  the Charter Board, not the Proprietor.

## Craft lessons from Movements Two and Three (measured, simulated, same-family)

- Movement Two came out bimodal (short beats plus 60–90-word chains); Movement Three
  over-corrected (forty-word sentences 0.4% against the formula's ~3.3%; median 7). Write
  both: clear 8–25 word narration sentences, short beats for contrast, and in each chapter a
  few well-built 35–55 word sentences with one main clause and clear subordination, readable
  aloud, in descriptive and reflective passages. No *and … and* chains over about 45 words.
- Scenes were too short and too frequent (sections averaging 625 words, 14.5 breaks per 10k
  against ~950 and ~8.7). Do not cut a continuous fight, dinner or board with a section break;
  break where place, time or viewpoint changes. Developed scenes near 900–1,000 words.
- A shared idiom spread to almost every mouth ("I'd want it said", "I'd like it known",
  "said nothing", blushing). Keep each person's voice distinct: Jude diffident ("I'd want it
  said"), Anwen "I want it said", Perrin alone "I'd like it known", Pru scarlet, Aske's price
  speeches, Orla rough and brief, Hart plain and gauge-like, Mara dry, Kellan sums, Coombe
  drills, not speeches. Ration "said nothing", negation runs and causal *since* (use *because*).
- Each figure, rule and tally arrives once. Check every sum, weekday, count of nights, minute
  and score against the calendar before it is spoken; the readers love the exact counting and
  catch the slips. Board totals must add up (a lane that sums to 5–4 is not 6–4).
- Door vocabulary: a **plank** is two like doors on one ground; a **stack** or a **lay** is
  unlike doors open and agreeing (the Ropewalk's *laid* doors are a lay); a **ward** is the hand
  door's tray; people are not "wards"; "hand door" is the hand tradition, not Kiva's cold hand.
- The contests that worked had one idea a child could state and an adult could appreciate,
  geometry stated, both sides adapting, and a visible cost. Teach rules through attempts.
