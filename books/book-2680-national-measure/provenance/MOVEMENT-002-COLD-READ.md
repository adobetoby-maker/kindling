## Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Chapter 9 opens on the countdown chalked on the shed door and Orla's sum ("four, in this city"). That is quiet and full of numbers. I was hooked at the Pit, when the voice on the steps says "Fours… Declare" (ch 9 l.197) and Coombe opens the cage (l.239). A three-husk pack that heals a cut made on the beat and falls to a cut made on the "and" is a fresh, readable puzzle. It is solved by the funniest person in the book ("I was a clock," l.345). After that the movement keeps paying out new problems:

- the lodger going to two agreeing doors (ch 11 l.247);
- Kellan writing Seven down, followed by the lane (ch 12);
- the Flask floor and "*Nobody!*" (ch 13 l.267);
- Rhea standing on the skin, and Pike crying on the tailboard (ch 14 l.349);
- stopping the mill wheel (ch 15 l.211);
- the kiln husk that is "not plain" (ch 15 l.485).

**Where it slipped.**
- **Ch 10:** the "make it walk" sheet and the turn. The uphill and downhill logic (l.193) takes two passes to follow.
- **Ch 11:** the night hunt at the tram sheds is a third run of a pattern I already knew: the van, Rhea's slate, then "What's there? Mace. Nairn. Pike."
- **Ch 14, first third:** the hand report, the Orla and Chair scene, and the tap rematch. All three are fine, and all three are low-pressure.
- **Ch 16 l.31:** Jude reads out the whole month's ledger. It is accurate, and I skimmed it.

**Who matters.** Perrin. He moves from "I'd like it known that I didn't see him" to holding a licence "under its captain's name" that he does not want. That is the clearest arc in the movement. Pike is the emotional floor under it, and Kiva is the eye that ties it together.

**What I expect next.** At the Saltmere gate, the fifth crossing on a sanctioned gate mast, read under Dray's rule, while the hand now sulks for two nights. Mara arrives in the first week. Orla goes looking for Hart. Someone notices what Kiva saw "when there was no roof."

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## The two readers

These are lenses, not research into real readers.

**A fluent thirteen-year-old** would love:
- the juniors pretending to be husks ("That's horrible. Do it again");
- Coombe as a hard, funny mentor;
- the lodger's creepiness;
- Perrin with his eyes shut;
- the mill.

They would follow the fights, because each fight turns on one idea a child can state. They would skim the ledgers, the slates, the Chair's office and Mara's letters. They would get lost in door vocabulary when "ward" means both a person and a door (ch 11 l.299), and in "since" used to mean "because" (about 100 uses of "since" across the movement, many of them causal). They would continue.

**An adult genre reader** would enjoy the competence-driven story: a system of powers with real costs, a ledger that actually adds up, losses that teach, and the kiln husk turning out not to be plain, which is a grown-up refusal of easy wish-fulfilment. They would notice:
- the mannerisms (23 knot-tying beats, 21 of them the exact phrase "tied a knot"; "I'd like it known" 11; "something moved at the corner of her mouth" in several mouths);
- the repeated van-hunt routine;
- rules introduced just before they are needed (ch 14 l.247, "falls away from the edge that cut it");
- scenes told from Kellan's, Orla's and Mara's point of view under chapter titles that say "Kiva:".

They would continue.

## Repair brief (three priorities)

The developed action stays at full length: the Pit pack, the lane, the Flask floor and the mill. None of these should be shortened.

### 1. Thin the repeated rituals and verbal tics

- **Where:** the whole movement.
  - There are 23 knot-tying beats: "tied a knot" 21 times (ch 9: 3, ch 10: 6, ch 11: 4, ch 12: 3, ch 13: 5), plus "tied another knot" and "tied the last knot" in ch 9.
  - "I'd like it known" occurs 11 times, and it has spread beyond Perrin.
  - "corner of … mouth" appears in Mara, Coombe, Kellan and Thrale.
  - "thirty years" is used for both Coombe and Vane.
  - The van-slate template runs in ch 11 and again in ch 14.
  - The month's ledger is read out in full at ch 16 l.31.
- **Issue:** mannerisms used as punctuation, plus a hunt routine repeated three times.
- **Effect on the reader:** the young reader feels the repetition and skims. The adult reader sees the mannerism, and the beats that matter (Coombe's knots, Perrin's "known") lose their charge.
- **Scope:** line-level cuts only.
  - Keep about a third of the knot beats, at Coombe's entrances and verdicts.
  - Return "I'd like it known" to Perrin alone.
  - Open the ch 14 van scene partway through the slate routine.
  - Compress the ch 16 month read to a few lines plus the final sum.
  - Give Vane a different marker of experience.
- **Preserve:** the ledger arithmetic, which adds up exactly. Keep enough knots that the coiled rope left on the step (ch 13) still pays off.

### 2. Make the rules and the point of view clear on one reading

- **Where:**
  - Door vocabulary: ch 11 l.299; ch 13 l.217, l.221, l.375.
  - The skin and slope chain: ch 10 l.193.
  - The rung rule introduced at the point of use: ch 14 l.247.
  - Coombe's lodger reversal: ch 13 l.107.
  - Unmarked point-of-view changes under "Kiva:" titles: ch 12 l.3 (Kellan), ch 14 l.29 (Orla), ch 16 l.133 (Mara).
- **Issue:** terms that collide, spatial logic carried by "since" clauses, and narrators who change without warning.
- **Effect on the reader:** a forced reread in the middle of a scene. The point-of-view jumps briefly disorient the reader about whose knowledge is on the page.
- **Scope:**
  - Swap single words ("ward" → "still door"; "plank" → name the unlike pair).
  - Turn one "since" chain in ch 10 into two short sentences.
  - Plant the rung-falls-away-from-the-edge rule earlier, as a single line.
  - Give Coombe one line explaining that a licence floor is not a demonstration.
  - Adopt a section-break convention or chapter-title convention for non-Kiva sections.
- **Preserve:** the Kellan opening of ch 12, which is among the strongest pages in the movement. Signal it; do not remove it.

### 3. Fix the small continuity slips

- **Where:** inconsistencies 1–8 above, plus the stray closing quotation mark at ch 14 l.143.
- **Issue:** wrong ownership, wrong tallies, and miscounted days or places.
- **Effect on the reader:** attentive readers, and anyone tracking the ledger, will catch them. They undercut the book's main pleasure: everything counts.
- **Scope:** a word or a phrase each. No restructuring.
- **Preserve:** the countdown device. On the 28th, "Sixteen" with "Nobody had told him to count it" is a fine closing beat.

## Uncertainty and trade-offs

- This is one model's simulated read of one movement. It does not predict how real readers or sales would respond, and it does not show which drafting setup is better.
- Most pacing complaints are about ritual rather than scenes, so trimming is low-risk. Trimming Coombe's knots too hard would flatten her, so the trade-off favours trimming by counting rather than by rewriting.
- The point-of-view question may be a deliberate series convention I cannot see without the plan. If so, item 2 shrinks to the door vocabulary and the geometry.
