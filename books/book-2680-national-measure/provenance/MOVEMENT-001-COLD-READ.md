## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** It caught on the first line: a parcel "tied by somebody with ten fingers", and a girl who can't press a wax seal and says so. Her answer "Says so, mostly" (ch01:63) gives the whole book's question in three words. The two voices saying *now* at drum nineteen (ch01:259) is the first real payoff. It solves a problem with no power and no meter, and it reads like a small detective story. Jude's question "What are we taking you to Saltmere for?" (ch02:181–185) became the spine I read the rest of the movement against. Other scenes held me firmly:
- Pru's lifting thumb (ch02:221)
- the litter drop "between *yours* and *mine*" (ch04:39)
- the iron test, where the "signal" turns out to be Kiva's own pulse (ch04:389)
- the frame going to dashes during a crossing (ch05:307)
- Pike's three-and-a-half things in the van (ch05:467–519)
- the Old Yard game, where Brask turns Kiva's own good habit into a tell (ch06:329)

The purse string at the coal wharf (ch07) is where every plant pays off, and it hit hard.

**Where it slipped.** Interest slipped in five places:
- The Charter Board notice and the talk that follows (ch02:3–110) is mostly rules.
- The first half of ch03 (method transcription and the roof deliberation, ch03:51–211) is important, but it is static.
- The Saltmere packet (ch04:125–237) piles drum, cell and tide detail onto a reader who is still holding ch01's mechanics.
- Kellan's interior section (ch06:417–end) is close to a rehearsal of the speech he gives at the table in ch08:205–271. The second time through, I knew what he would say.
- The ash arithmetic in ch08 is recited twice (table, then ash office). It is in character, but the second recital added nothing for me.

**Which person matters.** Kiva first. Then, unexpectedly, Pike, the square grey man who is put out by being seen. Perrin is the warmth. Kellan's departure is the most honest decision in the movement.

**What I expect next.** The team has 3.84 drams uncommitted, so the "hunt in the hills" Perrin predicts (ch08) has to pay before Saltmere. I expect the Marshal's floor in the second month, Pike's two February nights with Kiva holding the far edge, and a purse string or something like it on tidal ground at the Hunt. I also expect Kellan's nine red tags to cost Halden on a board marked on clean yield, and Jessamy Hart to reply in person at Saltmere.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 3. Canon, state, power, knowledge and disclosure checks

No editorial canon evidence was supplied. Everything here is internal consistency only.

### Verified internally consistent

All of these reconcile on 30-day months, which is the only internal assumption the text needs:
- **Calendar.** On the 17th of month 10, Kiva has "ten and four days" left, which lands on the 1st of the year (ch05 opening). The 1st of month 1 is a Friday (ch03), so Monday the 4th, Thursday the 7th, Saturday the 9th and 16th, and Friday the 29th all fall on the right days. The gate opens on Tuesday the 1st of month 3 and the Bench is on Monday the 14th, as the ch02 notice says. The "44" days (ch08) also checks. So do ch04's "five weeks and six days" on the 20th of month 11 and Orla's "three weeks and five days" on the 5th of month 12.
- **Ash ledger.** 25.21 − 0.57 = 24.64, then −15.0 = 9.64, −4.0 = 5.64, −1.8 = 3.84. The sealed 0.8 is unchanged throughout. The per-session spends sum correctly (ch02: 0.32; ch06: 0.57).
- **Re-reading deltas.** The numbers match what each speaker claims (ch05: Draw 3.1→1.6; Edge +0.1; Ward +1.9; Stance +0.3).
- **Old Yard score.** The carry-by-carry tally reaches 6–5 (ch06).
- **Bags at the wharf.** Four rungs at 60 drams give 240 drams by the book, and 198 were tinned (ch07).

### Apparent inconsistencies to resolve against the project ledger

1. **Real month names in a numbered calendar.** "November had gone by in small pieces" (ch04:7), "It's January" (ch05:483) and "February," said Pike (ch08:99). Elsewhere the text always uses "the tenth month", "the second month" and so on. ch04:7 is also internally wrong: the scene is the 20th of the eleventh month, so that month has not "gone by".
2. **"The twenty-ninth drum"** (ch03:297). ch01 lists the seven drums as 19, 20, 21, 25, 26, 27 and 28; 29 is one of the prime *counts*, not a drum. "A month to the day" fits the night of the 6th of month 10 at the pump house, so the intended referent may be the bank's streets.
3. **"Three nights ago"** (ch08:95). The wharf call came on Friday night and closed about 1:40 on Saturday; this scene is Sunday afternoon. That is two nights at most.
4. **Credit wobble** (ch08:349). Kiva lists "a mark that wanted holding before it was tested" among her own observations. In ch06:341 the idea is Perrin's, and earlier in ch08 Kiva herself tells him "You thought of it. I didn't." What Kiva actually saw was the signpost (ch06:329). A careful reader will notice the overclaim.
5. **Scheduling tension, not a contradiction.** In ch07's closing section Rhea gives Pike "two next month and two the month after" as nights with Kiva as his second. The month after is the third month, when the Saltmere gate opens on the 1st and the Lane runs to the 19th. It is workable, but worth confirming that a plan accounts for it.
6. **Knowledge boundary.** Kiva knows the surname "Hart" (ch03:173, ch03:449). The card is signed only "J.H.", and in the supplied text Orla never says the name to her. This is probably Book 1 canon; I cannot verify it here.
7. **POV labelling.** All eight chapter titles read "Kiva:", but sections in ch02, ch04, ch06 and ch07 are told from other viewpoints. This may be a deliberate series convention. I flag it for clarity, not as an error.

### Physical state (consistent across the movement)

- **Kiva:** two fingers of her left hand report pressure only; she has a bad knee (ch05, ch08); her single-door permission dates from the 1st.
- **Mara:** her left hand has come back and her right is still silent.

- **Dray:** she has moved from a short leg board to two sticks.

### Power limits (respected)

She makes one crossing (Thursday the 7th), uses only the still door on the 16th, and uses no door at the wharf. "The longer thing" never appears. The two-finger sense is used only as "a reason to look" (ch07:99–113), which matches its calibration in ch04. The frame's "Flame, with two held shut" and its dashes during the crossing stay consistent with the Chair's earlier statement that she has "no column".

### Reserved disclosures (handled with discipline on the page)

- Kiva keeps the roof/fever question and Lumb's leaf out of the copy she sends Hart, and gives evidential reasons for doing so (ch03:137–211).
- Orla never sees "the pile".

## 4. Repair brief (three priorities at most)

These are scoped to keep the developed action the owner asked for. Nothing below shortens a fight.

### Priority 1: orient the "far edge" at the climax (clarity at the movement's peak)
- **Location:** ch07:155–179. Secondary: ch07:121–125, where the cloth layout is first described.
- **Observed issue:** At the decisive moment the reader must place three things at once: the rising line, Pike's knee at the head, and the river-side corners held by Purvis and Hobday. "Far edge" is far from Pike; Kiva kneels on the east hem, a pace and a half down. One plain orienting clause is missing, so on a first pass a reader can briefly picture the fold coming from the wrong side.
- **Effect on the reader:** a beat of re-reading inside the scene's fastest stretch, which softens the "Pike. Far edge." line.
- **Proposed scope:** add one or two orienting phrases or punctuation fixes, such as naming the downhill river-side hem when the line first appears and when Kiva speaks. **Change no beats and cut no length.**
- **Preserve:** the six-to-fourteen count of the heap sinking, Kiva shutting her teeth before she speaks, the reach, the haul by the collar, the fold opening empty, and "That's skin."

### Priority 2: Kellan's decision is told twice
- **Location:** ch06:417–end (interior reasoning) against ch08:205–271 (the table speech).
- **Observed issue:** The same reasoning appears nearly word for word in both places: "the weak corner", "I looked for something better", the nine red tags and their consequence. As a result the table scene confirms what the reader already knows instead of revealing it.
- **Effect on the reader:** the movement's most honest character beat lands a little flat the second time, and the ch08 table runs long.
- **Proposed scope:** decide where the articulation lives. One option keeps ch06 on the felt weight and the decision ("You'll have my name") and holds back the "weak corner" phrasing and the red-tag implication for ch08. The reverse also works. This is trimming or relocation inside ch06's interior only. Dialogue stays as is unless it repeats.
- **Preserve:** "I want to know what I am", Birch's "it wanted more nerve to stop where you were", Kiva's "I'm glad you didn't [ask]", and the cuff figures handed over as something owed.

### Priority 3: front-loaded system vocabulary in the opening and the Charter notice
- **Location:** ch01:1–71 and ch02:3–110.
- **Observed issue:** The first pages lean on unexplained series terms before the stakes are clear. The Charter notice then delivers boards, marks, the roll, licences, clean yield and the warrant as one block of dialogue.
- **Effect on the reader:** a returning or younger reader has to work out the meaning before they can care. The adult lens copes; the thirteen-year-old lens skims.
- **Proposed scope:** add a few in-scene glosses through action, not a recap. For example, one clause on what "dark" and "doors" mean for Kiva right now, and one Kellan line on what the Hunt board means for *this* team. In ch02, consider carrying part of the rules exchange forward to where it bites: clean yield could wait for ch06's red tags, and the captain rules for ch08.
- **Preserve:** the mate exchange word for word, Kellan's "cheerful half of the sum" line, Noa's "I looked", and Dray's "Push, Marten".

### Mechanical fixes (outside the three priorities)
Clear §3 items 1 to 4 in a copyedit pass after checking them against STATE_LEDGER. Separately, decide whether the two catchphrases (§2, humour row) should thin out in ch08. That is a voice decision for the owner, not a repair.

## 5. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Lenses are not people.** The thirteen-year-old and adult views are heuristics applied by one model reader. They are not panel data and they support no sales or market claims.
- **Same reader, both lenses.** Both columns came from one reading, so they are correlated. Treat a gap between them as a hypothesis worth testing with a human reader.
- **Silent read.** The read-aloud scores come from a silent read. A recorded read-aloud would test them better.
- **What one movement can and cannot show.** It cannot show whether the slow middle (ch03–ch04) is a problem across the whole book, or only here as set-up for ch07's payoff. On this evidence the payoff largely justifies the set-up.
