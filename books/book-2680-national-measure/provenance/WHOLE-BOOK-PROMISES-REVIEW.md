## 7. Repeated shapes, power, secondaries, villains, Glory limits

- **Set pieces.** Each movement's set piece has a different shape. One is a night call in a coal wharf. Two is a hunt at
  a mill. Three is seeding lanes and a coast night. Four is a board in three kinds with a death. Five is three lanes and
  two hearings. Six is a scheduled daylight operation with a written plan.
- **The motif.** The two-word far-edge call recurs, from the Hythe (late, Pike hurt) to the Tanyard (in time, Pike
  whole). It is a rhyme across the book, not a repeated plot.
- **Fevers.** The fever beside a Glory master recurs three times, with variation: dream (Two), "Nothing came" (Three,
  Five). It is then inverted: no fever (ch. 46:345). That is controlled repetition.
- **Power.** Stakes rise with a new idea each time: packs, lay, coast forms, the stow's sleeve, and then a thing made of
  held water that must not spill. Kiva ends with no new door, two certified crossings, and a slower card. Her gain is a
  method other people call without her (ch. 47:107–133). There is no power inflation.
- **Secondaries.** The beats are spread across all ten named in §4 of the map. Noa, Pike, Aske, Peverell, Adlam, Yeo,
  Coombe and Lyle each close a small want. See §3 for lens balance.
- **Villains.** There are none. Halden's counsel is "honest" (ch. 36:35). Halden's clerk is "doing his work" (ch. 32:147).
  The Harbour Committee's chairman "had only wanted to hold something" (ch. 47:195). The Proprietor is the book's most
  sympathetic opponent.

## 9. Findings

### P1 — CONTRADICTION — Yeo tells Mrs. Penhale before her son has died

- **Location:** ch. 46:175.
- **Text:** "I know. Mrs. Yeo told me on Saturday. She came down to tell me before she carded you."
- **Problem:** Davy Penhale dies at Mallow Creek on Wednesday the 16th (ch. 32:301, 32:415; ledger M4.3). Jude's Company
  card is already pinned on the draft board when Kiva first sees the board, at dawn on Thursday the 17th (ch.
  33:249–253, "There were already eleven cards on it… one card near the middle, in the Company's green"). Jude places
  Yeo at the board on Wednesday night (ch. 35:50). The only Saturday before the carding is the 12th, four days before
  Mrs. Penhale's son died. She cannot have been asked then whether she minded Jude taking a place in his crew.
- **Evidence:** ch. 33:249–255; ch. 35:43–50 (the card text and "She was at the board on Wednesday night"); ch. 48:57
  ("Yeo carded me. On Saturday."), which shows that *carded* means a card put up, not signed. No movement review saw this
  because the card is in Five and the line is in Six. `MOVEMENT-006-EDITORIAL-REVIEW.md` has no entry for it.
- **Smallest fix (one phrase):** "on Saturday" → "on Wednesday night". She came to tell the mother the same night, before
  the card went up, which also deepens the scene. No other line depends on the day.

### P2 — CONTRADICTION — Kellan's "twice to counsel"

- **Location:** ch. 47:253.
- **Text:** "I said no to counsel twice. Twice to a card with a line on the back that said *given through the
  foundation's counsel, as the foundation's.*"
- **Problem:** On the page there is one such card and one written refusal (ch. 36:11–17 and 36:65–69: "*No. I'll give my
  figures under my own name… K. Renn.*"). Book One has no counsel at all (`grep -i counsel` on
  `books/book-2680-opening/manuscript/`: 0 hits). The Proprietor's "You've said it to this house twice" (ch. 47:247) is
  supported, but by a different pair: the dinner, where Kellan refuses to give her count ("I'll not say it at this table,
  sir", ch. 22:267), and the card. Kellan, who counts everything, gets his own count wrong while explaining it.
- **Smallest fix (one line):** "I said no to counsel twice. Twice to a card with a line on the back…" → "I said no at his
  table, and I said no to counsel, on a card with a line on the back…". The rest of the speech stands.

### P3 — CONTRADICTION — the Barrel weighs less than a Flask

- **Location:** ch. 46:125.
- **Text:** "Two hundred and eighty-six drams… and four-tenths."
- **Problem:** The book teaches the yield scale once, plainly, in ch. 2:71: "A Handful's four-and-twenty drams by the
  book, a Flask is the best part of five hundred." It places the Barrel above the Flask (ch. 2:47, "between a Flask and a
  Well"). It confirms the per-rung scale with the Hythe string: "a hundred and ninety-eight drams of two hundred and forty
  by the book" (ch. 7:529). Forty-four chapters later, the first Barrel on the page, the thing "nobody'd ever drawn this
  size into" a purse (ch. 46:97) and "heavier than anything she had ever carried" (ch. 44:195), weighs 286.4. That is
  under the Flask the reader was given, and barely over a four-rung string. The payoff is smaller than its own plant.
  Nobody on the page remarks on it.
- **Evidence:** `MOVEMENT-006-EDITORIAL-REVIEW.md:170` checked 286.4 only against shares and evidence, not against ch.
  2, so this is visible only at the whole-book layer. No dram on the book depends on the figure: it is all red, held as
  evidence, no share.
- **Smallest fix (owner/author choice):**
  - (a) Raise the one number at ch. 46:125 above five hundred, and carry it to `STATE_LEDGER.md` M6.3 and
    `MOVEMENT-006-CONTINUITY.md` §8, which both record 286.4.
  - (b) If the author means it to be light, add one clause at ch. 46:125 in the clerk's or Coombe's mouth, saying it is
    under a Barrel by the book and why, in the house manner of ch. 7:529 ("…of [N] by the book").
  - Either is one line. Do not change ch. 2.

### P4 — CLARITY — "seventy-seven years" of belief against "a year after the window"

- **Location:** ch. 47:183 ("*I believed for seventy-seven years that the lamps that keep time together are the ones that
  go white*") and ch. 47:233 ("I've believed one thing for seventy-seven years").
- **Problem:** The reading came "a year after the window, and I've had it since I was twenty-seven" (ch. 39:185). He was
  twenty-six at the window in 2604 (ch. 39:145) and is 103 now. That makes 2605 to 2681, which is seventy-six years.
  "Waited seventy-seven years to see the lamps do it again" (ch. 47:225) is right, because it counts from the window. The
  ch. 39 clause came from the Movement Five repair (ledger M5.7), and ch. 47 follows the map's older "seventy-seven" (§3).
  Few listeners will do the sum. Those who do will find the Proprietor, a counting man, off by one in his own sworn
  slip.
- **Smallest fix (one word, twice):** "seventy-seven" → "seventy-six" at ch. 47:183 and 47:233 only. Alternatively, the
  owner may accept 77 as a round figure in an old man's mouth and leave all three.

### P5 — CLARITY — "I shall have to" tell Birch

- **Location:** ch. 8:275.
- **Text:** "I've not said it to Birch. I shall have to. I thought that you might as well hear what I'm walking into."
- **Problem:** This is a stated intention that promises a scene. Birch is last on the page at ch. 12:61, as somebody to
  hide from, and the conversation about the nine red tags never happens or is reported. The substance is paid
  elsewhere: Halden License places only sixth on the Hunt, 8.2 clean with one remark (ch. 32:297), and Kellan's figures
  against the house carry the red-tag thread to ch. 36. So only the sentence dangles.
- **Note:** ch. 8 is an accepted chapter with an accepted audio edit, but that edit concerns ch. 8:195, not this line
  (`AUDIO-CLARITY-08-09-REPAIR.md:70`).
- **Smallest fix:** cut "I shall have to." (three words). Or add one clause later, for example in ch. 12, that he had
  told Birch and what Birch said. The cut is the smaller.

### P6 — CLARITY — Perrin's envy turn reported rather than staged

- **Location:** ch. 47:279 ("*I'd give my right arm for a board with only my name on it.*" … "He said so, in the loft, to
  my face").
- **Problem:** Map §9 promises that he "finds that being envied is worse". The only staged envy is ch. 35:61–65, where
  Gower has "nothing unkind in his face" and counts two cards over a pasty. Perrin already draws the conclusion there
  ("It feels like Gower eating a pasty and counting", ch. 35:79). The harder line in ch. 47 happens offstage, so the turn
  lands twice, the second time told. This is not a defect a reader would trip on; the arc's ending (the trunk crew, not
  calling *off*) is fully shown.
- **Smallest fix:** none required. Optionally, one clause at ch. 47:279 pointing back to the shown moment (the pasty at
  the draft board) so that the reported line reads as the same envy said harder.
