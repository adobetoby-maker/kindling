## 1. What kind of read this is

This is a **fresh-context simulated cold read**. It is not a human read, and it should not be treated as independent testing.

- **Fresh context.** I had no drafting conversation, plan, bible, canon file or earlier review in context. I read the eight supplied chapters in order (17 to 24) before forming any judgement.
- **Not independent.** The reviewer is the same model family that drafted the movement. A session-memory banner shown at startup also said that this system wrote chapters 17–24. My priors and blind spots are therefore likely to overlap with the author's. In particular, I may under-notice the voice tics that I flag below.
- **Tool use.** I used `grep` and `wc` on the eight chapter files in `manuscript/` only. The purpose was to fix exact line locations and count recurring phrases. Two grep patterns also matched one line each in chapters 10 and 16. I did not read those chapters or use them as evidence.
- **Seat labels.** The manuscript carried no model or seat labels, so nothing needed hiding. No other run is being compared, so section 8 of the rubric does not apply.

## 3. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**
- Jude at the rail counting waves: "It's every count at once" (ch17:19). This is a character-specific way into the sea, and the "count" motif then runs through the whole movement.
- The arrival at Saltmere, the low town lying below sea level, and the pumps' "four and four" felt through the boat's planking.
- The comb scene. The sealed tin's seventh reed stays silent, then the Tarn girl's grandfather's jar sings thin. The cost is moral, not physical, and it lands.
- Carrow's lay at the gate mast. The rule written to read *Kiva* now reads a whole town. This is the best single irony in the movement.
- Chapter 18's three carries. They are the action high point: easy to follow, with each side adapting to the other.
- Hart's barometer speech. Then Orla stops on "She's—" and Hart answers, quietly, "There."
- Chapter 20's slack-water cut. The silence through the pole is earned, and the count comes a beat late at dawn.
- The Hoop rising from the mud (ch21).
- The bench-book counts quickening from 48 to 57 (ch22).
- The Proprietor keeping his hand on the glass case, and Kiva's no (ch23).

**Where it slipped.**
- In chapter 17, the gate scene stops for a long passage on the history of the rule (around ch17:333).
- The comb queue and the Dray conversation run as a series of speeches.
- Chapter 19 opens with dressings and warrant logistics.
- Chapter 22 plays out of order: the noon hand check comes before the 11:30 visit.
- Chapter 24's back half is a chain of codas: board, Trewin, Orla, the yes/no book, Hart, Monday morning, banister. Each is good, but taken together they feel like a long goodbye.
- Across the movement, the same verbal habits come from almost every speaker: "I'd want it said", "I'd like it known", "went red/scarlet".

**Which person matters.** Kiva, chiefly for what she refuses: Hart's question, the Proprietor's price, the white-lamp disclosure. Close behind are Jude, whose "what is ash for" question finds an answer at the kitchen wall, and Pru, who moves from reserve to the catcher who watches hands. Hart and Orla carry the strongest adult scene.

**What I expect next.**
- The sealed prediction opening on the 23rd.
- Mara deciding whether to read or stop the Regiment.
- The Proprietor taking a second, less polite route.
- Perrin's Carry, and the seventh crossing at "Thursday at the soonest".
- Rhea's card ending on the thirtieth.
- The Longshore with Carrow and Lyle laid on the flood, set up by the lodger warning.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. Chapters 20 and 23 end with real pressure.

## 4. The two lenses

These are reading stances, not research into real audiences.

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.**
- Hooks: the Ropewalk fight, the kiddle and the purses, the Hoop, a 103-year-old man who never lifts his hand off a glass case, the fever, Pru being sick behind the van after her first lane. All of these would hold.
- Friction: the terminology load. Doors, stance, ward, edge, Hold, catcher, lay, stack, gapper, cliff, notch, slack, purse, fathom and Step all arrive together.
- Several words carry more than one meaning. "Hand" is both Kiva's literal cold hand and the ward or "hand door" ("She's a hand door", ch24:83). "Hold" is both Anwen's role and a reading ("Her Hold reads Edge three-one").
- Number-dense passages slow this reader down: dram hundredths, notch seconds, tide minutes.
- This lens would probably skim the keeper's room scenes in ch21 and ch23. It would also feel the "I'd want it said" habit as sameness, without being able to name it.

**Adult genre reader.**
- Will value the procedural texture, the restraint, and the fact that everyone in Seven loses honestly. The moral argument also works: a cheap, quiet, communal technique (the lay) set against Meridian's careful gaps, mirrored by the Proprietor's "step the country".
- Will notice that the whole cast speaks in one courteous register, so characters sound alike.
- Will notice that everyone tells everyone everything. Adlam's line "Everybody in this town tells me everything" names it but does not fix it.
- Will notice the numbers that don't add up (section 6).

## 7. Repair brief (three priorities)

The developed action the owner asked for stays. **Do not shorten ch18's three carries or ch20's two slack-water cuts.** Both earn their length.

### Priority 1: thin the shared verbal habits so the characters sound different

- **Location.** All eight chapters. Counts: "I'd want" 28, "want it said" 11, "I'd like it known" 7, "so that it's said" 3, scarlet or "went red" 13, "in two stages" 5, "did not say anything" 14. Heaviest in ch22 (Jude, Kellan), ch24 (Perrin, Jude, Pru) and ch21.
- **Issue.** Jude, Perrin, Anwen, Aske, Hart, Orla, Kellan and Pru all use the same "say it before, not after" formula. Pru and Perrin share one blush.
- **Effect on the reader.** Characters blur into one voice. Read aloud, the formula turns from motif into tic. A thirteen-year-old feels the sameness; an adult names it.
- **Scope.** A line-level pass. Keep the formula where it is *about price or consent*: Aske's price speeches, Perrin's "beaten fairly" book line, Kiva's "I'm saying it so that it's said" before the Counting House. Make it Jude's signature and vary or cut it elsewhere. Keep Pru's scarlet and give Perrin a different physical tell.
- **Strength to preserve.** The thematic spine: saying a cost out loud *before* acting (Aske, the card, Rhea's "I wrote it so that I'd not have to decide it at one in the morning").

### Priority 2: fix the numbers and the clock

- **Location.** A1 (ch19:279), A2 (ch20:393), A3 (ch23:3), A4 (ch21:227–229), T1 (ch20:319/341), T2 (ch22:91–107), T3 (ch24:181 vs the closing scene), K1 (ch21:349).
- **Issue.** Scores, bag counts, night counts and minute stamps contradict each other in a book whose subject is exact counting.
- **Effect on the reader.** An attentive adult loses trust in the procedural texture that is the movement's main pleasure. Readers following Rhea's slate or the seeding board catch it.
- **Scope.** Sentence-level fixes:
  - Add one down for Seven in ch19 carry one or two.
  - Change "six bags" to "seven".
  - Change "two nights" to "three".
  - Change 6:35 to 6:33, or "twenty-one" to "twenty-three".
  - Make the ch20 opening time 6:10.
  - Move the noon/4/10 p.m. hand checks after the Adlam scene.
  - Settle whether Seven stands at the Bench.
  - Change Hart's "said" to what Mara actually said.

  Punctuation fix while in ch19:245: "Your sponsor's in town?"
- **Strength to preserve.** The lane geometry, Rhea's slate format, and the Hoop measurements (4′1″, then 4′6½″, then a sealed 4′11″). The numbers already carry the drama; they only need to agree.

### Priority 3: give a first-time reader footholds on overloaded terms and the ch17 exposition

- **Location.**
  - ch17 around line 333: the paragraph on the rule's history, opening "Kiva knew about the rule".
  - The first uses of "hand" as a door: ch18 crossing ("Hand. To Ember."), ch24:83 "She's a hand door".
  - "Hold" as both role and reading (ch22, Kellan's recital).
  - The "Kiva:" chapter headings over long sections from other viewpoints (ch18 Anwen, ch19 Orla, ch20 Rhea, ch21 Mara, ch22 Kellan).
- **Issue.** On one pass, the ward/hand door and Kiva's literal cold hand blur together. The rule's history arrives as a block in the middle of the scene.
- **Effect on the reader.** The younger lens stalls exactly where the stakes are being set up, at the gate and at the first crossing.
- **Scope.**
  - One clarifying clause the first time "hand door" appears in this movement.
  - Break the ch17 rule history into two or three beats around Carrow's walk to the cross, rather than one paragraph before it.
  - Optionally, add a short viewpoint cue at each section break that leaves Kiva. Most breaks already name the viewpoint character in their first sentence, so this is minor.
- **Strength to preserve.** The irony of the rule, written for Kiva and now reading a whole town, and Dray's "That's fairer". Both depend on the reader understanding the rule, so clarify it; don't cut it.

## 8. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Same-family reader.** This reviewer shares the author's model family. The voice-habit finding (Priority 1) is the one most likely to be *under*-weighted here, and it came out first anyway. An independent or human read should be expected to rate it at least as high.
- **Counting method.** The phrase counts come from `grep` and include narration as well as dialogue. They show density, not a target. No word-count or rhythm target was used for scoring.
- **Missing canon.** Without canon, items such as T5 (ferry or barge) and K2 (when Lyle told Carrow) may already be settled in earlier books or movements. Treat them as questions to check, not errors.
- **Limits of one movement.** This read covers one movement and cannot predict series-level reception or sales. The two lens scores are separate judgements; do not combine them into a release gate.
