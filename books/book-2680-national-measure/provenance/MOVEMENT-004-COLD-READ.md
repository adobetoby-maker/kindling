## Honesty about independence

This session has no drafting transcript, and I wrote none of the prose in this session. Two limits still apply:

1. **Same model family.** I can't confirm which model drafted the movement. If it was an Opus-class Claude model, my preferences probably overlap with the author's, so I may miss habits we share.
2. **Context leak.** Before I read anything, a session-start memory hook injected a short work log. It said this movement had just been drafted and gave labels such as "ch28 Perrin cracked rib, Anwen Tar Lane scene." So I knew roughly where the rib injury fell before I read it. I did not know about the death in ch 30–31 or how the rescue would work.

Nothing below claims to be independent reader testing, and nothing below is a sales forecast. The two reader "lenses" are editorial viewpoints, not demographic research.

## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where my interest caught.**
- **Ch 25, the gallery.** Kiva sees the needles *leaning* toward each other before anyone has found the fault cell. Then she looks down past the grey skirt and sees the water under the cells. The scene turns her restriction (she may not stand at the Bench) into her advantage, and that was the first moment I felt the book's engine running.
- **Ch 26, "Say the price."** The ladder logic is simple: a rung is iron set in granite, so a still door can plant on it. The scene doesn't play Perrin's ribs as a surprise. He states the cost and pays it.
- **Ch 27, the eleventh rung.** Kiva doesn't say *set* until his heel resettles. It's the best single beat in the movement. It's quiet, it's specific, and it pays off the word she offered the night before.
- **Ch 29, the slack.** Her fingers go silent, she raises a flat hand, and four posts stand up straight.
- **Ch 30, the closing line.** "Nothing on Mallow Creek had turned but the stow." A real cliff.
- **Ch 31, "Be the nearest thing."** The rescue is built from rules already planted: the fold goes to the nearest line, so the catcher becomes the nearest thing. It feels earned, not invented on the spot.

**Where it slipped.**
- **Ch 25, the Clerk's rules.** The rule block ("Six fours to a heat… salted from a red bag… two cells keeping one count is a step") is the densest stretch of exposition here. I followed it, but only just.
- **Ch 26, the tide tables.** Mara and Hart's columns are interesting to an adult, but they stall a reader who wants to get back to the lock.
- **Ch 28, the draw and the board.** A long run of reading the sheets out loud.
- **Ch 32, after the board.** The printer, the pump house, the Mercury and Ivey all come after the emotional peak. Each scene is good alone, but together they spread out the grief, and several of them restate facts we already have (see Priority 2).

**Who matters.** Perrin carries ch 25–28. Pru becomes the heart of ch 29–31 ("I could carry that"; the far-edge call; the doormat tray). Penhale is a short, strong secondary life, sharp enough that his death costs something. Kiva is steady at the centre as the one who *sees and says*, and the movement makes that her true skill without stating it as a lesson. Trewin's late, deep laugh is a lovely small gift.

**What I expect next.** The Lane on Thursday, with Seven ninth and one mark off the roll. Aske's "Thursday, soonest" for Kiva's seventh door. A letter from the Chair, which Orla tells Kiva to answer "in the same words." The 23rd at 6:09, with thirty-six hands taught by Hart. Halden's patent claim on all red ash. Whether the stow "hearing the turn" ties to Hart's springs "appointment" and Noa's 12:44 hair.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation, and I'd move straight to the Lane.

## 5. Repair brief (three priorities)

The owner asked for developed action, so **the ch 27 climb, the ch 30 Skerry Pill fight and the ch 31 pass keep their full length.** All three fixes are at sentence level.

### Priority 1 — Make the stow's swing trackable at the climax
- **Location.** ch 30:373–377 (from "It swung on the post…" to "…with Bridie Hebden under it"), plus the first two paragraphs of ch 31.
- **What I saw.** The sleeve swings "across the channel, to face the sea," yet comes down "on the mud on the west side of the channel." The reader isn't told which bank the deep-bend post stands near, which way the closed end travels, or where Bridie stands relative to the post. The "two ends" explanation comes only after Penhale is already under the sleeve.
- **Effect on the reader.** At the most important moment, a younger reader may lose track of who is under what, and an adult will reread. The horror is there, but it's blurred.
- **Proposed fix.** Three to six clauses. Give one fixed bearing for the far post (for example, "on the west side of the bend"). Replace "across the channel" with the arc the sleeve actually travels (from downstream to inland, over the west mud). Mention the closed end once *before* it lands on Penhale. Punctuation and word order are enough; add nothing to the scene's length.
- **Keep.** The contrast that the slack is still holding ("the grass stood up straight"). Penhale going in "as a man goes to a gate he has opened every day of his life." Kiva's unanswered "*Far edge*."

### Priority 2 — Telegraphing in ch 29, then restating in ch 31–32
- **Location.** ch 29:169 and ch 29:341 (Penhale twice says he has never gone onto the mud, plus "I'll not, likely"). ch 32 Kiva-to-Noa and Kiva-to-Ivey (the fact that it "turned before the water" is restated; it appears eight times across the movement). Coombe repeats "I'll read it" (ch 32:445).
- **What I saw.** In ch 29, an adult genre reader can predict Penhale's death from the doubled "never gone onto the mud" plus the mother line. After the death, the same facts come back almost word for word in Yeo's slate, the Rhea/Yeo yard scene, the Noa ledger and the Ivey shop scene.
- **Effect on the reader.** Shock turns into confirmation in ch 30. Then the aftermath feels like summary rather than discovery, which is where the 13-year-old lens most likely drifts.
- **Proposed fix.** Remove one of the two "never gone onto the mud" lines in ch 29. Keep "my mother likes it" once there and let Yeo echo it once in ch 31. In ch 32, cut Kiva's account to Ivey down to what is new: the far edge is "when the thing decides," a sleeve has two ends, and the book is written for a sheet. Keep Noa's ledger entry as the single formal record. Fix C from §3 at the same time.
- **Keep.** The fathom count ("He stopped at three"). Coombe adding Penhale's name to the printer's page. Ivey's "I'll have a second." Pike standing at the tailboard for three fathoms. The curlew. These echoes earn their place, and the cuts should be made around them.

### Priority 3 — Thin the catchphrases and reused similes, mainly in ch 28 and ch 32
- **Location.** "I'd want it said": ch 28 ×6, ch 32 ×7. Perrin's "I'd like it known" variants in ch 26–27. Repeated images: "frost on a window" (×3, ch 30–31), "like a sack going into a loft" (ch 26 and ch 27, at both the Ropewalk second run and Seven's finish), "as though nodding were a thing that had to be done properly" (ch 27 and ch 32), "the way a referee holds up a hand" (ch 25 and ch 27), "the warm low sound" (×3).
- **What I saw.** These tags give characters their voices and some are clearly deliberate motifs. At this density, though, they stop sounding like the characters and start sounding like the author.
- **Effect on the reader.** Read aloud, the repetition is very noticeable. Jude's grief lines in ch 32 lose force because the same formula carried his small remarks in ch 28. Overall warmth suffers slightly.
- **Proposed fix.** Line edits only. Cut Jude's formula to about two uses per chapter and save it for his weightiest lines: the bank and the sea in ch 32 ("I'd want it said that I've seen it come in now. Over somebody") should stay. Give each repeated simile one home and vary the others. Fix the two unclear references at the same time: ch 25:405 "He asked her, in her hand" → make clear it is the Chair's hand, and ch 27:241 Aske's position (D in §3).
- **Keep.** Kellan saying his sums out loud. Pettit's "I'm saying it about you." Pru's "It gets more usual. That's not the same." Perrin's "It's not better. Being envied." These lines work because they're rare and specific.

## 6. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **Not addressed here.** The length and number of viewpoints in ch 32 (14 sections, 9,389 words) could reasonably be called a fourth priority. I left it out because each strand plants something later movements likely need: Mara's thirty-six hands, Coombe's page, Halden's claim, Carrow learning one door. Deciding to tighten its structure needs the plan, which I didn't see.
- **Steep standings swings.** Seven goes from sixth to third to ninth. That's believable as a measure of honest work, but a reader who wants progress rewarded may feel the Hunt result is punishing. I think it's right; it remains a judgment call.
- **One movement isn't enough** to judge series-level patterns in the catchphrases, the "Kiva:" heading convention, or reserved disclosures. Treat findings G and H as questions for the owner.
- **No run comparison was made.** This is a single reading of a single draft, with no seat labels to hide.
