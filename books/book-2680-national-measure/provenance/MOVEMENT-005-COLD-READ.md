## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** At once. A boy with ink on his thumbs comes knocking at five in the morning, carrying a page with a dead man's name set at the top. It is concrete, sad and civic all at once. Interest held all through Aske's window. Perrin admitting he lifted the boards ("I'd like it known that I'd do it again" / "That's why it's one day") is the best small scene in the opening. It caught hard in the Tarn lane (ch33). Kiva sitting on the litter ("It's trying very hard at sitting") and the two catchers taking the dummy on their elbows is fresh, funny and earned. The lamp-man's second wire in ch40 ("if the girl can say it to a Board under her name he can say it to his warden under his") is the emotional peak of the movement for me. It pays off a seed planted in ch34 and is the movement's thesis made into an event.

**Where it slipped.** In the long proceedings. The Salt Hall in ch36 runs from the patent claim through the bench books, Kellan's figures, Peverell, Hart, Mara, Perrin, the sister and the Clerk before Kiva stands. Each part is good, but the chain is long and procedural. Then ch40 reads the evidence aloud again, including a clerk's summary of the Proprietor scene we watched one chapter earlier. The Holloway return is read in full three times (ch34, ch36, ch40). Interest also slipped at the ch37 climax, because the box declaration ("and no change … I'll hold you to it") seems to rule out the crossing that the scene then offers as a live choice (see §4, Priority 2). And once, it slipped into confusion: in ch39 the Proprietor quotes the Mercury reporting details of the ferry dream that, as far as the text shows, only Kiva's four ever heard (§3).

**Who matters.** Kiva first. She makes three real choices in this movement: keeping the crossing, putting her dream on the table, and telling the Proprietor about the white lights. Then Pru, whose arc from "I tore them" to calling the whole pen is the movement's clearest growth line. Then Mara, for holding back ("Who first?" instead of "Are you sure?"). Dray and Carrow are the best opponents in the book so far.

**What I expect next.** The Wednesday dawn slack: thirty-six fitters, thirty-six jars, the bank taken out of step at 6:09, and the sealed prediction opened at noon. I expect someone on the roll, probably Dray and Carrow, to be on the wall. I expect Kiva's kept crossing to be asked for. I expect Rhea's card on the 30th, and Kellan's new card to come out "as a sum."

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitating. The movement ends with a clear deadline (three days), a promised event, and personal threads left open on purpose.

## 4. Repair brief — three priorities

The owner asked to keep the developed action. **None of these priorities shortens a lane.** Every proposed change is at the level of a phrase or a few sentences, except the evidence trim in Priority 3, which is outside the fights.

### Priority 1 — Fix the five factual slips, starting with the Mercury leak

- **Location:** ch39 l197 (K1); ch33 l401/l415 (K2); ch35 l89 and l265 (K3); ch38 l171 (K4); ch39 l301 (K5).
- **Observed issue:** A knowledge-boundary break (the Mercury reports ferry details nobody published), a scoring error of one point, two wrong weekdays, a row placed behind Kiva when it is in front, and an age that doesn't match the page's date.
- **Effect on the reader:** K1 is the serious one. It sits inside the movement's most important private scene and makes an attentive reader ask who told, which pulls them out just as the Proprietor asks his question. K2–K5 are the kind of slips that audiobook listeners and sports-minded young readers notice, and they make the lane arithmetic, which the book relies on, look careless.
- **Proposed scope:** Line-level. For K1, either give the Proprietor a source inside the text (for example, Adlam heard something reported back from the shop) or limit what he asks to what Kiva said in the Salt Hall ("You said the ferry. Was it the same?"), and let her volunteer *lower, nearer, going out* before he asks about white. For K2, remove the down from the third carry or have the marshal rule it a half (keeping 6–2). For K3, change "Saturday" to "Thursday" twice. For K4, put the Westerley girl in a row behind Seven (the tenth row or lower) or change "behind" to "ahead." For K5, change "thirteen" to agree with the page.
- **Strength to preserve:** The ch39 white disclosure itself. Kiva's reasoning ("That was choosing a side by leaving a thing out of a column"), the Proprietor's hand over his eyes, and "It doesn't prove me right" should stay word for word.

### Priority 2 — Make the stakes of the ch37 climax match the declaration rule

- **Location:** ch37 l149 ("and no change" / "Then I'll hold you to it"), through the third-carry choice (Perrin "had opened his mouth," Dray "waiting for the count") and the aftermath ("You'd have had it. With the count.").
- **Observed issue:** The box declaration seems to rule out the very crossing the climax presents as a temptation. Perrin's open mouth and Dray's waiting both treat as possible something the marshal has just said he will hold them to.
- **Effect on the reader:** The best character beat in the lane, Kiva shaking her head and Perrin shutting his mouth, loses force, because the reader suspects the choice was never real. Dray's line "That's the fairest lane I've ever lost" also depends on the choice having been real.
- **Proposed scope:** Two to four sentences. Either (a) have the marshal or Perrin establish that "no change" is a courtesy with a stated cost, not a rule (for example, a penalty point for changing after declaring "no change"), so that crossing would have won the lane but cost a point and broken her word to Dray; or (b) keep it binding and turn the climax into Kiva refusing to *break* her declaration when breaking it would win, with Perrin's open mouth reframed as him offering to take the penalty on himself. Either way, Dray watching Perrin's mouth then makes sense.
- **Strength to preserve:** The whole three-carry lane at its current length. Pru calling the pen with Kiva silent ("It was like being a door in somebody else's house"), Kiva kneeling by the head of the litter by accident, and Dray's "That's a thing I'll not know now… Ever."

### Priority 3 — Cut repeated evidence and repeated phrases outside the lanes

- **Location:** The Holloway return read in full in ch34 (Noa's desk), ch36 (Salt Hall) and ch40 (dock). The Proprietor's evidence dramatized in ch39 and then summarized again by the thin clerk in ch40. Annick's lines quoted in ch39 and read again in ch40. Phrase counts across ch33–40: "pressed to his side" 11, "I'd sooner" 13, "scarlet" 12, "the warm sound" 10, "She found that" 10, "had/taken that off" 9, "want it said / like it known" 6, "Anwen never went red" said twice in identical words (ch34, ch38).
- **Observed issue:** Material the reader already holds is read aloud in full again inside the story. Separately, several characters share one turn of phrase, and a few phrases become noticeable when heard.
- **Effect on the reader:** The hearings in ch36 and ch40 lose momentum just before their payoffs (Kiva standing; the lamp-man's new wire; the Clerk naming the unroofed sky). In audio, the repeated phrases blur the voices together. The young-reader lens loses the most here.
- **Proposed scope:** In ch40, compress the clerk's re-reading of the Proprietor's evidence to one summary sentence plus Kiva's quoted answer, which the room needs to hear. In ch36, give only the Holloway line that gets the laugh rather than the whole form. Leave ch34 and ch40's *new* Holloway wire in full. Vary or drop about a third of the repeated phrases listed above, keeping one signature phrase per character (Perrin's hand at his side, Pru's colour) and spreading out the rest. Do not touch lane prose.
- **Strength to preserve:** Noa's new Holloway wire in ch40 and his admission ("I'd no business to… I'd like it written that I did it"). The Clerk's closing ruling ("for want of a better… and give it back the moment anybody finds one"). The Chair's third "Witnessed" line.
