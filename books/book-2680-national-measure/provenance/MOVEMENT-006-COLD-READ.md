## 1. Reader response (no scores)

**Where my interest caught**
- Ch 41 opens with the latch ritual, then hands Kellan the Proprietor's card straight away (41:3 and the card text). That card is a concrete, personal reason to care before any explanation starts.
- Aske's unprompted pricing of a second crossing (41, "Here's the thing I haven't said") made me expect it would be needed.
- Coombe's rope-end drill (41:327) is the best small scene of the opening chapter. It is physical and a little absurd, and it solves a real problem.
- Ch 42 caught me hardest. Dray's "It's looking at the pipes" (42:51) turns the plan on its head. Then the ring turns to look at Anwen. I felt a chill there and did not want to stop.
- In ch 42, Kellan asks the Proprietor what happens "when you let go of a pump that's only got you". He gets the confident answer "simpler, not harder" (42:299), and Kiva's unease ("She could not have said why she did not like it") sets it up as a trap. I was waiting for it to spring.
- Ch 44 is the peak. The far-edge call at the sill, Bannock burned, the first crossing, Lyle's "I'm saying it before", Aske counting the ten (44:315), Perrin's declaration (44:335), the run up the dock, the hand going, and the cut on a count read through her still door. Every beat raises the cost.
- In ch 45, "It's one hold. Not two" (45:73) pays off the Tuesday question exactly. Kellan's "Twenty-two" (45:107) is the emotional climax of a secondary character, and it lands.

**Where my interest slipped**
- The long position map in ch 43 (43:131 onward). It is useful for later tracking, but it stops the forward motion just after the Proprietor scene had built pressure.
- The opening of the sealed paper in ch 46 (46:215 onward). It is precise and rewards close attention, but it re-reports, line by line, events I had watched an hour earlier in story time.
- Chapters 46–48 overall. After the climax there are about 24 scene sections of resolution (8 in ch 46, 9 in ch 47, 7 in ch 48). Many of them share one shape: someone offers something, someone declines or accepts, and someone says they would like it said or written. By the farewells in ch 48, I felt that pattern repeating.

**Who matters most:** Kiva first. Close behind are Kellan, whose arc in this movement is the surprise, and Mara, whose restraint about touching cells finally ends at case nineteen. Pike's "two words, once" thread reaches the reader too.

**What I expect next:** the hull's light, a stranger who answered the nine seconds. Also the first morning the left hand says "yes", and the commission's first bank called by someone who is not Kiva.

**Would I keep reading?** Yes, without hesitation. The final hook (48:303) is strong and earned.

## 3. Continuity checks: physical state, power limits, knowledge, and what is held back

No canon file was supplied. Each item below is an inconsistency **within the supplied text**, cited by chapter and line.

**Apparent inconsistencies (most important first)**

1. **The salt deadline: 24 minutes or 22.** At 45:83, Mara says that if the Proprietor has let go "by twenty-four minutes past, there's time". At 45:101, Kellan says "If you lift at twenty-four, there's not." Mara's own numbers support Kellan: the slack ends at 6:30, the salt and count take four minutes, and the pumps take three more. A reader following the clock will be confused at the tensest moment of ch 45.
2. **Where the second crossing is planted.** Kiva tests the granite sill (43, "Second mark... It's good"). But she plants while kneeling in the slurry "at the sill's foot" (44:187, 44:209), and her still door goes "into the granite at the sill's foot". Then the cask "stood on the sill" (44:209–211). Wace is "a pace back on the dock floor" (44:175) and also "behind her". So the plant spot was never tested, and the space is hard to picture. That clashes with her own rule from ch 41: "If my fingers say a mark keeps time, it's not a mark."
3. **Hart learns of the struck line before it happens.** In ch 46, Hart tells Orla, after four in the afternoon, "I heard. Noa Bexley told the landing at noon" (46:315). The Chair struck the line at four (46, the Orla section opening). Also, at noon Hart was at the box on the timber pond (46:215 onward), not on the landing.
4. **The first line of the limits.** In ch 41, Perrin says "It can't be done by strangers... It's on Rowan's sheet. It's the first line under the limits" (41:275). Chapters 47:133 and 48:227 name a different first line: "A caller who has been inside both gaps."
5. **The size of the catchers' plank.** At 43:247 the text says "four girls and Yarrow". But the plank has four members, Pru, Treen, Marten and Yarrow (43 map; 44, "Marten and Yarrow had the two east quarters"), so the phrase implies five.
6. **The hour of the daily check moves.** Hart's lesson (47:53) depends on "the same question at the same hour every day", and the ritual is set in the morning (41:3; 46, "a quarter to seven"). At 48:267 it happens "on Thursday evening... because it was the hour". (The count "ninth day of *no answer*" is correct for 30-day months.)
7. **"Ten days" before the slack.** The Clerk says the paper was sealed "ten days before it came" (46:259). It was written on the 11th, and the slack came on the 23rd, which is twelve days.
8. **Mara hears a quiet count from too far away.** At 45:9, Mara, on the pump-house landing, hears Aske say "*one*" and "*ten*". Aske was at the sill, about 120 paces or more across the basin, and 44:315 says he "did not raise his voice". In ch 43, Kiva could not hear Perrin's declaration from the dock floor, so this sets a different hearing range.
9. **One section steps outside Kiva's view.** At 44:215–221, the text describes Noa, Perrin, Orla and the Chair, then says "Kiva did not see any of them." Elsewhere the movement is careful about this: Ivey's closing is reported later through Pru, and ch 45 says "She knew afterward, because the whole town knew afterward." This one passage breaks that rule.
10. **"Sanctioned" or not.** The Chair's two entries (46:295 and the entry before it) describe the same mast and the same hour. One is "read... from a sanctioned mast". The other is "not a sanctioned event". The difference is probably deliberate (a mast is sanctioned, an event may not be), but the wording reads as a contradiction.

**Physical state, all consistent within the movement:** Kiva's left reading hand keeps two dead fingers, and the hand is gone to the wrist after the second crossing. She writes left-handed until Wednesday and right-handed after. The Ember bar is on her right fist, over peeled knuckles. Jude's left arm leaves its sling on Monday, and Tuesday is "the second day". Bannock is told he will stand by Friday and is on a bench in bandages on Friday. The Master is in town for one hour fourteen minutes (5:50 to 7:04). Kiva's doors lie down for 49 minutes (about 6:15 to 7:04). The tin holds 2.0, 1.2 is used and 0.8 is left. The Board order is dated the 25th (Friday). All of these check out.

**Open questions about cost**
- The first crossing (6:11:40) brings Aske's forecast that the seventh "went past the elbow" and would begin "within the hour". The text never shows that cost happening, or being replaced by the second crossing's cost. The reader is left unsure.
- Jude's and Gower's fists are wrapped in grey "to the wrist and up his forearm" at 44:355. Two minutes later they light edges for the cut. The only aftermath shown is white knuckles.

**Power limits**
- Kiva reads a count through her still door for the first time at 44:439. The movement prepares this: in ch 42 and ch 43 her still door "wanted to march" near the Proprietor. Within the text it feels earned, but it is a **new ability introduced at the crisis**. Check it against series canon.
- The three doors are refused and not used, which is consistent.
- The Master's ward at 45 paces still pulls at Kiva's still door. The text explains this against the sister's forty-pace measurement, which is consistent.

**Knowledge boundaries:** These are handled well, apart from item 9. Kiva learns about the three accounts only when the box is opened (46), and she is surprised.

**Details held back on purpose:** The disk stays "cold" at every point where it is checked. The colour of the lamps' points is withheld on purpose, and the Board "will not ask". The dream stays unnamed, and Kiva's condition at 48 keeps it hers.
- **Could not verify:** whether Kiva's dream is already public in canon. The Clerk names "three people who have dreamed" in front of an open gathering that includes the Mercury reporter (46). Kiva's only reaction is to the three accounts, not to being named in public. Check this against canon.

## 4. Repair brief (three priorities at most)

**Priority 1: make the climax's clock and geometry readable in one pass**
- **Where:** 44:175–213 (the sill plant); 45:83 and 45:101 (the deadline); 45:9 (what Mara hears).
- **What is wrong:** The untested plant spot sits in the slurry, and Kiva and Wace's positions around it are unclear. Mara's and Kellan's deadlines disagree. Mara hears an unraised count from about 120 paces away.
- **Effect on the reader:** At the two highest-tension points of the movement, an attentive reader stops to do the arithmetic or redraw the picture, instead of feeling the moment.
- **Proposed scope:** Change only a few sentences. (a) Put the second plant clearly on the tested granite: she kneels on the sill's edge, or a clause shows her trusting the tested stone. Alternatively, have her name the risk of an untested spot in one clause. (b) Make the deadline the same in both speeches, or have Kellan correct Mara out loud as part of his sum. (c) Have Mara hear *of* the count, for example from Polglase's call, or remove Aske's count from what she hears. Punctuation and placement changes will fix most of this.
- **Keep:** The full length of ch 44's action and its beat order. Aske's ten-count, with each number carrying a clause of the price, is the best set piece in the movement. Do not trim it.

**Priority 2: vary the shared verbal habit and tighten the resolution's cadence**
- **Where:** "I'd want it said / known / written" and "I'd like that written" appear 21 times. Ch 46 has 6, ch 48 has 4, and the rest are spread across 41–47. Pru also "went scarlet" 4 times in ch 48.
- **What is wrong:** One habit belongs to about a dozen speakers: Jude, Perrin, Anwen, Aske, Mara, Hart, the Proprietor, Dray, Pru, Carrow, Kellan. Each of chapters 46–48 has 7–9 scene sections that resolve in the same declarative way.
- **Effect on the reader:** The voices start to sound alike, and the resolution feels longer than it is. A thirteen-year-old may skim it, and an adult may notice the pattern.
- **Proposed scope:** Keep the phrase where it is a character's own signature. Jude's "I'd want it said" is earned, and Dray's "so it's mine if it's wrong" is hers. Rewrite roughly half the other uses into each speaker's own idiom. No scene needs cutting. If the owner wants a lighter ending, the sealed-paper reading (46:215 onward) is the best section to compress into a summary, because it re-reports events the reader has just seen.
- **Keep:** Penhale's kitchen (46), Hart's "Don't stop" (47:53), Ivey calling the pass (47:107), Rhea's "no" (48), and the final hull (48:303). These are the resolution's strongest beats.

**Priority 3: a pass of small continuity fixes**
- **Where:** 46:315 (Hart heard at noon), 41:275 against 47:133 and 48:227 (the first line of the limits), 43:247 (four girls and Yarrow), 48:267 (evening against morning), 46:259 (ten days against twelve), 44:215–221 (the step outside Kiva's view), and the first crossing's unaccounted cost (44 to 46).
- **What is wrong:** Each one is a small mismatch. Together they show the seams.
- **Effect on the reader:** Each costs a moment of doubt. The limits-line mismatch and the hour-of-the-check mismatch matter most, because each undercuts a lesson the book teaches deliberately.
- **Proposed scope:** Edit a word or clause at each location. For the first crossing, one line from Aske in ch 46 is enough ("the seventh's bill folded into the eighth's", or whatever fits canon).
- **Keep:** The movement's precise accounting of time and quantity, which is largely accurate: 1h14m, 49 minutes, 2,052 strokes, 290 years, the 2.0/1.2/0.8 tin. Fix the outliers rather than loosening the precision.

## 5. Tradeoffs and uncertainty

- **How much repair to do.** Priorities 1 and 3 are low-risk sentence edits. Priority 2 changes how the characters sound, so it needs the owner's judgment about which characters own the phrase.
- **How certain these scores are.** The clarity scores (5 for lens A, 6 for lens B) carry the most doubt. Someone who has read the earlier movements will know the vocabulary, and a first-time reader of this book would not. This review saw only Movement Six, so it cannot tell which situation the scores should reflect.
- **Comparing with other runs.** This review used the full rubric on all eight chapters. If it is compared with another seat's run, that run should cover the same chapters with the same rubric. Results from one movement do not show which seat is better across a whole series.
- **What I checked mechanically.** The line numbers, word counts and phrase counts came from searching `manuscript/chapter-41.md` to `chapter-48.md`, and they match the supplied text. The internal arithmetic (dates, durations, quantities) I checked by hand.
