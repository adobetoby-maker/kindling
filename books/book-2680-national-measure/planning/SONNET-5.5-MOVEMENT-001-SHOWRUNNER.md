## 4. Every packet change and why

All changes are insertions or one-clause corrections to existing bullets. The packet grew by
49 lines and lost 12.

1. **Where we enter: Mara's sheets.** Was "have gone to the coast." Now: tied, waiting on the
   dresser for the coast boat on Monday the 17th. *Why:* Book One chapter 48 (line 295) has
   Mara say "That's for the coast boat on Monday." `STATE_LEDGER.md` says "sent." The
   manuscript governs. It also hands Opus a physical, consequential first action on the
   first free day.
2. **Measure as a bill and a date.** Added the exact interval: first board Monday the 14th
   of the third month, 44 days after Saturday the 30th of the first month; road south due
   by the end of the second month, 30 days on. *Why:* `BOOK_MAP.md` gives Movement Two's
   entry pressure as "five weeks," which is loose (verified: 30 and 44 days; Monday to
   Monday checks). A "date" on the last page has to be true.
3. **Formula, viewpoint.** Orla's natural cutaway is her deciding to tell, not "with the
   card." *Why:* Book One chapter 48 ends on Orla alone reading Hart's card. A second
   reading would be a repeated scene.
4. **Warrant arithmetic.** Replaced "The book that had 'something over' does not" with the
   real figures. *Why:* it was false. 25.53 less 15.0 leaves 10.53; the planned Ember
   crossings and Coombe's floors (1.8 and 4.0) leave 4.73 before any call reserve. The book
   does not run dry here; its surplus is spoken for. Which five heads depends on Kellan.
   Whether the warrant is paid or only posted is left open, with the exactness requirement.
5. **Orla and the dream.** Added: Orla reads the green book weekly and does not know Kiva
   dreams. *Why:* `STATE_LEDGER.md` section 7. The Hart-copy scene can otherwise leak the
   dream to Orla by accident.
6. **Re-reading, two-door figure.** Added that it is one declared Ember-to-still crossing
   on a sanctioned mast, read by Noa, costing that week's go (about 0.30 of her own half
   dram), never a stack; and that the figures show summer and autumn work, not the dark
   weeks. *Why:* a stack read on the Chair's instruments is an incident under her
   conditions, and "dashes with two" has no lawful route to the frame otherwise.
7. **Drum work.** Added: Kiva is not Bench-passed, touches no municipal cell, stays on the
   pavement per Mara's rule. *Why:* chapter 36 ("not at the Bench"), the Chair's conditions,
   and Movement Six continuity section 10 (only Bench-passed students touch municipal
   cells). Her use at a drum is ground and count.
8. **Second van night.** Added that the thing met should be the first, failed meeting with a
   Movement Two behavior (a count-keeping pack, or a folding hem); and that if Pike's hands
   are the cost, the card's "second to Pike, six nights, two per month" stands and the four
   later nights must be decided and recorded. *Why:* criterion 5, and because Pike's hands
   would otherwise silently void the card.
9. **Finger test.** Grounded in Book One: "something's pushing" is their word; chapter 41
   shows the heel and last two fingers reading while the first two said only "pavement." Test
   them alone, against a steady push and a counted one. *Why:* it keeps the finding lawful
   (pressure sense, not magic) and honest about failing.
10. **Human contest.** Not the Halden License learning fight (Movement Two's, with Kellan
    opposite); if sanctioned, the Chair's 2681 conditions must be written. *Why:* prevents
    stealing Movement Two's set piece, and the ledger records that no 2681 conditions exist.
11. **Escalation ladder.** One sentence: private, then professional, then institutional, each
    stage making the next dearer; scene order still the author's. *Why:* the packet's
    obligations are many and unordered, and the ladder protects against a checklist novel
    without becoming a chapter plan.

## 6. Unresolved risks Opus must manage

1. **The dream and Hart's copy.** The green book is read weekly by Orla, who does not know.
   Whatever Kiva withholds must be a line she chooses on the page (Annick's phrase, the 2604
   keeper's line, a private note), and nothing may let Orla infer it. Hart's replies, if any,
   stay in his four-line register, with no meeting and no forgiveness.
2. **Pike's hands versus the card.** Decide and record what happens to the February and March
   nights. Pike's recovery of about six weeks is consistent with the Stroud figure in chapter
   43, but it lands inside the card's term.
3. **Kiva's agency.** Keep her acts as acts: asking Brask, Wace or Dray to hold counts,
   naming parts that are not hers, writing the columns, the edge-of-rule choice. A catcher
   who watches and is told things would fail the dominance check.
4. **The rule-edge choice must cost something.** If she speaks before the bag is sealed, the
   card and Rhea's trust take a mark. If she holds, a master pays for her silence. Either is
   fine; a free third option is not.
5. **Calendar crib.** Eleventh month Mondays: 1, 8, 15, 22, 29. Twelfth month Wednesdays: 1,
   8, 15, 22, 29. First month Fridays: 1, 8, 15, 22, 29; Saturdays: 2, 9, 16, 23, 30. The
   1st of the first month is a Friday and the movement ends Saturday the 30th. Aske's "eleven
   weeks and four days" from Monday the 10th to the new year is correct (81 days).
6. **The seven drums are an early engine.** The ledger says one a day, so they finish within
   about a week of the 17th unless the page gives a reason (the wardens' stock is finite and
   of unknown tune; a pinch that is not quite plain is a fair way for one drum to refuse).
7. **Money and ash must close to the hundredth** at the exit: every dram spent, lent, earned
   or refused entered; the red bags untouched; no free practice fuel; the 0.8 reserve opened
   only for a shown medical need.
8. **Progression density at 80 per 10,000** can turn the page into a stat block. Carry it by
   attempts, corrections and arguments, not by quoted figures.
9. **Rhythm.** Book One's clipped dialogue is a standing pull. Keep long, well-ordered
   sentences in narration (observation, spatial setup, reasoning), and do not chase the
   secondary figures.
10. **Rival thinness.** Dray is in a chair and Coombe is announced only. A letter, a note on
    the re-reading, or a glimpse from a window would keep rivalry alive without breaking the
    boundary. Optional.
11. **Sanctioned versus practice.** If the contest is a sanctioned event, write the Chair's
    2681 conditions. A yard contest avoids this.
12. **Name checks** for any new minor name against `universe/NAME_REGISTRY.md` and the
    manuscripts, as the packet requires.
