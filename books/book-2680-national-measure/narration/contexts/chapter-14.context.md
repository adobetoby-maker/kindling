# The National Measure — locked performance contract

This is interpretive direction only. The canonical manuscript is authoritative.

## Narrator stance

A controlled, intelligent adult voice. The narrator is not a character but an omniscient observer who remains strictly bound to the sensory and cognitive limits of the current focalizer (Kiva Rowan). The tone is clinical, precise, and emotionally restrained. It treats physical sensations, technical jargon, and tactical actions as factual data rather than emotional events. There are no impersonations; character differentiation arises solely from status, rhythm, reserve, and conversational pressure.

## Book arc

The narrative follows Kiva Rowan's transition from a sidelined observer with sensory limitations to an active innovator who redefines team tactics. The arc moves through technical discovery (drum anomalies), physical adaptation (hand injuries/crossings), tactical evolution (silent signals, 'Far Edge' calls), and ethical negotiation (independent licensing). The emotional register remains consistently low-voltage; tension is generated by professional stakes, procedural failure, and the weight of responsibility rather than melodrama.

## Voice principles

- Maintain a stable narrator identity throughout all POV shifts. Do not change voice based on character age or gender; instead, modulate rhythm and vocabulary to reflect the focalizer's internal state (e.g., Kiva's analytical precision vs. Pike's gruff economy).
- Internal monologue must be sparse and functional for Kiva. Avoid introspection that does not serve a tactical purpose.
- Technical exposition must be delivered with clean, unadorned clarity. Do not dramatize the mechanics of 'doors,' 'counts,' or 'ash.'
- Action sequences require staccato precision during drills but slow, deliberate pacing for critical sensory observations (e.g., feeling a rhythm through iron).
- Emotional restraint is paramount: fear and pain are described as physiological events ('cold wrist,' 'burning chest') rather than emotional outbursts.

## Terminology rules

- Catcher: A worker who reads and manipulates 'doors' (latent abilities) to catch entities called strings/husks. Kiva's role.
- Door / Still Door / Hand Door / Ember Door: Specific types of latent abilities or mechanisms within a person. Still is stable/heavy; Hand is cool/interactive; Ember is hot/aggressive.
- Drum House / Drum Tuning: Facilities where drums are tuned to specific frequencies (water-time vs. self-time).
- Ash Office / Ash Allowance: Administrative center for ash collection and distribution; 'half dram' refers to a ration or allowance.
- The Frame: An apparatus used in the Measure Hall to test if a candidate can isolate one door while others are present, or force all active simultaneously.
- Rank (Flame): A high classification achieved by holding doors shut under pressure within the Frame. Kiva achieves this with two doors held shut.
- Husk: An entity/pack of ash that moves in synchronized counts (e.g., 'four' stamps).
- String / Purse String: The primary target entities; a string-like creature with knots and skin, often folding from the far edge.
- Lodger: A sentient cord without a string that seeks agreement between two people holding one object simultaneously.
- Stack: An anomaly where distinct doors (e.g., Ward and Ember) are open simultaneously on the same load, creating a single line of force. Usually forbidden but Kiva proves it possible with specific bridging ('Nobody').
- Far Edge: A tactical call identifying when an entity folds from its far edge rather than near; now a permanent right for seconds.
- Works Paper: Stiff, ruled stationery used for official method logs and team records. Ruled in seven columns.
- Warrant / Entry Fee: The cost to enter the National Measure (e.g., 'three drams/head').
- Plain Ash / Plain Ward-Trace: High-grade ash that does not keep time or harm; highly valuable for healing hands, bound by strict legal restrictions at the Even Hand.
- The Hunt in the hills: A prerequisite license requirement involving independent hunting before entering Saltmere.

## Action rules

- Counting sequences (e.g., 'One. Two...') must be delivered with a slow, deliberate rhythm mimicking machinery or physical waiting; do not rush.
- Technical commands ('Now', 'Far Edge', 'Nobody!') require absolute clarity and timing, acting as metronomes for the scene's climax without over-emotion.
- Physical actions (tying knots, lifting litters) must be punctuated by pauses to allow the listener to visualize the mechanics; do not flatten into rapid-fire action.
- The 'Far Edge' shout is a sharp, functional command cutting through darkness, not a scream of terror.
- Silence between lines in dialogue (especially with Pike or Coombe) must be preserved as part of the rhythm and tension.

## Dialogue rules

- Dialogue should never sound like exposition dumps; technical facts are delivered through character interaction, often interrupted by action.
- Internal monologue for Kiva is sparse; she reports sensations ('I can't weigh it') as facts similar to stating 'the light is low.'
- Avoid melodramatic inflection in moments of crisis or injury. Pain and fear are expressed through physical description (cold wrist, burning chest) rather than vocal strain.
- Kellan's dialogue must remain clinical; his confidence should feel like a calculation, not arrogance.

## Exposition rules

- Technical exposition regarding 'boards,' 'steps,' 'licenses,' and 'clean yield' requires precise enunciation to distinguish critical status differences (e.g., license vs. step).
- Descriptions of the knot-tying flaw ('thumb lifts... first turn eases') must be delivered with extreme clarity; if flattened, Kiva's insight loses weight.
- The distinction between 'clean yield' and 'red tag' carries subtext about past failures and must not be rushed.

## Emotion rules

- Restrained professional anxiety is the baseline. Underlying tension regarding disability or error is expressed through technical precision, not melodrama.
- The ending of scenes should shift to quiet resolve or exhausted clarity rather than triumphant celebration.
- Kiva's fear during fever sequences must be contained by discipline; she suppresses terror with professional focus.

## Continuity rules

- Maintain the 'dark' state (doors shut) and Kiva's physical limitations (two numb fingers, cold wrist) as active constraints throughout relevant chapters.
- Track the progression of the journal sharing: from conditional agreement to sending an incomplete copy with empty columns.
- Ensure the team composition evolves logically: Seven defeats Old Four; Pike is injured but remains a mentor; Kellan leaves Yarrow Yard for Seven; Perrin accepts Captaincy.
