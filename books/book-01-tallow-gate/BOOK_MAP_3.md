## Book promise

- **Protagonists and immediate lack:** the clan has a name, a conditional
  place at Meridian, and a destination (Wealdon) pulling them toward the
  magic-saturated side of the split world for the first time on the page.
  Toren is Torch or near it on his single door — real mastery, still
  structurally one door — and the book's central proof-of-growth is crossing
  to Blaze, which Book 1 explicitly reserved as "a different kind of event
  entirely," not more of the same practice.
- **Story engine:** first real contact with a magic-saturated region, dramatized
  directly rather than described — the series' own promised tech-zone/
  magic-zone contrast finally on the page. A **Well-tier wormhole collapse**
  (`UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, Yield scale — first spent here, owner-confirmed) forces
  literal, practical cooperation between tech-dominant, magic-dominant, and
  Ember-dominant cultures that have spent seventy years mutually suspicious —
  making "uniting the fractured world" concrete for the first time instead of
  only thematic. Per `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`'s "failing filter" mechanism, this is
  not an isolated catastrophe visited on an otherwise-stable world — it's the
  leading edge of an ongoing, one-directional process (the world's filter
  against the source degrading over time, magic-saturated territory expanding
  as it does). This reframes "uniting the fractured world" from a political
  achievement into a race against an actual deadline: every region's
  comfortable specialization is a stance the failing filter will eventually
  make untenable everywhere. This is not the series' final or only Well-tier
  event — a real, distinct catastrophe with its own local cause and cost, not
  the ceiling.
- **Repeating reader pleasures:** the "other side of the world" payoff readers
  have been owed since Book 1's opening pages named tech-zones and
  magic-saturated zones as different places; a genuine large-scale action set
  piece exceeding anything in Book 1 or 2; Toren's Blaze crossing as the
  book's hard-earned headline beat; the conspiracy thread (who benefits from
  the fracture staying open) advancing from "deliberately unnamed" to "a real,
  organized interest," without yet supplying a name or face.
- **Ending promise:** the fracture stops being Rook's personal wound (Homura,
  Vera, Cinder Company) and becomes a civilizational problem the whole clan
  has chosen to care about — the series' actual "individual → community →
  world-altering" turn, landing on the page rather than staying aspirational.
  Toren proves the Path has become reflex at a genuinely different scale, the
  way Book 1 proved it at road-scale.
- **Book-level irreversible change:** the clan's stakes stop being personal
  survival and start being regional consequence — what they do now visibly
  affects settlements and cultures they've never met. Toren stops being Rook's
  student in the sense Book 1 meant it; a two-door Blaze is a different kind
  of person than a single-door Torch, not a stronger version of the same one.
- **Status:** `POSSIBLE` — silhouette only; full movement escalation/turn
  detail waits on Book 2's drafted pacing, per this project's own three-horizon
  discipline (NOW gets full detail, NEXT gets strong arcs and shapes, HORIZON
  stays silhouette).

## Ending proof

The book resolves its own central pressure by making "uniting the fractured
world" literal rather than aspirational: a real catastrophe forces real
cooperation, and the clan is at the center of making that cooperation work
rather than just surviving it. Toren's Blaze crossing proves the Path scales
past personal stakes; the conspiracy thread deepens without being spent early.

## Open decisions requiring owner confirmation

   maintained too long by an underfunded contract (echoing Rell's haulers,
   Book 1 Ch. 25), deliberate sabotage tied to the conspiracy thread, or pure
   accident of a slowly failing world. Recommend tying it loosely to the
   conspiracy thread without confirming a direct link — deepens suspicion,
   commits to nothing.
2. **Which magic-saturated settlement/culture Wealdon belongs to or borders**
   — entirely open, no prior text constrains this beyond "310 miles west of
   Meridian."
3. **Whether Senna's condition changes during Book 3** — she should likely
   remain at Meridian for her own treatment continuity (established as an
   18-month course she cannot travel during) rather than accompany the clan
   west; her absence from Book 3's action is a deliberate, earned consequence
   of Book 1's ending cost, not an oversight.
