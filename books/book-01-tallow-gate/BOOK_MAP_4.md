# Book 4 Map — Living Document

Status: `HORIZON` — deliberately kept at silhouette depth. No movement table.
Per this project's own three-horizon discipline, Book 4 is beyond the NEXT
window (Books 2–3) and gets a promise plus guardrails, not architecture —
locking further detail now would be false certainty ahead of Book 2 and 3's
actual pacing.

## Book promise (one sentence, per HORIZON depth)

The clan becomes a faction with a real footprint — recruiting, training,
sought out rather than only seeking — and Toren begins teaching, not just
learning, mirroring Rook's own arc in reverse; the series' central conspiracy
(who benefits from the fracture staying open) becomes nameable, a real
antagonist with a shape, without delivering the full reveal the cross-series
Artifact rule permanently withholds.

## Character turn

Toren mirrors Rook's own Book 1 arc, inverted: mentor, not student. Rook's
long arc (revenge → chosen mentorship, proven in Book 1, tested and held in
Book 2) gets a second movement — likely a reconciliation beat with a Cinder
Company survivor, or a resolution to the ambiguity between his private dream
and the cross-series Dream-Covenant mechanism — rather than more combat with
the same antagonist repeated a third time.

## Ending change

The series turns from "community power" toward "world-altering power" — the
doorstep of the endgame scale, not the endgame itself. Per `SERIES_MAP.md`'s
own far-horizon mapping (individual → community → world-altering → galactic →
universal), Book 4 should land the clan clearly inside "world-altering" territory
without reaching for "galactic" — that stays genuinely far horizon, unscheduled.

## Shared canon beat (owner-confirmed, cross-edition)

**The Mother and the Father myth surfaces more directly here** — not resolved,
consistent with its deep-`RESERVED` status since Book 1. The intended shape:
someone approaching real multi-door mastery has an experience that makes the
myth impossible to dismiss as incidental folklore anymore, without confirming
it as authorial fact. This mirrors how Rook's own private Satori theory is
handled — a belief a character can hold and teach as true without the text
itself confirming it.

## What this book must not do (guardrails, not restrictions on invention)

- No Glory-tier event or character. That rung is explicitly reserved past any
  currently-planned book (`UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, the flame ladder).
- No explanation of the disk's true origin. The cross-series Artifact rule
  (`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`) is permanent, not a Book 1–3 placeholder — Book 4
  does not get an exception.
- No new mentor figure who out-authorities Rook. His arc has been building
  since Book 1; a Book 4 authority figure who supersedes him would undercut
  four books of earned narrative weight rather than complicating it.
- No second Well-tier collapse used as a repeated beat. If Book 4 needs a
  large-scale threat, it should be a different kind of escalation — political,
  conspiratorial, or a Well-tier event with a genuinely distinct cause and
  cost, not a rerun of Book 3's set piece.

## Open, unscheduled

Everything else — antagonist's specific name/shape, the reconciliation beat's
target, whether Wyck's or Dessa's arcs reach a comparable headline moment to
Toren's Book 3 Blaze crossing, the conspiracy's eventual (far-horizon, likely
past Book 4) resolution shape. None of this is decided; deciding it now would
be false certainty ahead of Books 2 and 3 actually being drafted.
