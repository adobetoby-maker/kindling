## Book promise

- **Protagonists and immediate lack:** Toren Voss, 13, newly Kindled and newly
  orphaned, has almost no power and no authority. Rook, a solo hunter mid-revenge-road
  against Cinder Company, has both — and no interest in a clan. Book 1 is what happens
  when the boy who needs a mentor and the man who's been dreading exactly this
  responsibility get stuck with each other.
- **Story engine:** A road-tutelage. Rook teaches through real fights with real
  monsters, not simulation — the Path (the recognition that Ember-power and
  rift-magic are the same force, reached from within) has to become reflex, and reflex
  only comes from stakes that are actually real. Every lesson costs something. The
  book is equally Rook's internal arc — relenting a revenge road for a mentorship he
  didn't choose — as it is Toren's growth arc.
- **Repeating reader pleasures:** Levels/Brand growth read against real stakes each
  time; live-fire training beats (Miyagi-style, but the wounds are real); the
  found-family clan forming under a mentor who resists the role even as he fills it;
  monster-hunting as both threat and currency; the slow-burn mystery of the Satori and
  Rook's private dream about a carrier he was always going to have to find.
- **Ending promise:** the group reaches Meridian, Senna's fate resolved; Cinder
  Company's immediate threat is answered; Rook chooses the mission (and the clan) over
  the kill at real cost; at least one close friend does not survive the book; Toren
  proves the Path has become reflex by blending Edge and Stride under real pressure
  for the first time; and the Satori yields one genuine, costly piece of truth — not a
  full reveal.
- **Book-level irreversible change:** Rook stops being a man on a revenge road who
  happens to be dragging kids behind him, and becomes a mentor who has chosen that as
  his actual mission — on the page, at a cost he can't take back. Toren stops
  receiving power and starts owning it.
- **Status:** `POSSIBLE` — architecture restructured, awaiting owner confirmation on
  flagged items below before promotion to `PLANNED`.

## Opposition and off-page motion

- **Riftspawn** — the training curriculum itself, not just a hazard. Every set piece
  through the book is also a lesson; escalates in the back half toward one larger
  encounter tied to wormhole instability near Meridian.
- **Cinder Company** — human antagonist, personally tied to Rook's grief, not to any
  tech/magic/Ember allegiance (Rook holds no such grudge against any interface — his
  wound is personal betrayal, not ideology). Off-page through the first half (rumor,
  wreckage, a name spoken and dropped); converges with the road in the back half,
  forcing Rook's central choice.
- **Meridian's authority** — not yet an active antagonist in Book 1; establishes
  itself in the closing movement as institutionally wary of the Kindled, seeding
  Book 2.

## Ending proof

Book 1 resolves its own central pressure on two fronts at once: Toren's growth (power
becomes owned, not received — proven on the page by the Edge/Stride blend) and Rook's
internal arc (revenge relented for a mission actively chosen, proven on the page by
the Cinder Company confrontation). Senna reaches treatment. The group that arrives at
Meridian is smaller and it is a clan, not a rescue party. The disk yields its first
real clue. Meridian's suspicion of what they now are opens Book 2's changed world.

## Open decisions requiring owner confirmation (recommended defaults stated; will
proceed on these unless redirected)

1. **Senna's fate and the route.** Recommended: Rook's road can reasonably pass near
   or through Meridian's territory regardless of his own quest, given his established
   ease moving through all three regional cultures — the destination survives the
   restructure without requiring a direct-route framing. Senna survives treatment, at
   a cost still to be named.
2. **The costliest loss.** Recommended: Wyck's friction arc resolves in sacrifice
   during the Movement 5 Cinder Company confrontation — the clearest way to convert
   "owed his life twice, resented both times" into something finally given freely. If
   the owner wants a second loss beyond Garrick and Wyck to fully honor "close
   friends will die" (plural), the strongest second candidate is someone within
   Cinder Company's ranks who Rook recognizes and can't save either — landing the
   cost on Rook's side of the ledger, not just the youths'.
3. **Rook's private dream vs. the cross-series Dream-Covenant.** Currently scoped as
   two related but distinct things — see `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`. Recommended: keep them
   deliberately ambiguous in Book 1; do not resolve whether they're the same event.
4. **The Hoid figure** (`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`, Mechanism 3) is NOT Rook — his
   extended teaching role breaks that mechanism's own rules (never appears twice,
   never explains, never teaches at length). Recommended: the Hoid figure gets one
   separate, brief, unexplained cameo somewhere in Book 1, entirely apart from
   Rook's arc.
5. **Cross-series proposal to `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`.** The Path/soul/unbound
   framework and the Nine Channels/Artifact mapping are drafted and ready but not
   written into that shared file. Recommended: present them for a discrete approval
   pass once Movement 1 is drafted and proven on the page.

## Retired alternatives

- **Toren's family journey as the book's spine, with Rook joining reluctantly** —
  retired. The owner's restructure inverts this: the youths join Rook's road, and his
  internal arc (revenge relented for mentorship) now carries equal weight to Toren's
  growth arc, not a supporting one.
- **Mentor as the series' Hoid figure** — retired; incompatible with the owner's
  extended-training intention. See Open Decision 4 for the reconciled version.
- **Destination as a tech-hardened enclave chasing myth-answers directly, or as
  ground zero of the first wormhole** — retired in both cases in favor of Senna's
  medical need, which survives the tutelage-road restructure as a persistent,
  personal deadline running underneath Rook's quest rather than competing with it.
