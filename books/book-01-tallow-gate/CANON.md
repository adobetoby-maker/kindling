# Tallow Gate — Canon (edition-specific)

This is the Tallow Gate edition's own authoritative world/power-system canon.
It is not shared with the Hobb's Wall edition except where explicitly noted —
see the repository README's "Two editions of Book 1" section for what's
deliberately kept identical (the flame-ladder Rank system, the Yield
threat-grading system, the Riftspawn worked-power-echo principle) versus what
has diverged (named characters, locations, Cinder Company's specific history).

# Universe Bible — Living Document

Status: `OWNER` for historical facts and the core power theory (owner-dictated, not
model invention); `POSSIBLE` for mechanics/vocabulary the model has formalized for
drafting use, and clearly flagged where a name is still open for the "play with
names" round.

## Timeline anchors

- **2080** — the Fall. Stratospheric nuclear detonations of unknown origin; EMP
  hardens surviving tech. World order reshapes around which powers were hit hardest.
- **2080–2090** — failed restoration decade.
- **~2090+** — Mars disk (recovered pre-Fall during a terraforming survey) activates;
  unstable wormholes begin. Fragments of another galaxy arrive; a residual, mostly-
  unused portion of the human genome/brain begins to awaken, first in the youngest
  generation.
- **~2110–2130ish** — Senna Voss exposed to reactor radiation on a scavenger run
  "two decades" before the book opens; magic-adjacent medicine stabilizes but cannot
  cure her.
- **2150** — the book opens. Toren Voss, 13, undergoes his Kindling.

## The Kindling

`OWNER`. The awakening ceremony/event, fixed to age 13. Grants access to power —
through a device (an Ember), through what looks like granted magic, or through
disciplined internal cultivation. Which door a given person walks through first is
mostly accident of circumstance, culture, and region — not a difference in what's
actually happening inside them.

## The core truth — the Path

`OWNER`, load-bearing for the whole series. Kept as "the Path" deliberately — a
structural echo of the same word doing similar work in a related series, per owner
instruction.

**Ember-users** believe the power lives in the device. They treat the Ember as the
source and themselves as its operator.

**Magic-users** believe the power is granted to them from outside — from the rift,
from whatever reached through the wormholes and touched them. They learn to control
something they think was given, not something they already had.

**The truth beneath both false models:** the power is the same thing in every case.
It does not live in the Ember. It does not arrive from the rift as a gift. It comes
from within — a mind/soul-level connection that the Ember, or the rift-touched
portions of the awakened brain, merely interface with. **An Ember-user's power and a
magic-user's power are not two different forces that happen to look similar. They
are the same force**, reached through two different doors that each side mistakes for
the whole house.

This is not a truth anyone arrives at by being told. It has to be trained into
reflex — "second nature," in the owner's words — through real stakes, real
consequences, and hard lessons that cannot be shortcut. Understanding it
intellectually and being able to act on it under pressure are different skills, and
the gap between them is most of what Rook has to teach.

**A practitioner who has actually internalized this** — reflexively, not just as a
belief — stops being limited to whichever "external source" they think granted their
power. They can draw on either interface, or both, because they've stopped mistaking
the door for the house. **Others call this unbound** — plain English, not a formal
classification, describing what such a person can visibly do: draw on any interface
freely. It's the outside view of the phenomenon, the word people who've watched it
happen reach for.

**Rook has his own, more precise word for the inside of it: Satori.**

## Satori — Rook's word for the Source

`OWNER`, load-bearing. Where "unbound" describes what a person can *do* once they've
broken the single-interface limit, Satori is Rook's word for the *realization itself*
— the moment of seeing past the Kindling's filter to the actual source of all power,
and embracing it. He didn't invent the word; it comes from Vera, whose family —
Ember-tradition, Japanese-descended — carried it and much of their tradition with
them into the post-collapse world, and Rook kept it the way he kept her Ember: because
it was hers, and because nothing English says it as exactly.

**What Satori is, precisely:** the Kindling teaches everyone a filtered
understanding — a door. Most people spend their whole lives mastering their door and
mistaking it for the house. Satori is the moment the door disappears and what's
behind every door turns out to be the same light. A person who reaches it can use
"all magical things" — not because they've learned every system, but because they've
stopped needing systems to reach what the systems were always reaching for.

**The mechanism, in Rook's own teaching terms:** an empty mind and an open soul,
connected. "Empty mind" is not blankness — it's clearing out the false model each
tradition installs (the belief that power lives in the Ember, or that it's granted
by the rift) so nothing is left standing between a person and what's actually there.
"Open soul" is the same clearing done on the other side — the seat of identity no
longer guarded by fear of what it might mean to admit the power was always internal.
Connect the two and a person reaches **the whole**, not just the sliver the Kindling
switched on. This is Rook's answer to the oldest half-truth every tradition
half-remembers: that people only use a fraction of their brain's real capacity.
Homura's scans back it up literally — the Kindling activates a small, specific
region; Satori, on the rare scan Homura ever caught mid-event, lit up markedly more
of it. What the Kindling opens is a door. What Satori reaches is the room the door
was always standing in.

**The one finding Homura was certain of, and the one Rook teaches first:** Ember
Kindling and magic Kindling light up the exact same region. Not a similar region —
the same one, down to the instrument's resolution. There is no tradition-based
difference to find, because there was never a difference to begin with. What Homura
also measured, and what took longer to trust, is that a practitioner's raw power
correlates with how much of that region — and beyond it, how much of the whole
brain — is actively connected, not with which door they came through or how hard
they trained a single technique. **The working theory, Rook's own: greater power is
not learned, it's connected.** More brain joined to the source means more of the
soul actually reaching the source, and the soul is the part of a person that isn't
only mortal. Rook's plainest version of it, the one he says to Toren eventually:
*"You're not getting stronger. You're getting less in your own way — the eternal
part of you was always bigger than the mortal part knew how to use."*

This is, structurally, the same thing `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md` calls **the Source** —
the single unfiltered power behind every civilization's system, visible only to
someone who's had their world's filter stripped away. This series arrives at that
concept through Rook's lived experience and Vera's inherited vocabulary rather than
a dream; the underlying structure is the shared one, the rendering is native to this
world, exactly as that document's own design intends.

**The disk's name — locked: Satori.** Rook calls it that on purpose, and the
overlap with the state's name is not an accident of naming, it's the whole theory in
one word. **Rook's private theory — his belief, not confirmed fact:** he has come to
believe the object Toren carries — his Satori — is the physical manifestation of a
person's Satori: that somewhere, once, someone saw all the way through, and what was
left of that seeing took physical form. He further believes that when a person
completes every power set available to them — masters everything an interface can
teach, the "god level" end of the road — the Satori they carry (theirs, or perhaps
any Satori touched by that completion) stops being necessary, because it was only
ever a record of a soul not yet finished becoming what it was reaching for. **This
is Rook's conviction, taught to Toren as truth and never contradicted on the page —
but it is this series' own myth about the object, not a cross-series authorial
reveal.** Per `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`'s design (and its own precedent — Valdris has
its own "aevun" theory about its disc, held with the same weight, never confirmed as
universal), no other series' instance of the Artifact is bound by what Rook believes
about this one — "Satori" as a name and a theory belongs to this series alone. The
natsukashi feeling the object gives Toren — a recognition he can't explain — is, in
this telling, exactly what Rook says it is: proximity to a piece of someone else's
finished soul. Whether that's literally true in the cosmology every series shares
is, and stays, unanswered.

**Rook's proof for the underlying theory (not the Satori-specific belief) — the
science, not the myth:** he was not an observer at the research site — he was a
subject. See "Homura" below for the full account.

## The soul, the heart, and moral weight

`OWNER`, load-bearing. The awakened portion of the brain is not purely a power
organ — it is where the soul occupies the body, and it reaches down into the
heart/chest. This is not metaphor within the story; it is physically real and
Homura's instruments could measure its effects, even without being able to
image the soul directly.

**No separate storage term — locked.** The soul itself is what holds source-power;
it needs no other name. Before Satori, the soul fills only through whatever door a
person's Kindling gave them — the Ember, for Toren and Rook — narrow, slow, easily
emptied. After Satori, it fills straight from the source itself — deep, fast, and
this is the actual mechanism behind fast recovery from real wounds, not a separate
trained "technique." **Rook's teaching line: "Your soul isn't where the power goes.
Your soul is what fills."**

This also resolves what a completed Satori (the disk Toren carries) actually marks,
in Rook's belief: not a technique mastered, but a soul that filled all the way to
capacity. See "Satori — Rook's word for the Source" above.

**Moral weight.** This connection is what lets a person feel the true cost of a
choice rather than reason their way around it — it is why Rook's judgment of people
is faster and more reliable than logic alone would produce, and part of why he
recognizes something in Toren before he has any evidence he could explain out loud.

**Heartfire.** The same connection is what seals a soul-bond between two people —
not chosen, recognized. Two hearts "burning within them" at once, in the owner's
words. It does not care which door either person reaches their power through: a
magic-touched heart and an Ember-touched heart catch Heartfire the same as any
other pairing, because the connection was never about the interface. Rook and Vera
(see Homura) are Book 1's example, though Rook has never needed the
research to know it was real.

## Homura

`OWNER`, load-bearing — Rook's full backstory, reserved for a mid-book reveal (see
`BOOK_MAP.md` Movement 4). Do not front-load this; Rook tells it once, deliberately,
to Toren specifically, "after some time."

Before the events of this book, Rook — born to a magic-tradition family — was
recruited as a test subject at **Homura**, a research site studying the
awakened brain across every tradition without prejudice between them: Ember-users,
magic-users, and disciplined mind-only practitioners alike. He was promised
something concrete in return for his participation — working default: protection and
medical resources for his home village, the same shape of need that drives Garrick
and Toren toward Meridian for Senna. (Open for the naming round if the owner wants a
different specific promise.)

At Homura he met **Vera** (surname open — see Name Registry), an
Ember-tradition test subject from a Japanese-descended clan whose people carried
their traditions — language fragments, ritual, vocabulary like "Satori" — into the
post-collapse world alongside their Ember practice. They caught Heartfire. It was
through loving her, not through the instruments, that Rook first suspected the two
traditions were one thing wearing two coats — and it was at the Station, working
alongside her, that he learned to use an Ember himself for the first time. **The
Ember he carries and teaches with now is hers.** So is most of what he loves and
keeps without quite explaining why — small habits, words, ways of marking a meal or
a rest, that read to the youths as just Rook's way of doing things, and are actually
Vera's, kept alive the only way he has left to keep her.

Homura's findings — that every tradition draws on the same force, provable
in brain scans no one could argue with — could have ended the split world's mutual
suspicion. It never reached anyone. **Working default: Cinder Company was hired to
hit the Station** (by whoever benefits from the fracture staying open — deliberately
`OPEN`, a Book 2+ question, not needed for Book 1). Vera died there. Rook survived,
carrying her Ember, and eventually returned to what should have been safety — his
home village — where, later, Cinder Company's friendly fire during a defense action
killed his family too, without him ever realizing until much later that the same
company had already taken Vera and buried the one piece of research that could have
made every subsequent death, including hers, mean something.

**Why this changes the revenge arc:** killing Cinder Company would avenge the
people. Teaching the Path — finishing, in a living student, what Homura's
paperwork never got to say — avenges what they actually destroyed. This is the real
shape of Rook's Book 1 turn: not choosing kids over vengeance in the abstract, but
recognizing that revenge was always the smaller, easier debt, and the real one was
never going to be paid by a kill.

## Fast recovery — mechanism, not technique

Retired: an earlier working name, "the Bank," framed fast recovery as a separate
trained skill. Superseded — the mechanism is the soul itself (see "The soul, the
heart, and moral weight" above). Recovery speed is a direct function of how a
person's soul fills: through a narrow interface (Ember or rift-granted magic,
slow, easily emptied) before Satori, or straight from the source (deep, fast) after.
There is no separate technique to learn beyond reaching Satori itself — which is
exactly why it can't be taught as a checklist. Rook can point students at the
door; he can't carry them through it.

**Rook trains through real fights with real monsters, not simulation.** Wounds are
genuinely real. A student who hasn't reached Satori heals at ordinary human speed —
the pressure that makes every other lesson land, since nothing about training is
abstract when a wound won't close fast.

**Rook can heal two ways and demonstrates both, on different people, without
favoring one:** magic-adjacent healing arts (learned from magic-users he trained
under) and Ember-boosted recovery (learned from Ember-users he trained under). Both
are ways of topping up someone else's soul from outside when their own can't
fill fast enough on its own. He does not treat one as more legitimate than the
other — that refusal to pick a side is itself part of the lesson.

## The Ember

Replaces the owner-flagged "CAD." The personal conduit device granted at Kindling.

**Two configurations, one Ember, never both at once below unbound mastery:**

- **Edge** — the weapon extension. Reshapes to whatever the wielder's need and
  training call for: blade, spear-point, twin knives. The more complex or larger the
  shape, the more mental effort it costs to hold.
- **Stride** — the transport extension. Reinforced limb-struts or a light frame that
  grants burst speed, hard leaps, or a short glide — powered the same way, by
  directed mental effort through the awakened portion of the brain.

An ordinary Ember-user picks one configuration at a time and pays for it in
concentration and stamina. **Unbound mastery** (Rook; eventually Toren, as the book's
growth arc) means holding both at once, or blending them — a bladed strike thrown out
of a full-speed glide, for instance — which is the single clearest, most visible proof
in a fight that someone has actually internalized the Path rather than just
performing competence with a device.

**Edge's shape-repertoire is rank-gated, not open from day one (`OWNER`, adopted
from the parallel Hobb's Wall draft — kept identical by agreement, since this is
part of the shared doors/Blaze framework).** A person doesn't earn access to
every blade shape at once just by reaching Ember:

- **Ember** — one instinctive shape, whatever the mind reaches for under real
  pressure the first time, not chosen deliberately.
- **Flame / Fire** — one *chosen* shape, made reliable through repetition —
  Rook's coaching pattern of telling a student to keep an "ugly" shape rather
  than switch is exactly this: reliability at this stage beats range.
- **Torch** — the full held-and-switched repertoire (blade, spear-point,
  twin-blades) becomes available, switchable mid-engagement. This is the
  single-door ceiling's actual content, not just "more power" — Torch is where
  versatility, not just strength, becomes possible.

**Each of the three doors has a named native domain (`OWNER`, adopted, same
source).** Opening a second door doesn't hand a person a second, separate
skillset — it blends that door's domain into the practitioner's own, which is
the real mechanism of growth past a single door's Torch ceiling, not an
ever-longer menu bolted onto the first door:

- **The Ember's domain: Edge + Stride** — offense and transport.
- **Magic's domain: Ward + Sustain** — defense and restoration (healing,
  shielding, sensing falls under Sustain's broader reach).
- **Disciplined cultivation's domain: Resilience** — endurance, recovery,
  the body and soul's own capacity to keep taking the work.

A two-door Blaze draws on both domains at will, blended rather than switched
between — this is what "holding both at once" (above) actually means
mechanically, not just narratively.

Magic, by contrast, is the party's primary answer for sustain — healing, sensing,
wards — filling the gap the Ember's two hard-edged configurations don't cover.
Combined, Ember and magic give a practitioner the full kit: offense, transportation,
and defense/sustain. Neither alone is complete; that incompleteness is precisely
the "different windows on the same house" lesson made physical.

## Riftspawn

Replaces the owner-flagged "Arcon." Creatures arriving through unstable wormholes.
**Behavior locked, established on the page in Movement 1 (Chapters 1–6) and now
canon:**

- Physical: slate-grey, wet-looking hide; every limb carries one extra joint, so
  they fold and unfold rather than step — smooth, unhurried, and this is the visual
  signature of the species. A chest seam that brightens with speed/effort, roughly
  coal-colored. No visible eyes; they demonstrably track and orient regardless.
  Communicate by a throat-click, relayed between individuals across distance.
- Terrain: cross standing or moving water without wading — do not treat water as a
  safe zone or an obstacle that slows them.
- On defeat: collapse into warm grey ash, light still crawling through it. The ash
  does not disperse on wind; it sinks into the ground. Rift-touched people feel it
  physically (Dessa: "it's not warm, you're feeling it because you're rift-touched
  and it's going into the ground"). Rook can draw it up through a flat palm and
  redirect it as a healing method — see "Fast recovery" below.
- **Core behavioral rule, now taught in-world by Rook and known to the party:**
  they orient toward worked power — Ember or magic, no distinction — and close on
  the *nearest* source, not the biggest, brightest, or strongest. Orienting/looking
  is a free action; closing distance costs them time, which is the exploitable gap.
  A character deliberately drawing attention with a bright display can pull their
  *attention* but not necessarily their approach if something else is genuinely
  closer — this cost Wyck Tanner his leg in the Movement 1 climax when Toren drew
  every head in a room without accounting for who was actually nearest to what.

## What Riftspawn are (`OWNER`, deepened — adopted from the parallel Hobb's Wall
draft, owner-confirmed)

**Working theory — Homura's own, measured but never proven beyond doubt, the
thread cut short when the station fell. Rook teaches it because it's the best
account anyone ever gave him, not because he can prove it — this is the correct
epistemic register for any character repeating it on the page.**

Riftspawn are not creatures that came through the wormholes so much as *matter*
that did — raw, unfiltered source-power snagged loose by an unstable tear and given
monstrous shape by nothing more than the hostility of a world it doesn't belong in.
It never passed through a door. No Kindling shaped it, no soul organizes it, no
Ember or magic-tradition gave it a discipline to hold itself together with. It is
power with no one home to hold it — which is exactly why it comes apart into ash
the instant something interrupts whatever crude shape it was wearing, and exactly
why that ash is such clean fuel. A person's own expended reserve has to be borrowed
from another *person* and repaid, with interest, because it already belongs to
someone. Riftspawn ash belongs to no one. That is the whole difference, and it is
why hunting is the frontier's actual economy, not just its danger.

**Variation — the different Riftspawn are different raw expressions of the same
force.** If a Riftspawn is raw power wearing a shape with nobody home, then
different Riftspawn are echoes of different worked-power expressions gone feral —
the same underlying force the Ember, magic, and the soul itself all reach for,
showing up unshaped and undirected. This is the design rule new types get checked
against going forward, not a bestiary to fill in wholesale ahead of need. Book 1's
own first use of it is **a Breach** — see `BOOK_MAP.md`.

## Yield — the Riftspawn grading scale (`OWNER`, locked)

Riftspawn grade by what they yield when they die, not by an arbitrary level number.
Every Riftspawn dissolves into reserve-ash on defeat (already canon, both drafts);
what varies is how much, and that quantity is directly measurable by anyone who
harvests it — a frontier economy built on hunting rift-things would grade threats
in exactly the units it already uses to trade the ash itself. This keeps the system
diegetic: nobody reads a floating number over a monster's head, but a warden who's
been doing this for twenty years can look at a kill and say what it was worth, the
same way a butcher grades a carcass.

**The scale — named by container, not by letter or number:**

| Grade | Yield | Established example | Threat profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Handful** | Barely worth collecting individually | Husks | Weak alone, numerous, mindless. The swarm tier — volume is the actual danger, not individual grade. A hundred Handfuls kill you as dead as one Barrel. |
| **Flask** | A meaningful, tradeable draw | Verge-hounds / baseline Riftspawn | Mid-tier, pack-tactical or individually competent. What both drafts' second skirmish featured. |
| **Barrel** | Rare, dangerous to harvest, genuinely valuable | *New this pass:* **a Breach** | Solitary apex threat from a major tear. The first tier where a fight isn't a foregone conclusion even for someone unbound. Reserved for the Movement 6 set piece already planned near Meridian — do not spend a Breach earlier than that. |
| **Well** (`HORIZON`, not Book 1) | Catastrophic, tied to an actual wormhole *collapse* rather than a tear | Unnamed | Series-endgame scale. No Book 1 or Book 2 event should reach this without a specific decision to spend it. |

**Volume is a separate axis from grade, deliberately.** A Handful-grade swarm is
still an apocalypse-epic set piece — the scale grades the individual, not the
encounter. Both drafts' Movement 1 ambush sequences (a dozen-plus husks/Riftspawn
at once) are exactly right and need no adjustment; they're volume doing its job.

**A Breach, specifically:** bigger, rarer, born of a major tear rather than a
hairline one. Concrete design left open for whoever drafts the Movement 6 scene,
but it should read as a step-change, not a bigger husk — something that makes even
Rook spend effort rather than economy. Do not introduce a Breach casually; its
first appearance should be an event the party (and the reader) recognizes as new.

## The flame ladder — the fighter-grading scale (`OWNER`, locked; retires the
numbered "Tier" system below)

Retired: a numbered Tier system tied explicitly to "how many of the nine channels
a person can reach" was cut. Reason: it capped at "Tier Nine," which numerically
echoes the disk's nine grooves closely enough that a future scene describing
someone reaching the top of the scale would functionally confirm what the disk
represents — exactly the kind of quiet reveal `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`'s "no series
ever explains the artifact" rule exists to prevent. Caught via cross-session
review before it was ever spent on the page.

**Replacement: the flame ladder.** Named stages, not numbers — matched, by
deliberate choice, to the identical scale adopted in the parallel Hobb's Wall
draft, so the one piece of shared cosmology in this project that's meant to
eventually translate across the other series in the universe has a consistent
vocabulary from the start rather than two competing ones.

**Kindled → Ember → Flame → Fire → Torch → Blaze → Glory**

**What the ladder actually measures (reconciled across both drafts — this
definition is now shared, not just the wording):** not door-count directly, but
how much of a person is actually lit — consistent with the already-locked canon
above that power is "not learned, it's connected," a gradient of how much brain
and soul are joined to the source, not a binary. A single door can be lit brighter
and brighter through nothing but time and discipline, with no second door ever
opening. **Kindled through Torch are all reachable on a single door.** Torch is
specifically that door's ceiling — the most one channel can ever carry, however
long a person has had it. **Blaze is the separate, qualitative threshold**: not
"more of the same," but the point where door-count itself changes — unbound,
multi-channel. Glory is full completion, every door, maximal flow.

- **Kindled** — day one. The state immediately after the ceremony, no reliable
  manifestation yet. Everyone starts here, no exceptions.
- **Ember** — early, inconsistent single-door competence — brief, weak
  manifestation. Toren's Book 1 opening state (four inches, two seconds).
- **Flame** — a real, dependable single-door manifestation, growing control but
  not yet mastery. Wyck: two years of daily discipline, forty-to-fifty-five second
  holds. Solidly here.
- **Fire** — advanced single-door competence, most of what one door can do,
  still short of the ceiling. Unoccupied by name in Movement 1 — a waypoint
  between Wyck's stage and Garrick's, not every rung needs a Book 1 example.
- **Torch** — the ceiling of a single door, however long a person has carried it.
  **Garrick — thirty-one years, and this is where he lived and died.** The worked
  example for what this rung means: real mastery, genuinely dangerous, and still,
  structurally, one door. This is also as far as Toren should climb by Book 1's
  end if his growth stays single-door — the more likely and more interesting
  version of his arc, since crossing to Blaze this early would outrun the book's
  own pacing.
- **Blaze** — Rook's range. The door-count threshold crossed — unbound,
  multi-channel, held with intent. This is what makes his combat performance read
  as categorically different rather than just "better," and it's not reachable by
  getting better at one door; it's a different kind of event entirely.
- **Glory** — the `HORIZON` "god level" endpoint already scoped in
  `SERIES_MAP.md` — full completion, the disk no longer necessary because the soul
  it was recording has nothing left to reach for. Not a Book 1 or Book 2 concern.

**Duration, control, and complexity are not a separate axis from the ladder
anymore — they're what moves a single-door person up it.** Wyck's forty-five
seconds against Toren's four isn't just flavor, it's the actual distance between
Flame and Ember. What stays separate is *crossing to Blaze*, which duration and
control alone cannot buy — that one is a different kind of event, not more of the
same practice.

**How this reads without a HUD:** a mentor's or a warden's blunt assessment, said
in dialogue, never a number on screen — "you're barely Ember and you want to
stand at point" is a plausible line, not a stat block. Tool for Movement 2 onward,
not a retrofit onto Movement 1.

**Blaze has its own room to grow (`OWNER`, adopted).** The three known doors this
world actually has — the Ember, the rift-granted magic tradition, and disciplined
internal cultivation, all named under "The Kindling" above — give "more than one
door" real graduated texture instead of a single leap from Torch straight into
full unbound. A two-door Blaze draws on two of the three at will (their two
domains blended, per "The Ember" above — Edge+Stride merged with whichever of
Ward+Sustain or Resilience the second door brings); a three-door Blaze has
genuinely mastered all three known traditions and their domains; full
unbound — any door needed — is the further step past even that, no longer
limited to the three traditions anyone's named. This leaves real, visible room
for a Blaze-rank character to keep growing on the page rather than the rank
reading as a ceiling.

**Rook is a confirmed two-door Blaze — Ember and magic (`OWNER`, locked, no
retcon).** He tells Meridian's intake officer exactly this at Book 1's close
("Ember and magic. Both," Ch. 27), and the magic-door ability has already been
on the page since Movement 1: the ash-draw healing method, described since
Chapter 4 as a way of drawing a defeated Riftspawn's residue up through a flat
palm and redirecting it, distinct from and shown alongside his Ember-contact
healing method. That ash-draw method is his door's native **Sustain** — the
same unlabeled-until-now pattern the parallel Hobb's Wall draft used for its
own Rook's Ch. 3 "shut the road" (now labeled Ward). Nothing about this
requires touching locked manuscript prose; it only formalizes what was already
shown.

**What "immortality" actually means, mechanistically — the shedding (`OWNER`,
adopted, deep-`HORIZON`).** Every rank transition happens in a change, not a
slope — real and embodied, not gradual drift. Ordinary mortality, underneath
everything else already established about the soul, runs on the same clock every
human body has always run on: the oxygen free radicals ordinary blood carries as a
byproduct of being alive, doing slow accumulating damage to the body's own DNA
across a lifetime until the body fails. This is the same "soul fills narrow and
slow before Satori, deep and fast after" scaling already established, named down
to its actual biological floor. Homura's instruments measured the correlation,
indirectly: practitioners who'd taken a genuine jump in rank tested lower
afterward for exactly that kind of cellular damage — a finding nobody at the
station lived long enough to carry further before Cinder Company ended the work
along with everything else it took. Every rank up pushes back against that clock a
little; this is the mechanism behind the faster healing already established as
recovery scales with rank. **At Glory, for the first time, the clock does not
slow — it stops.** The ordinary blood of mortality is shed, and what fills a
person in its place carries none of that damage forward. This is why Glory alone
is spoken of as immortality, and why the transition can't be survived unassisted —
shedding the only blood a body has ever run on, all at once, is not something
anyone has done alone. It is lent, the same borrowed-and-repaid economy that
sustains the light itself.

**The Reading** — a formal, rare Homura-tradition diagnostic, distinct from the
ladder words people use casually. A deliberate, felt self-assessment for
precision or dispute, expressed as a plain fraction or band a character would
actually say aloud ("maybe a fifth of the room's lit"), never mid-combat, never
as page furniture. The ladder words are what people say to size each other up in
ordinary dialogue; a Reading is for when the word alone isn't enough.

## The Mother and the Father — the Makers (`OWNER`, adopted from the parallel
Hobb's Wall draft, owner-confirmed for this book too, deep-`RESERVED`)

**Clean-room note first:** this is a structural idea, not a borrowed one. Where
the touchstone genre convention runs to a large, populated pantheon of higher
beings (many named figures, a Greco-Roman-style divine cast), the direction here
narrows this series to two: a Mother and a Father, and nothing between them and
the Kindled. No tier names, deity names, or specific invented terminology are
imported from any published work — the ladder (Kindled through Glory) is this
series' own, and the two Makers are this series' own.

**The myth, as it's actually held in-world:** somewhere behind everything — the
Fall, the wormholes, the Kindling itself — the oldest half-remembered tellings
hold that there were, and are, two: a Mother and a Father, the Makers of the
universe. Not a court, not a pantheon, not a bureaucracy of lesser gods — two.
Every Kindled person is, in the oldest tellings, an unfinished child of theirs,
and the whole climb from Kindled up through Glory is understood — where it's
believed at all — as the Path leading back toward what the Makers already are.
This is **myth, held with varying literalness by different people and regions**,
not an authorially confirmed fact — consistent with how Rook's private Satori
theory is handled elsewhere in this document: a belief a character can hold and
teach as true without the text itself confirming it.

**Working default for how it touches the world-building already in place:** the
Kindling wakes "a residual, mostly-unused portion of the human genome/brain" —
the myth's answer to *why that door exists in anyone at all* is that the Makers
put it there, or left it there, when they first drew off enough of what other
series' own mythologies call the Source to make mortals who could someday walk
the same road they did. Whether that's literally true, a later civilization's
story about something stranger and colder, or pure comfort invented after the
Fall the way Senna describes the Kindling ceremony itself being invented — stays
open, on purpose, indefinitely. Tech-zones like Meridian default to treating it as
folk religion; magic-saturated regions are more likely to hold it as living
belief. This gives later books room to texture different settlements differently
without any of it being decided yet.

**Naming default:** "the Mother" and "the Father" — plain functional titles, in
keeping with this series' own naming convention (the Path, the Kindling, the
Ember, the Source are all titled the same way). No personal names are assigned.

**Reserved status — this is deep HORIZON, not Book 1 material.** It does not
appear on the page as doctrine, explanation, or confirmed fact anywhere in Book 1.
The only permitted surface in Book 1 is incidental, unexplained folk texture if a
scene calls for it — an oath, a curse, a phrase a background character uses
without comment ("Mother's mercy," "by the Father's word") — never spoken by
Rook, never glossed, never connected on the page to the Kindling, the Path, or
Glory. Anything beyond incidental color is a Book 2+ decision.

**Relationship to the shared cross-series Source:** this stays this series' own
myth for now, exactly like the Satori-object theory above — it is *not* written
into `craft/connections/CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md` and makes no claim on what any
other series' cosmology is. Any future proposal to connect the Mother and Father
to the shared Source goes through the same discrete approval pass already
required for any other `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md` edit — not folded in silently here.

## Rook's private dream (distinct from the cross-series Dream-Covenant)

`POSSIBLE`. Rook carries a recurring personal premonition — not, as currently scoped,
the codified cross-series Dream-Covenant mechanism (`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`
Mechanism 1), which is reserved for a protagonist's single on-page instance at a
high-pressure moment and has not yet been placed in this series. Rook's dream is his
own, earlier, personal burden: a sense that he will one day be responsible for
protecting — or training — whoever carries a particular disk. This shapes why he
takes Toren's group in at all, beneath the surface reason (he can't leave kids to
die): some part of him has been braced for this specific responsibility for a long
time, and finding out Toren is a carrier is what turns "reluctant rescuer" into
"the mentorship becomes the mission that outweighs revenge."

His recognition of Toren is not purely dream-driven — it runs through the same
soul/heart connection described above. Part of why he can't walk away is felt before
it's reasoned: the same faculty that let him know Vera at Homura lets him
know, faster than he can justify, that this particular boy and this particular disk
are his to answer for.

The relationship between Rook's private dream and the series' one true
Dream-Covenant instance is deliberately left open — they may turn out to be the same
event, recalled imperfectly, or genuinely separate. That ambiguity is intentional and
should not be resolved casually in Book 1 prose.

## The split world

- **Tech-zones** — regions that retained functioning hardware; Meridian is one.
- **Magic-saturated zones** — regions where the wormhole-borne power dominates;
  cultures here adapted quickly.
- **"Alien tech"** — a category of technology that functions specifically inside
  magic-saturated zones, apparently acting as a conduit for magic infusion. Central
  unsolved-science thread for the series; not resolved in Book 1. Rook's ease moving
  between tech-dominant, magic-dominant, and Ember-dominant regions — he can blend
  into any of them — is a direct, demonstrated expression of the Path: he isn't
  loyal to one interface, so no region's culture reads him as an outsider on that
  basis.
