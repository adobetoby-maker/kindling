## Identity

- **Series:** untitled post-apocalyptic LitRPG / progression fantasy, working
  constellation name "Kindling" (project directory name only — not a locked title).
- **Genre seat:** Post-apocalyptic progression fantasy (LitRPG mechanics: levels,
  Brands, loot-as-magic-reserves), Earth-based near-future SF/fantasy hybrid.
- **Intended length:** `OPEN` — multi-book, matching the operator's stated horizon of
  "the day's challenges and future coming challenges."
- **Status:** `POSSIBLE`.

## Series promise and repeating reader pleasures

Growth read against real cost; found-clan leadership as the power fantasy; the slow
unspooling of the Satori's myth across books, never a single reveal; a world where
the apocalypse and the magic system are the same historical event, so every answer
about the power system is also an answer about what actually happened in 2080.

## NEXT — following three books

Full silhouettes below, in `BOOK_MAP_2.md`, `BOOK_MAP_3.md`, `BOOK_MAP_4.md`
respectively (movement-level detail for Book 2, entry/turn/end silhouettes for
Books 3–4 per the standard NEXT/HORIZON tiering — deeper detail waits until the
prior book is drafted, per this project's own established discipline).

**Book 2** opens directly from Book 1's close — nothing invented that Book 1
didn't already seed: the 30-day setter-line decision (Dessa), the 30-day
company-name auto-fill, Senna's 18-month treatment (9 months bedbound, the rest
wall-confined), Rook's own choice not to have Quist write to Undern about Cinder
Company, and the Hester Voss / Wealdon archive thread as the disk mystery's next
fair pull. The Satori's myth advances by one more real, costly clue — not a
reveal. Rook's converted loyalty is tested twice: once by Meridian's
institutional doctrine (real-stakes teaching vs. a safety-first enclave), once
by Cinder Company's remnant resurfacing by circumstance, not his own pursuit —
proving his Book 1 choice was a real change, not a one-time cost.

**Book 3** takes the clan out of Meridian and into a magic-saturated region for
the first time on the page — the series' own promised contrast (tech-zone vs.
magic-zone) finally dramatized directly rather than described. A **Well-tier
wormhole collapse** — owner-confirmed, first spent here — forces real
cross-cultural cooperation. Per the newly-confirmed **failing filter**
mechanism (`UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, "The split world"), this isn't an isolated
disaster: the world's filter against the source is degrading one-directionally,
magic-saturated territory expands as it does, and Well-tier collapses are that
process's leading edge — meaning more than one such event is expected across
the series at escalating severity, not a single spent capstone. This reframes
"uniting the fractured world" from a political achievement into a race against
an actual deadline. The conspiracy behind who hired Cinder Company to hit
Homura (deliberately `OPEN` since Book 1) gets its first real thread: not a
name yet, but confirmation it's an organized interest, not one bad company.
Toren's crossing to Blaze is the book's central, hard-earned proof-of-growth
beat, reserved since Book 1 explicitly for later pacing.

**Book 4** consolidates the clan into an actual faction with a real footprint —
recruiting, training, being sought out rather than only seeking. Rook's own
long arc (revenge → chosen mentorship) gets a second movement: reconciliation
with a Cinder Company survivor, or a resolution to his private-dream/Heartfire
ambiguity, rather than more combat with the same antagonist. The series'
central conspiracy becomes nameable — a real antagonist with a shape — without
delivering the full reveal the cross-series Artifact rule permanently withholds.

## HORIZON — later-book silhouettes

The Dream-Covenant mechanism (`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md` Mechanism 1) is reserved for a
later book at a genuinely high-pressure moment — not Book 1. See `BOOK_MAP.md` Open
Decision 3.

**The series endpoint (`OWNER`, far horizon — not Book 1, likely not Book 2 or 3):**
Rook's private theory (`UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, "Satori") describes a tier beyond
unbound — "god level," complete mastery of every power set — at which the Satori a
person carries is no longer necessary and dissolves, being (in his belief) nothing
more than a record of a soul not yet finished becoming what it was reaching for.
This is the series' deepest long-term promise: individual power (Book 1) → community
power (the clan, Book 1's close) → world-altering power (uniting the fractured
world against Riftspawn, per the owner's stated big-picture goal) → and eventually,
far beyond any currently-planned book, the "god level" tier that would let humanity
travel beyond this universe. This maps directly onto `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`'s own
Cosmic Arc Map rungs (individual → community → world-altering → galactic →
universal) — this series has a plausible path to all five, which none of the other
four currently claim to reach on the page. Nothing here is scheduled; it is the
shape the series is reaching toward.

**Cross-property ambition (`OWNER`):** this series is intended, over multiple books
and explicitly not forced into Book 1, as connective tissue toward the owner's own
existing cosmology (*The Fractured Path*, *Good Bones*, *Boundary Universe*) via the
structural, never-explicit mechanism in `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`. Genre tone/pacing
touchstones named by the owner (*He Who Fights With Monsters*, *Iron Prince*) are
inspiration only — see `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md` "Series ambition" for the clean-room
posture governing that. "Tech apocalypse with magic" as the genre-fusion thesis is
this series' distinct contribution to the shared cosmology.
