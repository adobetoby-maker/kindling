# Hobb's Wall — locked performance contract

This is interpretive direction only. The canonical manuscript is authoritative.

## Narrator stance

Controlled, intelligent adult observer. Most of the book remains close to Toren Voss without merging with his youthfulness as the narrator's primary tone; Chapter 28 deliberately widens toward Senna and the group. Mark that widening through attention and emotional pressure, never through a new narrator identity. The voice stays stable, analytical, and emotionally restrained, prioritizing clarity of action over dramatic flair.

## Book arc

A journey from mundane survival through escalating tactical violence to exhausted, conditional refuge at Meridian. The emotional register moves from suppressed fear, through shock and grief, into pragmatic calculation and shared burden. The closing chapters do not offer a happy institutional resolution: the survivors are admitted and watched, Senna is still declining, and Toren ends with a warm stone he is not yet ready to examine.

## Voice principles

- Maintain Toren's sensory proximity without adopting his teenage voice; the narrator is older, cooler, and more observant than the protagonist.
- Differentiate characters by status, rhythm, reserve, and conversational pressure rather than accent or caricature.
- Avoid 'wonder' tones for magical elements; treat them as physical tools with mechanical costs.
- Prioritize silence and pause over exposition; let tactical details land through action descriptions.
- Do not switch narrator identity. The single voice must navigate all POVs by adjusting proximity to the character's immediate sensory experience.
- After every quoted character passage or heightened emotional cue, make the following narrative sentence a clean Calder base-voice restart: normal pitch center, grounded resonance, measured cadence, Australian-English placement, and adult observational distance. Never carry character posture into narration.

## Character continuity

### Toren Voss — Protagonist / POV Anchor

- Differentiation: Internal monologue is analytical, rhythmic (counting), and physically grounded. External speech is reactive, urgent, or fragmented during stress. The narrator filters his thoughts through a lens of observation rather than emotional outburst.
- Restraint: Shame and grief are internalized; never melodramatic.

### Rook (Elias Marsh) — Mentor / Leader (alive at Book 1 close)

- Differentiation: Economical, flat affect. Low register with high authority conveyed through silence and precision. No rising inflection for questions; commands are declarative statements.
- Restraint: Emotional vulnerability is shown only in physical collapse or brief moments of raw speech; otherwise stoic.

### Garrick Voss — Father / Leader (Deceased)

- Differentiation: Economical, low-status reserve. Short declarative sentences with a steady cadence implying absolute control.
- Restraint: No shouting; authority is quiet.

### Senna Voss — Grandmother / Elder (alive, still declining)

- Differentiation: Fragile but sharp. Breathiness and coughing are audible markers of illness, not weakness. Dry, ironic humor cuts tension.
- Restraint: Commands facts over comfort; voice is hollowed out by necessity.

### Marta Faust — Healer / Mage (Deceased)

- Differentiation: Sharp, breathless. Voice strains under the physical exertion of magic. Calm and steady in command despite collapse.
- Restraint: No emotional leakage; only physical reality.

### Wyck Tanner — Laborer / Skeptic (alive)

- Differentiation: Pragmatic, slightly cynical. Rhythm is clipped and efficient ('closing a ledger'). Dry laughter.
- Restraint: Silence carries weight; confrontation is delivered with terrifying calmness.

### Dessa Cole — Peer / Sensor / Satori practitioner (alive)

- Differentiation: Quiet intensity. Low, direct voice avoiding pity. Sharp and persistent in logic.
- Restraint: Frustration is stated as arithmetic; no whining.

### Bram Sark — Guard / Watcher (Deceased)

- Differentiation: Silent presence until threat appears. When speaking, a whisper or very low register conveying immediate danger without panic.
- Restraint: Treats death as routine maintenance.

### Milo Faust — Child / Light Bearer (Deceased)

- Differentiation: High-pitched urgency during action; quiet and supportive in aftermath. Voice goes 'thin' under stress.
- Restraint: Fear is a sudden realization, not cowardice.

### Nell Quill — Weighmaster / Authority

- Differentiation: Flat, rhythmic, transactional. 'Ledger' cadence in short declarative sentences.
- Restraint: Pity is subtle; returns quickly to business.

### Ott — Salvager / Informant

- Differentiation: Sinewy, weathered. Direct, unadorned rhythm with dry wit or professional boredom.
- Restraint: Threats are stated as inevitable consequences.

## Terminology rules

- Ember: A grey stone slab/tool responding to will; not magic but a conduit. Consistency: Always described physically (warm, cold, heavy) rather than magically glowing.
- Kindled / Kindling: The ceremony or state of potential access. 'Kindled to nothing' means failure to respond.
- Husk / Riftspawn: Non-sentient enemies; ignore injury, turn to dust/slick. Consistency: Never show them as intelligent or emotional.
- Stilt: Enemy moving in straight lines without acceleration; color of wet ash.
- Verge-hound: Canine-like creatures that 'shop' by noise on one side while attacking silently from the other.
- Satori / The Path: State of unbound access to power. Consistency: Described as emptiness/openness, not light or voice.
- The Sag: Moving patch of ground swallowing roads; spatial distortion (double shadows).
- Meridian: Destination; a guarded settlement with institutional procedures for Kindled persons.

## Action rules

- Start slow and rhythmic during routine/survival scenes.
- Accelerate to staccato, fragmented sentences during combat or panic (Toren's internal monologue).
- Slow dramatically for moments of realization, death, or tactical analysis; let silence carry the weight.
- Describe violence clinically; avoid romanticizing impact. Focus on physical sensation and immediate consequence.

## Dialogue rules

- No shouting unless physically impossible to contain (rare).
- Commands are statements, not questions with rising inflection.
- Emotional peaks are conveyed through silence or a drop in volume, not increased pitch.
- Exposition must be delivered conversationally by characters who know the facts; no lecturing.
- Bound character color tightly to the spoken line. Attribution and surrounding prose belong to the base narrator, and the first narrative sentence after dialogue is a deliberate vocal reset.

## Exposition rules

- Filter all technical details (magic mechanics, geography) through Toren's sensory experience and immediate processing.
- Do not pause action for explanation; weave lore into dialogue or internal realization during movement.
- Maintain the 'adult narrator' distance: explain concepts clearly without adopting a teacherly tone.

## Emotion rules

- Restraint is paramount. Characters suppress fear and grief until forced into action.
- Grief is shared or internalized; never performative weeping in the narrative voice.
- Tension comes from what is unsaid, not shouted threats.

## Continuity rules

- Track Toren's physical state (injuries, exhaustion) and magical status (warmth/cold of Ember) consistently across chapters.
- Maintain the group dynamic shift: from father-son protection, to co-survivors with shared secrets, to a fractured but mutually protective group, and finally to conditionally admitted survivors at Meridian.
- Ensure Rook's decline is gradual, marked by physical exhaustion and suppressed trauma before his final choice to abandon immediate revenge and protect the group. He survives Book 1.
