## 1. Premise and promise

Six hundred years after the night the sky lit up and every powered thing on Earth died,
people are born with a door in them that opens at thirteen. Most get one. Kiva Rowan got
three at once, in 2676, in a training yard under Meridian's south wall, and lay on the
ground with her eyes open while every lamp in the city went out and came back. She
dreamed the sky had no roof. Four years later she enters Meridian's collegiate circuit,
the regional feeder for the professional companies that fight what comes through the
tears in the world, and nobody can classify her, train her, or safely let her near a lit
cell.

**Series promise.** A girl with three doors and no category has to turn breadth into
control, in a season of school combat, spawn kills, earned ash, strained friendships, and
one civic emergency, and finds out by using it why the one thing in her school that nobody
can explain is shaped the way it is. Beneath that, a fever dream shared across centuries
shows Glory-level people converging as a thinning planetary filter disappears. The dream
never says whether they save the world, break it, or only decide what survives.

**Repeating pleasures.** Teams, standings, rematches, earned rank; students drilling to
kill Riftspawn and recover the finite ash that pays for the next attempt; rivalry, humor,
mentors with private stakes; terrain and teamwork beating raw output; professional rift
work where rescue, closure, and clean ash matter more than kills; a city whose lights,
pumps, and drains are part of every fight; and the question *why the post is crooked*,
answered by putting two hands on it.

## 2. History, 2080–2680, as events

**2080, the Fall.** Stratospheric detonations, source never identified. Light in the sky,
no felt blast, then every powered thing dies at once. A few hardened medical and research
sites keep working. Nobody ever learns who did it.

**2080–2090, the failed restoration.** Most infrastructure gone. The hardened sites ration
care at a fence, to people's faces.

**~2090–2095, the tears.** A disk brought back from a Mars survey before the Fall wakes in
a southern vault. Unstable wormholes open; things come through. Children of about
thirteen begin to do things nobody has words for. The words come later.

**~2130–2150, Meridian and the first teachers.** Meridian, a hardened medical enclave, is
where people bring the Kindled. A research valley called Homura finds that readings across
every tradition it can reach converge, and is destroyed before it can say so; one survivor carries the
finding out as a method. The first mixed-door teachers at Meridian lay a training lane
under the south wall along the hill drain, nine posts set as the drain runs, which is not
straight; the ninth stands out from the wall with a bar through its top. Students run it
dark, call the turn before the eighth, and say who has the bar. This is the first Home
Line: the field arm.

**2152.** A river-town orderly comes upriver to learn radiation recall and learns it by
running the lane in the dark. She asks why the ninth post leans. The trainer: *because
the road isn't straight.*

**~2170.** Sustain-trained Kindled pull active radiation burden out of living workers,
enter dead power sites in short timed rotations, and bring out components and the
plants' own drawings. Nobody is immune; fixed damage stays fixed; Ward does not move
radiation.

**~2182, Tamar Vey.** An itinerant teacher visits engineers trying to make ash burn longer
and shows, with a bowl and a pinch of ash, that ash can hold a shaped intention briefly
after the person lets go: the fire already makes heat; perhaps ash should hold the rules
around it. Mortal, fallible, not the inventor.

**~2195.** The first stable ash-nuclear plant. The first large output shows a failure
mode; the operator pulls the scram and the pattern holds the core in shutdown.

**~2245.** Licensed Glory-impressed patterns put compact reactors and ash cells in homes
and workshops. Fossil and large solar become backups. Price, licensing, and fraud arrive
with the wonder.

**~2360, the lift century.** Lift craft, flight rigs, powered tools, professional rift
companies. A rescue flyer chooses lives over a richer haul; *lives first, then clean ash*
becomes the professional standing.

**2390.** The Crooked Line School, now a college, raises the south wall and sets the lane's
other posts straight. The ninth will not shift. They leave it.

**2391.** The Halden Works patents the Step, a resonance its stepped ash banks share, and
claims all ash exhibiting it, however produced.

**2431–2461, Hesper Annick.** A Ward master of the Order of the Even Hand reaches Glory in
2431 and in 2446 holds a ward over the Eastbank flood-wall through the whole of a flood; the city puts
her on the wall in bronze. She has a recurring fever dream of the sky coming off. In 2461
she walks into the winter rifts and does not come back. The Order holds her dream as its
doctrine, calls it *the Unroofing*, and trains one door for a lifetime so as to be standing
when it comes. The Halden house claims her Ward patterns.

**~2600.** Rare Glory masters hold industrial-scale patterns, which makes titan houses and
near-monastic orders devoted to one door for life. Neither is the villain.

**2603.** A first-year breaks a collarbone on the ninth post in the dark. The School's
lane-drill committee, paid for by the Halden house, votes to run its lane lit and drop
the call. A student, asked why the post leans, quotes the old answer and laughs. The Home
Line's foreman on the committee says the Line will go on running its own lanes dark; the
chair says that is the Line's business.

**2643–2669, Ilse Corran.** Receives the plate from M. Gill at his death. Hauls a
Graywater probationer of nineteen (Rhea Sorn) out of a flooded Ferren culvert by the collar.
After the Tallow fire she comes to Coper Street and sits an hour on the stair with a child. The child, six,
whose mother died pulling a quench by hand, asks one thing the whole hour: *Will they say
why?* In 2669 Corran names her before the watch, saying she has looked a month for a
better reason and does not know that it wants one. The guardian is asked and says no; the
watch goes round her and never sends anyone to say so. Corran dies in 2673.

**2676.** Kiva Rowan, thirteen, opens all three doors in the Old Yard. Every lamp in the
city answers. She dreams the sky unroofed. The assessor tells her aunt she has no name for
what that was. Kiva asks why the post is crooked. Mara does not know.

**2680.** The six-hundredth anniversary season. Book One.

## 3. The power

**One source, three doors.** Everyone Kindled reaches the same force. The door is an
accident of circumstance: the **Ember** (a conduit object granted at Kindling, worked as
Edge or Stride), the **hand** (rift-granted magic: Ward, sensing, healing, the student
gate), and the **still** (disciplined internal cultivation: hold, plant, resonance). Each
tradition thinks its door is the house. The soul is what fills; a trained soul supplies
owned intention and pattern. Ash supplies fuel and nothing else.

**Rank and skill are different axes.** Kindled → Ember → Flame → Fire → Torch → Blaze →
Glory. Torch is the ceiling of one door; Blaze is where it stops staying inside one; Glory
is the apex, which stops ordinary aging and lets a master hold industrial patterns; it is
rare, cannot be bought or stolen, and is not Book One's business. A skilled Torch is
still a Torch. Rank is spoken, never displayed. Classification uses physical rigs, a
sanctioned mast whose pens notch, and accountable human rulings.

**The readouts people use.** *Hold*: how long a door stays open under load. *Draw*: ash
spent on a piece of work divided by the book expectation for it; 1.0 is standard, lower is
better. *Call*: the declared role or change, said aloud. *Control*: whether the door did
what you meant. These are system flavor: allowed, said where a character would say them,
kept sparse, never replacing what the moment feels like. Nobody in the story counts, times,
or tallies as a habit, and no scene is settled by a figure (§11).

**More than one door.** A **sequence** (the Home Line calls it *a crossing*) shuts one
door before the next opens and pays for an empty gap. A **stack** opens the second before
the first is shut: soft, cheap, open to anything near, forbidden. A **competition** is all
doors open with nobody choosing, which is what Kiva's Kindling was. Kiva's three-door order
is hand, Ember, still, *catch, carry, plant*, 0.59 of a dram with two gaps. Three clean in
all of Book One. It proves the configuration is real; it is not mastery, not a rank, and
not to be asked of her.

**Satori.** The word for the realization that the doors are one house, and the name of the
plate: nine rings, dark, on a cord, held of the Home Line and released to a bearer. It
warms once, after Kiva's first correct deliberate sequence, and is cold otherwise. It does
not teach, speak, answer, or act. Its origin is never explained in any book.

**The filter.** A planetary filter stands between the world and a larger Glory presence.
It is thinning. More Glory pressure reaches the world each generation, so prepared humans
hold stronger expressions and rifts produce more numerous, stranger, higher-yield spawn.
Institutions read these as an exceptional cohort and a bad rift season. The series slowly
proves they are one curve. Book One proves acceleration, not cosmology.

**The Unroofed Sky.** A fever dream a few people have had across centuries. Glory-level
figures come from every road at once; their lights touch until nobody can tell where one
stops; the sky is not torn but absent, and what is above comes down and in, full, paying
attention; every lamp, cell, and rift answers, some go out, some go white, one turns a
color with no word. The dreamer wakes before finding out why they gathered. It recurs at
bodily cost and never supplies a tactic. Its fixed end is the loss of the filter in Book
Three. Who gathers, who fights, who leads afterward is not fixed.

## 4. Riftspawn and ash

**Riftspawn** are source-power snagged loose by an unstable tear and given shape by a
world it does not belong to, with nobody home to hold it. Each expression echoes a worked
power gone feral. Forms by 2680: **husks** (mass and aggression), **strings** (a tear
paying out knots along a path, a new one rising as the tear runs on; cut one and the next
rises at once), **holds** (four husks standing a square with a cord between them and a
tear inside), **arms** (a string putting a limb into water or a lit cell and pumping it).
Spawn come at worked power and lit cells. A kill still leaves containment, rescue, and
recovery.

**Ash** is what spawn dissolve into: finite, ownerless fuel, described three ways.
**Yield**: Handful → Flask → Barrel → Well (a Flask is twenty Handfuls). **Grade**: purity
and stability; falsely stamped ash kills operators. **Resonance**: which expression it came
from and which patterns can use it; W (ward-trace) and I (induction) are common; *stepped*
ash, cut to the Halden patent, is this season's rare and dangerous kind. A kill's yield is
divided civic, casualty reserve, school, team, in that order; a student share buys training
cells, metered techniques, equipment, travel, and sealed medical reserve, and every dram
is reconciled. Killing does not raise rank. The soul grows through owned exertion,
correction, and reproducible control; ash buys the next attempt.

**Ash induction** runs the world: refined ash in a warded cell, an operator's stable
pattern, an assembly that converts the release to current. A tuned cell runs on its own
but never without fuel, meters, maintenance, and a shutdown path. Failure modes:
contaminated ash, false grading, pattern drift, runaway heat, rift attraction, and the
Step (a bank falling into one coupled engine, which walks along open links). Every
serious device has a meter, a shutdown path, and a hand that answers for it.

## 5. Places

**Meridian**: a hardened medical enclave grown into a regional learning and response
center on a hill above a river, with trams, flight lanes, and induction lamps everywhere.
**The Meridian Compact** is its four schools under one charter:

- **The Crooked Line School**, mixed-door field method. **The Old Yard**: a walled strip of
  packed earth under the south wall, sheds down one side, nine black posts, the ninth
  leaning with a bar through its top, a lamp over the first shed where Orla sits. The
  School's **core house** and its keeper.
- **Stonehand Hall**: body, load, contact, safe failure, recovery; the Kennel floors.
- **Wren House**: Ward, Sustain, medicine, ash engineering, radiation recall; the
  infirmary, the cold room, the reference shelf of plain ash.
- **The Home Line**: the field arm, suspicious of spectacle; runs its lanes dark. Its hall,
  the watch of seven, the holds on the Sag.

**The Meridian Collegiate Circuit** ends in **the Regional Gates** (LOCKED 2680: the owner
picked *the Gates* on 2026-10-28; options considered are in
`books/book-2680-opening/editor/NAME-OPTIONS.md`): the singles,
declared-role lanes, the Bench, carry, controlled Riftspawn response. Grounds: **the Pit**
(a floor with a dead lamp rim and a race under it), **the Great Floor**, the travelling
mast. Three divisions; placement by a Chair and a reader. The Circuit feeds **chartered
rift companies**, among them **Graywater** (Rhea Sorn's night van) and **Halden License**.

**The Halden Works**: the industrial house; yards, tram sheds, a technical office, the Step
patent. **The Municipal Works**, distinct from Halden: the city's banks, the **Ferren pump
house** (twenty-four cells; keeper, Lumb), the **Ferren main** (the storm drain, which
dives under the School's gate). **The Compact Library**: a place with a Reader; nobody's
plot runs through its shelves. **The Order of the Even Hand**: a Ward order with a house in
the hills. **The Eastbank flood-wall**, Annick in bronze. **The Hythe** (low streets with
cellars), **Stopgate** (the street below the School), **Weighbridge Street** (a district
that lost a core), **Coper Street** (the Rowans' kitchen). **Saltmere**, a port two
hundred miles off, host of the National Gates in 2681. Regional wormhole stations run
on schedules, expensive and bounded; nothing interplanetary.

## 6. What carries the plot (LOCKED 2680, owner approved 2026-10-28)

The book is about Kiva, her doors, her four, and the city. The things below are what
happens in the story where a document used to; none of them is about keeping, passing on,
or preserving anything.

1. **The post is a gate.** Everyone trains past the leaning ninth post. In Movement 6 the
   Home Line runs Team Seven dark through the Old Yard lane as a drill whose call is *say
   who has the bar*; Perrin takes the bar because the call says so. Kiva, planted at the
   ninth in the still door, feels water move under it. Mara, walking the Ferren main uphill
   from the pump house for the Works, has dropped a float at each manhole and watched for
   it at the next; above the School's gate, where the Works' plan shows the main running
   straight, the float never comes, and she cannot say where the main goes. Two to the
   bar; the screw turns until the bar comes up hard against silt in the groove; the shaft
   answers with a sound the whole yard hears, and under Kiva's hands the water falters
   rather than lying still. At the manhole below the gate, Mara's reading hand on the
   water feels the rise and fall falter. *It's a gate.* A penstock in a brick shaft eight
   feet down; the bar run home shuts the hill from the low streets. In the drill (Ch. 43)
   it is found, not shut: the penstock stays hanging on its screw (Ch. 44), silt jams it
   (Ch. 44), fitters work it from Tuesday (Ch. 45), and Perrin and Lowen run it home in
   Ch. 46. The street below is called Stopgate. Nobody counts anything; the gate is found
   by a call, a feel, a sound, and a float that does not arrive.
2. **Orla's debrief.** After every drill Orla asks her student, at the lane, out loud: *What
   went wrong. Who was hurt. What it cost. Second go. Why.* The Home Line asks the same at
   the cart. It is coaching, not a ceremony, and nobody writes the answers. Mara's three
   words on the back of the form are *Meter. Shutdown path. Hand.* The third is a person:
   whose hand is on it, who answers for it.
3. **The saying.** *Because the road isn't straight.* A flat answer to "why does it lean,"
   spoken by a trainer in 2152, quoted by a laughing student in 2603, said once by Vane in
   2680 when Perrin asks. No lore around it. After the gate it means what it says.
4. **The Order's belief.** The Even Hand believes the sky will come off and trains one
   door for a lifetime to be standing when it does; its Warden says what Annick saw, in
   Annick's images, to anyone the Order wants: a junior instructor on a stair in 2603 (to
   whom it gives ONE broken-off image and keeps the rest back, so that Kiva's own dream in
   Snapshot 11 carries the images in full and is not spoiled);
   Orla in 2664, who came asking about a two-door boy; Kiva in 2680, whom the Order wants
   for a year.
5. **The watch of seven.** The Home Line's custom for the plate: a bearer names the next
   before the watch and says why, aloud; the watch witnesses and does not choose, and can
   recall the plate on a vote for cause said before it. It sits twice in Book One: in
   Movement 4 Kiva stands before it and the Ferren, who sat the watch in 2669, says what
   Corran said that day and that Mara was asked and said no; in Movement 5 it votes on
   recall, two to five against.
6. **Lumb.** Old Lumb, the Ferren pump house keeper, sat three nights of fever forty years
   ago and dreamed the roof was off the house and every needle on his bank looking. He has
   never said so. Under the noise of the pumps in Movement 6 he says it to Kiva, because
   he knows the look.

Two props may exist as props, one clause each, never a scene's subject: the team's ash
accounts ("the book"), and the keepers' chalk (*T-0* on a rim or wall: fault, no cause
found).

## 8. Book One spine

Prologue plus 48 chapters in six movements of eight. The full movement table is
`books/book-2680-opening/BOOK_MAP.md` §4; this is the shape.

**Prologue, *The Leaning Post*.** Eleven dated snapshots, 2080 to 2676, each one person in
one place on one day with one concrete Turn. The post appears in snapshots 4, 10, and 11
only. Nothing is written, found, or read.

1. **Three Doors** (1–8). Placement with partial training and no safe category; a trial
   fails in an original way; Orla takes accountable sponsorship; Orla's debrief; Mara's
   three words.
2. **The Crooked Lane** (9–16). Spawn anatomy, kill roles, aborts, containment, grading; the
   first supervised Handful kill; the first share; a useful loss to human opponents.
3. **First Season** (17–24). Two-door attempts fail and cost; a learning fight against
   Lowen; a live Flask response at the Pit; the first crossing, seconds long; the plate
   warms after; a dream fragment with no tactic.
4. **The Real Call** (25–32). The Order's Warden comes for Kiva and says what Annick saw;
   Kiva learns Orla heard it first; Kiva stands before the watch and the Ferren says what
   Corran said in 2669; a professional shadow shift at Weighbridge Street becomes a real
   multi-type call.
5. **The Regional Gates** (33–40). The watch votes against recall; the six crossings; one
   trained three-door sequence; a long bracket; a hold forms at the Pit; rivals cooperate;
   Ysra comes to the mast; two fingers lose fine sensation for good; the Ferren bank falls
   into step.
6. **Why It Is Crooked** (41–48). The Step walks toward the School's core house; the dark
   drill, the float, the bar that comes up against silt, the gate found but not shut; a
   string in Stopgate pumps the main; Kiva takes the arm off the water, carries it north,
   plants it, gives the corner to three still-doors; Perrin and Lowen run the sluice home; the Line closes the tear; Lumb says what he dreamed;
   seated, Third Division, catcher; Graywater's bounded offer; *Anwen. My cuff.*

**Plants that must pay.** The post → the gate. The saying → it means what it says. Orla's
debrief → Kiva asks it of her own four, and the cost is said at the cart. The plate warms
once → the watch recognizes the bearer line, not an origin. Rival's model → rival owns a
climax task Kiva cannot. Friend's independent goal → friendship survives only after Kiva
stops assuming it. The dream → the Warden's images and Lumb's own dream show it is older
and wider than Kiva, unexplained. Students and spawn both stronger → one curve.

## 10. Tone and reader standard

Public byline Monroe Jackson; internal O'Connor 1.3 seat. Accessible one-pass prose at
about seventh-grade readability: 14.6-word mean sentence, 11-word median, roughly 28% of
sentences five words or fewer and 3% forty or more, short paragraphs, narration-ready
punctuation. About 87% Kiva's viewpoint; 13% purposeful cutaways, never a second
protagonist. Clean for a thirteen-year-old reader: no profanity, no sexual content, no
lingering gore; violence has consequence; losses land on the page. Humor is dry and comes
from people. Fights, drills, and emergencies get developed tactical space with trackable
terrain, positions, calls, costs, and aftermath; nothing is solved by a montage, a
lecture, a prophecy, or a document. Every climax capability is demonstrated and priced
earlier. Kiva is not secretly powerful; breadth is a control problem before it is an
advantage, and the cost it exacts is not reset by rank, ash, or victory.

## 11. Motif budget and banned phrasings

A beat is one scene-level moment; default budget is one beat per movement. The post: one
glance per movement in 1–5, the payoff chain in 6. The saying: three times in the book
(Snapshot 4, Snapshot 10, Ch. 43), never printed. The Order's belief: once in the
prologue, once in Movement 4. The watch: sits twice. Lumb's dream: once.

**Zero budget:** writing, logging, copying, filing, shelving, or reading a notebook, ledger,
column, card, margin, manual, or page as a scene's action or evidence; any character
explaining why records matter; any scene whose point is that something was kept, passed
on, or handed down; any chapter or snapshot that ends on a character writing. Ash accounts
appear only when a spend, save, or return decision is the scene, one clause otherwise.

**Banned phrasings:** "write it down" / "writing it down" in any tense; "somebody should" /
"someone should" as a reason to record; "for the record"; "put it in the book"; "put it in
the column"; "the why" as a noun; "keep a record" / "kept a record"; "so it isn't lost";
any line in which a character says why records matter.

**Also banned:** a status overlay; a secretly top-rank character; the plate teaching or
saving a scene; the dream supplying a tactic; a founder's disputed history becoming a
secret villain rewrite; a found document, log, journal, or captain's-log frame anywhere in
the universe; anything from the research corpus.

**Counting, tallying, and measuring as a motif: zero budget** (owner ruling, 2026-10-27:
"look to remove counting as a motif"; "only cut the tics"). What stays is the system's
readouts as flavor: Hold, Draw, Call, Control, a rank said aloud, a Grade, a yield class,
the named tiers and doors, a figure like 1.0 or 0.59 said where a character would say
it. Readouts are allowed as flavor, kept sparse, and never replace what the moment feels
like; a readout is not the thing that resolves a beat. What goes: counting as a theme or a character's trait
or comfort (nobody "keeps the count," counts to steady herself, counts as a method of
control, or is thought honest for counting); time in a drill, fight, or emergency given in
"counts" (use breaths, paces, seconds, or nothing); a tally, a timing, a reading, or a sum
as the thing that resolves a beat (a readout may change the plot once in the book, as
something a person witnesses on an instrument in front of another person, such as a notch
