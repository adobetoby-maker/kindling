## 1. Premise and promise

Six hundred years after the night the sky lit up and every powered thing on Earth died,
people are born with a door in them that opens at thirteen. Most get one. Kiva Rowan got
three at once, in 2676, in a training yard under Meridian's south wall, and lay on the
ground with her eyes open while every lamp in the city went out and came back. She
dreamed the sky had no roof. Four years later she enters Meridian's collegiate circuit,
the regional feeder for the professional companies that fight what comes through the
tears in the world, and nobody can classify her, train her, or safely let her near a lit
cell.

**Series promise.** A generation born into mature door schools finds that its institutions
kept the founders' methods and dropped the reason the methods worked. A three-door girl
has to find the reason by using it, through a season of school combat, spawn kills,
earned ash, strained friendships, and one civic emergency, before a thinning planetary
filter makes the divided traditions fatal. Beneath that, a fever dream shared across
centuries shows Glory-level people converging as the filter disappears. The dream never
says whether they save the world, break it, or only decide what survives.

**Repeating pleasures.** Teams, standings, rematches, earned rank; students drilling to
kill Riftspawn and recover the finite ash that pays for the next attempt; schools solving
one obstacle in incompatible ways; rivalry, humor, mentors with private stakes; terrain
and teamwork beating raw output; professional rift work where rescue, closure, and clean
ash count more than kills; old places, objects, sayings, and customs with a working reason
found by use, never by explanation; and the question *why the post is crooked*.

## 2. History, 2080–2680, as events

**2080, the Fall.** Stratospheric detonations, source never identified. Light in the sky,
no felt blast, then every powered thing dies at once. A few hardened medical and research
sites keep working. Nobody ever learns who did it.

**2080–2090, the failed restoration.** Most infrastructure gone. The hardened sites ration
care at a fence, to people's faces.

**~2090–2095, the tears.** A disk brought back from a Mars survey before the Fall wakes in
a southern vault. Unstable wormholes open; things come through. Children of about
thirteen begin to do things nobody has words for. The words come later.

**~2130–2150, Meridian and the first teachers.** Meridian, a hardened medical enclave, is
where people bring the Kindled. A research valley called Homura finds that readings across
nine traditions converge, and is destroyed by a mercenary company before it can say so;
one survivor carries the finding out as a method. The first mixed-door teachers at
Meridian lay a training lane under the south wall along the hill drain, nine posts set as
the drain runs, which is not straight; the ninth stands out from the wall with a bar
through its top. Students run it dark, call the turn before the eighth, and say who has
the bar. After every drill the crew answers seven questions aloud; the last is *why*.
This is the first Home Line: the field arm, the people who go out.

**2152.** A river-town orderly comes upriver to learn radiation recall and learns it by
running the lane in the dark. She asks why the ninth post leans. The trainer: *because
the road isn't straight.*

**~2170, the radiation return.** Sustain-trained Kindled pull active radiation burden out
of living workers while medicine treats exposure, enter dead power sites in short measured
rotations, and bring out components and the plants' own drawings. Nobody is immune; fixed
damage stays fixed; Ward does not move radiation and never will.

**~2182, Tamar Vey.** An itinerant teacher visits engineers trying to make ash burn longer.
With a bowl, a conductor, a pinch of ash, and a short-held pattern he shows that ash can
hold a shaped intention briefly after the person lets go: the fire already makes heat;
perhaps ash should hold the rules around it. Mortal, fallible, not the inventor.

**~2195.** The first stable ash-nuclear plant: ash holds containment and conversion,
nuclear fuel supplies the heat. The first large output shows a failure mode; the operator
pulls the scram and the pattern holds the core in shutdown. Every shutdown code since
opens with that lesson.

**~2245.** Licensed Glory-impressed patterns put compact reactors and ash cells in homes
and workshops. Fossil and large solar become backups. Price, licensing, and fraud arrive
with the wonder.

**~2360, the lift century.** Ash-electric lift craft, flight rigs, powered tools,
professional rift companies. A rescue flyer chooses lives over a richer haul; *lives first,
then clean ash* becomes the professional standing: closure, lives, infrastructure, clean
ash, violations.

**2390.** The Crooked Line School, now a college, raises the south wall and sets the lane's
posts straight, eleven days' work. The ninth will not shift. They leave it.

**2391.** The Halden Works patents the Step, a resonance its stepped ash banks share, and
claims all ash exhibiting it, however produced.

**2431–2461, Hesper Annick.** A Ward master of the Order of the Even Hand reaches Glory in
2431. In 2446 she holds a ward over the Eastbank flood-wall for nine days; the night the
water crests, every lamp in Meridian goes out at once with no fault found, and before light
a sister is sent down to the pump house to ask whether the lamps had gone. Annick has a
recurring fever dream she tells her sisters in her own words. In 2461 she walks into the
winter rifts and does not come back. The Order makes her dream its observance: once a
year, on the flood night, the sisters stand on the wall and say it aloud. They call it
*the Unroofing*. The Halden house claims her Ward patterns and sends a clerk to stand at
the far end of the wall.

**2466.** Thirza Bly, a Home Line bearer of the nine-ringed plate, tells the watch it
warmed once, after her first crossing, and never again. Every other bearer in five
hundred years, asked the same question, has said *nothing*.

**2471.** Tansy Wold, a woman with two doors, is entered on the Home Line's roll as
catcher. *Hand as before.* The Line's word for what she did is *a crossing*.

**~2600.** Rare Glory masters hold industrial-scale patterns, which makes titan houses and
near-monastic orders devoted to one door for life. Neither is the villain.

**2603.** The School's lane-drill committee, paid for by the Halden house, votes 11–6 to
retire the dark run and the spoken call from its standard lane. The posts are straight,
the call is "ceremonial," students laugh at the saying. The Warden of the Even Hand
objects once: those drills were paid for in injuries. The School keeps the lane and drops
the call. The Home Line keeps running it its own way, and nobody asks the Home Line.

**2604.** The Ferren pump house keeper sits three nights of fever and dreams the roof is off
the house with nothing over it, every needle on the bank looking, on a night the levee
lamps go out with no fault. He tells the keeper after him. It stays a pump-house story.

**2643–2669, Ilse Corran.** Receives the plate from M. Gill at his death. Pulls a child
from a culvert after the Tallow fire and sits an hour with her on a stair. The child, six,
whose mother died pulling a quench by hand, asks one thing the whole hour: *Will they say
why?* In 2669 Corran names her before the watch, saying she has looked a month for a
better reason and does not know that it wants one. The guardian is asked and says no; the
watch goes round her by custom and never sends anyone to say so. Corran dies in 2673.

**2676.** Kiva Rowan, thirteen, opens all three doors in the Old Yard. Every lamp in the
city answers. She dreams the sky unroofed. The assessor tells her aunt she has no name for
what that was. Kiva asks why the post is crooked. Mara does not know.

**2680.** The six-hundredth anniversary season. Book One.

## 3. The power

**One source, three doors.** Everyone Kindled reaches the same force. The door is an
accident of circumstance: the **Ember** (a conduit object granted at Kindling, worked as
Edge or Stride), the **hand** (rift-granted magic: Ward, sensing, healing, the student
gate), and the **still** (disciplined internal cultivation: hold, plant, resonance). Each
tradition thinks its door is the house. The soul is what fills; a trained soul supplies
owned intention and pattern. Ash supplies fuel and nothing else.

**Rank and skill are different axes.** Kindled → Ember → Flame → Fire → Torch → Blaze →
Glory. Torch is the ceiling of one door; Blaze is where it stops staying inside one; Glory
is the apex, which stops ordinary aging and lets a master hold industrial patterns. It is
rare, cannot be bought, granted, or stolen, and is not Book One's business. A skilled
Torch is still a Torch. Rank is spoken, never displayed. Classification uses physical
rigs, a sanctioned mast whose pens notch, and accountable human rulings.

**The numbers people use.** *Hold*: how long a door stays open under load. *Draw*: ash
spent on a piece of work divided by the book expectation for it; 1.0 is standard, lower is
better; a contested four-minute hold is expected to cost twelve hundredths of a dram.
*Call*: the declared role or change, said aloud. *Control*: whether the door did what you
meant.

**More than one door.** A **sequence** (the Home Line's older word is *a crossing*) shuts
one door before the next opens and pays for an empty gap. A **stack** opens the second
before the first is shut: soft, cheap, open to anything near, forbidden. A **competition**
is all doors open with nobody choosing, which is what Kiva's Kindling was. Kiva's
three-door order is hand, Ember, still, *catch, carry, plant*, 0.59 of a dram with two
gaps. Three clean in all of Book One. It proves the configuration is real; it is not
mastery, not a rank, and not to be asked of her.

**Satori.** The word for the realization that the doors are one house, and the name of the
plate: nine rings, dark, on a cord, held of the Home Line and released to a bearer. It
warms once, after Kiva's first correct deliberate sequence, and is cold otherwise. It does
not teach, speak, answer, or act. Its origin is never explained in any book.

**The filter.** A planetary filter stands between the world and a larger Glory presence.
It is thinning. More Glory pressure reaches the world each generation, so prepared humans
hold stronger expressions and rifts produce more numerous, stranger, higher-yield spawn.
Institutions read these as an exceptional cohort and a bad rift season. The series slowly
proves they are one curve. Book One proves acceleration, not cosmology.

**The Unroofed Sky.** A fever dream a few high-rank people have had across centuries.
Glory-level figures come from every road at once; their lights touch until nobody can tell
where one stops; the sky is not torn but absent, and what is above comes down and in,
full, paying attention; every lamp, cell, and rift answers, some go out, some go white,
one turns a color with no word. The dreamer wakes before finding out why they gathered.
It recurs under fever, ash overload, filter instability, or close Glory contact, at bodily
cost, and never supplies a tactic. Its fixed end is the loss of the filter in Book Three.
Who gathers, who fights, who leads afterward is not fixed.

## 4. Riftspawn and ash

**Riftspawn** are source-power snagged loose by an unstable tear and given shape by a
world it does not belong to, with nobody home to hold it. Each expression echoes a worked
power gone feral. Forms by 2680: **husks** (mass and aggression), **strings** (a tear
paying out knots along a path, each knot reading, a new one rising as the tear runs on;
cut one and the next rises at once), **holds** (four husks standing a square with a cord
between them and a tear inside), **arms** (a string putting a limb into water or a lit
cell and pumping it). Spawn come at worked power and lit cells. A kill still leaves
containment, rescue, and recovery.

**Ash** is what spawn dissolve into: finite, ownerless fuel, measured three ways.
**Yield**: Handful → Flask → Barrel → Well (a Flask is twenty Handfuls). **Grade**: purity
and stability; falsely stamped ash kills operators. **Resonance**: which expression it came
from and which patterns can use it; W (ward-trace) and I (induction) are common; *stepped*
ash, cut to the Halden patent, is this season's rare and dangerous kind. A kill's yield is
divided civic, casualty reserve, school, team, in that order; a student share buys training
cells, metered techniques, equipment, travel, and sealed medical reserve, and every dram
is reconciled. Killing does not raise rank. The soul grows through owned exertion,
correction, and reproducible control; ash buys the next attempt.

**Ash induction** runs the world: refined ash in a warded cell, an operator's stable
pattern, an assembly that converts the release to current. A tuned cell runs without
continuous soul expenditure but never without fuel, meters, maintenance, and a shutdown
path. Failure modes: contaminated ash, false grading, pattern drift, runaway heat,
operator backwash, rift attraction, and the Step (a bank falling into one coupled engine,
which walks along open links). Every serious device has a meter, a shutdown path, and a
hand that answers for it.

## 5. Places

**Meridian**: a hardened medical enclave grown into a regional learning and response
center on a hill above a river, with trams, flight lanes over the roofs, and induction
lamps everywhere. **The Meridian Compact** is its four traditions under one charter:

- **The Crooked Line School**, mixed-door field method. **The Old Yard**: a walled strip of
  packed earth under the south wall, sheds down one side, nine black posts, the ninth
  leaning with a bar through its top, a lamp over the first shed where Orla has sat
  twenty-one years. The School's **core house** and its keeper.
- **Stonehand Hall**: body, load, contact, safe failure, recovery; the plate; the Kennel
  floors.
- **Wren House**: Ward, Sustain, medicine, ash engineering, radiation recall, abort-line
  ethics; the infirmary, the cold room, the reference shelf of plain ash.
- **The Home Line**: the field arm, suspicious of spectacle. Its hall, the stair that
  creaks, the watch room, the watch of seven, the roll, the holds on the Sag, the night
  van.

**The Meridian Collegiate Circuit** ends in **the Regional Measure**: individual Measures,
declared-role lanes, the Bench (ashcraft), carry (rescue), controlled Riftspawn response.
Grounds: **the Pit** (a floor with a dead lamp rim and a race under it), **the Great
Floor**, the travelling mast, the Low Lane mast. Three divisions; placement by a Chair and
a reader. The Circuit feeds **chartered rift companies**, among them **Graywater** (Rhea
Sorn's night van) and the **Halden License** team.

**The Halden Works**: the industrial house; yards, tram sheds, a technical office, the Step
patent, money for committees and roofs. **The Municipal Works**, distinct from Halden: the
city's banks, the **Ferren pump house** (twenty-four cells; keeper, Lumb), the **Ferren
main** (the storm drain, which dives under the School's gate), the Brickfields trunk. **The
Compact Library**: a place with a Reader; nobody's plot runs through its shelves. **The
Order of the Even Hand**: a Ward order with a house in the hills. **The Eastbank
flood-wall**, Annick in bronze. **The Hythe** (low streets with cellars), **Stopgate** (the
street below the School), **Weighbridge Street** (a district that lost a core), **Coper
Street** (the Rowans' kitchen), **Tallow** (the fire). **Saltmere**, a port two hundred
miles off, host of the National Measure in 2681. Regional wormhole stations run on
schedules, expensive and bounded; nothing interplanetary.

## 6. The five mechanisms

Everything the older bibles did with ledgers, notebooks, columns, and found pages, this
universe does with these. If a draft reaches for a document to move the plot, it uses one
of these instead.

1. **The leaning post.** The ninth post of the Home Line lane, with a bar through it.
   Everyone trains past it. In Movement 6 it is the handle of a sluice gate: a penstock in a
   brick shaft eight feet down, on a raked screw inside the post; two to the bar; nine
   turns shuts the hill from the low streets. The lane was laid along the drain so that
   anyone who trained there would know the way to the gate in the dark.
2. **The seven questions.** The Home Line's spoken debrief after every drill, at the cart
   or the lane, in order: *What was it. What went wrong. Who was hurt. What it cost. How
   long to come back. Second go. Why.* The School's lane asks six and stopped asking the
   seventh in 2603. Nobody writes the answers; the crew hears them, and the one who was
   hurt says what it cost before anyone goes home.
3. **The saying.** *Because the road isn't straight.* The Home Line's stock answer to any
   "why," said flat; a joke that outlived its meaning. Spoken only, never printed or
   signed. It means exactly what it says.
4. **The Order's observance.** On the flood night each year the sisters of the Even Hand
   stand on the Eastbank wall and say Annick's dream aloud in her own words. The Order
   calls it *the Unroofing* and has made it doctrine. A guest may stand at the wall on
   spoken terms: the house sends word to the Halden endowment, which may send one person
   to stand too; a guest under eighteen brings her guardian.
5. **The watch of seven.** The Home Line's custom for the plate. A bearer names the next
   before the watch and says why, aloud; the watch witnesses and does not choose. At each
   release the watch asks *What did it do?* and remembers the answer. It can recall the
   plate for cause said before it. The Ferren, seventy, oldest on the watch, had the
   earlier answers from the officer who trained her.

Two props may exist as props, one clause each, never a scene's subject: the Line's roll
(names and years) and the keepers' chalk (*T-0* on a pump-house wall: fault, no cause
found).

## 10. Tone and reader standard

Public byline Monroe Jackson; internal O'Connor 1.3 seat. Accessible one-pass prose at
about seventh-grade readability: 14.6-word mean sentence, 11-word median, roughly 28% of
sentences five words or fewer and 3% forty or more, short paragraphs, front-loaded
progression teaching, narration-ready punctuation. About 87% Kiva's viewpoint; 13%
purposeful cutaways across four or five people, never a second protagonist. Clean for a
thirteen-year-old reader: no profanity, no sexual content, no lingering gore; violence
has consequence, staged through impact and aftermath; losses land on the page. Humor is
dry and comes from people. Fights, drills, and emergencies get developed tactical space
with trackable terrain, positions, calls, costs, and aftermath; nothing is solved by a
montage, a lecture, a prophecy, or a document. Every climax capability is demonstrated
and priced earlier. Kiva is not secretly powerful; breadth is a control problem before it
is an advantage, and the cost it exacts is not reset by rank, ash, or victory.

## 11. Motif budget and banned phrasings

A beat is one scene-level moment. Default budget for any recurring motif is one beat per
movement. The post: one glance per movement in 1–5, the payoff chain in 6. The saying:
spoken, never printed. The observance: overheard once, told of once, stood at once. The
watch: sits twice.

**Zero budget:** writing, logging, copying, filing, shelving, or reading a notebook, ledger,
column, card, margin, manual, or page as a scene's action or evidence; any character
explaining why records, reasons, or ledgers matter; any chapter or snapshot that ends on a
character writing. Ash accounts appear only when a spend, save, or return decision is the
scene, as "the book" or "the accounts," one clause otherwise. The roll and the chalk are
props, one clause each.

**Banned phrasings:** "write it down" / "writing it down" in any tense; "somebody should" /
"someone should" as a reason to record; "for the record"; "put it in the book"; "put it in
the column"; "the why" as a noun; "keep a record" / "kept a record"; "so it isn't lost";
any line in which a character says why records matter.

**Also banned:** a status overlay; a secretly top-rank character; the plate teaching or
saving a scene; the dream supplying a tactic; a founder's disputed history becoming a
secret villain rewrite; a found document, log, journal, or captain's-log frame anywhere in
the universe; anything from the research corpus.
