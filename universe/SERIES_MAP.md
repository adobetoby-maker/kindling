## Series promise and repeating reader pleasures

Growth read against real cost; found-clan leadership as the power fantasy; the slow
unspooling of the Satori's myth across books, never a single reveal; a world where
the apocalypse and the magic system are the same historical event, so every answer
about the power system is also an answer about what actually happened in 2080.

## Themes under pressure

- Power as connection, not acquisition — tested against every character who still
  believes power is granted or owned.
- Revenge versus a chosen mission — Rook's whole arc.
- Authority that hasn't been earned yet — Toren's whole arc, and Wyck's friction
  with it.
- What a myth costs the people who carry it before anyone confirms it's true.

## HORIZON — later-book silhouettes

The Dream-Covenant mechanism (`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md` Mechanism 1) is reserved for a
later book at a genuinely high-pressure moment — not Book 1. Rook's private
premonition stays deliberately ambiguous as to whether it is the same event.

**The series endpoint (`CANON`, far horizon):** Rook's private theory describes a
tier beyond unbound — "god level," complete mastery of every power set — at which
the Satori a person carries is no longer necessary and dissolves, being (in his
belief) a record of a soul not yet finished becoming what it was reaching for. Long-
term promise arc: individual power (Book 1) → community power (the clan, Book 1's
close) → world-altering power (uniting the fractured world against Riftspawn) →
eventually, far beyond any currently planned book, the "god level" tier. Maps onto
`CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`'s Cosmic Arc Map rungs. Nothing here is scheduled.

**Cross-property ambition (`CANON`):** intended, over multiple books and explicitly
not forced into Book 1, as connective tissue toward the owner's existing cosmology
(*The Fractured Path*, *Good Bones*, *Boundary Universe*) via the structural,
never-explicit mechanism in `CONNECTIONS_BIBLE.md`. *He Who Fights With Monsters*
and *Iron Prince* are named tonal/pacing touchstones only — clean-room posture, no
reused named systems or specific invented terminology. "Tech apocalypse with magic"
is this series' distinct contribution to the shared cosmology.

## Retired possibilities and reasons

- Toren's family journey as the book's sole spine, with Rook joining reluctantly —
  retired. The road-tutelage restructure inverts this: the youths join Rook's
  ongoing quest, and his internal arc (revenge relented for mentorship) carries
  equal weight to Toren's growth arc.
- Mentor as the series' Hoid figure — retired; incompatible with his extended-
  teaching role, which breaks that mechanism's own rules. The Hoid figure gets a
  separate, brief, unexplained cameo elsewhere in Book 1 instead.
- "a Brand" as power-classification vocabulary, and a floating game-HUD progression
  display — both retired in favor of "the Path"/rank-and-Reading language; see
  `CANON.md` and `NAME_REGISTRY.md`.
