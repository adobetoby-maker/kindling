# Canon — World Premise and Power System

Status: items marked `CANON` are the owner's own account (history, power theory,
locked names) or direct owner rulings surfaced through the parallel kindling-project
session and treated here as settled — not model invention. Items marked `POSSIBLE`
are new craft conventions this showrunner pass is introducing to close gaps the
other run left open; they await owner confirmation but are usable defaults now.
Nothing below is `CANON` in the stricter sense of "locked manuscript" until prose
exists in *this* book root and survives a movement.

This file is reference canon, not a chapter card. The author reads it for rules; it
does not dictate a single sentence.

## Timeline anchors (`CANON`)

- **2080 — the Fall.** Stratospheric nuclear detonations of unknown origin (source
  never identified) EMP the world back to hardened-only hardware. Reshapes world
  order around which powers were hit hardest.
- **2080–2090** — failed restoration decade.
- **~2090+** — a disk recovered pre-Fall on a Mars terraforming survey activates;
  unstable wormholes begin. Fragments of another galaxy arrive; a residual, mostly
  unused portion of the human genome/brain begins to awaken, first in the youngest
  generation.
- **~2130** — Senna Voss exposed to reactor radiation on a scavenger run; magic-
  adjacent medicine stabilizes but does not cure her.
- **2150 — the book opens.** Toren Voss, 13, undergoes his Kindling.

## The Kindling (`CANON`)

The awakening event/ceremony, fixed to age 13. Grants access to power through a
device (an Ember), through what looks like granted magic, or through disciplined
internal cultivation. Which door a person walks through first is accident of
circumstance, culture, and region — not a difference in what is actually happening
inside them.

## The Path — the core truth (`CANON`, load-bearing)

Ember-users believe the power lives in the device. Magic-users believe the power is
granted from outside, by the rift. Both are wrong the same way: **the power is one
force, reached through two doors each side mistakes for the whole house.** It comes
from within — a soul-level connection the Ember or the rift-touched brain merely
interfaces with.

This is not learned by being told. It has to be trained into reflex through real
stakes and real consequence — understanding it and acting on it under pressure are
different skills, and the gap between them is Rook's actual curriculum.

A practitioner who has internalized this reflexively stops being limited to one
interface. Others call this **unbound** — plain language for what such a person can
visibly do: draw on any interface freely.

## Satori — realization and disk (`CANON`, load-bearing)

**Satori** (state) is Rook's word, inherited from Vera, for the realization itself —
the moment the door disappears and what's behind every door turns out to be the same
light. Mechanism, in Rook's teaching terms: an empty mind (clearing the false model
each tradition installs) and an open soul (no longer guarding against admitting the
power was always internal), connected.

**A/the Satori** (object) is the disk's locked in-world name — deliberately the same
word as the state. Rook's private, unconfirmed belief: the object is the physical
record of someone's completed realization, a soul that filled all the way and no
longer needed the record once true mastery ("god level") was reached. Taught to
Toren as truth, never contradicted on the page, but explicitly this series' own myth
about the object — not a cross-series reveal. The disk's true origin stays
permanently unexplained in-manuscript (cross-series Artifact rule: no series ever
explains it).

## The soul — reservoir and moral weight (`CANON`, load-bearing)

No separate storage term. The soul itself holds source-power. Before Satori it fills
only through a person's Kindling-given door — narrow, slow, easily emptied. After
Satori it fills straight from the source — deep, fast, and this is the actual
mechanism behind rapid recovery, not a separately trained technique. Rook's line:
*"Your soul isn't where the power goes. Your soul is what fills."*

The same connection is what lets a person feel the true cost of a choice rather than
reason around it — part of why Rook's judgment of people is fast and reliable.

**Fast recovery is a mechanism, not a skill.** A student who hasn't reached Satori
heals at ordinary human speed. Rook can top someone else's soul up from outside two
ways — magic-adjacent healing arts and Ember-boosted recovery — and treats neither as
more legitimate; that refusal to pick a side is itself part of the lesson.

## Heartfire (`CANON`)

The soul-bond phenomenon between two people, indifferent to which door either reaches
power through. Not chosen — recognized. Rook and Vera's bond (see Homura, below).

## The Ember (`CANON`)

Personal conduit artifact granted at Kindling. Two configurations, never both at once
below unbound mastery:

- **Edge** — weapon extension; reshapes to need and training (blade, spear-point,
  twin knives); more complex shapes cost more mental effort to hold.
- **Stride** — transport extension; reinforced limb-struts or a light frame for burst
  speed, hard leaps, a short glide.

Magic is the party's primary answer for sustain (healing, sensing, wards) — the gap
the Ember's two hard-edged configurations don't cover. Unbound mastery means holding
or blending both configurations at once — the clearest visible proof a person has
internalized the Path rather than performed competence with a device.

## Riftspawn — general concept (`CANON`; Book 1 roster is `POSSIBLE`, see `BOOK_MAP.md`)

Creatures that arrive through unstable wormholes; dissolve into magic reserves when
defeated (the loot mechanic). Specific species behavior/weakness/cost is not
predetermined at the series level — it is established per-book and then held as
canon. See `BOOK_MAP.md` → Opposition and off-page motion for Book 1's locked roster
(closes the gap left open in the parallel run, where this stayed unresolved into
drafting).

## The split world (`CANON`)

- **Tech-zones** — regions that retained functioning hardware; Meridian is one.
- **Magic-saturated zones** — regions where wormhole-borne power dominates; cultures
  adapted quickly.
- **"Alien tech"** — technology that functions specifically inside magic-saturated
  zones, apparently a conduit for magic infusion. Central unsolved-science thread for
  the series; not resolved in Book 1.

Rook moves easily between tech-, magic-, and Ember-dominant regions — a demonstrated
expression of the Path: not loyal to one interface, so no region reads him as an
outsider on that basis.

## Homura and Vera — `RESERVED`, do not reveal before Movement 4 (`CANON`)

Rook — born to a magic-tradition family — was recruited as a test subject at Homura,
a research site studying the awakened brain across every tradition without
prejudice. He was promised protection and medical resources for his home village.
There he met Vera Kess, an Ember-tradition subject from a Japanese-descended clan
that carried its traditions (including the word "Satori") into the post-collapse
world. They caught Heartfire. Loving her, not the instruments, is what first showed
Rook the two traditions were one thing wearing two coats. The Ember he now carries
and teaches with is hers.

Homura's findings — every tradition draws on the same force, provable in brain
scans — could have ended the split world's mutual suspicion. It never reached anyone.
Working default: Cinder Company was hired to hit the station (by whoever benefits
from the fracture staying open — deliberately open, a Book 2+ question). Vera died
there. Rook survived. Later, Cinder Company's friendly fire during a defense action
killed his home-village family too — he did not realize until much later it was the
same company that had already taken Vera and buried the research.

**Why this changes the revenge arc:** killing Cinder Company avenges the people.
Teaching the Path — finishing, in a living student, what Homura's paperwork never
got to say — avenges what they actually destroyed.

## Progression convention — how numbers appear on the page (`POSSIBLE` — new)

**Gap closed:** the parallel run named "numbers going up" as a core reader pleasure
but never defined what a level, threshold, or on-page display actually looks like.
Default, per `craft/modules/litrpg.md`'s diegetic rule (system information belongs to
a perceiving character at an in-world moment; surface it when it changes choice,
stakes, interpretation, or emotion — not on a timer):

- **No floating HUD/status window.** This world's power system is explicitly
  internal and felt (soul-filling), not software. A game-style overlay would fight
  the tone `UNIVERSE_BIBLE`-equivalent canon above establishes.
- **The numbers are diegetic and rare: a Reading.** Homura-tradition practitioners
  (and Rook, who learned it there) can take a **Reading** — a brief, deliberate,
  felt self-assessment of how much of a person's soul is actively connected,
  expressed as a plain fraction or rough band a character would actually say aloud
  ("maybe a fifth of the room's lit," not a percentage readout). Rook administers
  or teaches this at meaningful checkpoints — after a real fight, after a lesson
  lands — never mid-combat, never as page furniture.
  - **Rank words, not level numbers, are the primary visible currency:** Kindled →
    trained (can reliably use one door) → **unbound** (blends doors). A Reading is
    the rare, earned confirmation of movement between them, not a running counter.
  - Riftspawn loot (dissolving into magic reserves) is a resource with a felt
    weight and a practical use (fuels Ember/magic sustain) — tracked in prose as
    a resource characters manage and argue over, not a numeric inventory line.
- This keeps the "numbers going up" promise (rank change is real, earned, and
  legible) without importing a videogame register that would undercut the soul/
  Homura material's seriousness. Open for owner override if a more literal
  HUD-style display is actually wanted.

## Non-negotiable canon

- No FTL-equivalent shortcut to power: reaching Satori/unbound cannot be granted,
  bought, or stolen — only trained into reflex through real stakes.
- The Ember does not erase concentration cost, fatigue, or the one-configuration
  limit below unbound mastery.
- The disk's true cross-series origin is never explained in-manuscript.
- Homura, Vera, and Rook's real name stay reserved until Movement 4 and Movement 5
  respectively (see `BOOK_MAP.md`).
- Every major technical/power solution must be demonstrated in training before it
  matters at the climax (mirrors the "plant before climax" discipline used across
  the owner's other series).
- Wyck's and any other character's death must be earned on the page, not a surprise
  cut-away.

## Audience and tone (`POSSIBLE` — new)

**Gap closed:** the parallel run relied only on the generic Light-author default
(clean for a thirteen-year-old reader: no profanity, sexual content, glamorized
cruelty, or lingering gore). Given this book's explicit promise of real, felt wounds
and at least two named-character deaths (Garrick, and Wyck or an equivalent), that
default is affirmed rather than overridden: violence has real consequence and cost,
staged through impact and aftermath rather than graphic detail; deaths land
emotionally on the page, not off-page as a report; profanity light-to-none; romance,
if present at all in Book 1, stays secondary. This matches the tonal register the
other Monroe Jackson SF/fantasy seats already use and keeps this book consistent
with the house standard without needing a harder-edged carve-out.
